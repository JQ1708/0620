The Three States of Matter

According to the Kinetic Theory, all matter is made of tiny particles that are in constant, random motion. The amount of energy these particles have determines their state.

Solid

  • Arrangement: Regular lattice
  • Energy: Low
  • Movement: Vibrate in fixed positions
  • Properties: Fixed shape & volume
  • Density: High

Liquid

  • Arrangement: Randomly arranged
  • Energy: More than solids
  • Movement: Slide past each other
  • Properties: Fixed volume, takes shape of container
  • Density: High (usually less than solid)

Gas

  • Arrangement: Far apart, random
  • Energy: Highest energy
  • Movement: Move quickly (~500 m/s)
  • Properties: No fixed shape or volume
  • Density: Very low; can be compressed

These changes happen when particles gain or lose thermal energy. During melting and boiling, energy is absorbed to overcome forces between particles, so the temperature does not rise until the change is complete. This is shown as a plateau on a heating curve.

Gas Pressure & Temperature

Gas pressure is caused by particles colliding with the walls of their container. The temperature of a gas is a measure of the average kinetic energy of its particles. Changing the temperature has a direct effect on the pressure.

Heating a Gas in a Sealed Container

Increasing the temperature leads to an increase in pressure because:

  • Particles gain kinetic energy.
  • They move faster and more randomly.
  • They collide with the container walls more frequently and with more force.

Diffusion

Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration due to their random movement.

(Note: Diffusion does not occur in solids as the particles are fixed in place and can only vibrate.)

Key Factor: Lighter particles (lower Mᵣ) diffuse faster than heavier particles at the same temperature.

Ammonia (NH₃) vs. Hydrogen Chloride (HCl)

NH₃ (Mᵣ=17) is lighter than HCl (Mᵣ=36.5). It diffuses faster, so the white ring of NH₄Cl forms closer to the HCl end.
