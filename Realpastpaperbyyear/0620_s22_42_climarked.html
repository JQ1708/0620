The symbols of the elements of Period 3 of the Periodic Table are shown.
Na Mg Al Si P S Cl Ar
Answer the following questions about these elements.
Each symbol may be used once, more than once or not at all.
Write the symbol of the element which:
(a) forms a stable ion with a 2+ charge [1]
The element is Mg (magnesium).

Recap

Magnesium is in Group II of the Periodic Table. Elements in Group II form ions with a 2+ charge because they lose two electrons to achieve a stable outer shell.
(b) is the least reactive in the period [1]
The element is Ar (argon).

Recap

Argon is a noble gas (Group VIII/0). Noble gases are unreactive because they have a full outer shell of electrons, which is a stable electronic configuration.

Recap

Chlorine is added during water treatment (chlorination) to kill bacteria.
(d) forms an oxide which is the main impurity in iron ore [1]

Recap

Hematite (iron ore) often contains silicon(IV) oxide (sand) as an impurity. Calcium oxide reacts with silicon(IV) oxide to form slag.
(e) is an important component of fertilisers [1]
The element is P (phosphorus).

Recap

Crop plants need three major elements for healthy growth: nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium. Fertilisers are often called NPK fertilisers.
(f) is stored under oil [1]
The element is Na (sodium).

Recap

Group I metals like sodium are very reactive with oxygen and moisture in the air, so they are stored under oil to prevent reaction.
(g) is used in food containers [1]

Recap

Aluminium is used for food containers because of its resistance to corrosion due to a protective oxide layer.
(h) is found in the ore zinc blende. [1]

Recap

Zinc blende is zinc sulfide, ZnS. Ores are rocks containing a metal compound from which a metal can be extracted.

Q2 | 0620/42/M/J/22 | Acids, Bases and Salts
2 Calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂, is slightly soluble in water.
(a) Calcium hydroxide can be made by the reaction of calcium with water.
(i) Write the chemical equation for this reaction. [2]
Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂.

Recap

Metals high in the reactivity series (like calcium) react with cold water to produce a metal hydroxide and hydrogen gas.
(ii) Name another substance that reacts with water to form calcium hydroxide. [1]
Calcium oxide.

Recap

Some metal oxides (like calcium oxide) react with water to produce alkaline hydroxides. Calcium oxide reacts with water to form calcium hydroxide (slaked lime).
(b) When calcium hydroxide dissolves in water, it dissociates into ions and forms a weakly alkaline solution.
(i) Suggest the pH of aqueous calcium hydroxide. [1]
Any value between 8 and 12 (e.g., pH 10).

Recap

The pH scale is used to show how acidic or alkaline a substance is. Solutions above pH 7 are alkaline. Weak alkalis have pH values closer to 7 than strong alkalis (pH 13-14).
(ii) Give the formula of the ion responsible for making the solution alkaline. [1]
OH⁻.

Recap

Alkalis contain hydroxide ions, OH⁻, in aqueous solution.
(c) Limewater is a saturated solution of calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂(aq).
(i) Name the gas limewater is used to test for. [1]
Carbon dioxide.

Recap

Carbon dioxide turns limewater milky. This is a standard test for CO₂ gas.
(ii) Suggest what is meant by the term saturated solution. [2]
A solution that can dissolve no more solute at a given temperature.

Recap

A saturated solution contains the maximum concentration of solute dissolved in a solvent at a particular temperature.
(iii) Describe how you would make a sample of limewater starting with solid calcium hydroxide. [2]
Add excess solid calcium hydroxide to water, shake/mix, and then filter the mixture.

Recap

To make a saturated solution from a slightly soluble solid, add excess solid to ensure saturation, then filter to remove the undissolved solid (residue), leaving the clear solution (filtrate).
(iv) Describe how you would test for the presence of calcium ions in a sample of limewater.
test
observations [3]
Test: Add aqueous sodium hydroxide.
Observations: A white precipitate forms which remains insoluble in excess.

Recap

Ions of calcium give a white precipitate with sodium hydroxide which is insoluble in excess. Zinc and aluminium also give white precipitates but they dissolve in excess NaOH.
(d) A 25.0 cm³ sample of limewater is placed in a conical flask. The concentration of Ca(OH)₂ in the limewater is determined by titration with dilute hydrochloric acid, HCl.
(i) Name the item of apparatus used to measure the volume of acid in this titration. [1]
Burette.

Recap

A burette is used for measuring variable volumes of liquids accurately (usually in titrations). A volumetric pipette is used for fixed volumes.
(ii) State the type of reaction which takes place. [1]
Neutralisation.

Recap

Neutralisation is the reaction between an acid and a base (or alkali) to form a salt and water. H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l).
(iii) As well as limewater and dilute hydrochloric acid, state what other type of substance must be added to the conical flask. [1]
Indicator.

Recap

An indicator (e.g., methyl orange or thymolphthalein) is required in an acid-base titration to detect the end point via a colour change.
(iv) The equation for the reaction is shown.
Ca(OH)₂ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + 2H₂O
20.0 cm³ of 0.0500 mol/dm³ HCl reacts with the 25.0 cm³ of Ca(OH)₂.
Determine the concentration of Ca(OH)₂ in g/dm³. Use the following steps.

Calculate the number of moles in 20.0 cm³ of 0.0500 mol/dm³ HCl.
determine the number of moles of Ca(OH)₂ in 25.0 cm³ of the limewater.
Calculate the concentration of Ca(OH)₂ in mol/dm³.
Determine the concentration of Ca(OH)₂ in g/dm³ [5]
Moles HCl = 0.0500 x 0.020 = 0.001 mol.
Moles Ca(OH)₂ = 0.001 / 2 = 0.0005 mol.
Conc Ca(OH)₂ (mol/dm³) = 0.0005 / 0.025 = 0.02 mol/dm³.
Mr of Ca(OH)₂ = 40 + 2(16+1) = 74.
Conc Ca(OH)₂ (g/dm³) = 0.02 x 74 = 1.48 g/dm³.

Recap

Concentration (mol/dm³) = moles / volume (dm³). Concentration (g/dm³) = concentration (mol/dm³) x Mr (relative formula mass). Remember to convert cm³ to dm³ by dividing by 1000.

Q3 | 0620/42/M/J/22 | Atoms and Transition Elements
3 Transition elements are found in the middle block of the Periodic Table.
(a) Chromium has several isotopes. Manganese has only one isotope.
(i) State what is meant by the term isotopes. [2]
Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but a different number of neutrons.

Recap

Isotopes have the same proton number but different nucleon (mass) numbers. They have the same chemical properties because they have the same electronic configuration.
(ii) State the nucleon number of manganese. [1]
55.

Recap

Nucleon number (mass number) is the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus. It is found in the Periodic Table.
(iii) Complete the table to show the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in a ⁵²Cr³⁺ ion. [3]
(24 is the atomic number)
Protons: 24.
Neutrons: 28 (52 - 24).
Electrons: 21 (24 - 3).

Recap

Protons = atomic number. Neutrons = Mass number - Proton number. Electrons = Protons - charge (for positive ions, electrons are lost).
(b) One chemical property of transition elements is that they form coloured compounds.
(i) Give the colours of the following hydrated salts.
• hydrated copper(II) sulfate
• hydrated cobalt(II) chloride [2]
Hydrated copper(II) sulfate: blue.
Hydrated cobalt(II) chloride: pink.

Recap

Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate (white) turns blue when water is added. Anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride (blue) turns pink when water is added. These are tests for water.
(ii) State two other chemical properties of transition elements. [2]
1. They act as catalysts.
2. They have variable oxidation states.

Recap

Transition elements form coloured compounds, have variable oxidation states (e.g., Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺), and often act as catalysts.
(c) Transition elements and Group I elements are metals. They share many physical properties including the ability to:
• conduct electricity
• be hammered into shape.
(i) Explain why transition elements and Group I elements conduct electricity. [1]
Because of mobile electrons (or delocalised electrons).

Recap

Metals conduct electricity because they contain a sea of delocalised electrons that are free to move throughout the metallic lattice.
(ii) State the property that describes a material which can be hammered into shape. [1]
Malleability (or malleable).

Recap

Metals are malleable because the layers of positive ions can slide over each other when a force is applied, without breaking the metallic bond.
(d) Transition elements and Group I elements differ in other physical properties. Transition elements are harder and stronger than Group I elements.
Describe two other ways in which the physical properties of transition elements differ from Group I elements. [2]
1. Higher density.
2. Higher melting points.

Recap

Transition elements generally have high melting points and high densities compared to Group I metals (alkali metals), which are soft and have low densities.

Q4 | 0620/42/M/J/22 | Bonding
4 Fluorine and chlorine are halogens.
(a) Suggest the appearance of fluorine. [1]
Pale yellow gas.

Recap

Group VII elements (halogens) show a trend in color density. Fluorine is a pale yellow gas, chlorine is a yellow-green gas, bromine is a red-brown liquid.
(b) Fluorine reacts with sulfur to form a compound which has 25.2% sulfur by mass and a relative molecular mass of 254.
Determine the molecular formula of this compound. [3]
Moles S = 25.2 / 32 = 0.7875. Moles F = 74.8 / 19 = 3.94.
Ratio S:F is 1:5, so empirical formula is SF₅.
Relative mass of SF₅ = 32 + (5x19) = 127.
254 / 127 = 2.
Molecular formula = S₂F₁₀.

Recap

Empirical formula is the simplest whole number ratio of atoms. Molecular formula shows the actual number of atoms. Use mass/Ar to find moles, then find the ratio.
(c) Nitrogen trichloride, NCl₃, is a covalent compound.
Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement in a molecule of NCl₃.
Show outer electrons only. [3]
N should share 1 pair of electrons with each Cl.
N should have 2 non-bonding electrons.
Each Cl should have 6 non-bonding electrons.

Recap

In covalent bonding, atoms share pairs of electrons to achieve a stable noble gas configuration (usually 8 electrons in the outer shell).
(d) Lithium chloride, LiCl, is an ionic compound.
Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement and charges of the ions in lithium chloride. [3]
Li ion: 2 crosses in the first shell (no outer shell electrons shown), charge +.
Cl ion: 7 dots and 1 cross in the outer shell (8 total), charge -.

Recap

Ionic bonding involves the transfer of electrons. Positive ions (cations) form by electron loss, negative ions (anions) form by electron gain.
(e) Explain, in terms of attractive forces between particles, why LiCl is a solid at room temperature but NCl₃ is a liquid with a relatively low boiling point. [3]
LiCl has strong ionic bonds (electrostatic attraction) between ions in a giant lattice.
NCl₃ has weak attraction (intermolecular forces) between molecules.
More energy is required to break the bonds in LiCl.

Recap

Ionic compounds have high melting points due to strong electrostatic forces. Simple molecular compounds have low melting points due to weak intermolecular forces.
