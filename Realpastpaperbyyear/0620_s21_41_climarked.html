Question 1

0620/41/M/J/21 Q1 | Topics: Industrial Processes, Separation Techniques

Give the name of the process that is used:

(a) to produce ammonia from nitrogen [1]

The process is the Haber process.

Recap

The Haber process is the industrial manufacture of ammonia (NH3) from nitrogen and hydrogen. The nitrogen is sourced from the air, and hydrogen often comes from reacting methane with steam. It is a reversible reaction (N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3) that uses an iron catalyst.

(b) to separate nitrogen from liquid air [1]

This process is fractional distillation.

Recap

Fractional distillation is used to separate a mixture of liquids with different boiling points. Liquid air is a mixture of nitrogen and oxygen, which have different boiling points, allowing them to be separated as they evaporate and condense at different temperatures in a fractionating column.

(c) to produce bromine from molten lead(II) bromide [1]

The process is electrolysis.

Recap

Electrolysis is the decomposition (breakdown) of an ionic compound, like lead(II) bromide, when it is molten or in aqueous solution. This breakdown is caused by the passage of an electric current.

(d) to separate an undissolved solid from an aqueous solution [1]

The process is filtration.

Recap

Filtration is a method used to separate an insoluble solid from a liquid. The liquid that passes through the filter paper is called the filtrate, and the solid left behind is the residue.

(e) to produce amino acids from proteins [1]

The process is hydrolysis.

Recap

Hydrolysis is the chemical breakdown of a compound, such as a protein or a polyester, by reaction with water. This process is often sped up by heating with an acid or an alkali.

(f) to separate a mixture of amino acids. [1]

The process is chromatography.

Recap

Paper chromatography is used to separate a mixture of soluble substances (like amino acids or dyes) based on their different affinities for the stationary phase (paper) and the mobile phase (solvent). Since amino acids are colourless, a locating agent is sprayed on the chromatogram to make the spots visible.

Complete the table to:

  • deduce the number of protons, electrons and neutrons in the magnesium atom and copper ion shown
  • identify the atom or ion represented by the final row. [5]
number of protons number of electrons number of neutrons
2512Mg 12
6529Cu2+ 36
... 17 18 20

For 2512Mg:

  • The number of protons is the proton number (bottom number), which is 12.
  • In a neutral atom, the number of electrons equals the number of protons, so electrons = 12.
  • The number of neutrons is the mass number (top number) minus the proton number: 25 - 12 = 13.

Recap

The proton number (or atomic number) defines the element. The mass number (or nucleon number) is the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus. In a neutral atom, protons = electrons.

For 6529Cu2+:

  • The number of protons is the proton number (bottom number), which is 29.
  • This is an ion with a 2+ charge, meaning it has lost 2 electrons. Number of electrons = 29 (protons) - 2 = 27.
  • The number of neutrons is given as 36. (Check: 29 protons + 36 neutrons = 65, which matches the mass number).

Recap

A positive ion (a cation) is formed when a neutral atom loses electrons. The charge on the ion tells you how many electrons were lost. A Cu2+ ion has lost two electrons.

For the final row:

  • The number of protons is 17, which identifies the element as Chlorine (Cl).
  • The mass number = protons + neutrons = 17 + 20 = 37.
  • The particle has 18 electrons but only 17 protons. It has gained 1 electron.
  • This makes it an ion with a charge of 1-.
  • The particle is the chloride ion, 3717Cl-.

Recap

A negative ion (an anion) is formed when a neutral atom gains electrons. Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same proton number) but with different numbers of neutrons (e.g., chlorine-35 and chlorine-37).

0620/41/M/J/21 Q3 | Topics: Bonding, Electrolysis, Structure

Potassium reacts with chlorine to form potassium chloride, KCl.

(a) Write a chemical equation for this reaction. [2]

2K + Cl2 → 2KCl

Recap

This is a synthesis reaction between a Group I metal and a Group VII non-metal (halogen). The equation must be balanced, ensuring there are the same number of atoms of each element on both sides. Chlorine is diatomic (Cl2).

(b) Potassium chloride is an ionic compound. Complete the diagram to show the electron arrangement in the outer shells of the ions present in potassium chloride. Give the charges on both ions. [3]

The diagram should show the potassium ion (K) with a + charge and a full outer shell of 8 electrons. The chloride ion (Cl) should have a - charge and a full outer shell of 8 electrons, showing one electron gained from potassium (e.g., 7 dots and 1 cross).

Recap

An ionic bond forms from the transfer of electrons from a metal atom to a non-metal atom. This results in oppositely charged ions (a cation and an anion) which are held together by strong electrostatic forces of attraction. Both ions achieve a stable noble gas electron configuration.

(c) Molten potassium chloride undergoes electrolysis.
(i) State what is meant by the term electrolysis. [2]

Electrolysis is the breakdown (decomposition) of an ionic compound, when molten or in aqueous solution, by the passage of an electric current.

Recap

An electrolyte is the molten or aqueous ionic compound that conducts electricity and is decomposed. The anode is the positive electrode, and the cathode is the negative electrode. Ionic solids cannot be electrolysed because their ions are not free to move.

(c)(ii) Name the products formed at the positive electrode (anode) and negative electrode (cathode) when molten potassium chloride undergoes electrolysis. [2]

Anode (positive) product: chlorine. Cathode (negative) product: potassium.

Recap

In the electrolysis of a molten binary ionic compound, the metal is formed at the cathode (negative electrode) because the positive K+ ions are attracted there and gain electrons (reduction). The non-metal is formed at the anode (positive electrode) because the negative Cl- ions are attracted there and lose electrons (oxidation).

(d) Concentrated aqueous potassium chloride undergoes electrolysis.
(i) Write an ionic half-equation for the reaction at the negative electrode (cathode). [2]

2H+ + 2e- → H2 (or 2H2O + 2e- → H2 + 2OH-)

Recap

In the electrolysis of aqueous solutions, water provides H+ and OH- ions. At the cathode, if the metal (K) is more reactive than hydrogen, hydrogen gas will be produced from the H+ ions.

(d)(ii) Name the product formed at the positive electrode (anode). [1]

Chlorine.

Recap

At the anode, if a halide (like Cl-) is present in concentrated solution, the halogen (Cl2) is produced. If the solution were dilute, oxygen (from OH-) would be produced.

(d)(iii) Name the potassium compound that remains in the solution after electrolysis. [1]

Potassium hydroxide (KOH).

Recap

As H+ ions are removed (as H2) and Cl- ions are removed (as Cl2), the ions remaining in the solution are K+ and OH- (from water). These form the alkali potassium hydroxide.

(e) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement in a molecule of chlorine, Cl2. Show the outer electrons only. [1]

The diagram should show two Cl atoms, each sharing one pair of electrons to form a single covalent bond. Each Cl atom must also have 6 non-bonding electrons (3 lone pairs) in its outer shell.

Recap

A covalent bond is a shared pair of electrons between two non-metal atoms. This allows both atoms to achieve a stable, full outer shell of electrons, like a noble gas. Chlorine is diatomic (Cl2).

(f) The melting points and boiling points of chlorine and potassium chloride are shown.

melting point / °Cboiling point / °C
chlorine–101–35
potassium chloride7701500

(i) Deduce the physical state of chlorine at –75 °C. Use the data in the table to explain your answer. [2]

Physical state: Liquid.
Explanation: This is because -75 °C is above the melting point (–101 °C) but below the boiling point (–35 °C).

Recap

A substance is a solid at temperatures below its melting point. It is a liquid at temperatures between its melting point and boiling point. It is a gas at temperatures above its boiling point.

(f)(ii) Explain, in terms of structure and bonding, why potassium chloride has a much higher melting point than chlorine. [3]

  • Particles: Potassium chloride (KCl) is made of ions (K+ and Cl-). Chlorine (Cl2) is made of neutral molecules.
  • Forces: In KCl, there are strong electrostatic forces of attraction (ionic bonds) between the oppositely charged ions in a giant ionic lattice. In Cl2, there are only weak intermolecular forces between the Cl2 molecules.
  • Energy: A large amount of energy is needed to overcome the strong ionic bonds in KCl, resulting in a high melting point. Only a small amount of energy is needed to overcome the weak intermolecular forces in Cl2, resulting in a low melting point.

Recap

The physical properties of a substance, like its melting point, depend on its structure and bonding. Giant structures (ionic, metallic, giant covalent) have high melting points. Simple molecular structures have low melting points because the intermolecular forces are weak, even if the covalent bonds *within* the molecule are strong.

0620/41/M/J/21 Q4 | Topics: Reversible Reactions, Equilibrium

Dinitrogen tetroxide, N2O4, decomposes into nitrogen dioxide, NO2. The reaction is reversible.

N2O4(g) ⇌ 2NO2(g)
colourless gas      brown gas

A gas syringe containing a mixture of dinitrogen tetroxide and nitrogen dioxide gases was sealed and heated. After reaching equilibrium the mixture was a pale brown colour.

(a) State what is meant by the term equilibrium. [2]

Equilibrium is reached in a reversible reaction when the rate of the forward reaction is equal to the rate of the reverse reaction, and the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant (in a closed system).

Recap

A reversible reaction (shown by the ⇋ symbol) is one where the products can react to re-form the original reactants. An equilibrium can only be established in a closed system, where no substances can enter or leave.

(b) The plunger of the gas syringe is pushed in. The temperature does not change. The mixture initially turns darker brown. After a few seconds the mixture turns lighter brown because the equilibrium shifts to the left.
(i) Explain why the mixture initially turns darker brown. [1]

When the plunger is pushed in, the volume decreases, which increases the concentration of all gases inside. The increased concentration of the brown NO2 gas causes the mixture to appear darker, before the equilibrium has time to shift.

Recap

For gases, pressure and volume are inversely related. Decreasing the volume increases the pressure and concentration (number of particles per unit volume) of the gas particles.

(b)(ii) Explain why the position of equilibrium shifts to the left. [1]

The equilibrium shifts to oppose the change (Le Chatelier's Principle). The increase in pressure is opposed by the equilibrium shifting to the side with fewer moles of gas. The left side has 1 mole of gas (N2O4), while the right side has 2 moles (2NO2). Therefore, the equilibrium shifts to the left (the side with fewer moles) to reduce the pressure.

Recap

Le Chatelier's Principle states that if a change in conditions (concentration, pressure, or temperature) is applied to a system at equilibrium, the position of equilibrium will shift to oppose that change. Increasing pressure always favours the side with fewer gas moles.

(c) The forward reaction is endothermic.
(i) State what happens to the position of equilibrium when the temperature of the mixture is increased. [1]

The position of equilibrium shifts to the right.

Recap

The forward reaction is stated to be endothermic (it absorbs heat, ΔH is positive). When the temperature is increased, the equilibrium shifts to oppose the change by favouring the endothermic direction (the forward reaction) to absorb the added heat.

(c)(ii) State what happens to the rate of the forward reaction and the rate of the backward reaction when the temperature of the mixture is increased. [2]

Rate of the forward reaction: increases.
Rate of the backward reaction: increases.

Recap

Increasing the temperature increases the rate of reaction for *both* the forward and backward reactions. This is because particles have more kinetic energy, leading to more frequent collisions and, more importantly, a greater proportion of collisions having energy equal to or greater than the activation energy (more successful collisions).

0620/41/M/J/21 Q5 | Topics: Salts, Stoichiometry, Moles

This question is about salts.

(a) Salts that are insoluble in water are made by precipitation. Lead(II) iodide, PbI2, is insoluble in water. All nitrates are soluble in water. All sodium salts are soluble in water. You are provided with solid lead(II) nitrate, Pb(NO3)2, and solid sodium iodide, NaI. Describe how you would make a pure sample of lead(II) iodide by precipitation. [5]

  1. Step 1: Dissolve the solid lead(II) nitrate in distilled water to make a solution.
  2. Step 2: Dissolve the solid sodium iodide in distilled water to make a separate solution.
  3. Step 3: Mix the two solutions together. A precipitate (solid) of lead(II) iodide will form.
  4. Step 4: Filter the mixture using filter paper and a funnel to separate the solid lead(II) iodide (the residue).
  5. Step 5: Wash the residue on the filter paper with distilled water to remove any soluble impurities.
  6. Step 6: Dry the solid, for example by pressing it between filter papers.
Chemical equation: Pb(NO3)2(aq) + 2NaI(aq) → PbI2(s) + 2NaNO3(aq)

Recap

Insoluble salts are made by precipitation. This involves mixing solutions of two soluble salts. The solubility rules help predict which salts are insoluble. For example, all nitrates are soluble, but lead(II) halides (like PbI2) are insoluble.

(b) Nitrates decompose when heated.
(i) When hydrated zinc nitrate is heated, oxygen gas is given off. Describe a test for oxygen. [2]

Test: Insert a glowing splint into a test tube of the gas.
Observations: The glowing splint will relight.

Recap

This is the standard chemical test for oxygen gas. The test for hydrogen is a 'squeaky pop' with a lit splint, and the test for carbon dioxide is turning limewater milky.

(b)(ii) Complete the equation for the decomposition of hydrated zinc nitrate. [2]
2Zn(NO3)2·6H2O → .....ZnO + .....NO2 + O2 + .....H2O

2Zn(NO3)2·6H2O → 2ZnO + 4NO2 + O2 + 12H2O

Recap

Balancing equations requires ensuring the number of atoms of each element is identical on both the reactant and product sides of the equation. Here, you must balance Zn, N, O, and H atoms.

(c) Some sulfates are hydrated.
(i) ...Describe how the student can check that all the water has been given off. [2]

The student should heat the remaining solid again, allow it to cool, and weigh it. This process of heating, cooling, and weighing must be repeated until the mass becomes constant. This indicates that all the water of crystallisation has been removed.

Recap

This experimental technique is known as 'heating to constant mass'. It is used to ensure that a thermal decomposition or dehydration reaction is complete before the final mass is recorded.

(c)(ii) In an experiment, 1.61 g of Na2SO4·xH2O is heated until all the water is given off. The mass of Na2SO4 remaining is 0.71 g. [Mr: Na2SO4, 142; H2O, 18] Determine the value of x... [4]


Calculate the number of moles of Na2SO4 remaining.
Moles = mass / Mr = 0.71 g / 142 g/mol = 0.005 mol.

Calculate the mass of H2O given off.
Mass of H2O = Initial mass - Final mass = 1.61 g - 0.71 g = 0.90 g.

Calculate the number of moles of H2O given off.
Moles = mass / Mr = 0.90 g / 18 g/mol = 0.05 mol.

Determine the value of x.
This is the molar ratio of H2O to Na2SO4.
Ratio = Moles of H2O / Moles of Na2SO4 = 0.05 mol / 0.005 mol = 10.
x = 10.

Recap

This is a stoichiometry calculation to find the formula of a hydrated salt. The 'x' represents the water of crystallisation. The method involves finding the mole ratio of the water to the anhydrous salt. The formula for calculating moles is: moles = mass / Mr.

0620/41/M/J/21 Q6 | Topics: Metal Extraction, Reactivity, Redox

This question is about iron.

(a) Iron is extracted from its main ore in a blast furnace.
(i) Name the main ore of iron used in the blast furnace. [1]

Hematite (which is largely iron(III) oxide, Fe2O3).

Recap

An ore is a naturally occurring rock from which a metal can be economically extracted. The main ore of iron is hematite, and the main ore of aluminium is bauxite.

(a)(ii) Name the substance that enters the blast furnace at A. [1]

Hot air.

Recap

Hot air is blown into the blast furnace to provide oxygen for the combustion of coke (carbon), which raises the temperature and produces the reducing agent, carbon monoxide.

(a)(iii) Name the substance that leaves the blast furnace at B. [1]

Molten slag (mostly calcium silicate).

Recap

Limestone (calcium carbonate) is added to the furnace to remove acidic impurities like silicon(IV) oxide. It decomposes to calcium oxide (a base), which reacts with the acidic impurity to form molten slag (CaSiO3). Slag is less dense than molten iron and floats on top.

(a)(iv) Give two reasons for using coke in the blast furnace. [2]

  1. It acts as a fuel. It burns in the hot air (combustion) to release a large amount of heat, raising the furnace temperature. (C + O2 → CO2)
  2. It produces the reducing agent. It reacts with carbon dioxide at high temperatures to form carbon monoxide (CO), which then reduces the iron ore. (CO2 + C → 2CO)

Recap

The reduction of iron(III) oxide is carried out by carbon monoxide in the main reaction of the blast furnace: Fe2O3 + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO2.

(b) Another ore of iron is iron pyrites, FeS2. Iron pyrites contains the positive ion, Fe2+. Deduce the formula of the negative ion in FeS2. [1]

The iron ion is Fe2+. Since the compound FeS2 is neutral, the negative ion must balance the +2 charge. The negative ion is the disulfide ion, which has the formula S22-.

Recap

The overall charge of an ionic compound must be zero. The total positive charge from the cations must balance the total negative charge from the anions. In this case, (+2) + (-2) = 0.

(c) Iron is a transition element. A list of properties of iron is shown.

  • Iron is a good conductor of electricity.
  • Iron forms soluble salts.
  • Iron forms coloured compounds.
  • Iron has variable oxidation states.
  • Iron acts as a catalyst.
  • Iron forms a basic oxide.
(i) Give two properties from the list in which iron differs from Group I elements. [2]

Properties differing from Group I:

  • Iron forms coloured compounds.
  • Iron has variable oxidation states.
    • (OR: Iron acts as a catalyst.)

Recap

Transition elements (like iron) are metals with characteristic properties: they form coloured compounds, have variable oxidation states (e.g., Fe2+ and Fe3+), and often act as catalysts. Group I elements form white compounds and have a fixed +1 oxidation state.

(c)(ii) Give two properties from the list in which iron is similar to Group I elements. [2]

Properties similar to Group I:

  • Iron is a good conductor of electricity.
  • Iron forms a basic oxide.
    • (OR: Iron forms soluble salts.)

Recap

Both transition elements and Group I elements are metals. Typical metallic properties include being good conductors of electricity and heat, being malleable and ductile, and forming basic oxides (which react with acids).

(d) Steel consists mainly of iron. Iron forms rust when it reacts with water and oxygen.
(i) Explain how the magnesium blocks prevent iron from rusting. [2]

This method is called sacrificial protection. Magnesium is more reactive than iron (it is higher in the reactivity series). This means magnesium loses electrons (is oxidised) more readily than iron. The magnesium block corrodes in preference to the iron, thus 'sacrificing' itself to protect the steel boat.

Recap

For rusting to occur, both oxygen and water must be present. Sacrificial protection works by providing a more reactive metal that gets oxidised (loses electrons) instead of the iron. This is a redox reaction.

(d)(ii) Explain why replacing the magnesium blocks with copper blocks will not prevent the bottom of the boat from rusting. [1]

Copper is less reactive than iron. If attached to the steel boat, the more reactive metal (iron) would lose electrons and corrode in preference to the copper, potentially accelerating the rusting of the boat.

Recap

A metal can only sacrificially protect a metal that is less reactive than itself. This is a key application of the reactivity series.

0620/41/M/J/21 Q7 | Topics: Organic Chemistry, Stoichiometry, Polymers

Many organic compounds contain carbon, hydrogen and oxygen only.

(a) An organic compound V has the following composition by mass: C, 48.65%; H, 8.11%; O, 43.24%. Calculate the empirical formula of compound V. [3]

  1. Divide percentage composition by relative atomic mass (Ar) for each element:
    C: 48.65 / 12.0 = 4.054
    H: 8.11 / 1.0 = 8.11
    O: 43.24 / 16.0 = 2.7025
  2. Divide each result by the smallest number (2.7025) to find the simplest ratio:
    C: 4.054 / 2.7025 = 1.5
    H: 8.11 / 2.7025 = 3.0
    O: 2.7025 / 2.7025 = 1.0
  3. Multiply the ratio to get whole numbers (x 2):
    C = 3, H = 6, O = 2
The empirical formula is C3H6O2.

Recap

The empirical formula shows the simplest whole number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound. It is calculated by finding the ratio of moles of each element.

(b) Compound W has the empirical formula CH4O and a relative molecular mass of 32. Calculate the molecular formula of compound W. [1]

  1. Calculate the mass of the empirical formula (CH4O):
    12 (C) + 4x1 (H) + 16 (O) = 32.
  2. Divide the relative molecular mass (Mr) by the empirical formula mass:
    32 / 32 = 1.
  3. The molecular formula is 1x the empirical formula.
The molecular formula is CH4O.

Recap

The molecular formula shows the actual number of atoms in one molecule. It is always a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula.

(c) Compounds X and Y have the same general formula. X and Y are both carboxylic acids. Compound X has the molecular formula C2H4O2. Compound Y has the molecular formula C4H8O2.
(i) Deduce the general formula of compounds X and Y. [1]

CnH2nO2 (or CnH2n+1COOH, where n starts from 1).

Recap

A homologous series, like carboxylic acids, can be represented by a general formula. For C2H4O2, n=2. For C4H8O2, n=4. Both fit the pattern CnH2nO2.

(c)(ii) Draw the structure of compound Y. Show all of the atoms and all of the bonds. Name compound Y. [3]

Name: Butanoic acid.
Structure:

(The structure should show 4 carbons in a chain. The last carbon has a =O and an -O-H. All other carbons are bonded to hydrogens to make a total of 4 bonds per carbon).

Recap

The displayed formula shows every atom and every bond. The prefix 'but-' means 4 carbon atoms. The -oic acid suffix indicates a carboxylic acid, which has the -COOH functional group.

(c)(iii) Give the name used to describe a 'family' of similar compounds with the same general formula... [1]

Homologous series.

Recap

A homologous series is a 'family' of organic compounds that have the same functional group and similar chemical properties. Their physical properties show a gradual trend (e.g., increasing boiling point) as the carbon chain length increases.

(d) Propene is an unsaturated hydrocarbon. The formula of propene is shown. CH3CH=CH2
(i) State the colour change observed when propene is added to aqueous bromine. [1]

From orange (or brown) to colourless.

Recap

This is the test for an unsaturated compound. Alkenes (like propene) contain a C=C double bond. They undergo an addition reaction with aqueous bromine, which decolourises the bromine water.

(d)(ii) Propene can be produced by cracking long chain alkanes... Complete the chemical equation... [2]
C15H32 → .......... + 2C3H6

C15H32 → C9H20 + 2C3H6

Recap

Cracking is the thermal decomposition of a long-chain alkane into a smaller alkane and one or more alkenes. The equation must be balanced for both carbon and hydrogen atoms.

(d)(iii) Propene can be converted into poly(propene). Name the type of polymerisation... [1]

Addition polymerisation.

Recap

Addition polymerisation occurs when many monomer units (which must be alkenes) join together to form a single long-chain molecule called a polymer. No other product is formed.

(d)(iv) Complete the diagram to show a section of poly(propene). [2]

The diagram should show a section of the polymer chain with at least two repeat units. It is a long single-bonded carbon chain. On alternate carbon atoms, there should be a CH3 group, and all other bonds are to hydrogen atoms.

Recap

During addition polymerisation, the double bond in each propene monomer breaks, allowing the monomers to link together to form the poly(propene) chain. The repeat unit is -[CH2-CH(CH3)]-.
