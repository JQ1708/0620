Recap

An element is a substance that contains only one type of atom. All the atoms in an element have the same proton number.

Bauxite is the main ore of aluminium, which means it is a rock containing a high concentration of an aluminium compound (aluminium oxide) from which the metal can be extracted.

Recap

An ionic compound is formed from the electrostatic force of attraction between oppositely charged ions. They are typically formed between metals (like sodium) and non-metals (like chlorine).

(g) a product of respiration [1]

The substance that is a product of respiration is carbon dioxide.

The substance that is a toxic product of incomplete combustion is carbon monoxide.

Recap

Incomplete combustion occurs when carbon-containing fuels burn in a limiting amount of oxygen. It produces carbon monoxide (CO), which is toxic because it reduces the blood's ability to carry oxygen.

The gas produced in the test for nitrate ions is ammonia.

The test for nitrate ions (NO₃⁻) involves warming the sample with sodium hydroxide and aluminium foil. This reduces the nitrate ions to ammonia gas (NH₃), which can be identified because it turns damp red litmus paper blue.

(a) State the meaning of the term electrolyte. [2]

An electrolyte is an ionic compound that, when either molten or in aqueous solution, conducts electricity and is decomposed by it (undergoes electrolysis).

Recap

Electrolysis is the decomposition of an ionic compound, when molten or in an aqueous solution, by the passage of an electric current. The substance that is decomposed is the electrolyte. Solid ionic compounds are not electrolytes because their ions are not free to move.

Reduction occurs at the cathode (Negative electrode). This is the Gain of electrons. In this case, hydrogen ions (H⁺) from the water gain electrons to form hydrogen gas (H₂). The equation must be balanced for both atoms and charge.

(iii) State two reasons why carbon (graphite) is suitable to use as an electrode. [2]

  1. It is chemically inert (unreactive) and will not react with the electrolyte or products.
  2. It is a good conductor of electricity.

Recap

Electrodes must be able to conduct electricity. They should also generally be inert, meaning they don't interfere with the chemical reactions. Graphite (a form of carbon) and platinum are common choices for inert electrodes.

The electron.

Recap

Electric current in metal wires (the external circuit) is a flow of delocalised electrons. In the electrolyte (the solution), the current is carried by the movement of ions.

Lead is a metallic element in Group IV. One of the ores of lead is galena, which is an impure form of lead(II) sulfide, PbS.
Lead also occurs in the ore cerussite, which contains lead(II) carbonate, PbCO₃.

(a) Calculate the relative formula mass, Mᵣ, of PbCO₃. [1]

Mᵣ(PbCO₃) = Aᵣ(Pb) + Aᵣ(C) + (3 x Aᵣ(O))
Mᵣ(PbCO₃) = 207 + 12 + (3 x 16)
Mᵣ(PbCO₃) = 207 + 12 + 48 = 267

Recap

The relative formula mass (Mᵣ) of a compound is the sum of the relative atomic masses (Aᵣ) of all the atoms shown in its formula.

(b) The Mᵣ of PbS is 239.
Calculate the percentage of lead by mass in PbS. [1]

% Pb = (Aᵣ(Pb) / Mᵣ(PbS)) x 100
% Pb = (207 / 239) x 100
% Pb = 86.6 % (or 86.61...)

Recap

The percentage composition by mass of an element in a compound is calculated by dividing the total mass of that element in the formula by the total relative formula mass, then multiplying by 100.

(c) The percentage of lead by mass in PbCO₃ is 77.5%.
Use this information and your answer to (b) to suggest whether it would be better to extract lead from PbCO₃ or PbS.
Give a reason for your answer. [1]

It would be better to extract lead from PbS (galena).
Reason: The percentage of lead is larger in PbS (86.6%) than it is in PbCO₃ (77.5%), so more lead can be obtained from the same mass of ore.

Recap

Ores are mined to extract a valuable metal. An ore with a higher percentage of the desired metal is generally more economical to process, as it yields more product per tonne of rock.

(d) When lead(II) carbonate is heated it decomposes into lead(II) oxide, PbO, and carbon dioxide.
Write a chemical equation for this reaction. [1]

Recap

This is a thermal decomposition reaction. Many Group 2 and transition metal carbonates decompose on heating to produce the metal oxide and carbon dioxide gas.

Acids react with metal carbonates to produce three products: a salt, water, and carbon dioxide. This is a neutralisation reaction.

(f) Lead(II) oxide and carbon dioxide are oxides of Group IV elements.

(i) Complete the diagram to show the electron arrangement in one molecule of CO₂.

The diagram should show two double bonds between the carbon and each oxygen atom (O=C=O).
Each oxygen atom should have two pairs of non-bonding electrons (lone pairs), and the carbon atom should have no non-bonding electrons.

Recap

In CO₂, carbon (Group 4) needs to share 4 electrons and oxygen (Group 6) needs to share 2 electrons to achieve a stable outer shell. This is achieved by forming two double covalent bonds.

(ii) The melting points of lead(II) oxide and carbon dioxide are shown.

Use your knowledge of structure and bonding to explain why lead(II) oxide has a much higher melting point than carbon dioxide.
Your answer should refer to:
• the types of particles involved
• the relative strength of the forces of attraction between the particles. [3]

Lead(II) oxide (PbO) has a giant ionic lattice structure, with particles that are ions (Pb²⁺ and O²⁻). It has strong electrostatic forces of attraction (ionic bonds) between these ions.

Carbon dioxide (CO₂) has a simple molecular structure, with particles that are individual molecules. It has weak forces of attraction (intermolecular forces) between its molecules.

A large amount of energy is needed to overcome the strong ionic bonds in PbO, but only a small amount of energy is needed to overcome the weak intermolecular forces in CO₂. Therefore, PbO has a much higher melting point.

Recap

Giant structures (like ionic lattices) have high melting points because strong bonds (ionic or covalent) must be broken. Simple molecular structures have low melting points because only weak intermolecular forces between the molecules need to be overcome.

(c) The Avogadro constant is the number of particles in 1 mole.
The numerical value of the Avogadro constant is 6.02 x 10²³.

(i) Calculate the number of molecules in 22.0 g of carbon dioxide, CO₂. [2]

Step 1: Find the Mᵣ of CO₂.
Mᵣ(CO₂) = 12 + (2 x 16) = 44

Step 2: Find the number of moles.
Moles = Mass / Mᵣ = 22.0 g / 44 g/mol = 0.5 moles

Step 3: Calculate the number of molecules.
Number of molecules = Moles x Avogadro constant
Number = 0.5 x (6.02 x 10²³) = 3.01 x 10²³ molecules

Recap

Number of moles = mass (in g) / relative formula mass (Mᵣ).

Number of particles (atoms, molecules, ions) = Number of moles x Avogadro constant (6.02 x 10²³).

(ii) Calculate the number of molecules in 6.00 dm³ of carbon dioxide gas at room temperature and pressure. [1]

Step 1: Find the number of moles.
Moles = Volume (dm³) / Molar Gas Volume (dm³)
Moles = 6.00 dm³ / 24 dm³ = 0.25 moles

Step 2: Calculate the number of molecules.
Number of molecules = Moles x Avogadro constant
Number = 0.25 x (6.02 x 10²³) = 1.505 x 10²³ molecules

Recap

One mole of any gas occupies 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure (r.t.p.).
Number of moles of gas = Volume of gas (in dm³) / 24 dm³.

Paper: 0620/43/O/N/21 Q5 | Topics: Acids, Bases, Salts, Titration

(a) Dilute sulfuric acid and aqueous sodium hydroxide can be used to prepare sodium sulfate crystals using a method that involves titration.

(i) Suggest why universal indicator is not suitable for this titration. [1]

Universal indicator is not suitable because it shows too many colour changes over a wide pH range, rather than a single, sharp colour change at the exact end point.

Recap

A good titration indicator must change colour sharply at the specific pH of the end point. Universal indicator is a mixture of different indicators and changes colour gradually, making it useful for estimating pH but not for accurate titrations.

(ii) Name an indicator that can be used in this titration. [1]

Methyl orange or phenolphthalein.

Recap

For a strong acid (H₂SO₄) and strong base (NaOH) titration, both methyl orange (red in acid, yellow in alkali) and phenolphthalein (colourless in acid, pink in alkali) are suitable indicators as they change colour at the end point.

(b) Describe how to prepare a pure sample of sodium sulfate crystals from the original solutions of dilute sulfuric acid and aqueous sodium hydroxide of the same concentrations. [5]

  1. Perform the titration once with the indicator to find the exact volumes needed.
  2. Repeat the titration using the same volumes of acid and alkali, but without the indicator, to get a pure solution of sodium sulfate.
  3. Heat the resulting solution in an evaporating basin to evaporate some of the water until the solution is saturated (at the crystallisation point).
  4. Cool the saturated solution to allow crystals to form.
  5. Filter the mixture to separate the crystals from the solution.
  6. Dry the crystals, for example, by pressing them between two pieces of filter paper.

Recap

To make a soluble salt from a soluble base (an alkali), a titration is required to find the exact reacting volumes. The indicator must be omitted in the final preparation to avoid contaminating the salt. The salt is then recovered by crystallisation (heating to saturate, cooling to form crystals, filtering, and drying).

(c) Sodium hydrogensulfate, NaHSO₄, dissolves in water to produce an aqueous solution, X, containing Na⁺, H⁺ and SO₄²⁻ ions.

State the observations when the following tests are done.

(i) A flame test is carried out on X. [1]

A yellow flame is observed.

Recap

A flame test is used to identify metal cations. The Na⁺ ion produces a characteristic yellow flame.

(ii) Copper(II) oxide is warmed with an excess of X. [2]

The black solid dissolves, and a blue solution is formed.

Recap

Solution X contains H⁺ ions, so it is acidic. Acids react with insoluble metal oxides (like black copper(II) oxide) in a neutralisation reaction to form a soluble salt and water. The blue solution is characteristic of aqueous copper(II) ions.

(iii) Acidified aqueous barium nitrate is added to X. [1]

A white precipitate is formed.

Recap

The test for sulfate ions (SO₄²⁻) is to add acidified barium nitrate (or barium chloride). A white precipitate of barium sulfate (BaSO₄) forms, which is insoluble in acid.

Paper: 0620/43/O/N/21 Q6 | Topics: Rates of Reaction, Catalysts

A student investigates the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide in the presence of a catalyst of manganese(IV) oxide.
2H₂O₂(aq) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g)

(a) State the meaning of the term catalyst. [2]

A catalyst is a substance that speeds up the rate of a reaction but remains unchanged chemically at the end of the reaction.

Recap

Catalysts provide an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy. This means more colliding particles will have sufficient energy to react, increasing the rate.

(b) The diagram shows the equipment the student uses.

The student uses this method: ... the mass ... is recorded at regular time intervals.

(i) Suggest why the mass of the flask and contents decreases as time increases. [1]

The mass decreases because oxygen gas is produced and escapes from the flask (which is not sealed, as shown by the cotton wool plug).

Recap

When investigating reaction rates, if a gas is produced and allowed to escape, the total mass of the flask and its contents will decrease. This mass loss can be measured over time to determine the reaction rate.

(ii) Describe what happens to the rate of the reaction as time increases. [2]

The rate of reaction decreases as time increases (the curve becomes less steep). Eventually, the rate becomes zero (the curve becomes horizontal) when the reaction stops.

Recap

The rate of a reaction is fastest at the start because the concentration of reactants is highest. As the reactants are used up, their concentration decreases, leading to fewer collisions per unit time, so the rate slows down.

(c) The student repeats the experiment at a higher temperature. All other conditions stay the same. The rate of reaction increases.

(i) Explain, in terms of collisions between particles, why the rate of reaction increases at a higher temperature. [3]

At a higher temperature, the particles gain more kinetic energy and move faster. This leads to two effects:
  1. There are more collisions per unit time.
  2. A greater proportion of these collisions will have energy greater than or equal to the activation energy, meaning more collisions are successful.

Recap

Both collision frequency and collision energy increase with temperature. The increase in the energy of collisions (having energy ≥ activation energy) is the more important factor in increasing the reaction rate.

(ii) Draw a line on the graph in (b) for the experiment at a higher temperature. [2]

The new line drawn on the graph should:
  1. Start from the same initial mass.
  2. Have a steeper gradient (slope) from the start, showing a faster reaction.
  3. Level off at the same final mass, as the total amount of product formed is the same.

Recap

Changing the temperature or concentration changes the rate (steepness of the curve) but not the total amount of product formed (the final level of the curve), as long as the limiting reactant remains the same.

(a) Ethanol is a member of the homologous series of alcohols.

Any two from:

  • They have similar chemical properties.
  • Consecutive members differ by a CH₂ group.

(b) Ethanol can be manufactured from ethene.
Ethene can be made from long chain hydrocarbons such as decane, C₁₀H₂₂.
Ethene is then converted into ethanol.

(i) Name the process used to obtain ethene from long chain hydrocarbons such as decane, C₁₀H₂₂. [1]

Cracking.

Recap

Cracking is the thermal decomposition of long-chain alkanes (which are in low demand) into shorter-chain alkanes (useful as fuel) and alkenes (useful for making polymers and alcohols). This process requires high temperature and a catalyst.

(ii) Complete the chemical equation to show the formation of ethene from decane, C₁₀H₂₂.

Chemical equations must be balanced. The number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides.
Reactants: 10 C, 22 H

(iv) Name the type of reaction occurring when ethene is converted into ethanol. [1]

Hydration (or addition).

Recap

It is an addition reaction because two molecules (ethene and water) join together to form a single product (ethanol). It is also called hydration because water is being added.

(v) Give one condition for the reaction in which ethene is converted into ethanol. [1]

Any one of:
  • High temperature (e.g., 300 °C)
  • High pressure (e.g., 60 atmospheres / 6000 kPa)
  • Catalyst (e.g., concentrated H₃PO₄ / phosphoric(V) acid)

Recap

The catalytic hydration of ethene requires specific conditions to ensure a fast reaction and a reasonable yield: 300 °C, 60 atm pressure, and an acid catalyst (phosphoric(V) acid absorbed on silica).

(c) Ethanol can also be produced by fermentation of carbohydrates such as glucose.
Give two advantages of manufacturing ethanol by fermentation compared to manufacturing ethanol from ethene. [2]

Any two from:
  • The raw materials (e.g., carbohydrates like sugar cane) are renewable.
  • It uses lower temperatures (e.g., 25-35 °C), so it requires less energy.

Hydration of Ethene: Uses non-renewable resources (crude oil) and high energy (high temp/pressure). It is a fast, continuous process and produces pure ethanol.
