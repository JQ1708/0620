Question 2 | 0620/42/O/N/24 | Topics: Electrochemistry, Extraction of Metals, Ionic Bonding

Aluminium is manufactured by the electrolysis of aluminium oxide.

(a) State the name of the main ore of aluminium. [1]

The main ore of aluminium is bauxite.

Recap

An ore is a rock that contains enough of a metal compound to make it economically worthwhile to extract the metal. Bauxite is the ore that contains aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃).

(b) Name the substance mixed with aluminium oxide to reduce the operating temperature of the process. [1]

The substance mixed with aluminium oxide is cryolite.

Recap

Aluminium oxide has a very high melting point (over 2000°C). It is dissolved in molten cryolite, which acts as a solvent and lowers the melting point of the electrolyte to around 1000°C, saving significant energy costs.

(c) Explain why the molten mixture in (b) conducts electricity. [1]

The molten mixture is an ionic compound and conducts electricity because it contains mobile ions (or ions that are free to move) which can carry charge.

Recap

For a substance to conduct electricity, it must contain charged particles that are free to move. In metals, these are delocalised electrons. In molten or aqueous ionic compounds (electrolytes), these are mobile ions.

(d) Table 2.1 contains some information about the processes which take place at the anode and the cathode.

(i) Complete Table 2.1: [3]

anode: 2O²⁻ → O₂ + ...e⁻

cathode: ...

anode: 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻

cathode: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al

Recap

In electrolysis, positive ions (cations) move to the cathode (negative electrode) and gain electrons (reduction). Negative ions (anions) move to the anode (positive electrode) and lose electrons (oxidation).

  • At the cathode: Al³⁺ ions gain 3 electrons: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al
  • At the anode: O²⁻ ions lose 2 electrons each. To form an O₂ molecule, two O²⁻ ions must react, losing a total of 4 electrons: 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻
(ii) State why the process at the anode is an oxidation. [1]

The process is oxidation because the oxide ions (O²⁻) lose electrons.

Recap

Redox reactions can be defined in terms of electron transfer:

  • Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons).
  • Reduction Is Gain (of electrons).
This is often remembered by the acronym OIL RIG.

(iii) Oxygen is formed at the anode.
Explain why the main gas given off at the anode is carbon dioxide and not oxygen. [2]

The anode is made of carbon (graphite). At the high temperatures of electrolysis, the oxygen produced reacts with the carbon anode (it combusts or burns), forming carbon dioxide (C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g)).

Recap

In the extraction of aluminium, the graphite (carbon) anodes must be replaced regularly. This is because the hot oxygen gas produced at the anode reacts with the carbon, burning them away to form CO₂.

(e) State why aluminium is used in food containers. [1]

Aluminium is used in food containers because it is resistant to corrosion.

Recap

Although aluminium is a reactive metal, it appears unreactive. This is because a thin, very strong, and unreactive layer of aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃) forms on its surface when exposed to air. This layer sticks firmly to the metal and protects it from further reaction or corrosion, making it safe for contact with food.

(f) Aluminium reacts with fluorine to form the ionic compound aluminium fluoride. Complete the dot-and-cross diagram in Fig. 2.1 of the ions in aluminium fluoride. Give the charges on the ions. [3]

The completed diagram shows:

  • The aluminium ion with a 3+ charge, and an empty outer shell (showing the second shell with 8 electrons, as crosses).
  • Three fluoride ions, each with a 1- (or -) charge.

Ionic bonds form when electrons are transferred from a metal to a non-metal.

Both ions achieve a stable noble gas electronic configuration (a full outer shell).

Question 3 | 0620/42/O/N/24 | Topics: Bonding, Rates, Equilibrium, Redox

Sulfur forms two chlorides, P and Q. Chloride P has the formula S₂Cl₂. Chloride Q has the formula SCl₂.

(a) Both chlorides are covalently bonded and have low melting points. Suggest, in terms of attraction between particles, why these chlorides have low melting points. [2]

These chlorides have simple molecular structures. They have weak forces of attraction (intermolecular forces) between the molecules. Only a small amount of energy is needed to overcome these weak forces.

Recap

Simple molecular compounds have low melting and boiling points. This is because the intermolecular forces (the forces *between* molecules) are weak. The covalent bonds *within* the molecules are strong, but they are not broken during melting or boiling.

(b) Chloride P, S₂Cl₂, forms when sulfur reacts with chlorine. Write the symbol equation for this reaction. [1]

2S + Cl₂ → S₂Cl₂

Recap

A symbol equation must be balanced. This means it must have the same number of each type of atom on both the reactant side (left) and the product side (right).

(c) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram in Fig. 3.1 of a molecule of chloride Q, SCl₂. Show outer electrons only. [3]

The completed diagram shows:

  • The central sulfur atom (e.g., dots) sharing one pair of electrons with each chlorine atom (e.g., crosses).
  • The sulfur atom has two lone pairs (4 non-bonding electrons).
  • Each chlorine atom has three lone pairs (6 non-bonding electrons) and one bonding pair.

Recap

A covalent bond is a shared pair of electrons.

  • Sulfur is in Group VI, so it has 6 outer electrons. It needs to share 2 to get a stable octet.
  • Chlorine is in Group VII, so it has 7 outer electrons. It needs to share 1 to get a stable octet.
Therefore, one S atom forms single bonds with two Cl atoms.

(d) Chloride P is converted to chloride Q by reaction with chlorine in a closed system. The reversible reaction reaches an equilibrium.

S₂Cl₂(g) + Cl₂(g) ⇌ 2SCl₂(g)

The forward reaction is exothermic.

Suggest two changes to the conditions which will result in a decrease in the concentration of chloride Q at equilibrium. [2]

1. Increase the temperature.

2. Decrease the concentration of S₂Cl₂ or Cl₂. (Alternatively: Decrease the pressure).

Recap

Le Chatelier's Principle states that if a change is made to an equilibrium, the system will shift to oppose the change.

  • Temperature: The forward reaction is exothermic (releases heat). To oppose an increase in temperature, the equilibrium will shift left in the endothermic (reverse) direction, decreasing the amount of SCl₂.
  • Pressure: There are 2 moles of gas on the left (1+1) and 2 moles on the right. Since the moles of gas are equal, changing the pressure will have no effect on the position of equilibrium.
  • Concentration: To decrease SCl₂, the equilibrium must shift left. This can be achieved by decreasing the concentration of a reactant (S₂Cl₂ or Cl₂).
*(Note: The mark scheme allows "decrease concentration" and "increase temperature". Decreasing pressure is not a correct answer here.)*
(e) The rate of the forward reaction in (d) is determined by collision theory.

(i) Define the term activation energy. [1]

Activation energy is the minimum energy that colliding particles must have in order to react.

Recap

For a reaction to occur, particles must collide with the correct orientation and with sufficient energy. This minimum energy is the activation energy (Eₐ). It is the 'energy barrier' for the reaction.

(ii) Give the symbol for activation energy. [1]

Eₐ

Recap

The symbol for activation energy is Eₐ. The symbol for enthalpy change (the overall heat energy change) is ΔH.

(iii) Complete Table 3.1 to show the effect, if any, when the conditions are changed. [5]
change to conditions effect on the frequency of collisions between particles effect on the proportion of collisions which have energy greater than or equal to the activation energy
concentration of chlorine is increased increases no change
temperature is increased increases increases
a catalyst is added no change increases

Recap

According to collision theory:

  • Increasing concentration (or pressure for gases) puts more particles in the same volume, so the frequency of collisions increases. It does not change the energy of the particles.
  • Increasing temperature makes particles move faster and with more energy. This increases the collision frequency AND increases the proportion of collisions with energy ≥ Eₐ.
  • Adding a catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy (Eₐ). This does *not* change the collision frequency, but it increases the proportion of collisions that are successful (as the 'energy barrier' is lower).

Alkenes are manufactured by cracking larger alkane molecules.

(a) State the source of the large alkane molecules used in cracking. [1]

Petroleum (or crude oil).

Recap

Petroleum is a fossil fuel and a mixture of hydrocarbons. It is separated by fractional distillation into fractions. Cracking is used to break down the large alkane molecules from less useful fractions (like fuel oil) into smaller, more useful molecules.

(b) State two conditions needed for cracking large alkane molecules. [2]

1. High temperature.

2. A catalyst (e.g., aluminium oxide or silicon dioxide).

Recap

Cracking is a process of thermal decomposition (breaking down with heat). It requires a high temperature (e.g., 400-700°C) and a catalyst to speed up the reaction.

Thermal decomposition

Thermal decomposition is the breakdown of a compound into two or more simpler substances using heat. Cracking fits this definition as it uses heat to break down large alkanes.
