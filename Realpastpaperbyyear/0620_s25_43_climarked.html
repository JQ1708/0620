Answer the questions using the list of substances.

State which of the substances:

(a) is a compound with a giant covalent structure

The substance is silicon(IV) oxide.

Recap

A giant covalent structure has a continuous three-dimensional network of covalent bonds. Examples include diamond, graphite, and silicon dioxide (silicon(IV) oxide). These structures typically have very high melting and boiling points because a large amount of energy is needed to break the strong covalent bonds.

(b) is an unsaturated hydrocarbon

The substance is propene.

Recap

Unsaturated compounds contain one or more C=C double bonds or C≡C triple bonds. Alkenes (like propene) are unsaturated hydrocarbons. A hydrocarbon is a compound containing only carbon and hydrogen atoms.

(c) is an amphoteric oxide

The substance is aluminium oxide.

Recap

Amphoteric oxides are oxides that react with both acids and bases (alkalis) to form a salt and water. Aluminium oxide and zinc oxide are common examples of amphoteric oxides.

(d) is a good conductor of electricity when solid

The substance is graphite.

Recap

Graphite is a form of carbon and a giant covalent structure. It is unusual because it conducts electricity. This is because each carbon atom forms only three covalent bonds, leaving one delocalised electron per atom that is free to move along the layers.

(e) contains simple molecules with 9 atoms

The substance is propene (C₃H₆, which has 3 + 6 = 9 atoms) or ethanol (C₂H₅OH, which has 2 + 5 + 1 + 1 = 9 atoms).

Recap

The molecular formula shows the actual number and type of each atom in one molecule. Propene's formula is C₃H₆ (3 carbon atoms and 6 hydrogen atoms). Ethanol's formula is C₂H₆O or C₂H₅OH (2 carbon atoms, 6 hydrogen atoms, and 1 oxygen atom).

(f) react together to form slag in the blast furnace

The two substances are silicon(IV) oxide and calcium oxide.

Recap

In the blast furnace, limestone (calcium carbonate) undergoes thermal decomposition to form calcium oxide, which is a basic oxide. This calcium oxide then reacts with acidic impurities in the iron ore, such as silicon(IV) oxide, to form molten slag (calcium silicate).

(g) belongs to a homologous series whose general formula is CₙH₂ₙ₊₂

The substance is propane.

Recap

A homologous series is a family of similar compounds with the same functional group and similar chemical properties. The general formula for the alkanes is CₙH₂ₙ₊₂. Propane (C₃H₈) fits this formula, where n=3 (2n+2 = 2*3+2 = 8).

(h) is manufactured by the catalytic addition of steam to ethene

The substance is ethanol.

Recap

Ethanol can be manufactured by two main methods. One is the catalytic addition of steam (H₂O) to ethene (C₂H₄). This is a fast, high-energy process that produces pure ethanol. The other method is fermentation.

(i) is a gas that is approximately 78% of clean, dry air.

The substance is nitrogen.

Recap

Clean, dry air is a mixture of gases. Its approximate composition by volume is 78% nitrogen (N₂), 21% oxygen (O₂), about 1% argon (Ar), and 0.04% carbon dioxide (CO₂).

Atoms are made of electrons, neutrons and protons.

(a) State which of these particles are found in the nucleus of an atom.

The protons and neutrons are found in the nucleus.

Recap

An atom consists of a central nucleus which contains protons (positive charge) and neutrons (no charge). Electrons (negative charge) are found outside the nucleus, moving in specific energy levels called electron shells.

(b) Atoms of the same element are known as isotopes.
³²S and ³⁴S are isotopes of sulfur.
(i) Complete Table 2.1 to show the number of electrons, neutrons and protons in one atom or ion of these isotopes.
isotope electrons neutrons protons

Here is the completed table:

Working:

  • Protons: The proton number (atomic number) of sulfur (S) is 16. This is the same for all its isotopes and ions.
  • ³⁴S (atom):
    • Neutrons = Mass Number - Proton Number = 34 - 16 = 18.
    • Electrons = Protons (because it is a neutral atom) = 16.
  • ³²S²⁻ (ion):
    • Neutrons = Mass Number - Proton Number = 32 - 16 = 16.

Recap

The number of protons in an atom is given by the proton number (atomic number) and defines the element. In a neutral atom, the number of electrons is equal to the number of protons. The number of neutrons is the mass number minus the proton number. For ions, a negative charge means electrons have been gained, and a positive charge means electrons have been lost.

(ii) Table 2.2 shows the relative masses and the percentage abundances of the two isotopes in a sample of sulfur.
relative mass of isotope percentage abundance of isotope
32 95
34 5
Calculate the relative atomic mass of this sample of sulfur to one decimal place.

The relative atomic mass is 32.1.

Working:

  1. Multiply each isotope's mass by its percentage abundance:
    • (32 × 95) = 3040
    • (34 × 5) = 170
  2. Add these values together:
    • 3040 + 170 = 3210
  3. Divide by 100 to find the weighted average:
    • 3210 / 100 = 32.1

Recap

Relative atomic mass ($A_r$) is the average mass of the isotopes of an element, taking into account their relative abundances. It is calculated as a weighted average:
$A_r = \frac{(\text{mass of isotope 1} \times \% \text{ abundance 1}) + (\text{mass of isotope 2} \times \% \text{ abundance 2}) + ...}{100}$

(iii) The relative atomic masses of all elements are compared to one atom of an isotope.
Identify this isotope.

The standard isotope is ¹²C (Carbon-12).

Recap

The relative atomic mass ($A_r$) is formally defined as the average mass of the isotopes of an element compared to $\frac{1}{12}$th (one-twelfth) the mass of an atom of carbon-12.

(c) Ions are atoms or groups of atoms that have gained or lost one or more electrons.
An oxygen atom has the electronic configuration of 2,6.
Give the formula of one atom and one positive ion that has the same electronic configuration as O²⁻.

An O²⁻ ion has gained 2 electrons, so its electronic configuration is 2,8.

Atom: Ne (Neon, proton number 10, has configuration 2,8)

Positive ion: Na⁺ (Sodium, 2,8,1, loses 1 electron) or Mg²⁺ (Magnesium, 2,8,2, loses 2 electrons) or Al³⁺ (Aluminium, 2,8,3, loses 3 electrons).

Recap

Atoms form ions to achieve a stable electronic configuration, which is usually a full outer shell of electrons, like the noble gases.
• Non-metals (like Oxygen, 2,6) gain electrons to form negative ions (e.g., O²⁻ is 2,8).
• Metals (like Sodium, 2,8,1) lose electrons from their outer shell to form positive ions (e.g., Na⁺ is 2,8).
• The noble gas Neon (Ne) naturally has the stable configuration 2,8.

Question 3

Paper: 0620/43/M/J/25 Q3 | Topics: Metals & Alloys, Bonding, Rates of Reaction, Salts

This question is about zinc and compounds of zinc.

(a) (i) Name the type of bonding in zinc.

The bonding is metallic bonding.

Recap

Metallic bonding is the strong electrostatic attraction between the positive metal ions in a metallic lattice and a 'sea' of delocalised electrons.

(ii) Name the two types of particles held together by the bonding in (a)(i).

The two types of particles are positive ions (or zinc ions) and delocalised electrons.

Recap

A metallic structure consists of a giant lattice of positive metal ions surrounded by a 'sea' of delocalised electrons. The attraction is between these two oppositely charged types of particles.

(iii) Name the type of particle whose movement allows zinc to conduct electricity.

The particles are delocalised electrons (or mobile electrons).

Recap

Metals are good electrical conductors because the delocalised electrons are mobile and free to move throughout the entire metallic lattice, carrying charge.

(b) (i) State the meaning of the term alloy.

An alloy is a mixture of a metal with other elements (which can be metals or non-metals).

Recap

Alloys are mixtures of metal atoms with atoms of other elements. Alloys are often harder and stronger than the pure metal because the different sized atoms disrupt the regular layers, preventing them from sliding over each other easily.

(ii) Name the substance that is present in brass, other than zinc.

The other substance is copper.

Recap

Brass is a well-known alloy made from a mixture of copper and zinc.

(c) Zinc sulfate crystals are made by the reaction between zinc carbonate and dilute sulfuric acid... (i) Give two observations which show that the zinc carbonate is in excess in step 1.

Two observations are:

  1. The solid stops dissolving (or solid zinc carbonate remains at the bottom).
  2. The fizzing (or bubbling/effervescence) stops, even when more solid is added.

Recap

Acids react with carbonates to produce a salt, water, and carbon dioxide gas, which is seen as fizzing. When preparing a soluble salt from an insoluble base or carbonate, the solid is added in excess to ensure all the acid is used up (becomes the limiting reactant). You know it's in excess when the reaction stops and undissolved solid remains.

(ii) Name the filtrate in step 2.

The filtrate is aqueous zinc sulfate (or zinc sulfate solution).

Recap

Filtration is a method used to separate an insoluble solid (the residue) from a liquid or solution (the filtrate). In this case, the excess solid zinc carbonate is the residue, and the desired zinc sulfate solution is the filtrate.

(iii) Name a compound, other than zinc carbonate, that can be added to dilute sulfuric acid to produce aqueous zinc sulfate in step 1.

A suitable compound is zinc oxide or zinc hydroxide.

Recap

Bases react with acids to produce a salt and water. Metal oxides and metal hydroxides are common bases. Therefore, zinc oxide (ZnO) or zinc hydroxide (Zn(OH)₂) would also react with sulfuric acid to form zinc sulfate.

(iv) State what is meant by the term saturated solution.

A saturated solution is one that contains the maximum concentration (or amount) of solute dissolved in a solvent at a particular temperature.

Recap

A solute is the substance that dissolves in a solvent to form a solution. A solution is saturated when no more solute can dissolve at that specific temperature. Crystallisation (step 4) occurs when a hot, saturated solution is cooled, as the solubility of most solids decreases at lower temperatures.

(v) Step 1 is repeated using large pieces of zinc carbonate instead of powdered zinc carbonate. All other conditions are the same. The rate of reaction decreases. Explain why the rate of reaction decreases. Give your answer in terms of particles.

The large pieces of zinc carbonate have a smaller total surface area compared to the same mass of powdered zinc carbonate. This means fewer particles are exposed to the acid, leading to a lower frequency of collisions between the reactant particles. A lower collision frequency results in a slower rate of reaction.

Recap

According to collision theory, the rate of reaction is affected by the frequency of successful collisions. Increasing the surface area of a solid reactant (by using a powder instead of large lumps) increases the number of exposed particles, which increases the collision frequency and therefore increases the rate of reaction.

(vi) Hydrated crystals form in step 4. State what is meant by the term hydrated.

A hydrated substance is one that contains water of crystallisation, which is water that is chemically combined into the crystal structure.

Recap

A hydrated solid contains water molecules fixed within its crystal lattice. This is known as water of crystallisation. A solid that does not contain this water is described as anhydrous. For example, hydrated copper(II) sulfate (CuSO₄·5H₂O) is blue, while anhydrous copper(II) sulfate (CuSO₄) is white.

Paper: 0620/43/M/J/25 Q4 | Topics: Equations, Bonding, Industrial Chemistry, Mole Calculations

This question is about compounds of sulfur.

(a) Sulfuric acid is manufactured in a four-stage process... (i) ...Balance the symbol equation for this reaction.
4FeS₂ + .....O₂ → .....SO₂ + .....Fe₂O₃

The balanced equation is:

4FeS₂ + 11O₂ → 8SO₂ + 2Fe₂O₃

Working:

  1. Balance Fe: 4 Fe on left, so need 2 Fe₂O₃ on right.
  2. Balance S: 4 FeS₂ means 4 x 2 = 8 S on left, so need 8 SO₂ on right.
  3. Balance O: Right side now has (8 x 2) + (2 x 3) = 16 + 6 = 22 O atoms.
  4. Balance O₂ on left: To get 22 O atoms, you need 11 O₂ molecules.

Recap

In a symbol equation, there must be the same number of each type of atom on both sides of the arrow. Balancing is done by placing numbers (coefficients) in front of the formulae. You should balance one element at a time and check all elements at the end.

(ii) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram in Fig. 4.1 to show the electronic configuration in an oxygen molecule, O₂. Show outer-shell electrons only.

An oxygen molecule contains a double covalent bond.

  • There should be two pairs of shared electrons (4 electrons total, e.g., 2 dots and 2 crosses) in the overlapping region.
  • Each oxygen atom should also have two non-bonding pairs (4 non-bonding electrons) in its outer shell.

Recap

Oxygen is in Group VI, so it has 6 outer shell electrons. To achieve a stable octet (8 electrons), each oxygen atom must share two electrons. They do this by forming a double covalent bond, where two pairs of electrons are shared between the two atoms.

(b) (i) State three typical conditions for the reaction between sulfur dioxide and oxygen in stage 2.

The three conditions are:

  1. Temperature: 450 °C
  2. Pressure: 2 atm (or 200 kPa)
  3. Catalyst: Vanadium(V) oxide (V₂O₅)

Recap

Stage 2 is the key step in the Contact process. The conditions are a compromise: 450 °C is high enough for a fast rate but low enough to give a good yield (since the forward reaction is exothermic). A vanadium(V) oxide catalyst is used to increase the rate of reaction. A low pressure of 200 kPa is used because the yield is already high at this pressure.

(ii) Write a symbol equation for the reaction that occurs in stage 2.

2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)

Recap

The Contact process involves the reversible reaction between sulfur dioxide and oxygen to produce sulfur trioxide. The reaction is exothermic (releases heat) and involves a decrease in the number of gas moles (3 moles of reactants to 2 moles of product).

(iii) Write the symbol equation for the reaction in stage 3.

SO₃ + H₂SO₄ → H₂S₂O₇

Recap

In the Contact process, the sulfur trioxide (SO₃) from stage 2 is not added directly to water, as this reaction is too violent. Instead, it is dissolved in concentrated sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) to form a thick liquid called oleum (H₂S₂O₇). This oleum is then safely diluted with water to produce more sulfuric acid.

(c) Give the oxidation number of carbon in:
C
CO₂

C: 0

CO₂: +4

Recap

There are several oxidation number rules:

  1. The oxidation number of an uncombined element (like C) is always 0.
  2. The oxidation number of oxygen in a compound is (usually) -2.
  3. The total oxidation number of all elements in a neutral compound (like CO₂) is 0.

Therefore, for CO₂: (Oxidation number of C) + (2 × -2) = 0. This means the oxidation number of C must be +4.

(d) ...Calculate the volume of CO₂(g)... from 4.20 g of NaHCO₃...
Calculate the number of moles of NaHCO₃ in 4.20 g.
Deduce the number of moles of CO₂(g) that are produced.
Calculate the volume of CO₂(g) produced in cm³.

Number of moles of NaHCO₃:
Moles = Mass / $M_r$ = 4.20 g / 84 = 0.0500 mol

Number of moles of CO₂:
From the balanced equation (H₂SO₄ + 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂SO₄ + 2CO₂), the molar ratio of NaHCO₃ to CO₂ is 2:2, or 1:1. Therefore, 0.0500 mol of NaHCO₃ produces 0.0500 mol of CO₂.

This calculation involves three steps:

  2. Use stoichiometry: Look at the ratio of moles in the balanced equation to find the moles of the desired substance.
  3. Find gas volume: Use the formula: Volume of gas (in cm³) = moles × molar gas volume (which is 24000 cm³ or 24 dm³ at r.t.p.).

Paper: 0620/43/M/J/25 Q5 | Topics: Chemical Equilibria, Organic Chemistry, Esters, Empirical Formulae

Ethanoic acid is manufactured in the reaction between methanol and carbon monoxide. An equilibrium mixture is produced.

CH₃OH(g) + CO(g) ⇌ CH₃COOH(g)

(a) State two characteristics of an equilibrium.

The two main characteristics are:

  1. The rate of the forward reaction is equal to the rate of the reverse reaction.
  2. The concentrations of all reactants and products remain constant (as long as the system is closed and conditions are unchanged).

Recap

A chemical equilibrium is a dynamic state in a reversible reaction. It can only be reached in a closed system. At equilibrium, both the forward and reverse reactions are still happening, but their rates are identical, so there is no overall change in the concentrations of the substances involved.

(b) ...The forward reaction is exothermic. (i) State the disadvantage of using a temperature:
below 300 °C
above 300 °C

Below 300 °C: The rate of reaction decreases and becomes too slow.

Above 300 °C: The yield of ethanoic acid decreases because the equilibrium shifts to the left (reactant side).

Recap

Changing temperature affects both the rate and the position of equilibrium.
• Rate: Lowering the temperature always decreases the rate of reaction (fewer particles have energy ≥ activation energy).
• Equilibrium: This is an exothermic reaction (releases heat). According to Le Chatelier's principle, increasing the temperature will shift the equilibrium in the endothermic direction (the reverse reaction) to absorb the extra heat. This decreases the yield of the product.

effect on the equilibrium concentration of CH₃COOH(g) effect on the rate of the forward reaction

Here is the completed table:

Recap

• A catalyst increases the rate of both the forward and reverse reactions equally. It does not affect the position of equilibrium or the final yield; it just helps the reaction reach equilibrium faster.
• Increasing the pressure pushes gas particles closer together, increasing the collision frequency and thus increasing the rate of reaction.
• For equilibrium, there are 2 moles of gas on the left (1 CH₃OH + 1 CO) and 1 mole of gas on the right (1 CH₃COOH). Increasing the pressure shifts the equilibrium to the side with fewer moles of gas, so it shifts to the right, increasing the yield/concentration of ethanoic acid.

aluminium, carbon, cobalt, magnesium, sodium

Reason: It is a transition element (or transition metal).

Recap

Transition elements (the block of metals between Groups II and III) and their compounds often function as catalysts. Cobalt is a transition element, whereas the other elements listed are in Groups I, II, III, or IV.

(c) (i) Name the carboxylic acid that contains only one carbon atom.

The name is methanoic acid.

Recap

In organic chemistry, prefixes indicate the number of carbon atoms in the main chain. The prefix for one carbon atom is meth-. Therefore, a carboxylic acid with one carbon atom (HCOOH) is called methanoic acid.

(ii) State the molecular formula of a carboxylic acid that contains four carbon atoms.

The molecular formula is C₄H₈O₂.

Recap

The general formula for a simple carboxylic acid can be written as CₙH₂ₙO₂. For an acid with four carbon atoms (n=4), the formula is C₄H₈O₂. This is butanoic acid. Alternatively, using the formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH, the 'n' refers to the alkyl chain. For butanoic acid, the chain is 'prop-', so n=3, giving C₃H₇COOH, which totals 4 carbons, 8 hydrogens, and 2 oxygens.

(d) (i) Draw the displayed formula of the ester which contains two carbon atoms.

This ester is methyl methanoate.

Recap

An ester is formed from a carboxylic acid and an alcohol. To get a total of two carbon atoms, one carbon must come from the acid and one from the alcohol.
• 1-carbon acid: methanoic acid (HCOOH)
• 1-carbon alcohol: methanol (CH₃OH)
They react to form methyl methanoate and water. The structure contains the ester linkage (-COO-).

(ii) Propyl butanoate is an ester. Name the carboxylic acid and alcohol that react to produce propyl butanoate.

Carboxylic acid: butanoic acid
Alcohol: propan-1-ol (or propanol)

Recap

When naming an ester, the first part of the name comes from the alcohol (e.g., 'propyl' comes from propanol) and the second part comes from the carboxylic acid (e.g., 'butanoate' comes from butanoic acid).

(e) An organic compound has the following composition by mass: C, 58.82%; H, 9.80%; O, 31.38%. Calculate the empirical formula of the compound.

The empirical formula is C₅H₁₀O₂.

Working:

  1. Divide % by $A_r$:
    • C: 58.82 / 12.0 = 4.901...
    • H: 9.80 / 1.0 = 9.80
    • O: 31.38 / 16.0 = 1.961...
  2. Divide by smallest:
    • C: 4.901 / 1.961 = 2.5
    • H: 9.80 / 1.961 = 5.0
    • O: 1.961 / 1.961 = 1.0
  3. Multiply to get whole numbers:
    • C: 2.5 × 2 = 5
    • H: 5.0 × 2 = 10
    • O: 1.0 × 2 = 2

This gives the simplest whole number ratio of C₅H₁₀O₂.

Recap

The empirical formula is the simplest whole number ratio of the different atoms in a compound. To calculate it, you divide the percentage by mass (or mass) of each element by its relative atomic mass ($A_r$) to find the mole ratio. Then, you divide all the resulting numbers by the smallest number to find the simplest ratio. If necessary, multiply to get whole numbers.

Paper: 0620/43/M/J/25 Q6 | Topics: Periodic Table, Group I, Group VII, Enthalpy Change

This question is about the Periodic Table.

(a) State the name given to Group I elements.

The name is the alkali metals.

Recap

The elements in Group I of the Periodic Table are known as the alkali metals. They are reactive, soft metals with low densities and one electron in their outer shell.

(b) State which Group I element is least reactive.

The least reactive Group I element is lithium (Li).

Recap

The reactivity of the alkali metals increases down the group. This is because as you go down the group, the single outer electron is further from the nucleus and less strongly attracted, so it is lost more easily. Therefore, lithium (at the top) is the least reactive.

(c) (i) Give two observations when lithium is added to water.

Any two from:

  • Fizzing (or bubbles produced / effervescence).
  • The solid dissolves (or disappears / gets smaller).
  • The solid floats on the surface.
  • The solid moves around on the surface.

Recap

Alkali metals react with water to form hydrogen gas (which causes fizzing) and an alkaline solution of the metal hydroxide. Lithium is less dense than water, so it floats. The reaction is less vigorous than that of sodium or potassium.

(ii) Write a symbol equation for this reaction.

2Li(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2LiOH(aq) + H₂(g)

Recap

The general equation for an alkali metal reacting with water is:
Metal + Water → Metal Hydroxide + Hydrogen
The equation must be balanced to ensure there are equal numbers of each atom on both sides.

(d) Describe two other physical properties of Group I elements that are different from transition elements.

Two differences are:

  1. Group I metals are soft (can be cut with a knife), whereas transition elements are typically hard and strong.
  2. Group I metals have low densities (e.g., lithium, sodium, and potassium float on water), whereas transition elements have high densities.

Recap

Transition elements (like iron or copper) are typical metals: hard, strong, with high melting points and high densities. Group I metals are unusual; they are very soft and have low densities and low melting points compared to transition elements.

(e) (i) Give the physical state and colour of chlorine at room temperature and pressure.

State: gas
Colour: pale yellow-green (or yellow-green)

Recap

The halogens (Group VII) show trends in their physical properties. At room temperature and pressure (r.t.p.):
• Chlorine (Cl₂) is a yellow-green gas.
• Bromine (Br₂) is a red-brown liquid.
• Iodine (I₂) is a grey-black solid.

(ii) ...Write an ionic equation for the reaction. (Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂)

Cl₂(aq) + 2Br⁻(aq) → 2Cl⁻(aq) + Br₂(aq)

Recap

This is a displacement reaction. Chlorine is more reactive than bromine, so it displaces bromine from its salt. To write the ionic equation, we show only the species that change. The K⁺ (potassium) ions are spectator ions and are omitted because they appear unchanged on both sides of the full equation.

(iii) ...Calculate the enthalpy change, ΔH, for the reaction...
I₂(g) + Cl₂(g) → 2ICl(g)
...using the bond energies in Table 6.1.
bondbond energy in kJ/mol
I–I150
Cl–Cl242
I–Cl218

The enthalpy change (ΔH) is -44 kJ/mol.

Working:

  1. Energy for bonds broken (Reactants):
    • 1 × (I–I) = 1 × 150 = 150 kJ
    • 1 × (Cl–Cl) = 1 × 242 = 242 kJ
    • Total energy IN = 150 + 242 = +392 kJ
  2. Energy from bonds formed (Products):
    • 2 × (I–Cl) = 2 × 218 = 436 kJ
    • Total energy OUT = -436 kJ
  3. Enthalpy Change (ΔH):
    • ΔH = Energy IN - Energy OUT = 392 - 436 = -44 kJ/mol

Recap

The enthalpy change (ΔH) of a reaction can be calculated using bond energies.
• Bond breaking (in reactants) is an endothermic process (energy is absorbed, positive value).
• Bond making (in products) is an exothermic process (energy is released, negative value).
The overall ΔH = Σ(Bonds Broken) - Σ(Bonds Formed).
Since the result is negative, this reaction is exothermic.
