Question 1 | 0620/41/O/N/23 | Topic: Air and Water, Organic Chemistry, Acid-Base
1 A list of gases is shown.
ammonia
carbon dioxide
carbon monoxide
ethene
fluorine
oxygen
sulfur dioxide
xenon

Answer the following questions using only the gases from the list.
Each gas may be used once, more than once or not at all.

Give the name of the gas that:
(a) causes acid rain [1]
The gas that causes acid rain is sulfur dioxide.

Recap

Acid rain is formed when sulfur dioxide (produced from burning fossil fuels containing sulfur) and oxides of nitrogen react with water in the atmosphere to form dilute acids. This causes damage to buildings and kills aquatic life.
(b) forms an alkaline solution when dissolved in water [1]
The gas that forms an alkaline solution is ammonia.

Recap

Ammonia is an alkaline gas. When it dissolves in water, it forms aqueous ammonium hydroxide which contains hydroxide ions (OH⁻), giving the solution a pH greater than 7.
(c) is inert [1]
The gas that is inert is xenon.

Recap

Noble gases like xenon are unreactive (inert) because they have a full outer shell of electrons, which is a stable electronic configuration.
(d) is a product of photosynthesis [1]
The gas produced by photosynthesis is oxygen.

Recap

Photosynthesis is the reaction between carbon dioxide and water in the presence of chlorophyll and light to produce glucose and oxygen.
(e) can form a polymer [1]
The gas that can form a polymer is ethene.

Recap

Ethene is an alkene containing a C=C double bond. It acts as a monomer in addition polymerisation to form poly(ethene).
(f) is produced in the test for nitrate ions. [1]
The gas produced in the test for nitrate ions is ammonia.

Recap

The test for nitrate ions involves adding sodium hydroxide and aluminium foil and warming. This reduces the nitrate to ammonia gas, which turns damp red litmus paper blue.
Question 2 | 0620/41/O/N/23 | Topic: Atoms, Elements and Compounds, Electrolysis
2 Boron and aluminium are Group III elements.
(a) Boron has only two naturally occurring isotopes, 10B and 11B.
Complete Table 2.1 to show the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in an atom of 11B. [2]

Table 2.1
number of protons number of neutrons number of electrons
Protons: 5; Neutrons: 6; Electrons: 5.

Recap

Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons. The proton number (atomic number) identifies the element.
(b) The relative atomic mass of boron to one decimal place is 10.8.
(i) Determine the relative abundance of 10B present in boron. Give your answer as a percentage. [1]
The relative abundance is 20%.

Recap

Relative atomic mass (Ar) is the average mass of the isotopes compared to 1/12th of the mass of a carbon-12 atom. It is calculated using the mass and percentage abundance of each isotope.
(ii) Use the relative atomic mass of boron to calculate the number of atoms in 0.540 g of boron. Give your answer in standard form. [2]
Moles = 0.540 / 10.8 = 0.05 mol.
Number of atoms = 0.05 x 6.02 x 1023 = 3.01 x 1022.

Recap

One mole of any substance contains 6.02 x 1023 particles (the Avogadro constant). Number of moles = mass / molar mass.
(c) Aluminium is extracted from its purified ore as shown in Fig. 2.1.
(i) Name the ore of aluminium. [1]
The ore of aluminium is bauxite.

Recap

Bauxite is the main ore of aluminium, which contains aluminium oxide.
(ii) The electrolyte contains aluminium oxide and one other substance. Name the other substance and explain why it is used. [2]
The substance is cryolite. It is used to lower the operating temperature (or melting point) and improve conductivity.

Recap

Cryolite acts as a solvent for aluminium oxide. This mixture melts at a much lower temperature than pure aluminium oxide, saving energy costs.
(iii) Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction at the cathode. [2]
Al3+ + 3e- → Al

Recap

At the cathode (negative electrode), positive aluminium ions gain electrons to form aluminium atoms. This is reduction.
(iv) Explain why the anodes need frequent replacement. [2]
The carbon anodes react with oxygen produced at the anode to form carbon dioxide gas, causing them to wear away.

Recap

The anodes are made of carbon (graphite). At high temperatures, carbon reacts with the oxygen gas evolved to form CO₂, so they must be replaced regularly.
(d) State two physical properties of aluminium that make it suitable for use in overhead electrical cables. [2]
Aluminium is a good conductor of electricity and has a low density.

Recap

Aluminium has low density and is a good electrical conductor. It is often reinforced with a steel core for strength in overhead cables.
(e) Explain the apparent unreactivity of aluminium. [2]
Aluminium reacts with oxygen to form a protective oxide layer which adheres to the surface and is unreactive.

Recap

Despite being high in the reactivity series, aluminium resists corrosion because of a thin, tough layer of aluminium oxide on its surface that prevents further reaction.
(f) Aluminium reacts with fluorine to form aluminium fluoride, AlF3, an ionic compound.
(i) Write the symbol equation for this reaction. [2]
2Al + 3F2 → 2AlF3

Recap

Ionic compounds are formed when metals react with non-metals. The equation must be balanced with correct formulae (Fluorine is diatomic F₂).
(ii) Complete Fig. 2.2 to show the electronic configuration of one aluminium ion and one fluoride ion. Show the charges on the ions. [3]
Aluminium ion: 2.8 structure (eight crosses in outer shell) with 3+ charge. Fluoride ion: 2.8 structure (7 dots, 1 cross) with - charge.

Recap

Positive ions form by electron loss (Al loses 3). Negative ions form by electron gain (F gains 1). Ions have the stable electronic configuration of a noble gas.
Question 3 | 0620/41/O/N/23 | Topic: The Periodic Table, Metals
3 Order of reactivity can be determined by displacement reactions.
(a) A student investigates the reactivities of four metals...
(i) Table 3.1 shows some of the results.
Complete Table 3.1 and place the four metals in their order of reactivity with the most reactive first. [3]
Order: Zn, Mn, Pb, Ag. (Table completion: Ag column all X; X in Pb; ticks in Zn).

Recap

A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from a solution of its salt. The order of reactivity can be deduced by observing which metals cause displacement.
(ii) Suggest why the metal nitrates and not the metal sulfates of these four metals are used as the aqueous solutions. [1]
All nitrates are soluble (or lead sulfate is insoluble).

Recap

Solubility rules state that all nitrates are soluble in water. Some sulfates (like lead, barium, calcium) are insoluble.
(iii) Write the symbol equation for the reaction between zinc and silver nitrate. [2]
Zn + 2AgNO3 → Zn(NO3)2 + 2Ag

Recap

This is a displacement reaction. Zinc is more reactive than silver, so it forms zinc nitrate and silver metal is deposited.
(b) The reactivity of Group VII elements can be investigated experimentally.
A student bubbles chlorine gas into a test-tube containing aqueous potassium bromide.
(i) Describe the colour change seen in the test-tube. [2]
From colourless to orange (or yellow/brown).

Recap

Chlorine displaces bromine from potassium bromide because chlorine is more reactive. Aqueous bromine is orange/red-brown.
(ii) Complete the ionic equation for this reaction. Include state symbols. [3]
Cl2(g) + 2Br-(aq) → Br2(aq) + 2Cl-(aq)

Recap

Ionic equations show only the species that change. Spectator ions (like K⁺) are omitted. Halogens (Group VII) are diatomic molecules.
(iii) Use the Periodic Table to identify the Group VII element which cannot displace any other Group VII elements. [1]
Tennessine (Ts) (or Astatine/At allowed).

Recap

Reactivity of halogens decreases down Group VII. The element at the bottom is the least reactive and cannot displace others.
Question 4 | 0620/41/O/N/23 | Topic: Chemical Energetics, Rate of Reaction
4 Aqueous hydrogen peroxide, H2O2, slowly forms water and oxygen...
(a) State the test for oxygen gas. [1]
Test: glowing splint. Observation: relights.

Recap

Oxygen supports combustion, which is why it relights a glowing splint. Hydrogen pops with a lighted splint.
(b) A student investigates the rate of formation of oxygen gas...
(i) State how the graph in Fig. 4.1 shows the rate of reaction at time t2 is lower than at time t1. [1]
The gradient is lower at t2.

Recap

The gradient (slope) of a volume-time graph represents the rate of reaction. A steeper slope means a faster rate.
(ii) Explain, using collision theory, why the rate of reaction at time t2 is lower than at time t1. [2]
The concentration of H2O2 particles decreases, so the frequency of collisions between particles decreases.

Recap

According to collision theory, rate depends on collision frequency. As reactants are used up, concentration falls, collisions become less frequent, and the rate decreases.
(iii) On Fig. 4.1, sketch the graph obtained when the experiment is repeated using aqueous hydrogen peroxide at a higher temperature. [2]
The curve is steeper initially but finishes at the same volume.

Recap

Higher temperature increases kinetic energy, leading to more frequent and energetic collisions (faster rate). The total amount of product depends on the limiting reactant, which hasn't changed.
(c) Calculate the concentration of the aqueous hydrogen peroxide in g/dm3... [5]
Moles O2 = 72/24000 = 0.003 mol.
Moles H2O2 = 0.003 x 2 = 0.006 mol.
Conc H2O2 (mol/dm3) = 0.006 / (20/1000) = 0.3 mol/dm3.
Mr H2O2 = 34.
Conc (g/dm3) = 0.3 x 34 = 10.2 g/dm3.

Recap

Concentration calculations involve moles, volume (converted to dm³), and relative formula mass (Mr). Mass = Moles x Mr.
(d) Suggest the identity of one other metal oxide which also catalyses this reaction. [1]
Copper(II) oxide (or other transition metal oxides).

Recap

Transition elements and their compounds often act as catalysts. A catalyst increases the rate of reaction by lowering the activation energy.
Question 5 | 0620/41/O/N/23 | Topic: Chemical Equilibria
5 Methane reacts with steam to produce hydrogen gas.
CH4(g) + H2O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + 3H2(g)
(a) State two features of an equilibrium. [2]
The rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction. The concentrations of reactants and products are constant.

Recap

Dynamic equilibrium occurs in a closed system where forward and backward reactions happen at the same rate.
(b) State and explain, in terms of equilibrium, what happens to the concentration of hydrogen when:
(i) the pressure is increased [2]
Concentration decreases. Equilibrium shifts to the left because there are fewer moles of gas on the left hand side.

Recap

Increasing pressure moves the equilibrium position to the side with fewer moles of gas molecules to reduce the pressure.
(ii) the temperature is increased [2]
Concentration increases. Equilibrium shifts to the right because the forward reaction is endothermic.

Recap

Increasing temperature favors the endothermic reaction (positive enthalpy change) to absorb the added heat.
(iii) a catalyst is used. [2]
No change. Catalysts do not affect the position of equilibrium.

Recap

Catalysts increase the rate of both forward and backward reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached faster but the yield remains the same.
(c) Methane is a greenhouse gas...
(i) Name a greenhouse gas found in clean, dry air. [1]
Carbon dioxide.

Recap

Clean, dry air contains small amounts of carbon dioxide (0.04%), which is a greenhouse gas responsible for maintaining Earth's temperature.
(ii) Explain, in terms of thermal energy, how greenhouse gases cause global warming. [3]
Greenhouse gases absorb thermal energy (infrared radiation) emitted by the Earth's surface and reduce thermal energy loss into space, leading to a higher atmospheric temperature.

Recap

Greenhouse gases like CO₂ and methane absorb infrared radiation reflected from the Earth's surface, trapping heat in the atmosphere (Global Warming).
Question 6 | 0620/41/O/N/23 | Topic: Organic Chemistry
6 Ethanol is manufactured by two methods...
(a) Method 1 takes place at room temperature and pressure. State two other conditions needed in method 1. [2]
Yeast and absence of air (anaerobic conditions).

Recap

Fermentation converts glucose to ethanol using yeast enzymes. Anaerobic conditions are essential to prevent oxidation of ethanol to ethanoic acid.
(b) State the typical temperature and pressure used in method 2. [2]
Temperature: 300 °C. Pressure: 6000 kPa (or 60 atm).

Recap

Hydration of ethene uses steam, high pressure (60 atm), 300°C, and a phosphoric acid catalyst to produce pure ethanol quickly.
(i) Name the alkene used in method 2. [1]
Ethene.

Recap

Alkenes contain a C=C double bond and can undergo addition reactions. Ethene (C₂H₄) is the monomer used here.
(ii) State why the reaction in method 2 is referred to as an addition reaction. [1]
Only one product is formed.

Recap

In an addition reaction, the C=C double bond breaks and a small molecule (like water) adds across it to form a single saturated product.
(c) The catalyst in method 2 is phosphoric acid...
(i) State what is meant by the term acid. [1]
Proton donor.

Recap

Acids are defined as proton donors (they release H⁺ ions in solution). Bases are proton acceptors.
(ii) State the meaning of weak in the term weak acid. [1]
Partial dissociation (or ionisation) in aqueous solution.

Recap

Weak acids (like ethanoic acid) only partially dissociate into ions, while strong acids (like HCl) dissociate completely.
(iii) Determine the oxidation number of phosphorus in the PO43- ion. Show your working. [2]
P + (4 x -2) = -3. P - 8 = -3. P = +5.

Recap

Oxidation numbers track electron distribution. Oxygen is usually -2. The sum of oxidation numbers in an ion equals the charge on the ion.
(d) Give one advantage of each method of production of ethanol. [2]
Method 1 (Fermentation): Uses renewable resources (glucose).
Method 2 (Hydration): High rate of reaction (or pure product).

Recap

Fermentation is a batch process using renewable crops but produces impure ethanol slowly. Hydration is a continuous process using finite crude oil but produces pure ethanol fast.
(e) Ethanol can be converted to ethanoic acid...
(i) Name the acidified oxidising agent. [1]
(ii) State, in terms of redox, what type of reagent ethanol is in this reaction. [1]
(i) Potassium manganate(VII).
(ii) Reducing agent.

Recap

Ethanol is oxidised to ethanoic acid. The agent causing this (KMnO₄) is reduced, making ethanol the reducing agent.
(f) Ethanoic acid reacts with calcium to form a salt and one other product.
(i) Name the salt. [1]
(ii) Write the formula of the salt. [1]
(iii) Identify the other product. [1]
(i) Calcium ethanoate.
(ii) (CH3COO)2Ca.
(iii) Hydrogen.

Recap

Carboxylic acids react with reactive metals to form a salt (ethanoate) and hydrogen gas. The calcium ion is Ca²⁺, so two ethanoate ions (CH₃COO⁻) are needed.
