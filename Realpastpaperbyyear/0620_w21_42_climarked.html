Recap

Both boiling and evaporation are changes from a liquid to a gas and require an input of energy. Boiling is a rapid change that occurs at the boiling point. Evaporation is a slower change that happens below the boiling point, only at the liquid's surface.

Recap

0620/42/O/N/21 Q4 | Topics: Energetics, Equilibrium, Bond Energy, Covalent Bonding

Chlorine reacts with carbon monoxide to produce phosgene gas, COCl₂(g). A catalyst is used.

Cl₂(g) + CO(g) ⇌ COCl₂(g)

The reaction is exothermic.

(a) Explain why the reaction is exothermic in terms of the energy changes of bond breaking and bond making. [3]

All chemical reactions involve breaking old bonds and making new ones. Bond breaking always requires energy (it is endothermic). Bond making always releases energy (it is exothermic). In an exothermic reaction, more energy is released when making new bonds (in the products) than the energy required to break the old bonds (in the reactants).

Recap

In any reaction, bond breaking is endothermic (energy is absorbed) and bond making is exothermic (energy is released). The overall enthalpy change ($\Delta H)$ is the difference between these. If $\Delta H$ is negative, the reaction is exothermic because more energy was released than absorbed.

(b) (i) Complete the energy level diagram for this reaction. On your diagram show:

  • the product of the reaction
  • an arrow representing the energy change, labelled $\Delta H$
  • an arrow representing the activation energy, labelled A. [3]

The completed diagram should show:

  1. A horizontal line for the product COCl₂(g) drawn at a lower energy level than the reactants.
  2. A downward vertical arrow pointing from the reactant energy level down to the product energy level, labelled $\Delta H$.
  3. A "hump" representing the reaction pathway. An arrow, labelled A, should point upwards from the reactant energy level to the peak of this hump.

Recap

A reaction pathway diagram shows energy changes. Because this reaction is exothermic, the products (COCl₂) are more stable and are at a lower energy level than the reactants. The overall energy released is the enthalpy change ($\Delta H)$. The activation energy (A or $E_a$) is the minimum energy required to start the reaction, represented by the energy "hump" from the reactants to the transition state.

(ii) State why a catalyst is used. [1]

A catalyst is used to increase the rate of the reaction.

Recap

A catalyst increases the rate of a chemical reaction but is not used up. It works by providing an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy ($E_a$), meaning a greater proportion of particle collisions will be successful.

(c) Describe and explain the effect, if any, on the position of equilibrium when:

(i) the pressure is increased [2]

Effect: The position of equilibrium shifts to the right (towards the product).

Explanation: There are fewer moles of gas on the right-hand side (1 mole of COCl₂) compared to the left-hand side (1 mole of Cl₂ + 1 mole of CO = 2 moles). Increasing the pressure favours the side with fewer moles of gas.

Recap

According to Le Chatelier's principle, if a change (like increasing pressure) is applied to a system at equilibrium, the system will shift to oppose that change. Increasing pressure is opposed by shifting to the side with fewer moles of gas. If the moles of gas are equal on both sides, pressure has no effect on the position of equilibrium.

(ii) the temperature is increased. [2]

Effect: The position of equilibrium shifts to the left (towards the reactants).

Explanation: The forward reaction is exothermic (releases heat). Increasing the temperature favours the endothermic (heat-absorbing) direction, which is the reverse reaction.

Recap

Increasing the temperature of a system at equilibrium will favour the endothermic direction. Decreasing the temperature will favour the exothermic direction. Since this forward reaction is exothermic (releases heat), increasing the temperature will push the equilibrium to the left.

(d) ... When one mole of chlorine reacts with one mole of carbon monoxide, 230 kJ of energy is released.

Some bond energies are shown in the table.

bond bond energy in kJ/mol
Cl–Cl 240
C=O (in COCl₂) 745
C–Cl 400

Use the information to calculate the energy of the bond between the C and the O in carbon monoxide, CO. [3]

Step 1: Energy absorbed to break bonds (Reactants)

Bonds broken = 1 x (Cl–Cl) + 1 x (C≡O in CO)

Energy in = 240 + x (where x is the bond energy of CO)

Step 2: Energy released making bonds (Products)

Bonds made = 2 x (C–Cl) + 1 x (C=O in COCl₂)

Energy out = (2 x 400) + 745 = 800 + 745 = 1545 kJ/mol

Step 3: Calculate using ΔH = Energy In - Energy Out

The reaction is exothermic and releases 230 kJ, so ΔH = -230 kJ/mol.

-230 = (240 + x) - 1545

-230 = x - 1305

x = 1305 - 230

x = 1075 kJ/mol

Bond energy in carbon monoxide, CO = 1075 kJ/mol

Recap

The overall enthalpy change ($\Delta H)$ of a reaction is the total energy absorbed to break bonds (reactants) minus the total energy released when making bonds (products). Remember that an exothermic reaction has a negative $\Delta H$ value. By substituting the known values into the equation, you can solve for the unknown bond energy.

(e) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement in a molecule of COCl₂. Show outer electrons only. [3]

The completed diagram should show:

  • A central carbon (C) atom.
  • Two chlorine (Cl) atoms and one oxygen (O) atom bonded to the carbon.
  • A double covalent bond (two pairs of shared electrons, e.g., 2 dots from C, 2 crosses from O) between C and O.
  • A single covalent bond (one pair of shared electrons) between C and each Cl.
  • The correct number of non-bonding (lone pair) electrons: 4 on the oxygen atom and 6 on each chlorine atom, so all atoms achieve a stable outer shell of 8 electrons.

Finally, add lone pair electrons to each atom until they all have a full outer shell (8 electrons).
