Question 1

Paper: 0620/42/O/N/20
Topics: Atomic Structure, Periodic Table

The electronic structures of some atoms and ions are shown.

(a) Write the letters, A, B, C, D, E, F, G or H, of the electronic structures which show:

(i) atoms of two different noble gases [2]

A and H

Recap

Noble gases (Group VIII) are unreactive because they have a full outer shell of electrons, which is a stable electronic configuration. Structure A (Helium) has 2 electrons (full first shell). Structure H (Argon) has 8 outer electrons.

(ii) an ion of a Group I element [1]

B

Recap

Group I elements form positive ions (cations) with a 1+ charge by losing one electron to achieve a stable outer shell. Structure B has a 1+ charge.

(iii) an ion of a Group V element [1]

D

Recap

Group V elements form negative ions (anions) with a 3- charge by gaining three electrons. Structure D has a 3- charge.

(iv) a pair of ions that could form a compound with the formula XY₂. [1]

C and G OR C and E

Recap

For a formula XY₂, you need one ion with a 2+ charge and two ions with a 1- charge to balance the electrical charge. C is 2+, while E and G are 1-.

(b) State which electronic structure, A, B, C, D, E, F, G or H, is incorrect. Explain why. [2]

Incorrect electronic structure: F

Explanation: The third shell / outer shell is being filled before the second shell is full; the second shell has 6 electrons but it should have 8 electrons.

Recap

Electrons occupy shells in order of energy. The first shell holds 2, the second holds 8, and the third holds 8. Shells must be filled before moving to the next level.

(c) State how many protons are found in the nucleus of ion C. [1]

12

Recap

Structure C is a 2+ ion with 10 electrons (2,8). A neutral atom would have 10 + 2 = 12 electrons. The proton number equals the number of electrons in a neutral atom.

(d) Use the Periodic Table to deduce:

(i) the chemical symbol for ion G [1]

H

Recap

Ion G has 1- charge and 2 electrons. Neutral atom has 1 electron. Atomic number 1 is Hydrogen.

(ii) the element which forms an ion with a 3+ charge and the same electronic structure as H. [1]

aluminium / Al

Recap

Structure H has 10 electrons (2,8). If an ion has a 3+ charge and 10 electrons, the neutral atom must have 10 + 3 = 13 protons. Element 13 is Aluminium.

Question 2

Paper: 0620/42/O/N/20
Topics: Acids, Salts, Electrolysis, Stoichiometry

Soluble salts can be made by adding a metal carbonate to a dilute acid.

(a) Give the formula of the dilute acid which reacts with a metal carbonate to form a nitrate salt. [1]

HNO₃

Recap

To make nitrate salts, you must use nitric acid. Similarly, sulfuric acid makes sulfates and hydrochloric acid makes chlorides.

(b) A student wanted to make hydrated iron(II) sulfate crystals, FeSO₄ • xH₂O, by adding excess iron(II) carbonate to dilute sulfuric acid. The student followed the procedure shown.

  • step 1: Add dilute sulfuric acid to a beaker.
  • step 2: Add small amounts of iron(II) carbonate to the dilute sulfuric acid in the beaker until the iron(II) carbonate is in excess.
  • step 3: Filter the mixture formed in step 2.
  • step 4: Heat the filtrate until it is a saturated solution. Allow to cool.
  • step 5: Once cold, pour away the remaining solution. Dry the crystals between filter papers.

(i) Why must the iron(II) carbonate be added in excess in step 2? [1]

To make sure all the sulfuric acid reacts / is used up.

Recap

The solid (carbonate) is added in excess so that the acid is the limiting reactant. This ensures no unreacted acid remains in the salt solution.

(ii) State two observations in step 2 that would show that iron(II) carbonate was in excess. [2]

1. No more fizzing / bubbling stops.
2. Solid remains / visible solid at the bottom.

(iii) Describe what should be done during step 3 to ensure there is a maximum yield of crystals. [1]

Rinse the residue (with distilled water).

Recap

Filtration separates the solid residue (excess carbonate) from the liquid filtrate (salt solution). Rinsing ensures all the salt solution passes through.

(iv) A saturated solution is formed in step 4. Describe what a saturated solution is. [2]

A solution that can dissolve no more solute at a specified temperature.

Recap

A saturated solution contains the maximum concentration of solute dissolved in a solvent at a particular temperature.

(v) Name a different compound that could be used instead of iron(II) carbonate to produce hydrated iron(II) sulfate crystals from dilute sulfuric acid. [1]

iron(II) oxide OR iron(II) hydroxide (Iron metal is also technically possible but reacts slowly).

Recap

Acids react with metal oxides, metal hydroxides, or carbonates to produce a salt and water (and CO₂ for carbonates).

(c) On analysing the crystals, the student found that one mole of the hydrated iron(II) sulfate crystals, FeSO₄ • xH₂O, had a mass of 278 g.
Determine the value of x using the following steps:
calculate the mass of one mole of FeSO₄
calculate the mass of H₂O present in one mole of FeSO₄ • xH₂O
determine the value of x. [3]

Mass of one mole of FeSO₄ = 56 + 32 + (4 x 16) = 152 g
Mass of H₂O = 278 - 152 = 126 g
Value of x = 126 / 18 = 7

Recap

Relative molecular mass (Mr) is the sum of the relative atomic masses. Water of crystallisation is water chemically combined in the crystal structure. 1 mole of water (H₂O) has a mass of 1 + 1 + 16 = 18g.

(d) Insoluble salts can be made by mixing solutions of two soluble salts. A student followed the procedure shown to make silver bromide, an insoluble salt.

  • step 1 Add aqueous silver nitrate to a beaker. Then add aqueous potassium bromide and stir.
  • step 2 Filter the mixture formed in step 1.
  • step 3 Dry the residue.

(i) State the term used to describe this method of making salts. [1]

Precipitation

(ii) Give the observation the student would make during step 1. [1]

Cream precipitate

Recap

Silver halides form precipitates. Silver chloride is white, Silver bromide is cream, Silver iodide is yellow.

(iii) Write the ionic equation for the reaction between aqueous silver nitrate and aqueous potassium bromide. Include state symbols. [3]

Ag⁺(aq) + Br⁻(aq) → AgBr(s)

(1 mark for AgBr product, 1 mark for correct ions, 1 mark for state symbols)

Recap

An ionic equation shows only the ions that change. Spectator ions (K⁺ and NO₃⁻) are omitted. The precipitate is a solid (s) formed from aqueous (aq) ions.

(e) Sodium chloride is an ionic salt. It can be made by reacting sodium with chlorine gas.
2Na(s) + Cl₂(g) → 2NaCl(s)
Calculate the volume of chlorine gas, in cm³, that reacts to form 2.34 g of NaCl.
The reaction takes place at room temperature and pressure. [3]

1. Moles of NaCl = mass / Mr = 2.34 / 58.5 = 0.04 mol
2. Moles of Cl₂ = 0.04 / 2 = 0.02 mol (Mole ratio 2:1)
3. Volume of Cl₂ = 0.02 x 24000 = 480 cm³

Recap

One mole of any gas occupies 24 dm³ (or 24000 cm³) at r.t.p. (Molar gas volume).

(f) Sodium chloride does not conduct electricity when solid, but does conduct electricity when molten.

(i) Explain why, in terms of structure and bonding. [3]

Ionic compounds contain ions. In the solid, the ions are fixed in a lattice and cannot move. When molten, the ions are mobile / free to move.

(ii) Name the product formed at the positive electrode when electricity is passed through molten sodium chloride. [1]

Chlorine

Recap

The positive electrode is the anode. Negative ions (anions) like chloride (Cl⁻) are attracted to it and lose electrons to form the non-metal gas.

(iii) State the type of change that occurs at the positive electrode in (ii). Explain your answer in terms of electron transfer. [2]

Type: Oxidation
Explanation: Electrons are lost.

Recap

OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss of electrons, Reduction Is Gain of electrons. Oxidation always happens at the anode.

(iv) Describe what else can be done to sodium chloride to allow it to conduct electricity. [1]

Dissolve it (in water) / Make an aqueous solution.

Topics: Periodic Table, Metals, Rusting

Group I metals are very reactive. Transition elements are also metals but are less reactive than Group I metals.

(a) State two physical properties of Group I metals which are similar to those of transition metals. [2]

Any two from:
• Shiny / lustrous
• Conduct electricity
• Conduct heat
• Malleable / Ductile

(b) Describe two ways in which the physical properties of Group I metals are different from those of transition metals. [2]

1. Group I metals have lower density.
2. Group I metals have lower melting points.
(Also: Group I metals are softer)

Recap

Transition metals are generally hard, dense, and have high melting points. Group I metals (Alkali metals) are soft enough to cut with a knife and have low densities (some float on water).

(c) When Group I metals are added to water they fizz and an alkaline solution forms.

(i) Name the gas given off. [1]

Hydrogen

(ii) Identify the ion present in the solution which makes the solution alkaline. [1]

Hydroxide / OH⁻

(iii) Write the chemical equation for the reaction between sodium and water. [2]

2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂

(1 mark for NaOH, 1 mark for correct balancing)

(d) When the transition element iron is added to water the iron rusts. When an iron object is coated with a layer of zinc, rusting is prevented.

(i) Name this process of coating iron objects with a layer of zinc. [1]

Galvanising

(ii) Explain how completely coating an iron object with a layer of zinc prevents rusting. [1]

It acts as a barrier preventing water/oxygen from reaching the iron.

(iii) Rusting of iron ships can be prevented by attaching zinc blocks to the hull of the ship. Explain how this prevents rusting. [2]

Zinc is more reactive than iron. Zinc corrodes/oxidises (sacrificially) in preference to iron.

Recap

Sacrificial protection occurs because a more reactive metal loses electrons more easily than iron, preventing the iron from oxidizing.

Topics: Organic Chemistry, Alkenes, Alkanes

Alkenes and alkanes are homologous series of compounds containing carbon and hydrogen atoms.

(a) State the name of the type of compound made from carbon and hydrogen atoms only. [1]

Hydrocarbon

(b) Alkenes take part in addition reactions.

(i) Describe what is meant by the term addition reaction. [1]

(Only) one product is formed.

Recap

An addition reaction is one where two or more molecules join together to form a single product. This usually involves breaking a C=C double bond.

(ii) Draw the structure of the product made in the addition reaction between propene and bromine. Show all of the atoms and all of the bonds. [2]

Structure of 1,2-dibromopropane:
Three carbons in a chain. Single bonds between carbons.
Bromine atoms on C1 and C2 (where the double bond was).
Hydrogen atoms on remaining bonds (C1 has 2 H, C2 has 1 H, C3 has 3 H).

(iii) Describe the colour change seen when propene is added to aqueous bromine. [2]

From orange to colourless.

Recap

This is the test for unsaturation (alkenes). Saturated compounds (alkanes) do not decolourise bromine water.

(iv) Draw the structures of molecules of two different alkenes which both undergo an addition reaction with steam to form butan-2-ol. Show all of the atoms and all of the bonds. [2]

1. but-1-ene (C=C double bond between first and second carbon).
2. but-2-ene (C=C double bond between second and third carbon).

(c) Propane undergoes a substitution reaction with chlorine. Write the chemical equation for the reaction between one molecule of propane and one molecule of chlorine. [2]

C₃H₈ + Cl₂ → C₃H₇Cl + HCl

Recap

In a substitution reaction (photochemical reaction, needs UV light), one hydrogen atom is replaced by a chlorine atom. HCl is always a product.

This question is about alcohols, carboxylic acids and esters.

(a) Ethanol will react with hot aqueous potassium manganate(VII) to form ethanoic acid.

Presence of an acid (acidified).

Oxidation

Oxidation of ethanol produces ethanoic acid. Potassium manganate(VII) is the oxidising agent (changes from purple to colourless).

(iii) The structure of ethanoic acid is shown.

Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement in a molecule of ethanoic acid. [3]

• All single bonding dot and cross pairs correct (C-H, C-C, C-O, O-H).

(b) Ethanoic acid is a weak acid and hydrochloric acid is a strong acid. Complete the table to show the similarities and differences in the properties of samples of these two acids of equal concentration. [6]

Property Dilute Ethanoic Acid Dilute Hydrochloric Acid
Extent of dissociation Partial Full / 100%
Colour after adding universal indicator Orange / Yellow Red (indicating lower pH)
Observation when magnesium ribbon is added Fizzing / Dissolving Fizzing / Dissolving (Observation happens quicker)

Recap

Strong acids completely dissociate (ionise) in water to release H⁺ ions. Weak acids only partially dissociate. Strong acids have a lower pH and react faster.

(c) Ethanoic acid will react with an alcohol to form the ester shown.

(i) Name the other product formed when ethanoic acid reacts with an alcohol to make this ester. [1]

Water

(ii) Give one condition needed when ethanoic acid reacts with the alcohol to make this ester. [1]

Acid catalyst (concentrated sulfuric acid) / Heat.

(iii) Draw the structure of the alcohol which was added to ethanoic acid to make this ester. Show all of the atoms and all of the bonds. [2]

Structure of propan-2-ol:
Three carbon chain. OH group on the middle carbon (C2).

Recap

The ester shown is formed from the acid part (CH₃COO-) and the alcohol part (-CH(CH₃)₂). This corresponds to the isopropyl group, so the alcohol is propan-2-ol.

(d) Polyesters can be manufactured from carboxylic acids and alcohols.

(i) State what is meant by the term functional group. [1]

The atoms/group of atoms which give a molecule its chemical properties.

(ii) Determine the empirical formula of hexanedioic acid (Structure: HOOC-CH₂-CH₂-CH₂-CH₂-COOH). [1]

C₃H₅O₂

Recap

Molecular formula: C₆H₁₀O₄. Divide all subscripts by 2 to get the simplest ratio: C₃H₅O₂.

(iii) Calculate the percentage by mass of oxygen present in ethanediol (HO-CH₂-CH₂-OH). Give your answer to the nearest whole number. [2]

Mr of Ethanediol (C₂H₆O₂) = (2x12) + (6x1) + (2x16) = 62.
Mass of Oxygen = 2 x 16 = 32.
Percentage = (32 / 62) x 100 = 52%

(iv) Complete the diagram to show a section of polyester manufactured from hexanedioic acid and ethanediol. Include all of the atoms and all of the bonds in the linkages. [2]

Diagram should show ester linkages (-COO-) connecting the blocks.
Acid block loses OH, Alcohol block loses H.

Recap

Condensation polymerisation releases a small molecule (water) when the monomers join. Polyesters are formed from a dicarboxylic acid and a diol.

(v) State the name of a polyester. [1]

Terylene
