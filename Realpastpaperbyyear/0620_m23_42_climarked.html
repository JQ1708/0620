Question 1

This question is about gases found in clean, dry air and gases found in polluted air.

(a) Name one gas found in clean, dry air which contributes to global warming. [1]

The gas found in clean, dry air that contributes to global warming is carbon dioxide.

Recap

Carbon dioxide (CO₂) and methane (CH₄) are known as greenhouse gases. These gases contribute to global warming by absorbing thermal energy reflected from the Earth's surface, which reduces the amount of energy lost to space.

(b) State the percentage of nitrogen in clean, dry air. [1]

The percentage of nitrogen in clean, dry air is 78%.

Recap

Clean, dry air is a mixture of gases. Its typical composition is 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, about 1% argon, and 0.04% carbon dioxide.

(c) Name the substance used to remove sulfur dioxide in flue gas desulfurisation. [1]

The substance used to remove sulfur dioxide in flue gas desulfurisation is calcium oxide.

Recap

Flue gas desulfurisation is a process to remove acidic sulfur dioxide (a cause of acid rain) from waste gases. This is often done by reacting the gases with a base, such as calcium oxide (CaO) or calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)₂).

(d) Nitrogen dioxide, NO₂, is formed in car engines. Name the equipment in a car exhaust used to remove the NO₂ formed in car engines. [1]

The equipment used to remove NO₂ from car engines is a catalytic converter.

Recap

A catalytic converter is part of a car's exhaust system. It contains catalysts (like platinum) that convert harmful pollutants into less harmful gases. For example, it converts oxides of nitrogen and toxic carbon monoxide into harmless nitrogen and carbon dioxide.

(e) All gases diffuse.

(i) Choose from the list of formulae the gas which diffuses most quickly.
NO₂     SO₂     CO     CO₂     CH₄ [1]

The gas that diffuses most quickly is CH₄.

Recap

Diffusion is the spreading out and mixing of different particles because of their random movement. The rate of diffusion depends on the relative molecular mass (Mᵣ) of the gas; gases with a lower Mᵣ diffuse faster.

(ii) Explain your answer to (i). [1]

CH₄ diffuses most quickly because it has the lowest relative molecular mass (Mᵣ = 16) compared to CO (28), CO₂ (44), SO₂ (64), and NO₂ (46).

Recap

Relative molecular mass (Mᵣ) is the sum of the relative atomic masses of all atoms in one molecule. Lighter particles move faster and therefore diffuse more quickly than heavier particles.

(f) State one adverse effect of carbon monoxide on human health. [1]

One adverse effect of carbon monoxide is that it is toxic (poisonous).

Recap

Carbon monoxide (CO) is a toxic gas produced during the incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels. It is dangerous because it reduces the ability of blood to transport oxygen around the body.

(g) Carbon dioxide, CO₂, is a reactant in photosynthesis. Name the two products of photosynthesis. [2]

The two products of photosynthesis are glucose and oxygen.

Recap

Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. The word equation is: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen. This process requires light and chlorophyll.

Question 2

Lithium, sodium and potassium are Group I elements.

(a) Name the type of bonding in these elements. [1]

The type of bonding in Group I elements is metallic bonding.

Recap

Metallic bonding consists of a regular lattice of positive metal ions surrounded by a 'sea' of delocalised electrons. The strong electrostatic attraction between the positive ions and the negative electrons holds the structure together.

The test is to use a lighted splint, which will cause a 'squeaky pop' if hydrogen is present.

Recap

Tests for gases are essential in practical chemistry. Other common tests include: oxygen (relights a glowing splint), chlorine (bleaches damp litmus paper), and carbon dioxide (turns limewater milky).

(ii) Suggest the pH of a solution of a strong alkali. [1]

A suggested pH for a strong alkali is pH 13 or pH 14.

Recap

The pH scale (from 0 to 14) measures acidity and alkalinity. Strong alkalis, which are completely ionised in water to produce a high concentration of OH⁻ ions, have very high pH values (e.g., 12-14). Strong acids have very low pH values (e.g., 0-2). A pH of 7 is neutral.

(iii) Name a substance which can be used to confirm the pH of a solution of a strong alkali. [1]

Universal indicator can be used to confirm the pH.

Recap

Universal indicator is a mixture of indicators which is used to determine pH of a solution by comparing its colour to the colours on a pH colour chart.

Recap

Group I metals (alkali metals) are very reactive. Their reactivity increases down the group. They react vigorously with water to produce a metal hydroxide (which is an alkali) and hydrogen gas.

Isotopes are atoms of the same element (so they have the same proton number) but different mass numbers. This is because they have different numbers of neutrons in their nucleus.

Question 3

The Haber process is used to manufacture ammonia.

(a) State the main source of each gas used in the Haber process. [2]

nitrogen ....................................
hydrogen ....................................

nitrogen: air
hydrogen: methane (or natural gas)

Recap

The Haber process is the industrial manufacture of ammonia (NH₃). The raw materials are nitrogen (obtained from the fractional distillation of liquid air) and hydrogen (often produced from the reaction of methane with steam).

(b) The equation for the Haber process is shown.
N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)
The reaction is reversible. The forward reaction is exothermic.

(i) State what is meant by the symbol ∆H. [1]

∆H stands for enthalpy change.

Recap

Enthalpy change (∆H) represents the overall thermal energy change during a chemical reaction at constant pressure.

(ii) ∆H for the forward reaction is –92 kJ / mol.
State why this value shows that the forward reaction is exothermic. [1]

This value shows the reaction is exothermic because the sign of ∆H is negative.

Recap

An exothermic reaction is one that releases thermal energy to the surroundings. This is shown by a negative ∆H value. An endothermic reaction (which absorbs energy) has a positive ∆H value.

(iii) State the typical conditions and name the catalyst used in the Haber process. [3]

temperature .................................... °C
pressure .................................... kPa
catalyst ....................................

temperature: 450 °C
pressure: 20 000 kPa (or 200 atm)
catalyst: iron

Recap

These conditions are a compromise. A lower temperature would give a higher *yield* (as the reaction is exothermic), but the *rate* would be too slow. A higher pressure would also increase the yield (fewer moles of gas on the right), but is very expensive. The iron catalyst increases the rate without affecting the yield.

(iv) Complete Table 3.1 to show the effect, if any, when the typical conditions in the Haber process are changed. Use only the words increases, decreases or no change. [4]

Recap

  • Temperature: Increasing temperature always increases the rate (more kinetic energy). Since the forward reaction is exothermic (negative ∆H), increasing temperature shifts the equilibrium to the left (reactants), decreasing the yield.
  • Pressure: Decreasing pressure decreases the rate (particles are further apart). The equation is N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) (4 moles of gas ⇌ 2 moles of gas). Decreasing pressure shifts the equilibrium to the side with more moles of gas (left), decreasing the yield.
  • Catalyst: A catalyst increases the rate of both forward and reverse reactions equally. It has no effect on the position of equilibrium or the final yield, but it allows equilibrium to be reached faster.

(v) Explain in terms of collision theory why increasing the temperature increases the rate of the reaction. [3]

Increasing the temperature gives the particles more kinetic energy, so they move faster. This leads to an increase in the frequency of collisions. More importantly, a higher proportion of these collisions will have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy, resulting in more successful (effective) collisions per second.

Recap

According to collision theory, for a reaction to occur, particles must collide with the correct orientation and with sufficient energy (the activation energy, Eₐ).

(c) Ammonia reacts with an acid to form ammonium sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄.

(i) State the formula of the acid used. [1]

The formula of the acid is H₂SO₄.

Recap

The name of the salt (ammonium sulfate) reveals the acid used. Sulfates are salts derived from sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄). Nitrates come from nitric acid (HNO₃) and chlorides come from hydrochloric acid (HCl).

(ii) State one use of ammonium sulfate. [1]

Ammonium sulfate is used as a fertiliser.

Recap

Ammonium salts and nitrates are common fertilisers because they provide nitrogen, an essential element for plant growth (needed for making proteins).

(iii) Calculate the percentage composition by mass of nitrogen in (NH₄)₂SO₄. [2]

Working:
  1. Find the relative formula mass (Mᵣ) of (NH₄)₂SO₄.
    Mᵣ = (2 × N) + (8 × H) + (1 × S) + (4 × O)
    Mᵣ = (2 × 14) + (8 × 1) + (1 × 32) + (4 × 16) = 28 + 8 + 32 + 64 = 132
  2. Find the total mass of nitrogen (N) in the formula (there are 2 N atoms).
    Mass of N = 2 × 14 = 28

percentage of nitrogen = 21.2 %

Recap

Percentage composition by mass is calculated by dividing the total mass of the element in one formula unit by the total relative formula mass (Mᵣ) of the compound, and then multiplying by 100.

Question 4

Copper is element 29 in the Periodic Table.

(a) Brass contains copper.

(i) Name the other metal in brass. [1]

The other metal in brass is zinc.

Recap

Brass is an alloy, which is a mixture of a metal with other elements. Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc.

(ii) State the term given to a mixture of a metal with another element. [1]

This mixture is called an alloy.

Recap

Alloys are often harder and stronger than the pure metals they are made from. This is because the different sized atoms disrupt the regular layers, preventing them from sliding over each other easily.

(b) Copper can be stretched into wires. Copper wires conduct electricity.

(i) Name the property of metals which means that they can be stretched into wires. [1]

This property is called ductility (or being ductile).

Recap

Metals have characteristic physical properties. They are malleable (can be hammered into shape) and ductile (can be drawn into wires). This is because the layers of positive ions in the metallic lattice can slide over one another.

(ii) Name the particles responsible for the conduction of electricity in solid copper. [1]

The particles responsible are electrons (specifically, delocalised electrons).

Recap

Metals are good electrical conductors because they possess a 'sea' of delocalised electrons. These electrons are mobile and are free to move throughout the entire structure, carrying charge.

One other physical property of transition elements is high melting point.

Hydrated copper(II) sulfate crystals are blue.

The test for water uses anhydrous copper(II) sulfate, which is a white powder. When water is added, it turns blue as it becomes hydrated.

(e) Copper(II) oxide is formed when copper(II) nitrate, Cu(NO₃)₂, is heated.
2Cu(NO₃)₂(s) → 2CuO(s) + 4NO₂(g) + O₂(g)

(i) State the class of oxide to which copper(II) oxide belongs. [1]

Copper(II) oxide is a basic oxide.

Recap

Metal oxides (like copper(II) oxide) are typically basic, meaning they react with acids to form a salt and water. Non-metal oxides (like sulfur dioxide) are typically acidic.

(ii) State the meaning of the Roman numeral (II) in the name copper(II) oxide. [1]

The Roman numeral (II) means the oxidation number (or oxidation state) of copper is +2.

Recap

Transition elements often show variable oxidation states. Roman numerals are used in the name to specify which state is present. For example, iron(II) oxide (FeO) contains the Fe²⁺ ion, while iron(III) oxide (Fe₂O₃) contains the Fe³⁺ ion.

(iii) 0.0200 moles of Cu(NO₃)₂ is heated.
Calculate the mass of 0.0200 moles of Cu(NO₃)₂. [2]

Working:
  1. Find the relative formula mass (Mᵣ) of Cu(NO₃)₂.
    Mᵣ = (1 × Cu) + (2 × N) + (6 × O) = 64 + (2 × 14) + (6 × 16) = 64 + 28 + 96 = 188
  2. Calculate the mass.
    Mass = moles × Mᵣ = 0.0200 mol × 188 g/mol = 3.76 g
mass = 3.76 g

Recap

The relationship between mass, moles, and relative formula mass (Mᵣ) is: Mass = Moles × Mᵣ.

(iv) Calculate the total volume of gas, in dm³, at r.t.p., produced when 0.0200 moles of Cu(NO₃)₂ is heated. [2]

Working:
  1. Find the mole ratio of reactant to gas products from the equation:
    2Cu(NO₃)₂ → 4NO₂ + 1O₂
    This is a 2 : 5 mole ratio (2 moles of reactant produce 4 + 1 = 5 moles of gas).
  2. Calculate moles of gas produced.
    Moles of gas = 0.0200 mol × (5 moles of gas / 2 moles of reactant) = 0.0500 mol
  3. Calculate the volume of gas at r.t.p. (room temperature and pressure).
    Volume = Moles of gas × Molar gas volume = 0.0500 mol × 24.0 dm³/mol = 1.20 dm³
volume = 1.20 dm³

Recap

At room temperature and pressure (r.t.p.), one mole of any gas occupies a volume of 24 dm³ (or 24,000 cm³). The stoichiometry (the mole ratio) from the balanced equation must be used to find the moles of product.

(v) Powdered aluminium reduces copper(II) oxide.
Write the symbol equation for this reaction. [2]

2Al + 3CuO → Al₂O₃ + 3Cu

Recap

This is a redox (reduction-oxidation) reaction. Aluminium is more reactive than copper. Therefore, aluminium displaces copper from its oxide. The aluminium is oxidised (gains oxygen) and the copper(II) oxide is reduced (loses oxygen).

Question 5

Propane, propene, propan-1-ol and propanoic acid are members of different homologous series. Molecules of these substances contain three carbon atoms.

(a) Explain why members of a homologous series have similar chemical properties. [1]

Members of a homologous series have similar chemical properties because they all have the same functional group.

Recap

A functional group is an atom or group of atoms (e.g., -OH for alcohols, -COOH for carboxylic acids) that determines the characteristic chemical properties of a homologous series.

(b) Name the homologous series to which propanoic acid belongs. [1]

Propanoic acid belongs to the carboxylic acids homologous series.

Recap

The name ending "-oic acid" (like propanoic acid) indicates a member of the carboxylic acid series. These compounds contain the -COOH functional group.

(c) State the general formula of the homologous series to which propanoic acid belongs. [1]

CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH

Recap

A general formula represents all members of a homologous series. For propanoic acid, n=2 (C₂H₅COOH). For ethanoic acid, n=1 (CH₃COOH). For methanoic acid, n=0 (HCOOH).

(e) Propane and propene can be manufactured by heating decane, C₁₀H₂₂, in the presence of a catalyst. One other product is formed.

C₁₀H₂₂ → ....................................

Recap

This is an example of cracking. The large alkane (C₁₀H₂₂) is broken down. The products must balance the reactants in terms of C and H atoms. Here, propane (C₃H₈) is a shorter alkane, while propene (C₃H₆) and butene (C₄H₈) are alkenes. (C atoms: 3+3+4 = 10. H atoms: 8+6+8 = 22).

This process is called cracking.

Cracking is essential in the oil industry because there is a higher demand for shorter-chain hydrocarbons (like gasoline) and alkenes (for making polymers) than is supplied by the initial fractional distillation of petroleum.
