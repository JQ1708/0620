Question 1 | 0620/42/M/20 | Organic Chemistry (Petroleum)
1 Petroleum is a useful natural resource.
The diagram shows how petroleum can be separated into useful substances.
(a) What is the name of the separation process shown in the diagram? [1]
The name of the separation process is fractional distillation.

Recap

Fractional Distillation of Petroleum: Petroleum (crude oil) is a mixture of hydrocarbons. It is separated into fractions containing molecules with similar boiling points by fractional distillation. The process occurs in a fractionating column where the temperature is high at the bottom and low at the top.
(b) Name the fraction leaving at:
A
B [2]
A is naphtha.
B is bitumen.

Recap

Uses of Petroleum Fractions:
  • Refinery gas: fuel for heating and cooking.
  • Gasoline (petrol): fuel for cars.
  • Naphtha: chemical feedstock.
  • Kerosene (paraffin): jet fuel.
  • Diesel oil: fuel for diesel engines.
  • Fuel oil: fuel for ships and home heating.
  • Lubricating oil: lubricants, waxes and polishes.
  • Bitumen: making roads.
(c) Refinery gas is a mixture of hydrocarbons.
One refinery gas is butane, C₄H₁₀
(i) Suggest the names of two other refinery gases. [2]
Two other refinery gases are methane and ethane (or propane).

Recap

Alkanes: Refinery gases usually contain short-chain alkanes (saturated hydrocarbons). The first four alkanes are methane (CH₄), ethane (C₂H₆), propane (C₃H₈), and butane (C₄H₁₀).
(ii) Write the chemical equation for the complete combustion of butane. [2]
2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O

Recap

Combustion: Complete combustion of hydrocarbons produces carbon dioxide and water. The general equation involves reacting the fuel with excess oxygen.
(iii) Name the toxic gas produced by the incomplete combustion of butane. [1]
The toxic gas is carbon monoxide.

Recap

Incomplete Combustion: When oxygen is limiting, incomplete combustion occurs, producing carbon monoxide (a toxic gas) and sometimes particulates (soot).
(d) Gasoline and kerosene are both fuels. They have different properties.
(i) Describe the differences in the properties given.
viscosity of the fuel
flammability of the fuel [2]
Kerosene is more viscous than gasoline, and gasoline is more flammable than kerosene.

Recap

Trends in Fractions: As the carbon chain length increases (going down the fractionating column):
  • Viscosity increases (they flow less easily).
  • Flammability decreases (they are harder to ignite).
  • Boiling point increases.
(ii) What difference in the molecules of gasoline and kerosene causes these differences in properties? [1]
Kerosene has molecules with a longer carbon chain compared to gasoline.

Recap

Molecular Structure: The physical properties of fractions depend on the size of the molecules. Larger molecules have stronger intermolecular forces, leading to higher viscosity and boiling points.
(e) Hydrogen fuel cells can be used to power vehicles.
Write the word equation for the overall reaction that takes place in a hydrogen fuel cell. [1]
hydrogen + oxygen → water

Recap

Fuel Cells: A hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell uses hydrogen and oxygen to generate electricity. The only product is water, making it a non-polluting energy source.
Question 2 | 0620/42/M/20 | Electrolysis (Aluminium)
2 Aluminium is extracted from its ore. The ore is converted into pure aluminium oxide, which then undergoes electrolysis as shown.
(a) (i) Name an ore of aluminium. [1]
An ore of aluminium is bauxite.

Recap

Ores: Bauxite is the main ore of aluminium. It contains aluminium oxide. Hematite is an ore of iron.
(ii) What is meant by the term electrolysis? [2]
Electrolysis is the breakdown of an ionic compound in molten or aqueous state by the passage of electricity.

Recap

Definition: Electrolysis is the decomposition of an ionic compound (the electrolyte) when molten or in aqueous solution by the passage of an electric current.
(b) Aluminium oxide has a melting point of about 2000°C but the electrolysis process operates at about 900°C.
(i) Name the compound added to aluminium oxide to reduce the operating temperature. [1]
The compound added is cryolite.

Recap

Cryolite: In the extraction of aluminium, molten cryolite is used as a solvent to dissolve aluminium oxide. This lowers the melting point of the electrolyte, saving energy.
(ii) Suggest one benefit to the environment of reducing the operating temperature. [1]
A benefit is less CO₂ emission.

Recap

Energy and Environment: Lowering the operating temperature reduces the energy required for heating. Since energy generation often involves burning fossil fuels, using less energy reduces carbon dioxide emissions and global warming.
(iii) Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction taking place at:
the negative electrode (cathode)
the positive electrode (anode) [4]
Cathode: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al
Anode: 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻

Recap

Half-Equations: At the cathode (negative), positive ions gain electrons (reduction). At the anode (positive), negative ions lose electrons (oxidation).
(iv) Explain why the anodes need frequent replacement. [2]
The anodes (carbon/graphite) react with the oxygen produced to form carbon dioxide.

Recap

Anode Wear: In aluminium extraction, the anodes are made of carbon. The oxygen formed at the high operating temperatures reacts with the carbon anodes, burning them away as CO₂.
(c) Aluminium oxide reacts with acids and with alkalis.
(i) What term is used to describe an oxide that reacts with acids and with alkalis? [1]
The term is amphoteric.

Recap

Amphoteric Oxides: Amphoteric oxides (e.g., aluminium oxide, zinc oxide) can react with both acids and bases to form a salt and water.
(ii) Aluminium oxide reacts with dilute sulfuric acid to form a salt.
State the name and write the formula of the salt formed. [2]
Name: aluminium sulfate
Formula: Al₂(SO₄)₃

Recap

Reactions of Acids: Acids react with metal oxides to produce a salt and water. Sulfuric acid forms sulfate salts.
(iii) Aluminium oxide reacts with dilute sodium hydroxide to form a salt and one other product.
Name the other product. [1]
The other product is water.

Recap

Base Reaction: When an amphoteric oxide reacts with an alkali (base), it acts as an acid, producing a salt (e.g., sodium aluminate) and water.
(iv) Aluminium hydroxide, Al(OH)₃, decomposes when heated to form aluminium oxide and water.
Write the chemical equation for this reaction. [2]
2Al(OH)₃ → Al₂O₃ + 3H₂O

Recap

Thermal Decomposition: Thermal decomposition is the breakdown of a compound into two or more substances by heating.
(v) Suggest the names of two other aluminium compounds that decompose when heated to form aluminium oxide. [2]
Two compounds are aluminium carbonate and aluminium nitrate.

Recap

Decomposition of Salts: Metal carbonates often decompose to the metal oxide and carbon dioxide. Metal nitrates decompose to the metal oxide, nitrogen dioxide, and oxygen.
Question 3 | 0620/42/M/20 | The Periodic Table & Bonding
3 The Periodic Table is a method of classifying elements.
(a) Identify the element which is in Group VI and Period 4. [1]
The element is selenium (Se).

Recap

Periodic Table Structure: Groups are vertical columns; Periods are horizontal rows. Period 4 starts at K and ends at Kr. Group VI contains O, S, Se, Te, Po.
(b) Calcium is in Group II and chlorine is in Group VII of the Periodic Table.
Explain, in terms of number of outer shell electrons and electron transfer, how calcium atoms and chlorine atoms form ions. Give the formulae of the ions formed. [5]
Calcium has 2 outer electrons and Chlorine has 7. Calcium atoms lose electrons to form Ca²⁺ ions. Chlorine atoms gain electrons to form Cl⁻ ions.

Recap

Ionic Bonding: Positive ions are formed by electron loss (metals). Negative ions are formed by electron gain (non-metals). The charge corresponds to the number of electrons lost or gained to achieve a full outer shell.
(c) Group V chlorides are covalent molecules. The boiling points of some Group V chlorides are shown.
NCl₃: 71°C
PCl₃:
AsCl₃: 130°C
SbCl₃: 283°C
(i) Suggest the approximate boiling point of PCl₃. [1]
Any number in the range 72-129 °C.

Recap

Trends: Properties in a group often show a trend. Here, boiling points increase down the group. PCl₃ is between NCl₃ and AsCl₃.
(ii) Explain the trend in boiling points in terms of attractive forces between particles. [2]
The attraction between molecules increases as the size of the molecules increases.

Recap

Intermolecular Forces: Simple covalent molecules have weak intermolecular forces. As molecular size increases, these forces get stronger, requiring more energy (higher temperature) to overcome.
(iii) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement in a molecule of PCl₃. Show outer electrons only. [3]
There are 3 pairs of electrons shared between P and Cl atoms (one for each P-Cl bond). There are 2 non-bonding electrons on P and 6 non-bonding electrons on each Cl.

Recap

Covalent Bonding: A covalent bond involves sharing a pair of electrons. Phosphorus (Group V) has 5 outer electrons and shares 3 to form bonds, leaving 1 lone pair. Chlorine (Group VII) has 7 and shares 1.
(d) PCl₃ reacts with chlorine, Cl₂, to form PCl₅. This reaction is exothermic and reaches an equilibrium.
PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g) ⇌ PCl₅(g)
(i) Describe two features of an equilibrium. [2]
The rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction, and the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant.

Recap

Equilibrium: An equilibrium reaction occurs in a closed system where reactants convert to products and products convert back to reactants at the same rate.
(ii) State the effect, if any, on the position of this equilibrium when the following changes are made. Explain your answers.
temperature is increased
pressure is increased [4]
Temperature increase: Shifts to the LHS (Left) because the forward reaction is exothermic.
Pressure increase: Shifts to the RHS (Right) because there are fewer moles of gas on the RHS.

Recap

Le Chatelier's Principle: Increasing temperature favors the endothermic direction. Increasing pressure favors the side with fewer moles of gas.
(iii) Explain, in terms of particles, what happens to the rate of the forward reaction when the reaction mixture is heated. [3]
The rate increases because particles have more energy (kinetic energy). This leads to more collisions per second and more particles having energy greater than the activation energy.

Recap

Collision Theory: Increasing temperature increases the kinetic energy of particles, increasing collision frequency and the proportion of successful collisions.

Moles Calculations: Moles = Mass / Mr. Use the stoichiometry (mole ratio) from the balanced equation to find moles of reactant from product.
(f) Lithium fluoride has ionic bonding.
(i) What is an ionic bond? [2]
An ionic bond is the attraction between oppositely charged ions.

Recap

Ionic Bonding: Strong electrostatic attraction between positive and negative ions in a crystal lattice.
(ii) Give two physical properties of ionic compounds. [2]
High melting/boiling point, and they conduct electricity when molten or aqueous (soluble in water).

Recap

Properties: Ionic compounds have high melting points due to strong lattice forces. They conduct electricity when ions are free to move (molten/dissolved).
Question 4 | 0620/42/M/20 | Transition Elements & Redox
4 Iron is a typical transition element.
Iron:
acts as a catalyst
forms coloured compounds
has more than one oxidation state.
(a) Name one major industrial process that uses iron as a catalyst and name the product made in this process. [2]
Process: Haber process
Product: Ammonia

Recap

Catalysts: Transition metals often act as catalysts. Iron is used in the Haber process to synthesize ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen.
(b) When aqueous sodium hydroxide is added to aqueous iron(II) sulfate, a precipitate forms.
(i) What colour is this precipitate? [1]
The precipitate is green.

Recap

Cation Tests: Iron(II) ions (Fe²⁺) form a green precipitate with sodium hydroxide. Iron(III) ions (Fe³⁺) form a red-brown precipitate.
(ii) Write the ionic equation for this reaction. Include state symbols. [3]
Fe²⁺(aq) + 2OH⁻(aq) → Fe(OH)₂(s)

Recap

Ionic Equations: In precipitation reactions, ions in solution form a solid. Spectator ions are ignored. State symbols are (aq) for aqueous ions and (s) for the precipitate.
(c) Iron(II) sulfate can be converted to iron(III) sulfate by potassium manganate (VII) at room temperature.
(i) What is the role of potassium manganate (VII) in this reaction? [1]
It acts as an oxidising agent.

Recap

Oxidising Agents: An oxidising agent oxidises another substance (takes electrons from it) and is itself reduced.
(ii) What condition must be used for this reaction to occur? [1]
The condition is the presence of an acid (acidified).

Recap

Reaction Conditions: Potassium manganate(VII) typically requires acidic conditions to function effectively as an oxidising agent in titrations or redox reactions.
(iii) In terms of electron transfer, what happens to the iron(II) ions in this reaction? [1]
They lose an electron.

Recap

Oxidation: Oxidation is the loss of electrons. Fe²⁺ loses an electron to become Fe³⁺.
(iv) State the colour change seen during this reaction.
from purple to [1]
from purple to colourless.

Recap

Colour Change: Potassium manganate(VII) is purple. When it is reduced to Mn²⁺ ions, the solution becomes colourless.
(d) Deduce the charge on the iron ion in each of these compounds.
FeF₃
Fe(NO₃)₃ [2]
FeF₃: 3+
Fe(NO₃)₃: 3+

Recap

Oxidation States: Fluoride (F⁻) and Nitrate (NO₃⁻) both have a 1- charge. To balance three of them, Iron must have a 3+ charge.

Recap

Polymers: Polymers are large molecules built up from small units called monomers.

The structure is methylpropene (a hydrocarbon with a C=C bond).

Recap

Monomer Identification: To find the monomer of an addition polymer, identify the repeat unit and replace the single C-C bond in the backbone with a double C=C bond.
(b) Polyamides are condensation polymers.
What does the term condensation mean when used to describe this type of polymer? [1]
It means a small molecule, such as water, is formed (eliminated) as a product.

Recap

Condensation Polymerization: In condensation polymerization, two different monomers join, and a small molecule (like water or HCl) is eliminated.

The diagram should show an amide link (-CONH-) connecting the blocks, with correct orientation.

Recap

Nylon: Nylon is a polyamide. It is made from a dicarboxylic acid and a diamine. The linkage formed is an amide linkage.

A synthetic polyamide is nylon.

Recap

Polyamides: Polyamides contain amide linkages. Nylon is a synthetic example. Proteins are natural polyamides.
