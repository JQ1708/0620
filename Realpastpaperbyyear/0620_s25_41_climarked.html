bauxite, calcium oxide, ethanol, graphite, methane, nitrogen, oxygen, propane, propene, sulfur dioxide
Answer the questions using the list of substances. Each substance may be used once, more than once, or not at all.
State which of the substances:
(a) is a basic oxide [1]
The correct substance is calcium oxide.

Recap

Basic oxides are typically oxides of metals. They react with acids to form a salt and water. For example, calcium oxide (a metal oxide) reacts with hydrochloric acid. Oxides of non-metals (like sulfur dioxide) are typically acidic oxides.
(b) contains carbon atoms only [1]
The correct substance is graphite.

Recap

Graphite and diamond are allotropes of carbon. This means they are different structural forms of the same element. Graphite has a giant covalent structure arranged in layers, which allows it to conduct electricity.
(c) is manufactured by fermentation [1]
The correct substance is ethanol.

Recap

Fermentation is the process where glucose is broken down by yeast, in the absence of oxygen (anaerobic conditions), to produce ethanol and carbon dioxide. The optimal temperature is around 25-35°C.
(d) is produced by the decomposition of vegetation [1]
The correct substance is methane.

Recap

Methane (CH₄) is the main constituent of natural gas. It is a potent greenhouse gas produced from the anaerobic decomposition of organic matter (like vegetation) and also from animal digestion.
(e) contains aluminium oxide [1]
The correct substance is bauxite.

Recap

Bauxite is the main ore from which aluminium is extracted. An ore is a rock containing enough of a metal compound (in this case, aluminium oxide) to make it economically viable to extract the metal.
(f) causes acid rain [1]
The correct substance is sulfur dioxide.

Recap

Sulfur dioxide (SO₂) is an acidic gas, primarily released from the combustion of fossil fuels that contain sulfur impurities. In the atmosphere, it dissolves in water and reacts with oxygen to form sulfuric acid, which falls as acid rain.
(g) is a simple molecule with 11 atoms [1]
The correct substance is propane.

Recap

Propane is an alkane with the formula C₃H₈. It has 3 carbon atoms and 8 hydrogen atoms, for a total of 11 atoms. It is part of a homologous series of saturated hydrocarbons.
(h) is produced when limestone thermally decomposes in the blast furnace [1]
The correct substance is calcium oxide.

Recap

Limestone is calcium carbonate (CaCO₃). In the high temperatures of the blast furnace, it undergoes thermal decomposition to form calcium oxide (CaO) and carbon dioxide. The basic calcium oxide is used to remove acidic impurities like silicon dioxide from the iron ore.
(i) is a gas that is approximately 21% of clean, dry air [1]
The correct substance is oxygen.

Recap

Clean, dry air is a mixture of gases. Its approximate composition is 78% nitrogen (N₂), 21% oxygen (O₂), about 1% argon, and 0.04% carbon dioxide.
(j) is a monomer in addition polymerisation. [1]
The correct substance is propene.

Recap

Monomers are small molecules that join together to form a large molecule called a polymer. Addition polymerisation involves monomers that have a C=C double bond, such as alkenes. Propene is an alkene (C₃H₆) and can polymerise to form poly(propene).

(b) Atoms of the same element are known as isotopes. ${}_{19}^{39}K$ and ${}_{19}^{41}K$ are isotopes of potassium.

Recap

  • Proton Number (bottom number) is the number of protons, which defines the element.
  • Nucleon Number (top number) is the total number of protons and neutrons.
  • Number of neutrons = Nucleon Number - Proton Number. (e.g., 39 - 19 = 20)
  • In a neutral atom, number of electrons = number of protons.
  • A positive ion (like K⁺) has lost electrons. A K⁺ ion has lost 1 electron (19 - 1 = 18).

relative mass of isotope percentage abundance of isotope

Recap

Relative atomic mass (Ar) is the weighted average mass of the isotopes of an element, relative to 1/12th the mass of a carbon-12 atom. It is calculated by summing the (mass × abundance) for each isotope and dividing by 100.

Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same number of protons) but with different numbers of neutrons (and therefore different nucleon/mass numbers). If the relative atomic mass is a whole number that matches a single nucleon number, it implies that isotope is the only one that exists naturally.

This question is about copper and compounds of copper.
(a) (i) Describe the bonding in a metallic element such as copper. [3]
Metallic bonding consists of a regular lattice of positive ions (or cations) which are surrounded by a 'sea' of delocalised electrons (or mobile electrons). The bonding is the strong electrostatic attraction between these positive ions and the delocalised electrons.

Recap

Metallic bonding is the force of attraction found in metals. The outer shell electrons are not held by any single atom and are free to move throughout the structure, becoming delocalised. This leaves a fixed lattice of positive metal ions.
(a) (ii) Explain how solid copper conducts electricity. [1]
Copper conducts electricity because the delocalised electrons are mobile and are free to move through the structure, carrying charge.

Recap

The ability to conduct electricity requires mobile charge carriers. In metals, these are the delocalised electrons. In molten or aqueous ionic compounds, these are mobile ions.
(b) Copper is in alloys such as brass.
(i) State one reason why alloys are more useful than pure metals. [1]
Alloys are generally harder or stronger than pure metals. (They can also be more resistant to corrosion).

Recap

An alloy is a mixture of a metal with other elements. In a pure metal, the atoms are arranged in regular layers that can slide over each other easily. In an alloy, the different-sized atoms disrupt these regular layers, making it more difficult for them to slide, which makes the alloy stronger and harder.
(ii) Name the substance that is present in brass, other than copper. [1]
The other substance in brass is zinc.

Recap

Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc. Stainless steel is an alloy of iron, chromium, and sometimes nickel.

Acids react with carbonates to produce a salt, water, and carbon dioxide. The carbon dioxide gas being released is observed as fizzing. Copper(II) compounds typically form blue aqueous solutions.

The reaction stops because all the sulfuric acid had reacted (it is the limiting reactant).

Recap

When preparing a soluble salt from an acid and an insoluble base or carbonate, the insoluble reactant is added in excess. This ensures all the acid is neutralised. The reaction stops when the acid is completely used up.

The residue is the unreacted copper(II) carbonate.

Recap

Filtration is used to separate an insoluble solid from a liquid. The solid left on the filter paper is called the residue. The liquid that passes through is called the filtrate.
(iv) Name a substance, other than copper(II) carbonate, that can be added to dilute sulfuric acid to produce aqueous copper(II) sulfate. [1]
A suitable substance would be copper(II) oxide or copper(II) hydroxide.

Bases react with acids to produce a salt and water. Metal oxides and metal hydroxides are bases. (Note: Copper metal does not react with dilute sulfuric acid as it is below hydrogen in the reactivity series).

This type of solution is described as saturated.

Recap

A saturated solution is one that contains the maximum amount of dissolved solute in a given amount of solvent at a specific temperature.

(c) Ammonia is converted into nitric acid in a two-step process. In step 1, ammonia and oxygen are passed over a catalyst.

Paper: 0620/41/M/J/25 Q5 | Topics: Chemical Equilibrium, Homologous Series, Esters, Empirical Formula
Methanol, CH₃OH, is manufactured by the reaction between hydrogen and carbon monoxide. An equilibrium mixture is produced.
2H₂(g) + CO(g) ⇌ CH₃OH(g)
(a) State what happens to the concentration of CH₃OH when the reaction is at equilibrium. Explain your answer in terms of rate of forward and reverse reactions. [2]
When the reaction is at equilibrium, the concentration of CH₃OH is no longer changing (it remains constant).
This is because the rate of the forward reaction (making CH₃OH) is equal to the rate of the reverse reaction (breaking down CH₃OH).

Recap

Dynamic equilibrium occurs in a reversible reaction within a closed system. At equilibrium, both forward and reverse reactions are still occurring, but their rates are equal, resulting in constant concentrations of all reactants and products.
(b) The reaction is carried out at a pressure of 75 atmospheres and a temperature of 250 °C.
(i) State two disadvantages of using a pressure below 75 atmospheres. [2]
1. The rate of reaction decreases.
2. The yield of methanol decreases (or the position of equilibrium shifts to the left).

Recap

There are 3 moles of gas (2 + 1) on the left and 1 mole of gas on the right. According to Le Chatelier's principle, increasing pressure favours the side with fewer moles of gas. Therefore, a lower pressure would shift the equilibrium to the left, decreasing the yield. A lower pressure also means particles are further apart, decreasing collision frequency and thus the rate.
(ii) Complete Table 5.1 using only the words increases, decreases or no change. [2]
effect on the concentration of CH₃OH(g) at equilibrium effect on the rate of the reverse reaction
catalyst is added ... ...
effect on the concentration of CH₃OH(g) at equilibrium effect on the rate of the reverse reaction
catalyst is added no change increases

Recap

A catalyst increases the rate of both the forward and reverse reactions equally by providing an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy. It does not affect the position of equilibrium or the final yield, but it allows equilibrium to be reached faster.
(iii) If a temperature of more than 250 °C is used, the yield of methanol decreases. State what can be deduced about the forward reaction. [1]
It can be deduced that the forward reaction is exothermic.

Recap

According to Le Chatelier's principle, if you increase the temperature, the system will try to cool down by favouring the endothermic (heat-absorbing) direction. Since increasing the temperature decreases the yield (favours the reverse reaction), the reverse reaction must be endothermic, and therefore the forward reaction is exothermic (heat-releasing).

Transition elements (like copper, iron, vanadium, nickel) and their compounds are often used as catalysts due to their variable oxidation states and ability to adsorb reactants onto their surface.

(e) An organic compound has the following composition by mass: C, 64.87%; H, 13.51%; O, 21.62%. Calculate the empirical formula of the compound. [2]
Step 1: Divide percentage by Ar for each element.

Paper: 0620/41/M/J/25 Q6 | Topics: Group VII (Halogens), Enthalpy Calculations, Group I (Alkali Metals)
This question is about the Periodic Table.
(a) State the name given to Group VII elements. [1]
Halogen(s)

Recap

The elements in Group VII of the Periodic Table (Fluorine, Chlorine, Bromine, Iodine) are known as the halogens. They are reactive non-metals that exist as diatomic molecules.
(b) State which Group VII element is most reactive. [1]
Fluorine (or F₂)

Recap

The reactivity of the halogens decreases down the group. As you go down the group, the atoms get larger and the outer shell is further from the nucleus, making it harder to attract an electron. Fluorine, at the top, is the most reactive.
(c) Give the physical state and colour of iodine at room temperature and pressure. [2]
State: solid
Colour: grey-black (or grey, or black)

Recap

There is a trend in the physical state of halogens at r.t.p.
  • Fluorine & Chlorine: Gas
  • Bromine: Liquid
  • Iodine: Solid
The colours also get darker down the group.
(d) When bromine is added to aqueous potassium iodide a displacement reaction occurs.
Br₂ + 2KI → 2KBr + I₂
(i) Write an ionic equation for the reaction. [2]
Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂

Recap

In this reaction, the potassium ion (K⁺) is a spectator ion. It exists as K⁺(aq) on both sides of the equation, so it is omitted from the ionic equation. This is a displacement reaction because bromine is more reactive than iodine and displaces it from its compound.
(ii) ...Calculate the enthalpy change, ΔH, for the reaction... [3]
I₂(g) + Br₂(g) → 2IBr(g)
bondbond energy in kJ/mol
I-I150
Br-Br193
I-Br175
Step 1: Energy required to break bonds (endothermic, +)
1 mol of I-I = 1 × 150 = 150 kJ
1 mol of Br-Br = 1 × 193 = 193 kJ
Total energy in = 150 + 193 = +343 kJ

Step 2: Energy released when forming bonds (exothermic, -)
2 mol of I-Br = 2 × 175 = 350 kJ
Total energy out = -350 kJ

Step 3: Calculate the enthalpy change (ΔH)
ΔH = Energy in - Energy out = 343 - 350 = -7 kJ/mol

Recap

The enthalpy change of reaction (ΔH) can be calculated using bond energies.
ΔH = Σ (Bonds Broken) - Σ (Bonds Formed)
Bond breaking is always endothermic (requires energy, positive value).
Bond making is always exothermic (releases energy, negative value).
Since the final answer is negative, this reaction is exothermic.
