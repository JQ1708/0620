0620/43/O/N/23 Q1 | Topic: Chemical Analysis & Organic Chemistry
1 A list of substances is shown.
barium nitrate, carbon monoxide, hydrated cobalt(II) chloride, copper(II) oxide, anhydrous copper(II) sulfate, ethane, potassium iodide, propene, sodium bromide, sulfur dioxide, zinc oxide
Answer the following questions using only the substances from the list.
Each substance may be used once, more than once or not at all.
Give the name of the substance that:
(a) gives a lilac colour in a flame test [1]
Potassium iodide.

Recap

A flame test can be used to identify some metal cations. Put a small sample on a platinum wire and observe the colour of the flame. Potassium ions give a lilac flame.
(b) forms a cream precipitate when its aqueous solution reacts with acidified aqueous silver nitrate [1]
Sodium bromide.

Recap

Aqueous silver nitrate is used to test for halides. Chlorides give a white precipitate, bromides give a cream precipitate and iodides give a yellow precipitate.
(c) is an acidic oxide [1]
Sulfur dioxide.

Recap

Acidic oxides react with bases. They are oxides of non-metals (e.g., sulfur dioxide).
(d) is an unsaturated hydrocarbon [1]
Propene.

Recap

Unsaturated compounds contain C=C double bonds. Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons (e.g., propene). They turn aqueous bromine from orange to colourless.
(e) is a product of incomplete combustion of fossil fuels [1]
Carbon monoxide.

Recap

The incomplete combustion of carbon-containing compounds produces carbon monoxide, which is toxic.
(f) is used to test for the presence of water. [1]
Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate.

Recap

Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate turns from white to blue when water is added. This is a chemical test for water.

(c) Determine the number of nucleons in D. [1]
33. (Note: Based on charge 2-, D must have 16 protons. 16 protons + 17 neutrons = 33).

Mass number (nucleon number) is the number of protons + neutrons in the nucleus.

2,8,8.

The first shell holds 2 electrons, the second and third hold 8. D has 18 electrons, so it fills as 2,8,8.

5 (or V).

The number of outer shell electrons is equal to the group number in Groups I to VII. F has 15 electrons (2,8,5), so it is in Group 5.
(f) State the period number of B. [1]
2.

Recap

The number of occupied electron shells is equal to the period number. B has 10 electrons (2,8), filling 2 shells.
0620/43/O/N/23 Q3 | Topic: Nitrogen & Haber Process
3 This question is about nitrogen and some of its compounds.
(a) Nitrogen is converted into ammonia, NH₃, in the Haber process.
(i) Nitrogen is obtained from air. State the percentage of nitrogen in clean, dry air. [1]
78%.

Recap

Clean, dry air contains 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, about 1% argon and 0.04% carbon dioxide.
(ii) State the source of hydrogen for the Haber process. [1]
Natural gas (or methane).

Recap

The Haber process uses hydrogen obtained by reacting methane (from natural gas) with steam.
(iii) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram in Fig. 3.1 for a molecule of ammonia. Show the outer shell electrons only. [2]
M1: Three single bonding pairs (1 dot and 1 cross in each overlap).
M2: One lone pair on Nitrogen (2 dots or 2 crosses).

Recap

A covalent bond is formed when atoms share a pair of electrons. Nitrogen has 5 outer electrons and shares 3 with Hydrogen atoms, leaving one lone pair.
(iv) Write a chemical equation for the reaction occurring in the Haber process and give the typical reaction conditions. Include units where appropriate. [5]
Chemical equation: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ (Must have reversible arrow).
Temperature: 450 °C.
Pressure: 200 atm (or 20,000 kPa).
Catalyst: Iron.

Recap

The conditions for the Haber process are 200 atm pressure, 450°C and an iron catalyst. A compromise temperature is used to give a good yield with a fast enough rate.
(b) Ammonia is converted into nitric acid. The first stage of this conversion uses a catalyst and occurs at a temperature of 900°C and a pressure of 5 atmospheres.
4NH₃(g) + 5O₂(g) ⇌ 4NO(g) + 6H₂O(g)
The forward reaction is exothermic.
(i) Suggest which of the following elements is most likely to be used as a catalyst. Draw a circle around your answer.
calcium lead platinum sodium sulfur [1]
Platinum.

Recap

Transition elements (like platinum or iron) and their compounds often act as catalysts.
(ii) State the oxidation number of nitrogen in: NH₃, NO [2]
NH₃: -3.
NO: +2.

Recap

Oxidation number rules: H is +1, O is -2. In NH3, x + 3(+1) = 0, so x = -3. In NO, x + (-2) = 0, so x = +2.
(iii) Use your answer to (ii) to explain whether the nitrogen in ammonia undergoes oxidation or reduction. [1]
Oxidation AND oxidation number increases (from -3 to +2).

Recap

Oxidation is an increase in oxidation number.
(iv) Complete Table 3.1 using the words increases, decreases or no change. [4]
Decreasing pressure: Yield increases, Rate decreases.
Decreasing temperature: Yield increases.
Removing catalyst: Yield no change.

Recap

Equilibrium: Decreasing pressure favors the side with more moles of gas (Right: 10 moles, Left: 9 moles). Decreasing temperature favors the exothermic direction (Right). Catalysts do not affect the position of equilibrium.
(v) Decreasing the temperature causes the rate of the forward reaction to decrease. Explain, using collision theory, why the rate of the reaction is slower at the decreased temperature. [3]
Kinetic energy of particles is lower.
Frequency of collisions is lower.
Fewer particles have energy greater than the activation energy (fewer effective collisions).

Recap

Decreasing temperature means particles move slower and have less energy. This reduces both the frequency of collisions and the proportion of successful collisions (those with energy > activation energy).
(c) In the second stage, nitric acid is produced. Balance the symbol equation for this reaction.
.....NO + .....O₂ + .....H₂O → .....HNO₃ [1]
4NO + 3O₂ + 2H₂O → 4HNO₃

Recap

Balancing equations involves ensuring the number of atoms of each element is the same on both sides.
0620/43/O/N/23 Q4 | Topic: Salts & Stoichiometry
4 This question is about sulfuric acid and salts that are made from sulfuric acid.
(a) Zinc reacts with dilute sulfuric acid. Aqueous zinc sulfate is one of the products.
(i) Name the limiting reactant. [1]
Dilute sulfuric acid.

Recap

The limiting reactant is the one not in excess. Zinc is added "until in excess", so the acid is limiting.
(ii) State two observations that indicate the zinc is in excess. [2]
1. Solid remains (undissolved).
2. Bubbling stops (effervescence stops).

Recap

When a metal reacts with an acid, bubbles of hydrogen are produced. When the acid is used up, bubbling stops and excess solid metal remains.
(iii) Name the filtrate. [1]
Aqueous zinc sulfate.

Recap

Filtration separates the residue (excess solid) from the filtrate (solution passing through).
(iv) Name two compounds which both react with dilute sulfuric acid to produce aqueous zinc sulfate. [2]
Any two from: Zinc oxide, Zinc carbonate, Zinc hydroxide.

Recap

Soluble salts can be made by adding excess metal, metal oxide, or metal carbonate to an acid.
(b)(i) State what is meant by the term saturated solution. [2]
A solution containing the maximum amount of solute dissolved at a given temperature.

Recap

A saturated solution contains the maximum concentration of solute dissolved in a solvent at a particular temperature.
(b)(ii) Explain why crystals form when the saturated solution cools down. [1]
Solubility decreases as temperature decreases.

Recap

Crystallisation involves cooling a saturated solution. The solubility of solids generally drops as temperature falls, forcing the solid out of solution.
(c)(i) State the term used to describe crystals that contain water of crystallisation. [1]
Hydrated.

Recap

Hydrated crystals contain water of crystallisation. Solids without it are anhydrous.
(c)(ii) State why step 4 is repeated until there is no change in mass. [1]
To ensure all the water of crystallisation is removed.

Recap

Heating to constant mass guarantees that the decomposition (loss of water) is complete.
(c)(iii) Determine the value of x in NiSO₄.xH₂O. [4]
Moles of NiSO₄ = 0.310 / 155 = 0.002.
Mass of H₂O = 0.454 - 0.310 = 0.144 g.
Moles of H₂O = 0.144 / 18 = 0.008.
Value of x = 0.008 / 0.002 = 4.

Recap

To find formula mass/water of crystallization: Calculate moles = mass / Mr. Find the ratio of moles of water to moles of salt.
0620/43/O/N/23 Q5 | Topic: Metals
5 This question is about iron.
(a)(i) Describe the bonding in a metallic element such as iron. [3]
Lattice of positive ions (cations).
Sea of delocalised electrons.
Strong attraction between positive ions and electrons.

Recap

Metallic bonding is the strong electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and the sea of delocalised electrons.
(a)(ii) Explain why iron conducts electricity when it is solid. [1]
Electrons move / are mobile.

Recap

Metals conduct electricity because the delocalised electrons are able to move through the metallic lattice.
(b) Iron is extracted from hematite in the blast furnace.
(b)(i) Give two reasons why coke is added to the blast furnace. [2]
1. Releases heat (when burned).
2. Reacts with carbon dioxide to form carbon monoxide (the reducing agent).
(Also accepts: reduces iron(III) oxide).

Recap

Coke (carbon) burns in air to produce heat and CO2. It then reacts with CO2 to form carbon monoxide, which reduces the iron ore.
(b)(ii) Explain how limestone removes the impurities in the hematite. [2]
Limestone decomposes to calcium oxide.
Calcium oxide reacts with silicon(IV) oxide (sand) to produce slag (calcium silicate).

Recap

Thermal decomposition of limestone produces CaO. CaO is a base that reacts with acidic SiO2 impurities to form slag.
(b)(iii) Write a symbol equation for the conversion of iron(III) oxide to iron in the blast furnace. [2]
Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂

Recap

Carbon monoxide reduces iron(III) oxide in the blast furnace to iron.
(b)(iv) Suggest why the iron produced in the blast furnace is molten. [1]
Temperature in the furnace is above the melting point of iron.
(c) Explain why the structure of alloys causes them to be harder and stronger than pure metals. [2]
Atoms have different sizes.
Layers cannot slide over each other.

Recap

Alloys contain atoms of different sizes, which disrupt the regular arrangement of layers, preventing them from sliding over each other easily.
(d)(i) Name the substances that react with iron to form rust. [1]
Oxygen AND water.

Recap

Rust is hydrated iron(III) oxide. Oxygen and water are needed for rusting to take place.
(d)(ii) Name the process in which zinc is used to coat iron to prevent rusting. [1]
Galvanising.

Recap

Galvanising is coating iron with a protective layer of zinc.
(d)(iii) Explain how the coating of zinc prevents rusting if the zinc is not scratched. [1]
Acts as a barrier (prevents oxygen/water reaching iron).
(d)(iv) When zinc is scratched the iron becomes exposed. Explain how the zinc continues to prevent rusting. [2]
Zinc is more reactive than iron.
Zinc is oxidised / loses electrons (sacrificial protection).

Recap

Sacrificial protection: A more reactive metal (zinc) corrodes instead of iron because it loses electrons more easily.
0620/43/O/N/23 Q6 | Topic: Organic Chemistry
6 (a) Esters are members of a homologous series of organic compounds. Give two characteristics that are the same for all members of a homologous series. [2]
Any two from: Same general formula, same functional group, similar chemical properties.

Recap

A homologous series is a family of similar compounds with similar chemical properties due to the same functional group.
(b) Ester X has the structure shown in Fig. 6.1. Name ester X. [1]
Ethyl propanoate.

Recap

Esters are named with the alkyl group from the alcohol first (Ethyl-) and the acid part second (-propanoate).
(c)(i) Ester Y has the structural formula HCOOCH₂CH₂CH₃. Name the alcohol and the carboxylic acid used to make ester Y. [2]
Alcohol: Propan-1-ol.
Carboxylic acid: Methanoic acid.

Recap

In ester formation, the alcohol provides the alkyl chain attached to the single O (Propyl), and the acid provides the C=O part (Methanoic).
(c)(ii) State the molecular formula of ester Y. [1]
C₄H₈O₂.
(d) Ester Z has the molecular formula C₄H₈O₂. State the empirical formula of ester Z. [1]
C₂H₄O.

Recap

Empirical formula is the simplest whole number ratio of atoms. Divide C4H8O2 by 2.
(e)(i) State the term used to describe the type of polymerisation used to produce polyesters and polyamides. [1]
Condensation.

Recap

Condensation polymerisation involves two molecules joining to form a larger molecule with the elimination of a small molecule (like water).
(e)(ii) Complete Fig. 6.3 to show the structures of the monomers used to produce nylon. [2]
M1: Dicarboxylic acid (2 -COOH groups displayed).
M2: Diamine (2 -NH2 groups displayed).

Recap

Nylon is a polyamide made from a dicarboxylic acid (HOOC-R-COOH) and a diamine (H2N-R-NH2).
(f)(i) State the name given to naturally occurring polyamides. [1]
Proteins.

Recap

Proteins are natural polyamides.
(f)(ii) Name the type of monomer which forms naturally occurring polyamides. [1]
Amino acids.

Recap

Amino acids contain both amine (-NH2) and carboxylic acid (-COOH) groups, allowing them to form polyamide chains.
