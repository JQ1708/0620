Question 1

Topics: States of Matter, Respiration, Ores, Chemical Tests, Organic Chemistry, Redox

1 A list of substances is shown. "aluminium oxide carbon dioxide carbon monoxide" "chlorine copper" "iron(III) oxide limestone glucose" "nitrogen oxygen" Answer the questions using the substances in the list. Each substance may be used once, more than once or not at all. State which substance is: (a) a product of respiration [1]
The substance that is a product of respiration is carbon dioxide.

Recap

Respiration is the chemical reaction that releases energy in living things. The overall word equation is glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water. This process is the opposite of photosynthesis, which consumes carbon dioxide and water to produce glucose and oxygen.
(b) the main constituent of hematite [1]
The main constituent of hematite is iron(III) oxide.

Recap

Hematite is the main ore of iron. An ore is a rock containing a compound from which a metal can be extracted. Iron is extracted from iron(III) oxide in the blast furnace.
(c) an element which has a sulfate that is used to test for water [1]
The element is copper.

Recap

The chemical test for water uses anhydrous copper(II) sulfate. This solid is white, but when water is added, it turns blue as it becomes hydrated copper(II) sulfate (CuSO₄·5H₂O).
(d) a colourless toxic gas [1]
The colourless toxic gas is carbon monoxide.

Recap

Carbon monoxide (CO) is a toxic gas. It is formed during the incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels, which occurs when there is a limiting amount of oxygen.
(e) a reactant in fermentation [1]
The reactant in fermentation is glucose.

Recap

Fermentation is the process used to manufacture ethanol. Glucose is broken down by yeast in the absence of air (anaerobic conditions) at a temperature of 25–35 °C. The products are ethanol and carbon dioxide.
(f) a reducing agent in the extraction of iron [1]
The reducing agent in the extraction of iron is carbon monoxide.

Recap

In the blast furnace, carbon monoxide reduces the iron(III) oxide (from hematite) to molten iron. Reduction is the loss of oxygen from a compound. The carbon monoxide itself is oxidised to carbon dioxide.
(g) a conductor of electricity when solid [1]
The substance that conducts electricity when solid is copper.

Recap

Metals are good electrical conductors. This is explained by metallic bonding, which is the electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and a 'sea' of delocalised electrons. These mobile electrons are able to move and carry charge.
(h) a gas that is approximately 21% of clean, dry air. [1]
The gas is oxygen.

Recap

Clean, dry air is a mixture of gases. Its approximate composition is 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, about 1% argon, and 0.04% carbon dioxide.

Question 2

Topics: Atomic Structure, Isotopes, Ions

2 (a) ³²₁₆S and ³³₁₆S are isotopes of sulfur. Use your knowledge of protons, neutrons and electrons to answer the following questions. (i) Describe how these isotopes of sulfur are the same and how they are different. same ... different ... [3]
Same: These isotopes are the same because they have the same number of protons (16 protons) and the same number of electrons (16 electrons).
Different: They are different because they have different numbers of neutrons. ³²S has 16 neutrons (32 - 16), while ³³S has 17 neutrons (33 - 16).

Recap

Isotopes are atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons. The number at the top (e.g., 32) is the mass number (protons + neutrons), and the number at the bottom (e.g., 16) is the proton number (atomic number).
(ii) Explain why each of these isotopes have an overall charge of zero. [1]
They have an overall charge of zero because the number of protons (which are positive) is the same as the number of electrons (which are negative), so the charges cancel out.

Recap

An atom is the smallest uncharged particle. Protons have a relative charge of +1, and electrons have a relative charge of -1. Atoms are uncharged because the number of protons equals the number of electrons.
(iii) Explain why both isotopes have the same chemical properties. [1]
Both isotopes have the same chemical properties because they have the same number of (outer shell) electrons.

Recap

The chemical properties of an element depend on its electronic configuration, specifically the number of outer shell electrons. Since isotopes have the same number of electrons, they have the same electronic configuration and therefore react in the same way.
(b) Sulfide ions, S²⁻, have the electronic structure 2,8,8. (i) Explain why sulfide ions have a charge of 2–. [1]
A neutral sulfur atom (proton number 16) has 16 protons and 16 electrons (structure 2,8,6). To form an S²⁻ ion, it gains two electrons. The ion has 16 protons (positive charges) but 18 electrons (negative charges), meaning it has two more electrons than protons, giving an overall 2- charge.

Recap

Negative ions (anions) are formed when atoms gain one or more electrons. Positive ions (cations) are formed when atoms lose one or more electrons.
(ii) Give the formula of: • an anion which has the same electronic structure as S²⁻ ... • a cation which has the same electronic structure as S²⁻. ... [2]
• Anion: Cl⁻ (or P³⁻)
• Cation: K⁺ (or Ca²⁺)

Recap

The electronic structure 2,8,8 is the stable configuration of an Argon (Ar) atom.
  • Chlorine (Cl, 2,8,7) gains one electron to form Cl⁻ (2,8,8).
  • Potassium (K, 2,8,8,1) loses one electron to form K⁺ (2,8,8).
Ions form to achieve a stable electronic configuration, usually a full outer shell of electrons, like the noble gases.

Question 3

Topics: Covalent Bonding, Intermolecular Forces, Haber Process, Acids & Bases

3 This question is about nitrogen and compounds of nitrogen. (a) Nitrogen molecules have the formula N₂. Some properties of nitrogen are shown: • melting point of –210 °C • boiling point of –196 °C • non-conductor of electricity when solid • insoluble in water. (i) Name the type of bonding between the atoms in an N₂ molecule. [1]
The bonding between the atoms is covalent.

Recap

A covalent bond is formed when atoms share a pair of electrons. In a nitrogen molecule (N₂), the two nitrogen atoms share three pairs of electrons, forming a triple covalent bond (N≡N).
(ii) Explain, in terms of attractive forces between particles, why nitrogen has a low melting point. [1]
Nitrogen has a simple molecular structure. It has a low melting point because only a small amount of energy is needed to overcome the weak force(s) of attraction between the N₂ molecules (intermolecular forces).

Recap

Simple molecular structures have low melting and boiling points. This is because the forces between the molecules (intermolecular forces) are weak. The strong covalent bonds *within* the molecules are not broken.
(iii) Explain why nitrogen does not conduct electricity. [1]
Nitrogen does not conduct electricity because it is a simple molecule and has no ions or no mobile electrons (delocalised electrons) to move and carry charge.

Recap

Substances conduct electricity if they contain mobile charged particles. Simple molecular structures do not conduct electricity in any state because they do not have mobile ions or delocalised electrons.
(b) Nitrogen reacts with hydrogen to form ammonia, NH₃, in the Haber process. State the essential conditions in the Haber process. Write an equation for the chemical reaction. [4]
The essential conditions are:
  • Temperature: 450 °C
  • Pressure: 200 atmospheres (or 20 000 kPa)
  • Catalyst: Iron
Equation: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃

Recap

The Haber process is a reversible reaction, shown by the ⇌ symbol. The conditions used are a compromise. The 450 °C is a compromise temperature that gives a good yield at a fast enough rate. The iron catalyst increases the rate of reaction but does not affect the position of equilibrium.
(c) Ammonia is made in the laboratory by heating ammonium chloride with calcium hydroxide. Balance the chemical equation for the reaction. ...NH₄Cl + Ca(OH)₂ → ...NH₃ + CaCl₂ + ...H₂O [1]
2NH₄Cl + Ca(OH)₂ → 2NH₃ + CaCl₂ + 2H₂O

Recap

In a balanced symbol equation, there must be the same number of each type of atom on each side of the equation. Here, balancing the 2 Cl atoms on the right with the NH₄Cl on the left (making it 2NH₄Cl) is the first step. This then requires 2NH₃ and 2H₂O to balance the N and H atoms.
(d) Hydrazine, N₂H₄, is another compound that contains nitrogen and hydrogen. Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement in a molecule of hydrazine. Show outer electrons only. [2]
The correct diagram shows:
  • A single covalent bond (one shared pair of electrons) between the two nitrogen atoms.
  • Two single covalent bonds from each nitrogen atom to two hydrogen atoms (four N-H bonds total).
  • Two non-bonding electrons (one lone pair) on each of the two nitrogen atoms.

Recap

Dot-and-cross diagrams show the electronic configuration for compounds, usually for outer electrons only. Nitrogen is in Group V, so it has 5 outer electrons. Hydrogen is in Group I, so it has 1 outer electron. Each atom shares electrons to achieve a stable configuration (a full outer shell).
(e) Ammonia and hydrazine are weak bases. The chemical equation for the reaction between one molecule of ammonia and one molecule of water is shown. NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ (i) State the meaning of the term base. [1]
A base is a proton acceptor (a proton is an H⁺ ion).

Recap

Acids and bases can be defined in terms of proton (H⁺) transfer. An acid is a proton donor, and a base is a proton acceptor.
(ii) Write a chemical equation for the reaction between one molecule of hydrazine, N₂H₄, and one molecule of water. [1]
N₂H₄ + H₂O ⇌ N₂H₅⁺ + OH⁻

Recap

Like ammonia, hydrazine is a weak base, so it is only partially dissociated (ionised) in water. This is shown by the reversible reaction symbol (⇌). The hydrazine molecule accepts a proton (H⁺) from a water molecule.

Question 4

Topics: Rates of Reaction, Catalysts, Organic Chemistry, Homologous Series, Esters, Empirical Formula

4 Ethanol is made industrially by the reaction of ethene with steam. The reaction occurs at a temperature of 300 °C and a pressure of 60 atmospheres. C₂H₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ C₂H₅OH(g) A catalyst is used in this reaction. The forward reaction is exothermic. (a) State what is meant by the term catalyst. [2]
A catalyst is a substance which increases the rate of a reaction and remains unchanged (or is not used up) at the end of the reaction.

Recap

Catalysts work by lowering the activation energy (Ea) of a reaction. This means that a greater proportion of collisions between particles will be successful (i.e., have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy).
(b) Complete the table using only the words increases, decreases or no change. [4]
effect on the rate of the forward reaction effect on the equilibrium yield of C₂H₅OH(g)
increasing the temperature ... ...
decreasing the pressure ... ...
effect on the rate of the forward reaction effect on the equilibrium yield of C₂H₅OH(g)
increasing the temperature increases decreases
decreasing the pressure decreases decreases

Recap

Rate: Increasing the temperature increases the rate of reaction because particles have more kinetic energy, leading to more frequent and energetic collisions. Decreasing the pressure decreases the rate because the gas particles are further apart and collide less frequently.
Equilibrium (Le Chatelier's Principle):
  • Temperature: The forward reaction is exothermic (releases heat). Increasing the temperature will shift the equilibrium to the left (in the endothermic direction) to absorb the added heat, thus decreasing the yield.
  • Pressure: There are 2 moles of gas on the left (1 C₂H₄ + 1 H₂O) and 1 mole of gas on the right (1 C₂H₅OH). Decreasing the pressure will shift the equilibrium to the side with more moles of gas (the left), thus decreasing the yield.
(c) Ethanol is a member of the alcohol homologous series. Members of the same homologous series have the same general formula. (i) State the general formula of alcohols. [1]
CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH

Recap

A general formula can be written for each homologous series, where 'n' is the number of carbon atoms. For example, alkanes are CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ and alkenes are CₙH₂ₙ.
(ii) State two general characteristics, other than the same general formula, of all homologous series. 1 ... 2 ... [2]
1. They have the same functional group.
2. They have similar chemical properties.
(Other valid answers: consecutive members differ by a -CH₂- group; their physical properties vary in a predictable manner/show trends).

Recap

A homologous series is a family of similar compounds with similar chemical properties due to the presence of the same functional group (e.g., the -OH group in alcohols).
(iii) One alcohol containing three carbon atoms is propan-1-ol. Draw the structure of one other alcohol containing three carbon atoms. Show all of the atoms and all of the bonds. Name the alcohol you have drawn. name ... [2]
(See placeholder for the structure diagram)
Name: propan-2-ol

Recap

Structural isomers are compounds with the same molecular formula (in this case, C₃H₈O) but different structural formulae. Propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol are 'position isomers' because the functional group (-OH) is attached to a different carbon atom in the chain.
(d) When alcohols react with carboxylic acids, esters are produced. (i) The structure of ester X is shown. Name ester X. [1]
ethyl butanoate

Recap

Esters are named based on the alcohol and carboxylic acid that formed them. The name begins with the alkyl group from the alcohol (the part attached to the single-bonded oxygen), which is 'ethyl' (from ethanol). It ends with the part from the carboxylic acid (the part with the C=O), changing the ending to '-oate', which is 'butanoate' (from butanoic acid).
(ii) Give the name of the alcohol and the carboxylic acid that react together to produce ester X. alcohol ... carboxylic acid ... [2]
Alcohol: ethanol
Carboxylic acid: butanoic acid

Recap

Esters are formed in a condensation reaction between a carboxylic acid and an alcohol, typically in the presence of an acid catalyst. A molecule of water is eliminated.
(e) Ester Y has the following composition by mass: C, 58.82%; H, 9.80%; O, 31.37%. Calculate the empirical formula of ester Y. [3]
Step 1: Divide percentage by relative atomic mass (Ar).
  • C: 58.82 / 12 = 4.901...
  • H: 9.80 / 1 = 9.80
  • O: 31.37 / 16 = 1.960...
Step 2: Divide all by the smallest number (1.960).
  • C: 4.901 / 1.960 = 2.5
  • H: 9.80 / 1.960 = 5.0
  • O: 1.960 / 1.960 = 1.0
Step 3: Multiply to get whole numbers (x 2).
  • C: 2.5 x 2 = 5
  • H: 5.0 x 2 = 10
  • O: 1.0 x 2 = 2
Empirical formula = C₅H₁₀O₂

Recap

The empirical formula is the simplest whole number ratio of the different atoms in a compound. It is found by dividing the mass (or percentage by mass) of each element by its relative atomic mass (Ar) and then finding the simplest ratio.
(f) Ester Z has the empirical formula C₃H₆O and a relative molecular mass of 116. Calculate the molecular formula of ester Z. [1]
Step 1: Calculate the empirical formula mass (Mr of C₃H₆O). (3 x 12) + (6 x 1) + (1 x 16) = 36 + 6 + 16 = 58 Step 2: Divide the relative molecular mass by the empirical formula mass. 116 / 58 = 2 Step 3: Multiply the empirical formula by this number. (C₃H₆O) x 2 = C₆H₁₂O₂
Molecular formula = C₆H₁₂O₂

Recap

The molecular formula shows the actual number and type of each atom in a molecule. To find it, divide the relative molecular mass (Mr) by the empirical formula mass. This gives a whole number, which you use to multiply the subscripts in the empirical formula.

Question 5

Topics: Metallic Bonding, Electrochemistry, Stoichiometry, Rates of Reaction, Acids & Bases

5 This question is about copper and its compounds. (a) Describe the bonding in a metallic element such as copper. You may include a diagram as part of your answer. [3]
Metallic bonding is the strong electrostatic attraction between a lattice of positive metal ions and a 'sea' of delocalised (or mobile) electrons.

Recap

This metallic bonding model explains the properties of metals:
  • Conduct electricity: The delocalised electrons are mobile and can move to carry charge.
  • Malleable and ductile: The layers of positive ions can slide over each other when a force is applied, without breaking the bond.
(b) A metal spoon is electroplated with copper. State what is used as: the positive electrode (anode) ... the negative electrode (cathode) ... the electrolyte. ... [3]
• Positive electrode (anode): Copper (the plating metal)
• Negative electrode (cathode): Spoon (the object to be plated)
• Electrolyte: An aqueous solution of a named copper salt (e.g., copper(II) sulfate solution)

Recap

Electroplating is a process that uses electrolysis to coat one metal with another. This is done to improve appearance or resistance to corrosion. The anode is always the plating metal, and the cathode is the object to be plated.
(c) ... [steps 1-5 to make crystals] ... Calculate the maximum mass of copper(II) sulfate crystals, CuSO₄·5H₂O, that can form using the following steps. Calculate the number of moles of H₂SO₄ in 50.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol / dm³ H₂SO₄. ... mol Deduce the number of moles of CuSO₄·5H₂O that can form. ... mol The Mr of CuSO₄·5H₂O is 250. Calculate the maximum mass of CuSO₄·5H₂O that can form. ... g [3]
Step 1: Moles of H₂SO₄ Volume = 50.0 cm³ = 0.050 dm³ Moles = Concentration x Volume Moles = 0.200 mol/dm³ x 0.050 dm³ = 0.01 mol
Step 2: Moles of CuSO₄·5H₂O From the equation: CuO + H₂SO₄ + 4H₂O → CuSO₄·5H₂O The molar ratio of H₂SO₄ to CuSO₄·5H₂O is 1:1. Therefore, moles of CuSO₄·5H₂O = 0.01 mol
Step 3: Max mass of CuSO₄·5H₂O Mass = Moles x Mr Mass = 0.01 mol x 250 g/mol = 2.5 g

Recap

This is a stoichiometric reacting mass calculation. The steps are:
  1. Calculate moles of the known substance: Moles = Concentration × Volume (in dm³).
  2. Use the molar ratio from the balanced equation to find the moles of the desired product.
  3. Calculate the mass of the desired product: Mass = Moles × Mr.
(d) State one observation that indicates the copper(II) oxide is in excess in step 1. [1]
A solid (the black copper(II) oxide powder) remains undissolved at the bottom of the beaker.

Recap

When preparing a soluble salt from an insoluble base (like CuO) and an acid, the base is added in excess. This ensures all the acid (the limiting reactant) is used up. The excess solid can then be easily removed by filtration.
(e) Step 1 is repeated without heating the dilute sulfuric acid. All other conditions are kept the same. The rate of reaction decreases. Give a reason why the rate of reaction decreases. Explain your answer in terms of particles. [3]
The rate decreases because the particles have less (kinetic) energy.
This means there are fewer collisions between particles per second (a lower collision frequency).
Also, a smaller percentage (or proportion) of these collisions will be successful, as fewer particles will have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy.

Recap

Increasing the temperature increases the rate of reaction for two main reasons (based on collision theory):
  1. Particles have more kinetic energy and move faster, leading to more frequent collisions.
  2. A greater proportion of these collisions will have energy above the activation energy (Ea), leading to more effective collisions.
The second reason is the more important one.
(f) Name a substance, other than copper(II) oxide, that can be added to dilute sulfuric acid to produce copper(II) sulfate in step 1. [1]
copper(II) carbonate OR copper(II) hydroxide

Recap

Acids react with metals (above hydrogen), metal oxides, metal hydroxides, and metal carbonates to produce a salt. Since copper is below hydrogen in the reactivity series, it will not react with dilute acid, but its oxide, hydroxide, or carbonate will.
(g) Name the process used to separate excess copper(II) oxide from aqueous copper(II) sulfate in step 2. [1]
Filtration

Recap

Filtration is a method used to separate an insoluble solid from a liquid. The solid left on the filter paper is called the residue, and the liquid that passes through is the filtrate.
(h) Suggest what is meant by the term saturated solution in step 3. [2]
A saturated solution is one that can dissolve no more solute... at a given temperature.

Recap

A solute is the substance that dissolves. The solvent is the liquid it dissolves in. Together they make a solution. The maximum amount of solute that can dissolve at a specific temperature creates a saturated solution.
(i) The phrase 'heating to dryness' means heating until no more water is given off. Explain why aqueous copper(II) sulfate is not heated to dryness in step 3. [1]
If heated to dryness, the water of crystallisation would be driven off, forming anhydrous copper(II) sulfate (which is a white powder) instead of the blue hydrated crystals.

Recap

Hydrated solids (like CuSO₄·5H₂O) contain water of crystallisation chemically combined in their crystal structure. Anhydrous solids do not contain this water. Heating hydrated crystals strongly will remove this water.

Question 6

Topics: Periodic Table, Group I Metals, Transition Elements, Group VII Halogens, Redox

6 The Periodic Table can be used to classify elements. (a) The Group I metals react with cold water. Transition elements do not react with cold water. (i) Describe two other differences in the chemical properties between Group I metals and transition elements. 1 ... 2 ... [2]
1. Group I metals have fixed oxidation states (always +1), whereas transition elements have variable oxidation states.
2. Group I metals (and their compounds) do not show catalytic behaviour, whereas transition elements (and their compounds) often act as catalysts.
(Other valid answer: Group I compounds are typically white/colourless, whereas transition element compounds are often coloured).

Recap

Transition elements are a block of metals in the Periodic Table with characteristic properties, including:
  • Forming coloured compounds
  • Having variable oxidation states (e.g., Iron can be Fe²⁺ or Fe³⁺)
  • Acting as catalysts
Group I metals do not share these chemical properties.
(ii) Describe the observations when potassium is added to cold water. Write a balanced equation for the reaction. Include state symbols. observations ... ... equation ... [5]
Observations: (Any 2)
  • The potassium melts (into a spherical shape).
  • It moves around (floats) on the surface of the water.
  • Bubbles of gas are produced rapidly (fizzes / effervescence).
  • A lilac flame is seen.
  • (May explode or dissolve/disappear).
Equation:
2K(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2KOH(aq) + H₂(g)

Recap

Group I metals (alkali metals) react with water to form a metal hydroxide and hydrogen gas. The reactivity of the alkali metals increases down the group. Potassium is more reactive than sodium, so its reaction is more vigorous, producing enough heat to melt the metal and ignite the hydrogen gas (which burns with a lilac flame characteristic of potassium).
(b) Transition elements are stronger than Group I metals. Describe two other differences in the physical properties of Group I metals and transition elements. 1 ... 2 ... [2]
1. Transition elements have high(er) melting points (while Group I metals have low melting points).
2. Transition elements have high(er) densities (while Group I metals have low densities).

Recap

Group I metals are relatively soft and have low densities (Li, Na, and K all float on water). Transition elements are typically hard, strong, and have high melting points and high densities.
(c) Some Group VII elements react with aqueous solutions containing halide ions. When aqueous bromine is added to aqueous potassium iodide a reaction occurs. The ionic half-equations for the reaction are shown. Br₂(aq) + 2e⁻ → 2Br⁻(aq) 2I⁻(aq) → I₂(aq) + 2e⁻ (i) Describe the colour change of the solution. original colour of potassium iodide solution ... final colour of reaction mixture ... [2]
Original colour of potassium iodide solution: colourless
Final colour of reaction mixture: brown (or red-brown)

Recap

Aqueous potassium iodide (containing I⁻ ions) is colourless. Aqueous bromine (Br₂) is orange (or red-brown). When they react, iodine (I₂) is formed, which is brown in aqueous solution.
(ii) State the name of the general term given to the type of reaction in which electrons are transferred from one species to another. [1]
Redox

Recap

Redox reactions are those where oxidation and reduction happen at the same time. This can be defined in terms of electron transfer:
  • Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons).
  • Reduction Is Gain (of electrons).
(iii) Identify the oxidising agent in this reaction. Give a reason for your answer. oxidising agent ... reason ... [2]
Oxidising agent: Br₂ (bromine)
Reason: It is reduced (it gains electrons, as shown in the half-equation Br₂ + 2e⁻ → 2Br⁻).

Recap

An oxidising agent is a substance that oxidises another substance and is itself reduced in the process. A reducing agent reduces another substance and is itself oxidised. In this reaction, I⁻ is the reducing agent.
(d) Use the key to complete the table to show the results of adding aqueous halogens to aqueous solutions of halides. One has been completed for you. [2]
The completed table should show:
  • Cl₂(aq) row: ✓ (reacts with KBr), ✓ (reacts with KI)
  • Br₂(aq) row: x (no reaction with KCl), ✓ (reacts with KI)
  • I₂(aq) row: x (no reaction with KCl), x (no reaction with KBr)

Recap

The reactivity of the halogens (Group VII elements) decreases down the group. The reactivity order is: Chlorine > Bromine > Iodine. A more reactive halogen will displace a less reactive halogen from its halide solution.
  • Chlorine displaces both bromide and iodide ions.
  • Bromine only displaces iodide ions.
  • Iodine cannot displace chloride or bromide ions.
