Question 1 | 0620/43/M/J/20 | Organic Chemistry
(a) The structures of five organic compounds, A, B, C, D and E, are shown.
Answer the questions that follow.
Each letter may be used once, more than once or not at all.
(i) Give the letter of the compound that is propan-1-ol. [1]
The compound that represents propan-1-ol is D.

Recap

Alcohols contain the -OH functional group. The prefix 'prop-' indicates there are 3 carbon atoms in the chain.
(ii) Give the letter of the compound that has the empirical formula CH2. [1]
The compound with the empirical formula CH2 is C.

Recap

The empirical formula is the simplest whole number ratio of the different atoms in a compound. For example, an alkene with molecular formula C4H8 has an empirical formula of CH2.
(iii) Give the letter of one compound that reacts with bromine in an addition reaction. [1]
The compound that reacts with bromine in an addition reaction is C.

Recap

Unsaturated hydrocarbons (alkenes) contain C=C double bonds and undergo addition reactions with bromine, turning aqueous bromine from orange to colourless.
(iv) Give the letter of one compound that reacts with chlorine to form the compound shown.
[1]
The compound that reacts with chlorine to form the product shown is B.

Recap

Alkanes are generally unreactive but undergo substitution reactions with chlorine in the presence of ultraviolet light.
(v) Give the letters of two compounds that can react with each other to form an ester. [1]
The two compounds are D and E (or A).

Recap

Esters are formed when a carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol in the presence of an acid catalyst. This produces an ester and water.
(vi) Give the letter of the compound that is in the same homologous series as hex-1-ene. [1]
The compound in the same homologous series is C.

Recap

A homologous series is a family of similar organic compounds with the same functional group and general formula. Hex-1-ene is an alkene (CnH2n).
(vii) Give the letter of one compound that is an acid. [1]
The compound that is an acid is A (or E).

Recap

Carboxylic acids contain the -COOH functional group and are weak acids.
(viii) Draw a structural isomer of compound D. Show all of the atoms and all of the bonds. [1]
The isomer is propan-2-ol. Structure should show the -OH group on the middle carbon: CH3-CH(OH)-CH3.

Recap

Structural isomers are compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural formulae. For alcohols, the -OH group can be in different positions.
(b) Some acids are described as weak acids. State the meaning of the term weak acid. [2]
A weak acid is an acid that can donate protons but partially ionises (or does not fully dissociate) in aqueous solution.

Recap

Weak acids and weak bases are partially dissociated (ionised) in aqueous solution. Strong acids are completely dissociated.
Question 2 | 0620/43/M/J/20 | The Haber Process
2 Ammonia is manufactured by the Haber process. (a) The equation for the reaction is shown. N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g)
(i) State what is meant by the symbol ⇌. [1]
The symbol means the reaction is a reversible reaction.

Recap

In reversible reactions (symbol ⇌), the products can react to form the original reactants.
(ii) State one source of hydrogen used in the manufacture of ammonia. [1]
Hydrogen is obtained from hydrocarbons (reacting with steam) or cracking.

Recap

The Haber process uses nitrogen from the air and hydrogen obtained by reacting steam with methane.
(b) The table shows some data for the production of ammonia.
pressure / atm temperature / °C percentage yield of ammonia
25035058
10045028
40045042
25055020
Deduce the effect on the percentage yield of ammonia of: increasing the pressure of the reaction increasing the temperature of the reaction. [2]
Increasing the pressure increases the yield. Increasing the temperature decreases the yield.

Recap

An increase in pressure increases the yield of ammonia because there are fewer moles of gaseous products. An increase in temperature decreases the yield because the forward reaction is exothermic.
(c) Explain, in terms of particles, what happens to the rate of this reaction when the temperature is increased. [3]
When temperature increases, the particles have more kinetic energy (move faster). This results in a greater collision rate (more collisions per second). Additionally, a greater proportion of collisions have sufficient activation energy to react.

Recap

Increasing temperature increases the rate of reaction because particles have more energy, increasing collision frequency and the number of particles with energy above the activation energy.
(d) Ammonia, NH3, is used to produce nitric acid, HNO3. This happens in a three-stage process. Stage 1 is a redox reaction. 4NH3 + 5O2 → 4NO + 6H2O
(i) Identify what is oxidised in stage 1. Give a reason for your answer. [2]
The substance oxidised is ammonia (or N/Nitrogen). The reason is that there is a gain in oxygen (or oxidation state of N increases from -3 to +2).

Recap

Oxidation is gain of oxygen, loss of hydrogen, or loss of electrons (increase in oxidation number).
(ii) In this reaction the predicted yield of NO is 512g. The actual yield is 384 g. Calculate the percentage yield of NO in this reaction. [2]
Percentage yield = (384 / 512) x 100 = 75 %.

Recap

Percentage yield = (experimental yield / theoretical yield) x 100.
(iii) The equation for the reaction in stage 2 is shown. 2NO + O2 → 2NO2 Which major environmental problem does NO2 cause if it is released into the atmosphere? [1]
NO2 reacts with water to form acid rain.

Recap

Oxides of nitrogen (and sulfur dioxide) react with oxygen and water in the atmosphere to form dilute acids, causing acid rain.
(iv) The equation for the reaction in stage 3 is shown. 4NO2 + 2H2O + O2 → 4HNO3 Calculate the volume of O2 gas, at room temperature and pressure (r.t.p.), needed to produce 1260 g of HNO3. Use the following steps. Calculate the number of moles of HNO3. Deduce the number of moles of O2 that reacted. Calculate the volume of O2 gas that reacts at room temperature and pressure (r.t.p.). [4]
Moles of HNO3 = 1260 / 63 = 20 moles.
Moles of O2 = 20 / 4 = 5 moles.
Volume of O2 = 5 x 24 = 120 dm3.

Recap

Moles = mass / Mr. Stoichiometry allows calculation of reacting moles. Volume of gas at r.t.p. in dm3 = moles x 24.
(e) The reaction in stage 3 is exothermic. 4NO2 + 2H2O + O2 → 4HNO3 Complete the energy level diagram for this reaction. Include an arrow that clearly shows the energy change during the reaction.
[3]
The product line should be lower than the reactant line. The arrow should point downwards from the reactant line to the product line.

Recap

In an exothermic reaction, the energy level of the products is lower than the energy level of the reactants. Thermal energy is released.
Question 3 | 0620/43/M/J/20 | Group VII and Bonding
3 Chlorine is in Group VII of the Periodic Table. (a) Two isotopes of chlorine are chlorine-35 and chlorine-37.
(i) State why these two isotopes of chlorine have the same chemical properties. [2]
They have the same number of electrons in the outer shell (or same electronic configuration).

Recap

Isotopes have the same chemical properties because they have the same electronic configuration.
(ii) Complete the table to show the number of electrons, neutrons and protons in each atom and ion.
[3]
35Cl: electrons = 17, neutrons = 18, protons = 17.
37Cl-: electrons = 18, neutrons = 20, protons = 17.

Recap

Proton number = number of protons. Mass number = protons + neutrons. In a neutral atom, electrons = protons. Negative ions have gained electrons.
(b) (i) Chlorine reacts with aqueous sodium bromide. The equation for the reaction is shown. Cl2 + 2NaBr → 2NaCl + Br2 State the type of reaction shown. [1]
This is a displacement (or redox) reaction.

Recap

A displacement reaction is one where a more reactive element replaces a less reactive element in a compound. Chlorine is more reactive than bromine.
(ii) Why is there no reaction between iodine and aqueous sodium bromide? [1]
Because iodine is less reactive than bromine.

Recap

The reactivity of the halogens decreases down the group.
(c) Magnesium reacts with chlorine to form magnesium chloride. Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement of the ions in magnesium chloride. Give the charges on the ions. The inner shells have been completed.
[3]
Magnesium ion: [2,8] configuration, charge 2+. Chloride ions: [2,8,8] configuration (8 electrons in outer shell), charge 1-.

Recap

Positive ions are formed by electron loss (metals). Negative ions are formed by electron gain (non-metals). Ionic bonds are the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.
(d) Hydrogen and chlorine react to form hydrogen chloride gas, as shown in the equation. H2 + Cl2 → 2HCl Calculate the energy change for the reaction between hydrogen and chlorine, using the following steps. Calculate the energy needed to break the bonds. Calculate the energy released when bonds are formed. Calculate the energy change for the reaction. (Bond energies: H-H = 436, Cl-Cl = 243, H-Cl = 432 kJ/mol) [3]
Energy to break bonds = 436 + 243 = 679 kJ.
Energy released = 2 x 432 = 864 kJ.
Energy change = 679 - 864 = -185 kJ/mol.

Recap

Bond breaking is endothermic (+). Bond making is exothermic (-). Enthalpy change = Bonds broken - Bonds formed.
Question 4 | 0620/43/M/J/20 | Separation and Analysis
4 (a) Filtration and chlorination are two stages in water treatment. State the purpose of each stage. [2]
Filtration: to remove solids (or insoluble substances).
Chlorination: to kill microbes (or sterilise).

Recap

In water treatment, insoluble materials are removed by filtration. Chlorine is added to kill bacteria.
(b) A student uses anhydrous copper(II) sulfate to test for the presence of water. (i) What colour change is seen if water is present? [2]
From white to blue.

Recap

Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate turns from white to blue when water is added.
(ii) The purity of a sample of water can be assessed by measuring its boiling point. How is the boiling point of water affected by impurities? [1]
The boiling point is increased (or higher than 100°C).

Recap

Impurities in a compound raise its boiling point and lower its melting point.
(c) Chromatography can be used to test the purity of substances. The diagram shows the chromatogram of a coloured substance.
(i) How does this chromatogram show that this substance is not pure? [1]
It has separated into more than one spot.

Recap

A pure compound gives only a single spot when chromatographed.
(ii) Draw a circle round the correct Rf value for the spot labelled X. 0.2 0.4 0.8 1.2 [1]
0.8

Recap

Rf = distance travelled by substance / distance travelled by solvent. It is always less than 1.
(iii) State how a colourless substance can be made visible on a chromatogram. [1]
Use a locating agent.

Recap

Locating agents are used to react with colourless compounds (like amino acids) to make them visible.
Question 5 | 0620/43/M/J/20 | States of Matter
5 (a) Complete the table about solids, liquids and gases.
[3]
Solid separation: touching.
Liquid arrangement: random.
Gas motion: random.

Recap

Solids have a regular arrangement and vibrate. Liquids are touching but random. Gases are far apart and move randomly.
(b) The graph shows the change in temperature as a sample of a gas is cooled.
Name the change of state taking place between A and B. [1]
Condensing (or condensation).

Recap

Condensation is the change in state from gas to liquid.
(c) A bottle of liquid perfume is left open at the front of a room. After some time, the perfume is smelt at the back of the room. Name the two physical processes taking place. [2]
Evaporation and diffusion.

Recap

Diffusion is the spreading out and mixing of particles due to random movement. Evaporation is the change from liquid to vapour below boiling point.

2Ca(NO3)2 → 2CaO + 4NO2 + 1O2

Thermal decomposition.

Add aqueous sodium hydroxide, then aluminium (foil) and warm. Ammonia gas produced turns damp red litmus blue.

Question 7 | 0620/43/M/J/20 | Metals and Electrolysis
7 Aluminium is extracted by electrolysis. Iron is extracted from its ore by reduction with carbon. (a) What is meant by the term electrolysis? [2]
Breakdown of a molten or aqueous ionic compound by the passage of electricity.

Recap

Electrolysis is the decomposition of an ionic compound when molten or in aqueous solution by the passage of an electric current.
(b) Name the main ore of aluminium. [1]
Bauxite.

Recap

Bauxite is the main ore of aluminium.
(c) (i) Explain why aluminium cannot be extracted by reduction with carbon. [1]
Aluminium is more reactive than carbon (or above carbon in the reactivity series).

Recap

Metal oxides above carbon in the reactivity series are extracted by electrolysis.
(ii) Describe the role of cryolite in the extraction of aluminium by electrolysis. [1]
To lower the melting point of aluminium oxide (solvent).

Recap

Cryolite dissolves aluminium oxide and lowers the melting point of the electrolyte.
(iii) Name the product formed at the positive electrode. [1]
Oxygen.

Recap

Oxygen is formed at the anode (positive electrode) during the electrolysis of molten aluminium oxide.
(iv) Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction at the negative electrode. [2]
Al3+ + 3e- → Al

Recap

Positive ions gain electrons at the cathode (reduction).
(d) Aluminium is used in overhead electricity cables. Give two properties of aluminium that make it suitable for use in overhead electricity cables. [2]
It conducts electricity and has low density.

Recap

Aluminium is a good conductor of electricity and has a low density.
(e) Iron is a transition element. (i) Iron forms hydrated iron(III) oxide when it rusts. Write a word equation to represent the formation of rust. [2]
iron + oxygen + water → hydrated iron(III) oxide

Recap

Rust is hydrated iron(III) oxide. Oxygen and water are needed for rusting.
(ii) Give two ways in which the properties of transition elements differ from the properties of Group I metals. [2]
Transition elements act as catalysts, form coloured compounds, or have variable oxidation numbers. (Also higher density/melting point).

Recap

Transition elements are metals with high melting points, high densities, form coloured compounds and act as catalysts.
Question 8 | 0620/43/M/J/20 | Polymers
8 (a) Part of the synthetic polymer, nylon, is shown in the diagram.
(i) Circle one amide linkage on the diagram. [1]
The amide linkage is the -CONH- group.

Recap

Polyamides such as nylon contain amide linkages.
(ii) Complete the structures of the two monomers that react to form nylon. [2]
One monomer is a dicarboxylic acid (HOOC-Box-COOH) and the other is a diamine (H2N-Box-NH2).

Recap

Polyamides are made by combining a dicarboxylic acid with a diamine.
(iii) Name the other product formed when nylon is produced. [1]
Water.

Recap

Nylon is a condensation polymer; a small molecule (water) is eliminated during formation.
(b) Items made from nylon are often disposed of by burying them in the ground. This is called landfill. Why is the disposal of nylon using landfill a problem? [1]
It is non-biodegradable (does not rot/break down).

Recap

Non-biodegradable plastics are those that cannot be broken down by organisms in soil or water.
(c) Give the name of a natural polymer. [1]
Protein (or carbohydrate/starch).

Recap

Proteins are natural polyamides made from amino acid monomers.
