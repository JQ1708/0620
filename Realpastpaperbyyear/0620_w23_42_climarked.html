Question 1 | 0620/42/O/N/23 | Atomic Structure & Periodic Table
1 Table 1.1 gives the electronic configurations of some atoms and ions, A to G.

Table 1.1
electronic configuration
A2,5
B2,8
C2,8,2
D2,8,4
E2,8,5
F2,8,6
G2,8,18,7
Answer the following questions about A to G.
Each letter may be used once, more than once or not at all.
State which of the atoms or ions, A to G, could be:

(a) a noble gas atom [1]
B

Recap

Noble gases (Group VIII/0) are unreactive because they have a stable electronic configuration. This means they have a full outer shell of electrons (e.g., 2,8 or 2,8,8).

(b) an atom of an element in Group VI [1]
F

Recap

The group number in the Periodic Table is the same as the number of electrons in the outer shell. Group VI elements have 6 outer electrons (e.g., 2,8,6).

(c) an atom with an atomic number of 14 [1]
D

Recap

The atomic number (proton number) equals the total number of electrons in a neutral atom. You can find this by summing the electrons in the configuration (e.g., 2 + 8 + 4 = 14).

(d) atoms from the same group [1]
A AND E

Recap

Elements in the same group have the same number of outer shell electrons. Both A (2,5) and E (2,8,5) have 5 outer electrons (Group V).

(e) a halogen atom [1]
G

Recap

Halogens are in Group VII and have 7 electrons in their outer shell (e.g., 2,8,18,7).

(f) an atom of an element which is a good conductor of electricity [1]
C

Recap

Metals are good electrical conductors. Elements in Groups I, II, and III are metals (e.g., C is 2,8,2 - Magnesium, Group II). Non-metals (Groups IV-VIII) are generally poor conductors.

(g) a stable ion of a Group V element [1]
B (or G is acceptable if interpreted as ion configuration)

Recap

Group V elements (e.g., N: 2,5) form stable ions by gaining 3 electrons to achieve a full outer shell, resulting in a 3- charge and a configuration like a noble gas (e.g., 2,8).

(h) an atom that forms an ion with a 2- charge. [1]
F

Recap

Negative ions (anions) are formed by electron gain. Group VI elements (e.g., 2,8,6) gain 2 electrons to fill their shell, forming a 2- ion.

Question 2 | 0620/42/O/N/23 | Isotopes & Transition Elements
2 Cobalt and copper are transition elements.
(a) Copper has two naturally occurring isotopes, 63Cu and 65Cu. Cobalt has only one naturally occurring isotope, 59Co.
(i) Complete Table 2.1 to show the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in the 59Co atom and the 65Cu2+ ion. [3]

Table 2.1

59Co: Protons = 27, Neutrons = 32, Electrons = 27.

65Cu2+: Protons = 29, Neutrons = 36, Electrons = 27.

Recap

Proton number (atomic number) determines the element. Neutrons = Mass number - Proton number. Positive ions have lost electrons (e.g., Cu2+ has lost 2 electrons).

(ii) Table 2.2 shows the relative abundance of the two naturally occurring isotopes of copper.
Table 2.2
isotope 63Cu (70%), 65Cu (30%)
Calculate the relative atomic mass of copper to one decimal place. [2]

Calculation: (63 x 70) + (65 x 30) = 6360.
6360 / 100 = 63.6.

Recap

To calculate relative atomic mass from isotopes: Multiply each mass number by its percentage abundance, sum these values, and divide by 100. This gives a weighted average.

(b) One physical property of transition elements such as copper and cobalt is that they are hard. Other metals such as lithium are softer.
State two other physical properties of copper and cobalt which are significantly different from lithium. [2]

1. High density.

2. High melting point.

Recap

Transition elements generally have high densities and high melting points compared to Group I metals (like lithium), which are soft with low densities and low melting points.

(c) Both copper and cobalt can form coloured compounds. Some of these compounds contain water of crystallisation.
(i) Define the term water of crystallisation. [2]

Water of crystallisation refers to water molecules present in hydrated crystals.

Recap

Hydrated crystals contain water of crystallisation chemically combined in their structure. Solids without this water are termed anhydrous.

(ii) State the colour and formula of hydrated cobalt(II) chloride crystals. [2]

Colour: Pink.

Formula: CoCl2.6H2O.

Recap

Anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride turns from blue to pink when water is added. This is a chemical test for water.

(iii) State the colour change seen when a few drops of water are added to anhydrous copper(II) sulfate. [2]

From white to blue.

Recap

Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate is white. When it becomes hydrated (by adding water), it turns blue.

(iv) State how this colour change can be reversed. [1]

By heating (the hydrated copper(II) sulfate).

Recap

This reaction is reversible. Heating hydrated salts removes the water of crystallisation, turning them back into the anhydrous form.

Question 3 | 0620/42/O/N/23 | Blast Furnace & Metals
3 Iron is manufactured in a blast furnace.
(a) Three of the starting materials added to the blast furnace are coke, iron ore and limestone. Name the other starting material added to the blast furnace. [1]

(Hot) air.

Recap

The raw materials for the blast furnace are hematite (iron ore), limestone, coke, and air (which provides oxygen for combustion).

(b) The source of iron in the blast furnace is Fe2O3. Fe2O3 is found in iron ore.
(i) Name the main ore of iron which contains Fe2O3. [1]
Hematite.

Recap

Hematite is the main ore of iron, containing iron(III) oxide.

(ii) The iron in Fe2O3 is reduced by reaction with carbon monoxide. The unbalanced symbol equation is shown.
Complete the equation.
Fe2O3 + .....CO → .....CO2 + .....Fe [1]

Fe2O3 + 3CO → 3CO2 + 2Fe

Recap

In the blast furnace, carbon monoxide reduces iron(III) oxide to iron.

(iii) State the change in oxidation number of iron in the reaction in (ii). [2]

From +3 to 0.

Recap

Oxidation number of an element in a compound is the charge it would have as an ion. Fe in Fe2O3 is +3. Pure elements (Fe) have an oxidation number of 0.

(iv) Explain how the change of oxidation number shows that iron has been reduced. [1]

There is a decrease in oxidation number.

Recap

Reduction is the decrease in oxidation number (or gain of electrons). Oxidation is the increase in oxidation number.

(c) The major impurity in iron ore is silicon(IV) oxide. Limestone is added to the blast furnace to remove this impurity.
Write two symbol equations to show how silicon(IV) oxide is removed. For each equation, state the type of chemical reaction that takes place. [4]

Eq 1: CaCO3 → CaO + CO2 (Thermal decomposition)

Eq 2: SiO2 + CaO → CaSiO3 (Acid-base reaction)

Recap

Limestone (calcium carbonate) decomposes by heat. The product, calcium oxide (basic), reacts with silicon(IV) oxide (acidic impurity) to form slag.

(d) Iron is converted to steel by mixing it with carbon and other elements.
(i) State the term given to a substance which is a mixture of a metal and other elements. [1]
Alloy.

Recap

Alloys are mixtures of a metal with other elements. They are often harder and stronger than pure metals because the different sized atoms prevent layers from sliding.

(ii) Name one element, other than carbon, mixed with iron in the making of stainless steel. [1]
Nickel or Chromium.

Recap

Stainless steel is an alloy of iron containing carbon, chromium, and nickel to resist corrosion.

(e) Preventing the rusting of steel is important. State the chemical name of rust. [1]
Hydrated iron(III) oxide.

Recap

Rust is hydrated iron(III) oxide, formed when iron reacts with oxygen and water.

(f) Steel can be coated with zinc to prevent rusting. This provides both a barrier method and sacrificial protection.
(i) State the term used for coating steel with zinc. [1]
Galvanising.
(ii) Describe another barrier method for preventing rusting. [1]
Painting / Greasing / Coating with plastic.

Recap

Barrier methods prevent oxygen and water from reaching the iron surface.

(iii) Explain how zinc provides sacrificial protection. [2]

Zinc is more reactive than iron.

Zinc reacts (oxidises/corrodes) in preference to / instead of iron.

Recap

In sacrificial protection, a more reactive metal corrodes to save the less reactive metal. The more reactive metal loses electrons more easily.

Question 4 | 0620/42/O/N/23 | Salt Preparation & Electrolysis
4 This question is about lead(II) chloride, PbCl2.
(a) A student prepares a sample of insoluble lead(II) chloride, PbCl2, by mixing aqueous solutions of two salts in a beaker.
(i) Identify two soluble salts suitable for making lead(II) chloride when mixed together. [2]

Lead nitrate.

Soluble chloride e.g., sodium chloride.

Recap

Insoluble salts are made by precipitation. You need two soluble reactants (e.g., all nitrates are soluble; Group I chlorides are soluble).

(ii) Write the ionic equation for the formation of lead(II) chloride by mixing aqueous solutions. Include state symbols. [3]

Pb2+(aq) + 2Cl-(aq) → PbCl2(s)

Marks for: PbCl2 product, Pb2+ + 2Cl- reactants, correct state symbols.

Recap

An ionic equation shows only the ions that react. Spectator ions are ignored. A precipitate is a solid (s) formed from aqueous (aq) ions.

(iii) List the steps the student should take in preparing a pure sample of lead(II) chloride from the mixture in the beaker. [3]

1. Filter.

2. Wash the residue with water.

3. Dry the residue.

Recap

To purify an insoluble salt: Filter to remove liquid, wash to remove soluble impurities, and dry.

(b) The student carries out an electrolysis experiment on molten lead(II) chloride using the apparatus shown in Fig. 4.1. Chlorine gas forms at the anode and escapes from the apparatus.
(i) Explain why lead(II) chloride needs to be molten before it will conduct electricity. [1]

To have mobile ions (ions that can move).

Recap

Ionic compounds conduct electricity only when molten or in aqueous solution because the ions are free to move and carry charge.

(ii) Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction occurring at the anode. [2]

2Cl- → Cl2 + 2e-

Recap

At the anode (positive electrode), negative ions lose electrons (oxidation) to form non-metal atoms/molecules.

(iii) State the test for chlorine gas. [2]

Test: (Damp) litmus (paper).

Observations: It is bleached / goes white.

Recap

Chlorine bleaches damp litmus paper. Ammonia turns damp red litmus blue.

(iv) Describe what is observed at the cathode. [1]

(Shiny) grey AND solid (or liquid metal).

Recap

At the cathode (negative electrode), positive metal ions gain electrons (reduction) to form the metal.

Question 5 | 0620/42/O/N/23 | Chemical Energetics
5 Chemical reactions can involve transfer of thermal energy.
(a) State the term used for the transfer of thermal energy during a reaction. [1]
Enthalpy change.

Recap

Enthalpy change (ΔH) is the heat energy transfer during a reaction. Exothermic = negative ΔH; Endothermic = positive ΔH.

(b) Tetrachloromethane gas, CCl4(g), reacts with steam as shown.
CCl4(g) + 2H2O(g) ⇌ CO2(g) + 4HCl(g)
The reaction is reversible. The forward reaction is exothermic.
(i) State what happens, if anything, to the rate of the forward reaction if the concentration of CCl4 is increased. Explain your answer in terms of collision theory. [3]

Rate of reaction increases.

More (CCl4) particles per unit volume.

Frequency of collisions increases.

Recap

Increasing concentration means more particles in the same space, leading to more frequent collisions and a faster rate.

(ii) State what happens to the position of equilibrium, if anything, when the pressure is increased. Explain your answer. [2]

Equilibrium moves to the left.

Because there are more moles of gas on the right hand side (5 moles vs 3 moles).

Recap

Increasing pressure shifts equilibrium to the side with fewer gas molecules to reduce the pressure.

(iii) Fig. 5.1 shows an incomplete reaction pathway diagram for the forward reaction.
On Fig. 5.1:
- insert the formulae of the reactants and products
- draw an arrow, labelled Ea, to show the activation energy
- draw an arrow, labelled ΔH, to show the transfer of energy in the reaction. [3]

1. Labels: CCl4 + 2H2O on reactant line, CO2 + 4HCl on product line.

2. Activation E: Upward arrow labelled Ea from reactant level to top of hump.

3. Energy change: Downward arrow labelled ΔH from reactant level to product level.

(iv) Define the term activation energy. [2]

The minimum energy that colliding particles must have to react.

Recap

Activation energy is the energy barrier that must be overcome for a reaction to occur.

(v) State one way in which the activation energy of a reaction can be changed. [1]

Decreased by adding a catalyst.

Recap

A catalyst lowers the activation energy, allowing more particles to have enough energy to react.

(c) The equation for the reaction between tetrachloromethane gas and steam can be represented as shown in Fig. 5.2.
Table 5.1 shows some bond energies.
C-Cl (340 kJ/mol), H-O (460 kJ/mol), C=O (805 kJ/mol).
ΔH = -130 kJ/mol.
Use the bond energies in Table 5.1 and the ΔH value for the reaction to calculate the H-Cl bond energy. [4]

1. Energy to break bonds: (4 x 340) + (4 x 460) = 3200 kJ/mol.

2. Energy released (CO2): 2 x 805 = 1610 kJ/mol.

3. Calculation: 3200 - (1610 + 4(H-Cl)) = -130.

4(H-Cl) = 3330 - 1610 = 1720. H-Cl = 430 kJ/mol.

Recap

Enthalpy change = Energy taken in (breaking bonds) - Energy given out (making bonds). Bond breaking is endothermic (+), bond making is exothermic (-).

Question 6 | 0620/42/O/N/23 | Organic Chemistry
6 A homologous series is a family of organic compounds whose members have similar chemical properties.
(a) Give two characteristics that are the same for all members of a homologous series. [2]

1. Same general formula.

2. Contain the same functional group.

Recap

A homologous series is a family of organic compounds with the same functional group and general formula (e.g., Alkanes: CnH2n+2).

(b) In terms of structure, state how one member of a homologous series differs from the next member of that homologous series. [1]
-CH2- unit.

Recap

Each member of a homologous series differs from the next by a CH2 unit.

(c) A, B and C are organic compounds.
A has molecular formula C12H24
B has the name tetradecane.
C has three carbon atoms and is in the homologous series with the general formula CnH2n+1COOH.
(i) Name the homologous series each organic compound belongs to. [3]

A: Alkenes (CnH2n).

B: Alkanes.

C: Carboxylic acids.

Recap

Alkanes (CnH2n+2) are saturated. Alkenes (CnH2n) are unsaturated (C=C). Carboxylic acids contain -COOH.

(ii) Name C and draw its displayed formula. [2]

Name: Butanoic acid (Total 4 carbons, n=3 in formula gives C3H7COOH).

Displayed Formula: CH3CH2CH2COOH (All bonds shown).

Recap

When naming carboxylic acids, count the C in the -COOH group. Propanoic acid has 3 carbons total, but the general formula CnH2n+1COOH uses 'n' for the alkyl chain.

(d) Amino acids are a homologous series where each member has the general structure shown in Fig. 6.1.
The R side chain contains carbon and hydrogen atoms only.
(i) An amino acid has a relative molecular mass of 103.
Deduce the formula of the R side chain in this amino acid. Show your working. [2]

Mass of fixed part (NH2-CH-COOH) = 14+2+12+1+12+32+1 = 74.

Mass of R = 103 - 74 = 29.

R = C2H5.

(ii) State the name given to the natural polyamides formed from amino acid monomers. [1]
Proteins.

Recap

Proteins are natural polyamides made from amino acid monomers via condensation polymerisation.
