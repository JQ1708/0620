Q1 | 0620/42/M/J/21 | Periodic Table / Period 3
The symbols of the elements of Period 3 of the Periodic Table are shown.

Na    Mg    Al    Si    P    S    Cl    Ar

Answer the following questions about these elements.
Each element may be used once, more than once or not at all.
Write the symbol of an element which:

(a) is malleable [1]

Na (Sodium), Mg (Magnesium), or Al (Aluminium) are acceptable answers.

Recap

Metals are malleable (can be hammered into shape) and ductile. This is because they consist of layers of ions that can slide over each other when a force is applied.

(b) has only two electrons in its outermost shell [1]

Mg (Magnesium).

Recap

The group number in the Periodic Table indicates the number of electrons in the outer shell. Magnesium is in Group II, so it has two outer electrons.

(c) forms an oxide which leads to acid rain [1]

S (Sulfur).

Recap

Sulfur dioxide is formed when fossil fuels containing sulfur are burned. This gas reacts with water in the atmosphere to form sulfuric acid, causing acid rain.

(d) forms an ion with a 2- charge [1]

S (Sulfur).

Recap

Elements in Group VI (like sulfur) need to gain two electrons to achieve a full outer shell, resulting in a 2- charge.

(e) is extracted from an ore called bauxite [1]

Al (Aluminium).

Recap

Bauxite is the main ore of aluminium, containing aluminium oxide. It is extracted by electrolysis.

(f) does not form an oxide [1]

Ar (Argon).

Recap

Argon is a noble gas (Group VIII). It has a full outer shell of electrons, making it unreactive (inert).

(g) forms an oxide with a macromolecular structure [1]

Si (Silicon).

Recap

Silicon(IV) oxide (silica) has a giant covalent structure similar to diamond, with high melting points and hardness.

(h) forms an amphoteric oxide [1]

Al (Aluminium).

Recap

Amphoteric oxides react with both acids and bases. Aluminium oxide and zinc oxide are common examples.

(i) exists as diatomic molecules [1]

Cl (Chlorine).

Recap

Elements in Group VII (Halogens) exist as diatomic molecules (e.g., Cl₂, Br₂).

(j) forms a binary compound with hydrogen that is a strong acid. [1]

Cl (Chlorine).

Recap

Hydrogen chloride (HCl) dissolves in water to form hydrochloric acid, which is a strong acid (fully dissociated).

Q2 | 0620/42/M/J/21 | Atomic Structure / Salt Preparation
2 Silver has an atomic number of 47.
(a) Naturally occurring atoms of silver are 107Ag and 109Ag
(i) State the name given to atoms of the same element with different nucleon numbers. [1]

Isotopes.

Recap

Isotopes are atoms with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.

(ii) Complete the table to show the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in each atom and ion of silver shown. [3]

For 107Ag: Protons 47, Neutrons 60, Electrons 47.
For 109Ag+: Protons 47, Neutrons 62, Electrons 46.

Recap

Proton number = Atomic number. Neutron number = Mass number - Proton number. Positive ions have lost electrons.

(iii) Complete this definition of relative atomic mass.
Relative atomic mass is the ________ mass of naturally occurring atoms of an element on a scale where the ________ atom has a mass of exactly ________ units. [3]

average (or mean) ... 12C (carbon-12) ... 12.

Recap

Relative atomic mass (Ar) is the average mass of isotopes compared to 1/12th the mass of an atom of carbon-12.

(iv) A sample of silver has a relative atomic mass of 108.0. Deduce the percentage of 107Ag present in this sample of silver. [1]

50%.

Recap

108 is exactly halfway between 107 and 109, indicating an equal abundance of both isotopes.

(b) Silver nitrate is a salt of silver made by reacting silver oxide with an acid. Write the formula of the acid which reacts with silver oxide to form silver nitrate. [1]

HNO₃ (Nitric acid).

Recap

Acids react with bases (metal oxides) to form salts. Nitric acid forms nitrate salts.

(c) Aqueous silver nitrate is a colourless solution containing Ag+(aq) ions.
(i) Describe what is seen when aqueous silver nitrate is added to aqueous sodium iodide, NaI(aq). [1]

Yellow precipitate.

Recap

Silver nitrate is used to test for halides. Chloride gives white, bromide gives cream, and iodide gives a yellow precipitate.

(ii) Write the ionic equation for the reaction between aqueous silver nitrate and aqueous sodium iodide. Include state symbols. [3]

Ag+(aq) + I-(aq) → AgI(s)

Recap

An ionic equation shows only the ions that react. Spectator ions are ignored. Precipitates are solids (s).

(d) In the positive test for aqueous nitrate ions, aqueous sodium hydroxide and one other substance are warmed with the nitrate ions.
Name this other substance and the gas formed. [2]

Substance: Aluminium (foil/powder).
Gas: Ammonia.

Recap

Nitrates are reduced to ammonia gas by aluminium in alkaline solution. Ammonia turns damp red litmus paper blue.

(e) When silver nitrate is exposed to sunlight, silver is formed.
Name the type of reaction which needs light to make it happen. [1]

Photochemical reaction.

Recap

A photochemical reaction depends on the presence of light to provide the activation energy.

(f) Members of one homologous series only react with chlorine in the presence of sunlight.
(i) Name a member of this homologous series. [1]

Methane (or any alkane).

Recap

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons. They react with chlorine in UV light via substitution.

(ii) Name two products that form when the compound in (i) reacts with chlorine. [2]

Chloromethane (or appropriate haloalkane) and Hydrogen chloride.

Recap

In a substitution reaction, a hydrogen atom is replaced by a chlorine atom, producing a chloroalkane and hydrogen chloride gas.

Q3 | 0620/42/M/J/21 | Stoichiometry / Carbonates
3 Sodium hydrogencarbonate is found in baking powder.
When sodium hydrogencarbonate is heated it forms three products.
2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂

(a) Name the type of reaction that takes place when sodium hydrogencarbonate reacts in this way. [1]

Thermal decomposition.

Recap

Thermal decomposition is the breakdown of a compound into two or more substances by heating.

(b) Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed at room temperature and pressure when 12.6g of NaHCO₃ is heated using the following steps: [4]

determine the mass of one mole of NaHCO₃
calculate the number of moles of NaHCO₃ used
determine the number of moles of carbon dioxide formed
calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed at room temperature and pressure.

Mass of one mole: 84 g (23+1+12+(16x3))
Moles used: 0.15 mol (12.6 / 84)
Moles CO₂: 0.075 mol (Ratio is 2:1, so 0.15 / 2)
Volume CO₂: 1.8 dm³ (0.075 x 24)

Recap

Moles = Mass / Mr.
Gas Volume = Moles x 24 dm³ (at r.t.p).

(c) Limewater is aqueous calcium hydroxide. Carbon dioxide turns limewater milky because a white precipitate forms.
Write the formula of:
• calcium hydroxide
• the white precipitate that forms when limewater turns milky. [2]

Calcium hydroxide: Ca(OH)₂
White precipitate: CaCO₃ (Calcium carbonate)

Recap

Limewater is a solution of calcium hydroxide. It reacts with carbon dioxide to form insoluble calcium carbonate.

Q4 | 0620/42/M/J/21 | Electrolysis
4 A student carries out an electrolysis experiment using the apparatus shown.
The student uses dilute aqueous sodium chloride.
(a) State the name given to any solution which undergoes electrolysis. [1]

Electrolyte.

Recap

An electrolyte is a molten or aqueous ionic compound that conducts electricity and decomposes during electrolysis.

(b) Hydroxide ions are discharged at the anode.
(i) Complete the ionic half-equation for this reaction. [2]
......OH⁻(aq) → ........................ + O₂(g) + 4e⁻

4OH⁻(aq) → 2H₂O + O₂(g) + 4e⁻

Recap

At the anode (positive electrode), hydroxide ions are discharged to form oxygen and water.

(ii) Explain how the ionic half-equation shows the hydroxide ions are being oxidised. [1]

They lose electrons.

Recap

Oxidation is the loss of electrons (OIL RIG).

(c) Describe what the student observes at the cathode. [1]

Fizzing / Bubbles (of gas).

Recap

In dilute solutions, hydrogen gas is produced at the cathode because hydrogen is less reactive than sodium.

(d) Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction at the cathode. [2]

2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂

Recap

Positive ions (cations) gain electrons (reduction) at the cathode.

(e) The student repeats the experiment using concentrated aqueous sodium chloride.
(i) Describe what the student observes at:
the cathode
the anode. [2]

Cathode: Fizzing (colourless gas/hydrogen).
Anode: Green gas (chlorine).

Recap

In concentrated halide solutions, the halide ion is discharged at the anode instead of hydroxide. Chlorine is a green gas.

(ii) The student added litmus to the solution after the electrolysis of concentrated aqueous sodium chloride.
State the colour seen in the solution. Give a reason for your answer. [2]

Colour: Blue.
Reason: Solution becomes alkaline (NaOH forms).

Recap

Removing H⁺ and Cl⁻ ions leaves Na⁺ and OH⁻ ions in solution, forming sodium hydroxide, which is an alkali.

(f) Carbon electrodes are used because they are inert. State another element that can be used instead of carbon. [1]

Platinum.

Recap

Electrodes must be inert (unreactive) to prevent them from reacting with the electrolyte or products. Platinum is often used.

Q5 | 0620/42/M/J/21 | Nitrogen Compounds / Bonding / Energy
5 This question is about compounds of nitrogen.
(a) Nitrogen reacts with lithium to form lithium nitride, Li₃N
(i) Write the chemical equation for the reaction between lithium and nitrogen. [2]

6Li + N₂ → 2Li₃N

Recap

Nitrogen exists as a diatomic molecule (N₂). Lithium is in Group I (valency 1) and Nitrogen is in Group V (valency 3), forming Li₃N.

(ii) Lithium nitride is ionically bonded. Complete the diagram to show the electronic structure of the nitride ion. Show the charge on the nitride ion. [2]

Outer shell with 8 electrons (5 dots/crosses + 3 crosses/dots).
Charge: 3-.

Recap

A nitride ion is formed when a nitrogen atom gains 3 electrons to complete its outer shell (2,8).

(b) Nitrogen reacts with fluorine to form nitrogen trifluoride, NF₃
(i) The chemical equation can be represented as shown.
N≡N + 3 F-F → 2 F-N-F (with F on N)
Calculate the energy change for the reaction between nitrogen and fluorine, using the following steps: [3]
Bond energies: N≡N: 945, F-F: 160, N-F: 300 kJ/mol.

Energy taken to break bonds: 1425 kJ (945 + 3*160)
Energy released when bonds formed: 1800 kJ (6 * 300)
Energy change: -375 kJ/mol.

Recap

Enthalpy change = (Energy of bonds broken) - (Energy of bonds formed). Breaking bonds is endothermic; forming bonds is exothermic.

(ii) Use your answer to (i) to deduce whether this reaction is endothermic or exothermic. Explain your answer. [1]

Exothermic. More energy is released in bond formation than is used in bond breaking.

Recap

Exothermic reactions have a negative enthalpy change because the products have less energy than the reactants.

(iii) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement in a molecule of NF₃. Use dots for nitrogen electrons and crosses for fluorine electrons. Show outer electrons only. [3]

Nitrogen bonded to 3 Fluorines (1 shared pair each).
Nitrogen has one lone pair.
Each Fluorine has 3 lone pairs.

Recap

Covalent bonds involve sharing pairs of electrons. Nitrogen (Group V) forms 3 bonds and keeps 1 lone pair.

(c) Lithium nitride melts at 813°C. Nitrogen trifluoride melts at –206°C. Explain in terms of attractive forces why lithium nitride has a much higher melting point than nitrogen trifluoride. [3]

Lithium nitride has strong ionic bonds (electrostatic attraction) between ions.
Nitrogen trifluoride has weak intermolecular forces between molecules.
More energy is required to break the strong ionic bonds.

Recap

Ionic lattices have high melting points. Simple covalent molecules have low melting points due to weak forces between molecules.

(d) Ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃, is a compound of nitrogen.
(i) Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in ammonium nitrate. [2]

Mr of NH₄NO₃ = 80. Mass of N (2 atoms) = 28.
Percentage = (28/80) x 100 = 35%.

Recap

% by mass = (Mass of element / Formula mass) x 100.

(ii) State a use of ammonium nitrate in agriculture. [1]

Fertiliser.

Recap

Nitrogen is essential for making proteins in plants to promote growth.

(iii) State the name of a compound that will displace ammonia from ammonium nitrate. [1]

Calcium hydroxide (or any strong base).

Recap

Alkalis react with ammonium salts to release ammonia gas when heated.

(e) Ammonia is a base which forms a weakly alkaline solution when dissolved in water.
(i) Define the term base. [1]

Proton acceptor.

Recap

Acids are proton (H⁺) donors. Bases are proton acceptors.

(ii) Suggest the pH of aqueous ammonia. [1]

Between 8 and 11 (e.g., 9 or 10).

Recap

Weak alkalis have pH values above 7 but lower than strong alkalis (13-14).

Q6 | 0620/42/M/J/21 | Organic Chemistry / Polymers
6 Molecules A and B can form condensation polymers.
A: HO- -OH (Diol)
B: HOOC- -COOH (Dicarboxylic acid)

(a) Each molecule has two identical functional groups.
(i) Name the functional group in B. [1]

Carboxylic acid.

Recap

The -COOH group is the functional group of carboxylic acids.

(ii) Draw the part of the structure of the synthetic polymer that would form when two molecules of A and two molecules of B combine. Show all of the bonds in the linkages. [3]

A chain showing ester linkages (-COO-) connecting the blocks. Must show 3 linkages correctly oriented and continuation bonds.

Recap

Polyesters are formed by the condensation polymerisation of a diol and a dicarboxylic acid. An ester link and water are formed.

(iii) Name the other product formed when molecules of A and B undergo polymerisation. [1]

Water.

Recap

In condensation polymerisation, a small molecule (usually water or HCl) is eliminated when monomers join.

(b) Molecule A is a simple sugar unit which can be made by hydrolysis of complex carbohydrates.
(i) Draw part of the complex carbohydrate that could be hydrolysed to make molecules of A. Include one linkage and show all of the bonds in the linkage. [1]

Blocks linked by an oxygen atom: -O-.

Recap

Complex carbohydrates are polymers of simple sugars joined by ether linkages (or glycosidic bonds, represented as -O-).

(ii) State two sets of conditions which could be used to hydrolyse the complex carbohydrate to form A. [2]

1. Acid and heat.
2. Enzymes.

Recap

Hydrolysis breaks polymers back into monomers using water. This is catalysed by acid or enzymes (biological catalysts).

(iii) Name the technique used to identify the individual sugar units made by the hydrolysis of a complex carbohydrate. [1]

Chromatography.

Recap

Chromatography separates mixtures of soluble substances. Locating agents are used for colourless sugars.

(c) Ethanol can be made from the simple sugar glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆.
(i) State the name of this process. [1]

Fermentation.

Recap

Fermentation is the breakdown of glucose by yeast in the absence of oxygen to form ethanol and carbon dioxide.

(ii) Complete the chemical equation for this reaction. [2]
C₆H₁₂O₆ → ...........................

C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂

Recap

Glucose produces ethanol and carbon dioxide during fermentation.
