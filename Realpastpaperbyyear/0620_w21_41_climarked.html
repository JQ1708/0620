Some elements are shown in the order they appear in the reactivity series. The most reactive element is at the top.

(a) Answer the questions using the list of elements. Each element may be used once, more than once or not at all.

(i) Identify: a non-metal [1]

The non-metal in the list is hydrogen.

Recap

The reactivity series is used to compare the reactivity of metals. It also includes hydrogen, which is a non-metal. Metals above hydrogen in the series can react with dilute acids to produce hydrogen gas, while metals below hydrogen cannot.

(ii) a metal which is stored under oil [1]

The metal stored under oil is sodium.

Recap

Group I metals, also known as the alkali metals, are extremely reactive with both air (oxygen) and water. To prevent these reactions, they are stored under oil.

(iii) the main component of steel [1]

The main component of steel is iron.

Recap

Steel is an alloy, which is a mixture of a metal with other elements. Specifically, steel is an alloy of iron mixed with controlled amounts of carbon or other metals (like nickel and chromium in stainless steel).

(iv) a metal with three electrons in the outer shell of its atoms [1]

The metal with three outer shell electrons is aluminium.

Recap

An element's position in the Periodic Table indicates its electron structure. Aluminium is in Group III, which means its atoms have three electrons in their outer shell. These electrons are lost to form an Al³⁺ ion.

(v) a metal found in brass [1]

A metal found in brass is copper (or zinc).

Recap

Brass is an alloy, which is a mixture of metals. It is made from copper and zinc. Alloys are often harder and more resistant to corrosion than pure metals.

(vi) a metal that forms chlorides of the type XCl₂ and XCl₃ [1]

The metal that forms both types of chlorides is iron.

Recap

Transition elements, like iron, are known for having variable oxidation states. This means they can form ions with different charges. Iron can form the Fe²⁺ ion (in Iron(II) chloride, FeCl₂) and the Fe³⁺ ion (in Iron(III) chloride, FeCl₃).

(b) Name the main ores of:

(i) zinc [1]

The main ore of zinc is zinc blende.

Recap

An ore is a rock that contains enough of a metal compound to make it economically viable to extract the metal. Zinc blende consists mainly of zinc sulfide (ZnS).

(ii) aluminium. [1]

The main ore of aluminium is bauxite.

Recap

Bauxite is the primary ore of aluminium. It is purified to produce aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃), which is then used in electrolysis to extract the pure aluminium metal.

(c) In an experiment, a sample of aluminium appeared less reactive than expected. Explain why. [1]

Aluminium has a protective oxide layer on its surface that stops it from reacting.

Recap

Although aluminium is high in the reactivity series, it appears unreactive because it rapidly reacts with oxygen in the air. This forms a very thin, tough, and unreactive layer of aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃) on its surface. This layer sticks firmly to the metal and protects it from further chemical attack.

(d) Name two metals from the list which are extracted by reduction of their ores using carbon. [2]

Two metals that can be extracted using carbon are zinc and iron. (Copper is also a correct answer).

Recap

The method of metal extraction depends on its reactivity. Metals below carbon in the reactivity series (like zinc, iron, and copper) can be extracted from their oxides by heating with carbon. The carbon acts as a reducing agent, removing the oxygen. More reactive metals (like aluminium or sodium) must be extracted using electrolysis.

(e) When zinc granules are added to aqueous copper(II) sulfate, a reaction occurs. During the reaction, a red-pink solid is formed and the solution becomes colourless.

(i) Name the red-pink solid. [1]

The red-pink solid is copper.

Recap

This is a displacement reaction. Zinc is more reactive than copper, so it displaces the copper from the copper(II) sulfate solution: Zn(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + Cu(s). The solid copper metal product is a red-pink solid.

(ii) Name the colourless solution. [1]

The colourless solution is zinc sulfate.

Recap

Copper(II) sulfate solution is blue because Cu²⁺ ions (a transition element ion) are coloured. When zinc displaces the copper, it forms zinc sulfate (ZnSO₄) solution. Zinc is not a transition element, and its compounds are typically colourless (or white as solids).

(iii) Explain, in terms of particles, why the rate of this reaction increases when the temperature is increased. [3]

When the temperature is increased, the particles have more energy and move faster. This leads to more collisions between particles per second. Crucially, a greater percentage of these collisions will be successful because the particles will collide with energy equal to or greater than the activation energy.

Recap

This explanation is based on the Collision Theory. For a reaction to occur, particles must collide with sufficient energy (the activation energy, Eₐ). Increasing temperature gives particles more kinetic energy. This increases both the frequency of collisions and, more importantly, the proportion of collisions that are effective (have energy ≥ Eₐ).

(iv) Suggest two other ways of increasing the rate of this reaction. [2]

Two other ways are:
1. Use a catalyst.
2. Use smaller granules of zinc (or zinc powder) to increase the surface area.
(A third way is to increase the concentration of the copper(II) sulfate solution).

Recap

The rate of reaction is a measure of how fast reactants are used up or products are formed. Besides temperature, the rate can be increased by:

  • Increasing the concentration of solutions (more particles in a given volume).
  • Increasing the surface area of solid reactants (more particles are exposed to react).
  • Adding a catalyst (which lowers the activation energy).

Paper: 0620/41/O/N/21 Q2 Topic(s): Atomic Structure, Purity, Water, Stoichiometry

This question is about copper and its compounds.

(a) Copper has two different naturally occurring atoms, ⁶³Cu and ⁶⁵Cu.

(i) State the term used for atoms of the same element with different nucleon numbers. [1]

The term is isotopes.

Recap

Isotopes are atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons. This means they have the same proton number (atomic number) but different nucleon numbers (mass numbers).

(ii) The atomic number of copper is 29. Complete the table to show the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in the particles of copper shown. [3]

⁶³Cu ⁶⁵Cu²⁺
protons
neutrons
electrons
⁶³Cu ⁶⁵Cu²⁺
protons 29 29
neutrons 34 36
electrons 29 27

Recap

• Protons: The proton number (atomic number) is 29, so both particles have 29 protons.
• Neutrons: Number of neutrons = nucleon number - proton number.
For ⁶³Cu: 63 - 29 = 34 neutrons.
For ⁶⁵Cu: 65 - 29 = 36 neutrons.
• Electrons: A neutral atom (⁶³Cu) has the same number of electrons as protons (29). A positive ion (Cu²⁺) has lost electrons. A 2+ charge means it has lost 2 electrons: 29 - 2 = 27 electrons.

(iii) The percentage of the naturally occurring atoms in a sample of copper is shown.
⁶³Cu 70%
⁶⁵Cu 30%
Deduce the relative atomic mass of copper in this sample. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]

Step 1: `(70 x 63) + (30 x 65)` = `4410 + 1950` = `6360`
Step 2: `6360 / 100` = 63.6
Relative atomic mass = 63.6

Recap

Relative atomic mass (Aᵣ) is the average mass of the naturally occurring isotopes of an element. It is a weighted average calculated by summing the (percentage abundance × isotope mass) for each isotope, and then dividing the total by 100.

(b) Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate is used to test for the presence of water. When this test is positive, hydrated copper(II) sulfate is formed.

(i) State the colour change seen during this test. [2]

from white to (light) blue

Recap

This is a standard chemical test for water. Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate (CuSO₄) is a white powder. When it comes into contact with water, it becomes hydrated copper(II) sulfate (CuSO₄·5H₂O), which is a blue crystalline solid.

(ii) Complete the chemical equation to show the reaction that takes place.
CuSO₄ + .......................... ⇌ CuSO₄·5H₂O [1]

CuSO₄ + 5H₂O ⇌ CuSO₄·5H₂O

Recap

The formula for hydrated copper(II) sulfate shows that five molecules of water of crystallisation combine with each formula unit of copper(II) sulfate. The ⇌ symbol signifies that this is a reversible reaction.

(iii) State how hydrated copper(II) sulfate can be turned back into anhydrous copper(II) sulfate. [1]

This is done by heating the hydrated solid.

Recap

Because the hydration reaction is reversible, the reverse reaction (dehydration) can be achieved by supplying energy. Gently heating the blue hydrated crystals will drive off the water of crystallisation, leaving the white anhydrous powder behind.

(iv) Describe a test for pure water. [2]

A test for pure water is to measure its boiling point, which should be exactly 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure. (Alternatively, measuring its freezing point, which should be exactly 0°C).

Recap

A pure substance has a sharp, fixed melting point and boiling point. Impurities, such as dissolved salts, will raise the boiling point (to >100°C) and lower the melting point (to <0°C).

(c) Aqueous copper(II) sulfate contains Cu²⁺(aq) ions.

(i) Describe what is seen when aqueous copper(II) sulfate is added to aqueous sodium hydroxide, NaOH(aq). [1]

A (light) blue precipitate is formed.

Recap

This is a standard test for cations. Aqueous sodium hydroxide is an alkali and provides OH⁻ ions. When added to a solution containing Copper(II) ions (Cu²⁺), an insoluble blue precipitate of copper(II) hydroxide, Cu(OH)₂, is formed.

(ii) Write the ionic equation for the reaction between aqueous copper(II) sulfate and aqueous sodium hydroxide. Include state symbols. [3]

Cu²⁺(aq) + 2OH⁻(aq) → Cu(OH)₂(s)

Recap

An ionic equation shows only the reacting particles. The sodium ions (Na⁺) from the NaOH(aq) and the sulfate ions (SO₄²⁻) from the CuSO₄(aq) are spectator ions; they remain dissolved in the solution and do not take part in the reaction. The product, copper(II) hydroxide, is an insoluble solid, so it is given the state symbol (s) for solid.

(d) When solid copper(II) nitrate is heated copper(II) oxide, nitrogen dioxide and oxygen are formed.

Calculate the volume of nitrogen dioxide formed at room temperature and pressure when 4.7 g of Cu(NO₃)₂ is heated. [4]

  2. Calculate the moles of the known substance using moles = mass / Mᵣ.

  4. Calculate the volume of gas using the equation: Volume (dm³) = moles × 24 dm³ (at r.t.p.).

(e) Write the chemical equation to show the action of heat on sodium nitrate, NaNO₃. [2]

2NaNO₃ → 2NaNO₂ + O₂

The thermal decomposition of metal nitrates depends on the metal's reactivity. Group I metal nitrates (like sodium nitrate) are very stable and decompose to form the metal nitrite (NaNO₂) and oxygen (O₂). Less reactive metal nitrates (like copper(II) nitrate) decompose further to form the metal oxide, nitrogen dioxide, and oxygen.

Paper: 0620/41/O/N/21 Q3 Topic(s): Electrolysis, Redox

This question is about electrolysis. Concentrated hydrochloric acid is electrolysed using the apparatus shown.

(a) Chloride ions are discharged at the anode.

(i) Complete the ionic half-equation for this reaction. [2]
........Cl⁻(aq) → ........(g) + .......e⁻

2Cl⁻(aq) → Cl₂(g) + 2e⁻

Recap

At the anode (the positive electrode), negative anions are attracted. Here, chloride ions (Cl⁻) lose electrons to form a neutral, diatomic chlorine molecule (Cl₂). Since two chloride ions are needed to make one Cl₂ molecule, a total of two electrons are lost.

(ii) State whether oxidation or reduction takes place. Explain your answer. [1]

Oxidation takes place, because the chloride ions lose electrons.

Recap

A simple way to remember redox definitions is OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons), Reduction Is Gain (of electrons). Since electrons are lost at the anode, oxidation always occurs at the anode.

(b) Describe what is seen at the cathode. [1]

Bubbles of a colourless gas are seen (or effervescence).

Recap

At the cathode (the negative electrode), positive cations are attracted. In aqueous solutions of acids, hydrogen ions (H⁺) are present. These ions are discharged to form hydrogen gas (H₂), which is colourless and appears as bubbles.

(c) Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction at the cathode. [2]

2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂

Recap

At the cathode, reduction occurs (gain of electrons). Two hydrogen ions (H⁺) each gain one electron to form a neutral, diatomic hydrogen molecule (H₂).

(d) The pH of the electrolyte is measured throughout the experiment.

(i) Suggest the pH of the electrolyte at the beginning of the experiment. [1]

A pH of 1 (any value from 0-2 is acceptable).

Recap

Hydrochloric acid is a strong acid. A concentrated solution of a strong acid has a very high concentration of H⁺ ions, which corresponds to a very low pH on the pH scale.

(ii) State how the pH changes, if at all, during the experiment. Explain your answer. [2]

The pH will increase. This is because H⁺ ions, which cause the acidity, are being removed from the solution as they are converted to H₂ gas at the cathode.

Recap

The pH scale is a measure of the concentration of H⁺ ions.
• High [H⁺] = low pH (acidic)
• Low [H⁺] = high pH (alkaline)
As the electrolysis removes the H⁺ ions, the solution becomes less acidic, and the pH increases (moves closer to 7).

(e) The electrolysis is repeated using molten lead(II) bromide. Describe what is seen at the: [2]

cathode

anode

cathode: A silver/grey solid (molten lead) is formed.
anode: Bubbles of an orange/brown gas (bromine) are formed.

Recap

In the electrolysis of a molten binary compound (containing only two elements), there is no water.
• At the cathode, the positive metal ions (Pb²⁺) gain electrons to form the liquid metal (lead, Pb).
• At the anode, the negative non-metal ions (Br⁻) lose electrons to form the non-metal element (bromine, Br₂).

(f) State two properties of graphite (carbon) which make it suitable for use as an electrode. [2]

1. It conducts electricity.
2. It is inert (unreactive) and has a high melting point.

Recap

Graphite is a giant covalent structure. It is suitable as an electrode because:
1. It conducts electricity due to its delocalised electrons that can move along the layers.
2. It is inert and has a very high melting point, so it does not react with the electrolyte or melt during electrolysis.

Paper: 0620/41/O/N/21 Q4 Topic(s): Contact Process, Equilibrium

Chalcopyrite, FeCuS₂, is used in the manufacture of sulfuric acid in the Contact process.

(a) In the first stage of the process, chalcopyrite reacts with oxygen in the air to produce sulfur dioxide, SO₂, iron(III) oxide and copper(II) oxide.
Complete the chemical equation for the reaction of FeCuS₂ with oxygen. [2]
4FeCuS₂ + 13O₂ → ............. + ................ + ...............................

4FeCuS₂ + 13O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃ + 4CuO + 8SO₂

Recap

This is the first stage of the Contact process, where the sulfur-containing ore is roasted in air (oxygen). This balancing equation requires matching the number of atoms of each element on both sides:
• Fe: 4 on left → needs 2 Fe₂O₃ on right.
• Cu: 4 on left → needs 4 CuO on right.
• S: 4 x 2 = 8 on left → needs 8 SO₂ on right.
• O: 13 x 2 = 26 on left → (2x3) + (4x1) + (8x2) = 6 + 4 + 16 = 26 on right. It is balanced.

(b) Sulfur dioxide is then converted to sulfur trioxide.
2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃
The reaction is exothermic. It is also an equilibrium.

(i) State two features of an equilibrium. [2]

1. The rate of the forward reaction is equal to the rate of the reverse reaction.
2. The concentrations of reactants and products are constant.

Recap

A chemical equilibrium is a dynamic state that can only be reached in a closed system. It is defined by two key features: the rates of the forward and reverse reactions are equal, and as a result, the macroscopic properties (like concentration and pressure) remain constant.

(ii) State the temperature and pressure used in this reaction. Include units. [2]

temperature: 450°C
pressure: 1–2 atm (or 100–200 kPa)

Recap

These are the standard compromise conditions for the Contact process. A high pressure would favour the product (3 moles of gas → 2 moles of gas), but a very high yield is already achieved at low pressure (1-2 atm), so high pressure is not economically necessary.

(iii) Name the catalyst used. [1]

Vanadium(V) oxide (or V₂O₅).

Recap

The catalyst used in the Contact process to speed up the conversion of SO₂ to SO₃ is vanadium(V) oxide. Vanadium is a transition element, and its compounds are often effective catalysts.

(iv) Explain why a catalyst is used. [1]

A catalyst is used to increase the rate of reaction.

Recap

A catalyst increases the rate of both the forward and reverse reactions equally by providing an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy. It does not change the position of equilibrium, but it allows equilibrium to be reached much faster, which is more economical.

(v) Describe and explain, in terms of equilibrium, what happens when the temperature is increased. [2]

The equilibrium shifts to the left hand side (towards the reactants). This is because the forward reaction is exothermic, so the system opposes the temperature increase by favouring the endothermic (reverse) reaction.

Recap

This is an application of Le Chatelier's Principle. The equation shows ΔH is negative, meaning the forward reaction is exothermic (releases heat). If the temperature is increased, the equilibrium will shift to oppose this change. It will favour the direction that absorbs heat, which is the endothermic (reverse) reaction. This shifts the equilibrium to the left.

(c) Concentrated sulfuric acid is a dehydrating agent. When glucose is dehydrated, carbon and one other product are formed.
Complete the equation to show the dehydration of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆ [2]
C₆H₁₂O₆ → .........C + ..............................

A dehydrating agent is a substance that removes water from other compounds. Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) contains hydrogen and oxygen atoms in a 2:1 ratio, the same as in water (H₂O). The dehydrating agent removes 6 molecules of water, leaving behind 6 atoms of solid black carbon.

Alkenes and carboxylic acids are both families of similar compounds with similar chemical properties. Alkenes and carboxylic acids have different reactions.

(a) State the term used for a 'family' of similar compounds. [1]

The term is homologous series.

Recap

A homologous series is a family of similar compounds with similar chemical properties because they have the same functional group. They also share a general formula and show a trend in physical properties.

(b) State the general formula of alkenes. [1]

CₙH₂ₙ

Recap

The general formula for the alkene homologous series is CₙH₂ₙ, where 'n' is the number of carbon atoms. This formula reflects the presence of one C=C double bond.

from orange to colourless

Recap

This is the standard test for unsaturation. Alkenes, like but-2-ene, contain a C=C double bond. They react with aqueous bromine (which is orange) in an addition reaction, breaking the double bond to form a single, colourless product.

An addition reaction is one where two or more molecules react to form only one product.

In an addition reaction, the C=C double bond of the alkene breaks and opens up, allowing other atoms (like Br₂, H₂, or H₂O) to add across the bond. No other product is formed.

poly(but-2-ene)
