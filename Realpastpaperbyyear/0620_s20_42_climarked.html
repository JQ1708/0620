Topics: States of Matter, Separation, Periodic Table

(a) Give the name of the process that:

(i) occurs when a gas turns into a liquid [1]

This process is called condensation.

Recap

Condensation is the change in state from gas to liquid. This process releases energy as particles slow down and move closer together.

(ii) occurs when a solid turns into a gas without first forming a liquid [1]

This process is known as sublimation.

Recap

Changes of state involve energy. Melting and boiling (or evaporation) absorb energy. Condensation and freezing release energy. Sublimation is a direct change from solid to gas, which absorbs energy.

(iii) is used to separate a mixture of liquids with different boiling points [1]

This separation technique is called fractional distillation.

Recap

Fractional distillation is used to separate a mixture of liquids with boiling points which are close to each other. The liquid with the lower boiling point turns to a vapour first, moves up the fractionating column, condenses, and is collected.

(iv) is used to extract aluminium from aluminium oxide [1]

This process is electrolysis.

Recap

Electrolysis is the decomposition (breakdown) of an ionic compound when molten or in aqueous solution by the passage of an electric current. Reactive metals like aluminium are extracted from their ores using electrolysis.

(v) is used to separate a mixture of amino acids. [1]

This separation is done using chromatography.

Recap

Paper chromatography is used to separate a mixture of soluble coloured substances using a suitable solvent. For colourless substances, like amino acids, a locating agent is sprayed on the chromatogram after it is run to make the spots visible.

(a) Magnesium reacts with fluorine to form the ionic compound magnesium fluoride.

The electronic structures of an atom of magnesium and an atom of fluorine are shown.

(i) Complete the dot-and-cross diagrams to show the electronic structures of one magnesium ion and one fluoride ion. Show the charges on the ions. [3]

The magnesium atom (electronic structure 2.8.2) loses its two outer shell electrons to form a Mg²⁺ ion with a stable 2.8 structure. The fluorine atom (2.7) gains one electron into its outer shell to form a F⁻ ion with a stable 2.8 structure. The diagram for Mg²⁺ should show 2 electrons in the first shell and 8 in the second. The diagram for F⁻ should show 2 electrons in the first shell and 8 in the outer shell (e.g., 7 dots and 1 cross).

Recap

Ions are charged particles formed when atoms gain or lose electrons. Positive ions (cations) are formed when atoms (usually metals) lose electrons. Negative ions (anions) are formed when atoms (usually non-metals) gain electrons. An ionic bond is the electrostatic force of attraction between oppositely charged ions.

(ii) What is the formula of magnesium fluoride? [1]

The formula is MgF₂.

Recap

The formula of an ionic compound is the simplest whole number ratio of ions. It can be found by balancing the positive and negative charges. Magnesium (Group II) forms a Mg²⁺ ion. Fluorine (Group VII) forms a F⁻ ion. To balance the charges, two F⁻ ions are needed for every one Mg²⁺ ion, giving MgF₂.

(iii) Magnesium fluoride does not conduct electricity when it is solid.

What can be done to solid magnesium fluoride to make it conduct electricity?

In your answer explain why magnesium fluoride conducts electricity when this change is made. [2]

To make it conduct electricity, the magnesium fluoride must be melted (molten) or dissolved in water. This allows it to conduct because the ions, which are in fixed positions in the solid lattice, become mobile (free to move) and can carry the charge.

Recap

Ionic compounds conduct electricity only when molten or in aqueous solution. They do not conduct when solid because the ions are held in fixed positions in the giant ionic lattice and are not free to move. When molten or dissolved, the ions are mobile and can move to carry charge.

(b) Carbonyl fluoride, COF₂, is a covalent compound. The structure of a molecule of COF₂ is shown.

Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement in a molecule of carbonyl fluoride. Show outer shell electrons only. [3]

The completed diagram shows one double bond between the carbon and oxygen atoms (a pair of dots and a pair of crosses shared). It also shows a single bond between the carbon and each fluorine atom (one dot and one cross shared). The oxygen atom has four non-bonding electrons (2 lone pairs), and each fluorine atom has six non-bonding electrons (3 lone pairs) to complete their octets.

Recap

A covalent bond is formed when atoms share a pair of electrons. A double covalent bond is formed when two pairs of electrons are shared between two atoms. Atoms share electrons to achieve a stable noble gas electron configuration (usually a full outer shell).

(i) Explain, using your knowledge of structure and bonding, why magnesium fluoride has a high melting point. [2]

Magnesium fluoride has a giant ionic lattice structure. There are strong electrostatic forces of attraction (ionic bonds) between the oppositELY charged ions (Mg²⁺ and F⁻). A lot of energy is needed to overcome these strong forces, resulting in a high melting point.

Ionic compounds have high melting and boiling points. This is because they form a giant ionic lattice held together by strong ionic bonds (electrostatic forces) which require a large amount of thermal energy to break.

(ii) Explain, using your knowledge of structure and bonding, why carbonyl fluoride has a low melting point. [2]

Carbonyl fluoride (COF₂) has a simple molecular structure. While the covalent bonds within the molecules are strong, the forces of attraction between the molecules (intermolecular forces) are weak. Recap

Simple molecular structures have low melting and boiling points. This is not because the covalent bonds are weak, but because the intermolecular forces (the forces between molecules) are weak and require very little energy to overcome.

Topics: Sulfuric Acid, Contact Process, Equilibrium, Salts

(a) Sulfuric acid is made from sulfur in a four-stage process.

stage 1 Sulfur is converted into sulfur dioxide.

stage 2 Sulfur dioxide is converted into sulfur trioxide.

stage 3 Sulfur trioxide is converted into oleum.

stage 4 Oleum is converted into sulfuric acid.

(i) How is sulfur converted into sulfur dioxide in stage 1? [1]

Sulfur is heated or burned in air (or oxygen).

Recap

Sulfur (S) is a non-metal. It reacts with oxygen (O₂) when burned to form sulfur dioxide (SO₂). This is the first step in the Contact process. S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g)

(ii) Describe how sulfur dioxide is converted into sulfur trioxide in stage 2.

Your answer should include:

  • an equation for the reaction
  • the temperature used
  • the name of the catalyst used.
[3]

Equation: 2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃ (or 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g))

Temperature: 450°C

Catalyst: Vanadium(V) oxide

Recap

This is the key stage of the Contact process. The essential conditions are:

  • Temperature: 450°C (a compromise temperature)
  • Pressure: 200 kPa (or 2 atm)
  • Catalyst: Vanadium(V) oxide (V₂O₅)

(iii) The reaction in stage 2 can reach equilibrium.

What is meant by the term equilibrium? [2]

Equilibrium is reached in a reversible reaction when the rate of the forward reaction is equal to the rate of the backward reaction. At this point, the concentrations of the reactants and products remain constant (in a closed system).

Recap

An equilibrium reaction (shown by the ⇌ symbol) is one where reactants form products at the same rate as products re-form reactants. This can only happen in a closed system. The position of equilibrium indicates the relative amounts of reactants and products.

(b) Sulfur trioxide is converted into oleum, H₂S₂O₇, in stage 3.

What is sulfur trioxide reacted with to convert it into oleum? [1]

Sulfur trioxide is reacted with concentrated sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄).

Recap

In the Contact process, sulfur trioxide is not added directly to water as the reaction is too violent and creates a hazardous fog. Instead, it is dissolved in concentrated sulfuric acid to form oleum (H₂S₂O₇). SO₃ + H₂SO₄ → H₂S₂O₇

(c) Oleum is converted into sulfuric acid in stage 4.

Write a chemical equation for the conversion of oleum, H₂S₂O₇, into sulfuric acid. [2]

The chemical equation is H₂S₂O₇ + H₂O → 2H₂SO₄.

Recap

The oleum (H₂S₂O₇) produced in stage 3 is then safely diluted with water to produce concentrated sulfuric acid. This two-step process (SO₃ → oleum → H₂SO₄) is safer than reacting SO₃ directly with water.

(d) When copper is reacted with hot concentrated sulfuric acid, sulfur dioxide gas is formed.

Balance the chemical equation for this reaction. [1]

Cu + ........H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + SO₂ + ........H₂O

The balanced equation is Cu + 2H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + SO₂ + 2H₂O.

Recap

This reaction shows that hot, concentrated sulfuric acid acts as an oxidising agent (it oxidises Cu to Cu²⁺ and is itself reduced to SO₂). This is different from a typical acid-metal reaction, as copper is below hydrogen in the reactivity series and does not react with *dilute* acids.

(e) Sulfur dioxide is a reducing agent.

starting colour of the solution ...............................................

Recap

Acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII) (KMnO₄) is a strong oxidising agent and has a characteristic purple colour. When it reacts with a reducing agent (like SO₂), the manganate(VII) ion is reduced (to Mn²⁺), which is colourless. This is a common test for reducing agents.

(f) When sulfuric acid reacts with ammonia the salt produced is ammonium sulfate.

Write the chemical equation for this reaction. [2]

The chemical equation is H₂SO₄ + 2NH₃ → (NH₄)₂SO₄.

Recap

This is an acid-base reaction. Sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) is an acid, and ammonia (NH₃) is a base. They react in a neutralisation reaction to form a salt, ammonium sulfate. Note that water is not produced in the reaction between an acid and *ammonia gas* (only when reacting with *ammonium hydroxide*).

(g) Barium sulfate is an insoluble salt.

Barium sulfate can be made from aqueous ammonium sulfate using a precipitation reaction.

(i) Name a solution that can be added to aqueous ammonium sulfate to produce a precipitate of barium sulfate. [1]

A solution of barium chloride or barium nitrate.

Recap

An insoluble salt is made by a precipitation reaction. This involves mixing two soluble salts. To make insoluble barium sulfate (BaSO₄), you need one soluble salt containing barium ions (like barium nitrate, Ba(NO₃)₂, as all nitrates are soluble) and one soluble salt containing sulfate ions (like ammonium sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄, as all ammonium salts are soluble).

(ii) Write an ionic equation for this precipitation reaction. Include state symbols. [2]

The ionic equation is Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s).

Recap

An ionic equation shows only the ions that take part in the reaction. The ammonium (NH₄⁺) and nitrate (NO₃⁻) ions are spectator ions; they are present in the solution but do not react, so they are omitted from the ionic equation. State symbols are required: (aq) for aqueous ions and (s) for the solid precipitate.

Topics: Rates of Reaction, Collision Theory, Moles

Oxygen is produced by the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide. Manganese(IV) oxide is the catalyst for this reaction.

(a) What is meant by the term catalyst? [2]

A catalyst is a substance that speeds up a reaction (increases the rate of reaction) but remains chemically unchanged at the end of the reaction. It works by providing an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy.

Recap

A catalyst is a substance which increases the rate of a chemical reaction but is not used up in the reaction. It does this by lowering the activation energy (Eₐ), which means a greater proportion of collisions will be successful.

(b) A student measures the volume of oxygen produced at regular time intervals using the apparatus shown. Large lumps of manganese(IV) oxide are used.

A graph of the results is shown.

What happens to the rate of this reaction as time increases?

In your answer, explain why the rate changes in this way. [4]

The rate of reaction decreases as time increases (the curve becomes less steep). This is because the concentration of the reactant (hydrogen peroxide) decreases as it is used up. According to collision theory, this leads to fewer collisions per unit time (a lower collision frequency) between reactant particles. The reaction eventually stops when all the hydrogen peroxide is used up (the limiting reactant).

Recap

The rate of reaction is fastest at the beginning (steepest gradient) and decreases as the reaction proceeds. This is because the concentration of reactants decreases, leading to a lower collision frequency. The reaction stops when the limiting reactant is fully consumed, and the graph becomes horizontal.

(c) The experiment is repeated using the same mass of manganese(IV) oxide. Powdered manganese(IV) oxide is used instead of large lumps. All other conditions stay the same.

Sketch a graph on the axes in (b) to show how the volume of oxygen changes with time. [2]

The new graph line sketched on the axes would start at the origin (0,0), be steeper than the original line (showing a faster rate), and then level off at the same final volume of oxygen (as the amount of reactant is the same).

Recap

Using a powder instead of lumps increases the total surface area of the solid catalyst. This leads to an increased frequency of collisions between the reactant particles and the catalyst, resulting in a faster rate of reaction.

(d) In terms of particles, explain what happens to the rate of this reaction when the temperature is increased. [3]

When the temperature is increased, the particles gain kinetic energy and move faster. This leads to two important effects:

  1. Particles collide more frequently.
  2. A greater number (or proportion) of particles will have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy.
This means that more collisions are successful (or fruitful), which increases the rate of reaction.

Recap

Increasing the temperature increases the rate of reaction. According to collision theory, this is mainly because particles have more kinetic energy, so a greater proportion of collisions have energy above the activation energy (Eₐ), making them effective. The increased collision frequency is a minor factor.

(e) The equation for the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide is shown.

2H₂O₂(aq) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g)

25.0 cm³ of aqueous hydrogen peroxide forms 48.0 cm³ of oxygen at room temperature and pressure (r.t.p.).

Calculate the concentration of aqueous hydrogen peroxide using the following steps.

  • Calculate the number of moles of oxygen formed.
  • Deduce the number of moles of hydrogen peroxide that decomposed.
  • Calculate the concentration of hydrogen peroxide in mol/dm³.
[3]

Moles of oxygen formed:
Volume of O₂ = 48.0 cm³. The molar gas volume at r.t.p. is 24000 cm³.
Moles = Volume / Molar Volume = 48.0 / 24000 = 0.002 mol.

Moles of hydrogen peroxide decomposed:
From the equation, the mole ratio of H₂O₂ : O₂ is 2:1.
Therefore, moles of H₂O₂ = Moles of O₂ x 2 = 0.002 x 2 = 0.004 mol.

Concentration of hydrogen peroxide:
Volume of H₂O₂ = 25.0 cm³ = 0.025 dm³ (since 1000 cm³ = 1 dm³).
Concentration = Moles / Volume (in dm³) = 0.004 / 0.025 = 0.16 mol/dm³.

Recap

One mole of any gas occupies 24 dm³ (or 24000 cm³) at room temperature and pressure (r.t.p.).
Moles of gas = Volume of gas (in cm³) / 24000
Concentration (mol/dm³) = Moles of solute / Volume of solution (in dm³)

Write a chemical equation for this decomposition. [2]

The balanced chemical equation is 2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂.

Topics: Electrolysis

Electrolysis of concentrated aqueous sodium chloride using inert electrodes forms chlorine, hydrogen and sodium hydroxide.

(a) What is meant by the term electrolysis? [2]

Electrolysis is the breakdown (decomposition) of an ionic compound, when molten or in aqueous solution, by the passage of electricity (an electric current).

Recap

An electrolyte is a molten or aqueous ionic compound that conducts electricity and is decomposed by it. Electrodes are the rods (often graphite or platinum) that carry the current into and out of the electrolyte.

(b) Name a substance that can be used as the inert electrodes. [1]

A suitable substance is graphite (a form of carbon) or platinum.

Recap

Inert electrodes are used when you do not want the electrode itself to react with the electrolyte or the products of electrolysis. Graphite is a common choice as it is a non-metal that conducts electricity and is relatively unreactive.

(c) Write an ionic half-equation for the formation of hydrogen during this electrolysis. [1]

The ionic half-equation is 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂.

Recap

Hydrogen gas is formed at the cathode (the negative electrode). Positive hydrogen ions (H⁺) from the water are attracted to the cathode, where they gain electrons. This gain of electrons is called reduction.

(d) Give the formulae of the four ions present in concentrated aqueous sodium chloride. [2]

The four ions are:

  • Na⁺ (from sodium chloride)
  • Cl⁻ (from sodium chloride)
  • H⁺ (from water)
  • OH⁻ (from water)

Recap

When an ionic compound dissolves in water, its ions are free to move. Water itself also partially dissociates (ionises) to a small extent, forming H⁺ and OH⁻ ions (H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻). Therefore, an aqueous solution always contains these four types of ions.

(e) Explain how sodium hydroxide is formed during this electrolysis. [2]

In the electrolysis, H⁺ ions are discharged at the cathode (forming H₂) and Cl⁻ ions are discharged at the anode (forming Cl₂). These ions are therefore removed from the solution. The Na⁺ and OH⁻ ions are left behind (remain) in the solution, forming aqueous sodium hydroxide (NaOH).

Recap

In the electrolysis of aqueous solutions, the product formed at each electrode depends on the relative reactivity of the ions. At the cathode, hydrogen is produced in preference to reactive metals (like sodium). At the anode (in concentrated solutions), a halide (like chloride) is produced in preference to oxygen.

(a) Propane reacts with chlorine in a photochemical reaction as shown.

Alkanes are generally unreactive, but they undergo substitution reactions with halogens. In this reaction, a hydrogen atom from the alkane is replaced (or substituted) by a chlorine atom.

(ii) What condition is needed for this photochemical reaction to occur? [1]

The condition needed is ultraviolet (UV) light.

A photochemical reaction is a reaction that is initiated by light. The ultraviolet light provides the activation energy needed to break the Cl-Cl bonds in the chlorine molecules, starting the reaction.

(iii) Draw two structural isomers of compounds with the formula C₃H₇Cl. Show all of the atoms and all of the bonds. [2]

The two structural isomers are 1-chloropropane (where the Cl atom is on an end carbon) and 2-chloropropane (where the Cl atom is on the middle carbon).

Recap

Structural isomers are compounds that have the same molecular formula (e.g., C₃H₇Cl) but different structural formulae (the atoms are arranged in a different way).

(b) Propene reacts with chlorine in an addition reaction as shown.

C₃H₆ + Cl₂ → C₃H₆Cl₂

(i) State why this is an addition reaction. [1]

It is an addition reaction because two molecules (propene and chlorine) join together to form only one product. This happens because the double bond in propene breaks and becomes a single bond, allowing the chlorine atoms to add across it.

Recap

An addition reaction is a reaction in which two or more compounds react to form only one product. Alkenes (which are unsaturated) undergo addition reactions, while alkanes (which are saturated) do not.

(ii) The structures of the reactants and products of this reaction are shown.

Some bond energies are shown in the table.

bondbond energy in kJ/mol
C–C347
C=C612
C–H413
C–Cl339
Cl–Cl242

Calculate the energy change for the reaction between propene and chlorine using the following steps.

  • Calculate the energy needed to break the bonds.
  • Calculate the energy released when bonds are formed.
  • Calculate the energy change for the reaction between propene and chlorine.
[3]

Energy needed to break bonds (Endothermic):
In this reaction, the C=C bond breaks and a Cl-Cl bond breaks. (The C-H and C-C single bonds are unchanged, so they don't need to be included in both sides of the calculation).
1 x (C=C) = 612 kJ
1 x (Cl-Cl) = 242 kJ
Total energy in = 612 + 242 = 854 kJ.

Energy released when bonds are formed (Exothermic):
A new C-C single bond is formed, and two new C-Cl bonds are formed.
1 x (C-C) = 347 kJ
2 x (C-Cl) = 2 x 339 = 678 kJ
Total energy out = 347 + 678 = 1025 kJ.

Overall energy change:
Energy change (ΔH) = Energy in (broken) − Energy out (formed)
ΔH = 854 − 1025 = –171 kJ/mol.

Recap

Bond breaking is an endothermic process (energy is absorbed, so the value is positive). Bond making is an exothermic process (energy is released, so the value is negative). The overall enthalpy change (ΔH) for a reaction is calculated as:
ΔH = Sum of (bond energies of bonds broken) − Sum of (bond energies of bonds formed)
A negative final value indicates an exothermic reaction.

(d) Compound A can be used as a single monomer to produce two different polymers.

The addition polymer forms by breaking the C=C double bond. The repeat unit structure would be –[CH(COOH)–CH(CH₂OH)]– with extension bonds shown at each end.
