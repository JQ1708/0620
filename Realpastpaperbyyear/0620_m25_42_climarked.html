Using numbers only, state the:

(a) percentage of oxygen in clean, dry air [1]

The percentage of oxygen in clean, dry air is 21%.

Recap

Clean, dry air contains 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, about 1% argon and 0.04% carbon dioxide.

(b) typical operating temperature, in °C, used in the Haber process [1]

The typical operating temperature used in the Haber process is 450 °C.

Recap

The conditions for the Haber process (used to make ammonia) are 20 000 kPa (200 atm) pressure, 450 °C, and an iron catalyst. A compromise temperature is used to balance reaction rate and equilibrium yield.

(c) number of atoms in a diatomic molecule [1]

The number of atoms in a diatomic molecule is 2.

Recap

The Group VII elements (halogens) are examples of elements that exist as diatomic molecules (e.g., Cl₂, Br₂). Other diatomic elements include hydrogen (H₂), nitrogen (N₂), and oxygen (O₂).

(d) maximum number of electrons in the second electron shell of an atom [1]

The maximum number of electrons in the second electron shell of an atom is 8.

Recap

Electronic configuration is the arrangement of the electrons in shells. The maximum number of electrons in the first shell is two. For elements up to calcium, the second and third shells have a maximum of eight electrons.

(e) number of hydrogen atoms in an alkane with 7 carbon atoms [1]

The number of hydrogen atoms is 16.

Recap

A general formula can be written for each homologous series. For alkanes, the general formula is CₙH₂ₙ₊₂. If n = 7, then the number of hydrogen atoms is (2 x 7) + 2 = 16.

(f) number of particles in one mole, in standard form. [1]

The number of particles in one mole is 6.02 x 10²³.

Recap

A mole is the amount of substance that contains 6.02 x 10²³ defined particles (such as atoms, molecules, or ions). This specific number is known as the Avogadro constant.

Topics: Ionic Bonding, Electrolysis

This question is about ionic compounds.

(a) State what is meant by the term ionic bond. [2]

An ionic bond is the strong electrostatic attraction between ions that have opposite charges.

Recap

An ionic bond is the electrostatic force of attraction between positive and negative ions. Positive ions (cations) are formed when atoms, usually metals, lose one or more electrons. Negative ions (anions) are formed when atoms, usually non-metals, gain one or more electrons.

(b) Potassium sulfide, K₂S, is an ionic compound.

Complete the dot-and-cross diagram in Fig. 2.1 of the ions in potassium sulfide.

Show the charges on the ions. [3]

The completed diagram should show:

  • Two potassium ions, each with a charge of '+'. Each K⁺ ion should have 8 electrons in its third shell (e.g., 8 dots), having lost its original single outer electron.
  • One sulfide ion, with a charge of '2-'. The S²⁻ ion should have 8 electrons in its third shell (e.g., 6 crosses and 2 dots), showing it has gained two electrons.

Recap

Dot-and-cross diagrams show the electronic configuration for compounds. When drawing the electronic configurations of ions, remember to put the charge at the top right of the ion, just outside the square brackets. Each ion in a simple ionic structure achieves the noble gas configuration (a full outer shell).

(c) Ionic compounds form giant ionic lattices.

(i) Fig. 2.2 shows part of the giant ionic lattice structure of sodium chloride.

Complete the diagram in Fig. 2.2 to show the ions present. Use '+' for sodium ions and '–' for chloride ions. One chloride ion has been completed for you. [2]

The diagram should be completed to show a regular, alternating arrangement of positive '+' ions (sodium) and negative '–' ions (chloride) in all three dimensions. Each '+' ion should be surrounded by '–' ions, and each '–' ion by '+' ions.

Recap

An ionic lattice is a regular arrangement of alternating positive and negative ions in three dimensions. This giant structure is held together by strong ionic bonds, which is why ionic compounds have high melting and boiling points.

(ii) State the name given to any positive ion. [1]

A positive ion is called a cation.

Recap

During electrolysis, cations (positive ions) move to the cathode (the negative electrode). Anions (negative ions) move to the anode (the positive electrode).

(d) Ionic compounds can be decomposed by the passage of an electric current using inert electrodes.

(i) State the name of this process. [1]

This process is called electrolysis.

Recap

Electrolysis is the decomposition (breakdown) of an ionic compound, when molten or in an aqueous solution, by the passage of an electric current. The substance being decomposed is called the electrolyte.

(ii) Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction which takes place at the anode when molten potassium bromide, KBr, is decomposed by the passage of an electric current. [2]

2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻

Recap

In electrolysis, negative ions (anions) move to the anode (positive electrode) and lose electrons. This process is oxidation. Positive ions (cations) move to the cathode (negative electrode) and gain electrons. This is reduction. Ionic half-equations are used to show this electron transfer.

(iii) Name the products and state the observations at the negative and positive electrodes when dilute aqueous potassium bromide, KBr, is decomposed by the passage of an electric current. [5]

  • product at the negative electrode: hydrogen
  • observations at the negative electrode: bubbles (of gas)
  • products at the positive electrode: oxygen and water
  • observations at the positive electrode: bubbles (of gas)

Recap

In the electrolysis of dilute aqueous solutions, water molecules also ionise (H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻). At the cathode, hydrogen is discharged (2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂) because potassium is more reactive than hydrogen. At the anode, oxygen is discharged (4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻) because the solution is dilute and the halide (Br⁻) ion concentration is low.

Topics: Group VII (Halogens), Redox

The halogens are a group of elements in the Periodic Table.

Chlorine is a member of this group.

(a) State the group number of the halogens. [1]

The group number is VII (or 17).

Recap

The Group VII elements in the Periodic Table are known as the halogens.

(b) State how many halogens there are in this group. [1]

There are 6 halogens in this group (Fluorine, Chlorine, Bromine, Iodine, Astatine, Tennessine).

Recap

Elements in the same group of the Periodic Table have similar chemical properties because their atoms have the same number of electrons in their outer shell. For Group VII, this is 7 outer electrons.

(c) Suggest the identity of the halogen which:

(i) has the highest density [1]

The halogen with the highest density is tennessine (Ts).

Recap

There is a trend in the physical properties of halogens. The density, melting point, and boiling point all increase down the group as the molecules get larger.

(ii) is the most reactive. [1]

The most reactive halogen is fluorine (F).

Recap

The reactivity of the halogens decreases down the group. This is because atoms higher in the group can gain an electron more easily to complete their outer shell.

(d) State the name of the negative ions (anions) formed by halogens. [1]

The negative ions formed by halogens are called halides (e.g., chloride, bromide).

Recap

Halides are salts of the halogens. In ionic compounds, the ending of the non-metal part changes from -ine to -ide (e.g., chlorine becomes chloride).

(e) State how many occupied electron shells there are in a bromine atom. [1]

A bromine atom has 4 occupied electron shells.

Recap

The number of occupied electron shells in an atom is equal to the period number. Bromine (Br) is in Period 4 of the Periodic Table.

(f) Name the noble gas which has the same electronic configuration as a Br⁻ ion. [1]

The noble gas is krypton.

Recap

When atoms form ions, they lose or gain electrons to achieve a stable electronic configuration, which is usually a full outer shell. This is the same electronic configuration as the nearest noble gas.

(g) Aqueous chlorine, Cl₂, reacts with aqueous potassium iodide, KI.

One of the products formed is iodine, I₂.

(i) Complete and balance the ionic equation for the reaction between Cl₂ and I⁻ ions. State symbols are not required. [2]

Cl₂ + .....I⁻ → .................... + I₂

Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂

Recap

This is a displacement reaction. A more reactive halogen (chlorine) will displace a less reactive halogen (iodine) from a solution of its halide (iodide ions).

(ii) Explain why this reaction is defined as a redox reaction.

Give your answer in terms of electron transfer. [2]

This is a redox reaction because electrons are transferred. The iodide ions (I⁻) are oxidised because they lose electrons to form iodine. The chlorine molecule (Cl₂) is reduced because it gains electrons to form chloride ions.

Recap

Redox reactions are those where oxidation and reduction take place at the same time. A useful mnemonic is OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons), Reduction Is Gain (of electrons).

(h) Give the colour and state of iodine at room temperature and pressure. [2]

  • colour: grey-black
  • state: solid

Recap

At r.t.p. chlorine is a yellow-green gas, bromine is a red-brown liquid, and iodine is a grey-black solid. This shows a trend of increasing melting and boiling points down the group.

Topics: Reversible Reactions, Energetics, Equilibrium, Bond Energies

Carbonyl chloride, COCl₂, is manufactured by reacting carbon monoxide with chlorine.

CO(g) + Cl₂(g) ⇌ COCl₂(g)

ΔH = –105 kJ / mol

The process takes place in a closed system, and an equilibrium is reached.

The conditions for this process are 200 °C and 200 kPa.

(a) Explain what is meant by the term closed system. [1]

A closed system is one where no reactants or products can enter or leave.

Recap

A closed system is one where there is no loss or gain of matter from the surroundings. This is essential for a reversible reaction to reach equilibrium.

(b) State what the symbol ΔH represents. [1]

The symbol ΔH represents the enthalpy change (of the reaction).

Recap

Enthalpy change, ΔH, is the thermal energy change which takes place during a chemical reaction at constant pressure.

(c) State how the value of ΔH shows that the forward reaction is exothermic. [1]

The forward reaction is exothermic because the value of ΔH is negative.

Recap

An exothermic reaction releases thermal energy to the surroundings and has a negative enthalpy change (ΔH < 0). An endothermic reaction absorbs thermal energy from the surroundings and has a positive enthalpy change (ΔH > 0).

(d) Deduce the value of ΔH for the reverse reaction. Include a sign in your answer. [1]

The value of ΔH for the reverse reaction is +105 kJ / mol.

Recap

In reversible reactions (shown by the ⇌ symbol), the enthalpy change for the reverse reaction is equal in magnitude but opposite in sign to the forward reaction. If the forward reaction is exothermic (negative), the reverse must be endothermic (positive).

(e) Complete Table 4.1 to show the effect, if any, on the concentration of COCl₂(g) at equilibrium when the following changes to the conditions are applied.

Use only the words increases, decreases or no change. [4]

change to conditions effect on the concentration of COCl₂(g) at equilibrium
the temperature is increased
some CO is added
the pressure is increased
a catalyst is added
change to conditions effect on the concentration of COCl₂(g) at equilibrium
the temperature is increaseddecreases
some CO is addedincreases
the pressure is increasedincreases
a catalyst is addedno change

Recap

Le Chatelier's Principle states that if a change is made to a system at equilibrium, the position of equilibrium will shift to oppose the change.

  • Temperature: The forward reaction is exothermic (ΔH = –105). Increasing the temperature favours the endothermic (reverse) reaction, so equilibrium shifts left, decreasing the yield of COCl₂.
  • Concentration: Increasing the concentration of a reactant (CO) will cause the equilibrium to shift right to use up the added reactant, increasing the yield of product (COCl₂).
  • Pressure: There are 2 moles of gas on the left (1 CO + 1 Cl₂) and 1 mole of gas on the right (1 COCl₂). Increasing the pressure will shift the equilibrium to the side with fewer moles of gas (the right), increasing the yield of COCl₂.
  • Catalyst: A catalyst increases the rate of both the forward and reverse reactions equally. It does not affect the position of equilibrium, it only allows equilibrium to be reached faster.

⁵⁵Mn ⁴²Ca²⁺

protons2520

Recap

  • Proton number (atomic number) is the number of protons in the nucleus. (Mn=25, Ca=20)
  • Mass number (nucleon number) is the sum of protons and neutrons.
  • Number of neutrons = Mass number – Proton number. (For Mn: 55 - 25 = 30. For Ca: 42 - 20 = 22).
  • In a neutral atom, the number of electrons equals the number of protons. (For Mn: 25).
  • In a positive ion (cation), electrons have been lost. A Ca²⁺ ion has lost 2 electrons. (For Ca²⁺: 20 - 2 = 18).

(b) Manganese forms several oxides. The formulae of some of these oxides are shown.

MnO, Mn₂O₃, Mn₃O₄, MnO₂, Mn₂O₇

(i) Suggest why manganese is expected to form coloured oxides. [1]

Manganese is a transition element, and transition elements typically form coloured compounds.

Recap

Transition elements (the block of metals between Groups II and III) have characteristic properties, including high densities, high melting points, acting as catalysts, and forming coloured compounds.

(ii) State which other property of manganese is shown by the formation of several oxides. [1]

This shows that manganese has variable oxidation states.

Recap

Another key property of transition elements is their ability to form ions with variable oxidation states (e.g., Mn(II) in MnO, Mn(IV) in MnO₂, Mn(VII) in Mn₂O₇).

(iii) State the formula of manganese(II) oxide. [1]

MnO

Recap

The Roman numeral (II) indicates the oxidation number (or charge) of the metal ion. Manganese(II) is Mn²⁺. The oxide ion is O²⁻. To form a neutral compound, one Mn²⁺ ion balances one O²⁻ ion, giving the formula MnO.

(c) Mn₃O₄ is found in an ore of manganese. Manganese metal can be extracted from Mn₃O₄ using aluminium as the reducing agent.

(i) Define the term reducing agent. [2]

A reducing agent is a substance that reduces another substance and is itself oxidised in the process.

Recap

In terms of electrons, a reducing agent gives electrons to another substance. In terms of oxygen, it often removes oxygen from another substance. In terms of oxidation number, it causes a decrease in the oxidation number of another substance.

(ii) Complete the symbol equation by inserting the formula of the missing product and balancing the equation. [2]

.....Mn₃O₄ + .....Al → ................... + .....Mn

3Mn₃O₄ + 8Al → 4Al₂O₃ + 9Mn

Recap

This is a metal displacement reaction (a type of redox reaction) based on the reactivity series. Aluminium is more reactive than manganese, so it can displace manganese from its oxide. Aluminium (the reducing agent) is oxidised to aluminium oxide.

(d) MnO₂ reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid as shown in the equation.

MnO₂ + 4HCl → MnCl₂ + 2H₂O + Cl₂

(i) Calculate the volume of chlorine gas formed, in cm³, at r.t.p. when excess MnO₂ reacts with 50.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol / dm³ HCl.

Use the following steps.

Calculate the number of moles of HCl used.

........................................................... mol

Determine the number of moles of Cl₂ formed.

........................................................... mol

Calculate the volume of Cl₂ formed.

........................................................... cm³ [3]

Calculate the number of moles of HCl used.
Volume in dm³ = 50.0 / 1000 = 0.0500 dm³
Moles = Concentration x Volume = 0.200 mol/dm³ x 0.0500 dm³ = 0.0100 mol

Determine the number of moles of Cl₂ formed.
From the equation, the mole ratio of HCl to Cl₂ is 4 : 1.
Moles of Cl₂ = Moles of HCl / 4 = 0.0100 / 4 = 0.00250 mol

Recap

This calculation requires three steps:

  1. Find moles of the known substance: Number of moles = Concentration (in mol/dm³) x Volume (in dm³).
  2. Use stoichiometry (the mole ratio from the balanced equation) to find the moles of the target substance.
  3. Convert moles of gas to volume: Volume of gas (in dm³) = moles of gas x 24. (Note: 24 dm³ = 24000 cm³).

(ii) Describe a test for chlorine gas.

test ...........................................................

observations ........................................................... [1]

test: Place damp litmus paper (blue or red) in the gas.

observations: The litmus paper is bleached (turns white).

Recap

Gases have specific chemical tests:

  • Ammonia (NH₃): Turns damp red litmus paper blue.
  • Carbon dioxide (CO₂): Turns limewater milky (cloudy).
  • Chlorine (Cl₂): Bleaches damp litmus paper.
  • Hydrogen (H₂): Gives a 'squeaky pop' with a lighted splint.
  • Oxygen (O₂): Relights a glowing splint.

(iii) Explain, in terms of collision theory, why decreasing the temperature decreases the rate of this reaction. [3]

Decreasing the temperature causes the particles to have less kinetic energy and move more slowly. This leads to two effects:

  1. The frequency of collisions between reactant particles decreases.
  2. A lower percentage (or proportion) of these collisions will have energy greater than or equal to the activation energy, so fewer collisions will be successful.

Recap

Collision theory explains reaction rates. For a reaction to occur, particles must collide with the correct orientation and with sufficient energy. This minimum energy is called the activation energy (Eₐ). Increasing the temperature increases the kinetic energy of particles, leading to more frequent and more energetic collisions, thus increasing the reaction rate.
