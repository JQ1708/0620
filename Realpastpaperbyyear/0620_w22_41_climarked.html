Ionic compounds form giant ionic lattices. (ii) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement and charges of the ions in potassium sulfide. [3]

Recap

Ionic bonds form between metals and non-metals. Positive ions are formed when metal atoms lose electrons. Negative ions are formed when non-metal atoms gain electrons. Both ions achieve a stable, noble gas electron configuration (a full outer shell).

(iv) Calculate the volume, in cm³, of hydrogen gas formed when 2.34 g of potassium is added to excess water at room temperature and pressure. [3]

Use the following steps.

Calculate the number of moles of potassium added.

Determine the number of moles of hydrogen gas formed.

Calculate the volume of hydrogen gas formed.

Step 1: Moles of K (Aᵣ of K = 39)

Moles = Mass / Aᵣ = 2.34 g / 39 = 0.0600 mol

Step 2: Moles of H₂ (from equation 2K → 1H₂)

Mole ratio K : H₂ is 2 : 1

Topics: Haber Process, Reversible Reactions, Rates of Reaction, Chemical Equilibrium (Le Chatelier's Principle)

Ammonia is made in an industrial process starting with nitrogen. The equation for the reaction is shown.

N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃

(a) Name the industrial process used to make ammonia. [1]

Haber (process)

Recap

The Haber process is the industrial process used to manufacture ammonia (NH₃) from its elements, nitrogen and hydrogen.

(b) State the raw material from which nitrogen is obtained. [1]

air

Recap

The raw materials for the Haber process are nitrogen and hydrogen. Nitrogen is obtained from the air (which is ~78% nitrogen). Hydrogen is typically produced from methane (natural gas) reacting with steam.

(c) State what is meant by the symbol ⇌. [1]

The reaction is reversible.

Recap

The ⇌ symbol indicates a reversible reaction, meaning the products (ammonia) can decompose back into the reactants (nitrogen and hydrogen). When the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the backward reaction, a state of equilibrium is reached.

(d) State the temperature and pressure used in this industrial process. [2]

temperature = ................................. °C

pressure = ................................. atm

temperature = 450 °C

pressure = 200 atm (or 20,000 kPa)

Recap

The essential conditions for the Haber process are a temperature of 450 °C, a pressure of 20,000 kPa (or 200 atm), and an iron catalyst. These are compromise conditions to balance the rate of reaction and the equilibrium yield.

(e) Name the catalyst used in this industrial process. [1]

Iron (or Fe)

Recap

A catalyst is a substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction but is not used up. The catalyst used in the Haber process is iron.

(f) The forward reaction is exothermic. State the effect, if any, on the position of the equilibrium when the following changes are made. Explain your answers. [4]

temperature is reduced

pressure is reduced

Temperature is reduced:

The position of equilibrium moves to the right-hand side (products). The system tries to oppose the change by favouring the exothermic (heat-releasing) reaction, which is the forward reaction.

Pressure is reduced:

The position of equilibrium moves to the left-hand side (reactants). The system tries to oppose the change by favouring the side with more (gaseous) moles. There are 4 moles of gas on the left (1 N₂ + 3 H₂) and only 2 moles of gas on the right (2 NH₃).

Recap

Le Chatelier's Principle states that if a change is made to an equilibrium, the system will shift to oppose the change.

  • Temperature: Increasing temperature favours the endothermic direction. Decreasing temperature favours the exothermic direction.
  • Pressure: Increasing pressure favours the side with fewer moles of gas. Decreasing pressure favours the side with more moles of gas.

(g) Explain, in terms of particles, what happens to the rate of reaction when the temperature is reduced. [3]

When the temperature is reduced, the rate decreases. This is because the particles have less kinetic energy and move more slowly. This leads to fewer collisions per second. Furthermore, a smaller proportion of these collisions will have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy, meaning fewer collisions are successful.

Recap

According to collision theory, increasing temperature increases the rate of reaction for two reasons: 1. Particles gain kinetic energy and move faster, leading to more frequent collisions. 2. A greater proportion of these collisions have energy equal to or exceeding the activation energy (Eₐ), making them effective. Reducing the temperature has the opposite effect on both factors.

(h) Give the formula of the compound formed when sulfuric acid reacts with ammonia. [1]

(NH₄)₂SO₄

Recap

Ammonia (NH₃) is a base and reacts with acids in a neutralisation reaction to form ammonium salts. Sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) is a dibasic acid, so it provides two H⁺ ions. Each ammonia molecule accepts one H⁺ to become an ammonium ion (NH₄⁺). Therefore, two ammonia molecules are needed to form ammonium sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄.

addition

Recap

An addition reaction is one where two or more molecules join together to form a single product. This is characteristic of unsaturated compounds like alkenes, as the double bond breaks to form new single bonds.

(d) Part of the energy profile diagram of this reaction is shown.

(i) The reaction is exothermic. Complete the energy profile diagram for this reaction. [3]

Include:

  • the position of the products
  • an arrow to show the activation energy, labelled as A
  • an arrow to show the energy change for the reaction.

1. Draw a horizontal line for the products (C₂H₄Br₂) at a lower energy level than the reactants (since it's exothermic).

2. Draw an arrow labelled 'A' (for activation energy) pointing upwards from the reactants' line to the peak of the energy 'hump'.

3. Draw a downward arrow (for enthalpy change, ΔH) starting from the reactants' line and ending at the products' line.

Recap

A reaction pathway diagram shows the energy changes during a reaction.

  • Activation energy (Eₐ) is the minimum energy colliding particles need to react, shown as the 'hump' from reactants to the peak.
  • In an exothermic reaction, energy is released, so the products are at a lower energy level than the reactants. The enthalpy change (ΔH) is negative.

(ii) ... Use the bond energies in the table to calculate the energy change in this reaction. [3]

Use the following steps.

Calculate the energy needed to break bonds.

Calculate the energy released in making bonds.

Determine the energy change in this reaction.

Step 1: Energy needed to break bonds (Reactants)

Bonds broken: 4 x (C-H) + 1 x (C=C) + 1 x (Br-Br)

Energy in = (4 x 410) + (1 x 610) + (1 x 190)

Energy in = 1640 + 610 + 190 = 2440 kJ

Step 2: Energy released in making bonds (Products)

Bonds made: 4 x (C-H) + 1 x (C-C) + 2 x (C-Br)

Energy out = (4 x 410) + (1 x 350) + (2 x 290)

Energy out = 1640 + 350 + 580 = 2570 kJ

Step 3: Energy change (ΔH)

Energy change = Energy in - Energy out

Energy change = 2440 - 2570 = -130 kJ/mol

Recap

Bond breaking is an endothermic process (energy is absorbed). Bond making is an exothermic process (energy is released). The overall enthalpy change of a reaction (ΔH) can be calculated as:
ΔH = (Sum of bond energies of bonds broken) - (Sum of bond energies of bonds made). A negative result means the reaction is exothermic.
