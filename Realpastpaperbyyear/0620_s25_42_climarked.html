Using numbers only, state the:

(a) percentage of nitrogen in clean, dry air [1]

The percentage of nitrogen in clean, dry air is 78%.

Recap

Clean, dry air is a mixture of gases. Its typical composition is 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, about 1% argon, and 0.04% carbon dioxide.

(b) typical operating temperature of the Contact process in °C [1]

The typical operating temperature of the Contact process is 450 °C.

Recap

The Contact process, which makes sulfuric acid, uses essential conditions: a pressure of 200 kPa (or 2 atm), a temperature of 450 °C, and a vanadium(V) oxide (V₂O₅) catalyst.

(c) number of metals in Period 3 of the Periodic Table [1]

There are 3 metals in Period 3 of the Periodic Table (Sodium, Magnesium, and Aluminium).

Recap

In the Periodic Table, metallic character decreases across a period from left to right. Elements in Groups I, II, and III (like Na, Mg, and Al) are metals that tend to lose electrons to form positive ions.

(d) number of halogens which are gases at r.t.p. [1]

There are 2 halogens which are gases at room temperature and pressure (r.t.p.): Fluorine and Chlorine.

Recap

The physical states of the halogens (Group VII) at r.t.p. show a clear trend. Fluorine and Chlorine are gases, Bromine is a red-brown liquid, and Iodine is a grey-black solid.

(e) typical temperature for the fermentation of aqueous glucose in °C [1]

The typical temperature for the fermentation of aqueous glucose is between 25 to 35 °C.

Recap

The fermentation of glucose to produce ethanol has specific conditions. It requires yeast (which provides enzymes), a temperature of 25–35 °C, and the absence of air (anaerobic conditions).

(f) number of unbranched structural isomers of C₄H₈ which decolourise aqueous bromine [1]

There are 2 unbranched structural isomers of C₄H₈ that decolourise aqueous bromine: but-1-ene and but-2-ene. (Note: but-2-ene also has E/Z isomers, but they are not different structural isomers in this context).

Recap

Structural isomers are compounds that have the same molecular formula but different structural formulae. Only unsaturated hydrocarbons (like alkenes) will decolourise aqueous bromine, turning it from orange to colourless.

(g) number of covalent bonds in one molecule of ethanol [1]

There are 8 covalent bonds in one molecule of ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH). This includes 5 C-H bonds, 1 C-C bond, 1 C-O bond, and 1 O-H bond.

Recap

A covalent bond is formed when atoms share a pair of electrons. A displayed formula can be used to show all the atoms and all the bonds within a molecule.

(h) number of carbon atoms in one molecule of propyl butanoate. [1]

There are 7 carbon atoms in one molecule of propyl butanoate. (The 'propyl' part from the alcohol has 3 carbons, and the 'butanoate' part from the carboxylic acid has 4 carbons).

Recap

When naming esters, the first part of the name comes from the alkyl group of the alcohol (e.g., 'propyl' from propanol, 3 carbons) and the second part comes from the carboxylic acid (e.g., 'butanoate' from butanoic acid, 4 carbons).

Paper 0620/42/M/J/25 Q2 | Topics: Structure and Bonding (Covalent)
This question is about covalent compounds.

(a) State what is meant by a covalent bond. [2]

A covalent bond is a pair of electrons that are shared between two atoms.

Recap

A single covalent bond is formed when two atoms share a pair of electrons. This sharing allows both atoms to achieve a stable outer shell of electrons, often resembling a noble gas configuration.

(b) Chlorine(I) oxide, Cl₂O, is a simple molecule with covalent bonds.

(i) State what is meant by (I) in the name chlorine(I) oxide. [2]

The Roman numeral (I) represents the oxidation number (or oxidation state) of the chlorine atom. In this compound, the oxidation number of chlorine is +1.

Recap

Oxidation numbers provide information about the degree of oxidation of an element. Roman numerals are often used in the names of compounds to show the oxidation number of a particular element, especially for transition metals or non-metals with variable oxidation states.

(ii) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram in Fig. 2.1 of a molecule of chlorine(I) oxide. Show outer electrons only. [3]

To complete the diagram:

  1. Draw one shared pair of electrons (one dot, one cross) between the central Oxygen and each Chlorine atom.
  2. Add 4 non-bonding electrons (as two pairs of dots) to the Oxygen atom's outer shell.
  3. Add 6 non-bonding electrons (as three pairs of crosses) to each Chlorine atom's outer shell.

Recap

A dot-and-cross diagram is used to show the arrangement of outer shell electrons in a covalent molecule. Electrons from one atom are shown as dots (•) and electrons from the other atom are shown as crosses (x) to illustrate the sharing in covalent bonds.

(iii) Explain, in terms of structure and bonding, why Cl₂O boils at a low temperature and does not thermally decompose into its constituent elements, Cl₂ and O₂. [3]

Cl₂O has a simple molecular structure. It boils at a low temperature because only a small amount of energy is needed to overcome the weak forces of attraction between the molecules (intermolecular forces).

It does not thermally decompose easily because a large amount of energy would be required to break the strong covalent bonds between the Cl and O atoms *within* the molecules.

Recap

Compounds with simple molecular structures have low melting and boiling points. This is because the forces between the molecules (intermolecular forces) are weak and easily overcome. The covalent bonds *within* the molecules remain strong.

(iv) Give two reasons why liquid Cl₂O is a poor conductor of electricity. [2]

  1. There are no ions (or charged particles) present to move and carry charge.
  2. There are no mobile electrons (or delocalised electrons) to move and carry charge.

Recap

For a substance to conduct electricity, it must contain mobile charged particles. Simple molecular structures do not conduct electricity because they do not have any mobile ions or delocalised electrons.

(c) Carbon and silicon(IV) oxide both exist as giant covalent structures.

(i) Name a giant covalent structure of carbon which conducts electricity. [1]

Graphite.

Recap

Graphite is an allotrope of carbon with a giant covalent structure arranged in layers. Unlike diamond, graphite conducts electricity.

(ii) Identify the particles responsible for the conduction of electricity in this covalent structure of carbon. [1]

Electrons (specifically, delocalised electrons).

Recap

In graphite, each carbon atom forms three covalent bonds. The fourth outer electron is delocalised, creating a 'sea' of electrons that are mobile and can move along the layers, allowing graphite to conduct electricity.

(iii) Silicon(IV) oxide contains silicon atoms, Si, and oxygen atoms, O. Fig. 2.2 shows part of the giant covalent structure of silicon(IV) oxide. Complete the diagram in Fig. 2.2 by adding the symbol for each of the 9 atoms shown. [2]

To complete the diagram:

  • The central atom, bonded to four other atoms, is Si (Silicon).
  • The four atoms bonded to the central Si are O (Oxygen).
  • The remaining four atoms at the edges, each bonded to an oxygen, are Si (Silicon).

Recap

Silicon(IV) oxide (SiO₂) has a giant covalent structure. Each silicon atom is bonded to four oxygen atoms, and each oxygen atom is bonded to two silicon atoms, creating a strong, continuous 3D lattice.

Paper 0620/42/M/J/25 Q3 | Topics: Electricity and Chemistry
This question is about electrolysis.

(a) Complete the definition of electrolysis by filling in the missing words. [3]

The decomposition of or compounds when by the passage of an electric current.

The decomposition of ionic compounds when molten or in aqueous solution by the passage of an electric current.

Recap

Electrolysis is the decomposition (breakdown) of an ionic compound. This can only happen when the ions are free to move, which occurs when the compound is either molten (liquid) or dissolved in an aqueous solution.

(b) Electrolysis of aqueous copper(II) sulfate is carried out using the apparatus shown in Fig. 3.1. The electrodes are made of platinum.

(i) State whether the mass of the cathode increases, decreases or remains the same when platinum electrodes are used. [1]

The mass of the cathode increases.

Recap

During the electrolysis of aqueous copper(II) sulfate with inert electrodes (like platinum), copper metal (Cu) is deposited onto the cathode (the negative electrode). This causes the cathode's mass to increase.

(ii) Describe the change in appearance, if any, of the electrolyte when platinum electrodes are used. [1]

The blue colour of the electrolyte becomes paler and eventually fades to colourless.

Recap

The blue colour of the electrolyte is due to the presence of aqueous copper(II) ions (Cu²⁺). As these ions are removed from the solution (by being deposited as copper metal at the cathode), their concentration decreases, causing the blue colour to fade.

(iii) Describe what is seen at the anode when platinum electrodes are used. [1]

Bubbles (of gas) are seen.

Recap

At the anode (positive electrode), hydroxide ions (OH⁻) from the water are discharged in preference to sulfate ions. This produces oxygen gas, which is seen as bubbles.

(iv) Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction at the anode. [3]

4OH⁻(aq) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g) + 4e⁻

Recap

Ionic half-equations show the gain or loss of electrons at an electrode. At the anode, oxidation occurs (loss of electrons). In this case, hydroxide ions lose electrons to form water and oxygen.

(c) The electrolysis is repeated using copper electrodes.

(i) State whether the mass of the cathode increases, decreases or remains the same when copper electrodes are used. [1]

The mass of the cathode increases.

Recap

Just like with inert electrodes, copper metal is deposited at the cathode, causing its mass to increase. The ionic half-equation at the cathode is: Cu²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → Cu(s).

(ii) Describe the change in appearance, if any, of the electrolyte when copper electrodes are used. [1]

There is no change in the appearance of the electrolyte; the blue colour remains constant.

Recap

When copper electrodes are used (an active anode), the anode itself dissolves or oxidises (Cu(s) → Cu²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻). This replaces the copper ions that are being deposited at the cathode. Since the concentration of Cu²⁺ ions remains constant, the blue colour does not change.

(d) Aluminium is extracted from its purified ore by electrolysis.

(i) Name this ore of aluminium. [1]

Bauxite.

Recap

An ore is a rock from which a metal can be economically extracted. The main ore of aluminium is bauxite, which is a rock containing aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃).

(ii) Name the substance added to this process to reduce the operating temperature. [1]

Cryolite.

Recap

Aluminium oxide has a very high melting point (over 2000 °C). To make electrolysis possible at a lower temperature (around 1000 °C), the aluminium oxide is dissolved in molten cryolite. The cryolite acts as a solvent and lowers the melting point of the electrolyte.

(iii) Explain why the anodes need to be continually replaced during this process. [2]

The anodes are made of carbon (graphite). At the high operating temperature, the oxygen gas produced at the anodes reacts with the carbon, burning them away to form carbon dioxide (C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g)).

Recap

In the extraction of aluminium, the positive carbon anodes must be replaced regularly. This is because the oxygen produced at the anode (from the O²⁻ ions) is hot enough to react with the carbon, effectively burning the anode away.

(b) Chromium can be mixed with nickel and other elements to form stainless steel. Name the type of substance formed when a metal is mixed with other elements. [1]

An alloy.

Recap

(c) (NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇ is a compound containing chromium. The negative ion in (NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇ is Cr₂O₇²⁻.

(i) State the sum of the oxidation numbers in the Cr₂O₇²⁻ ion. [1]

The sum of the oxidation numbers is -2.

Recap

One of the oxidation number rules states that the total oxidation number of all the elements in a polyatomic ion (like SO₄²⁻ or Cr₂O₇²⁻) is equal to the charge on the ion.

(ii) The oxidation number of each O in Cr₂O₇²⁻ ions is -2. Determine the oxidation number of each Cr in Cr₂O₇²⁻ ions. Show your working. [2]

Let the oxidation number of Cr be 'x'.

  1. Total charge from oxygen = 7 × (-2) = -14.
  2. Total charge from chromium = 2 × x = 2x.
  3. Sum of oxidation numbers must equal the ion's charge: 2x + (-14) = -2.
  4. Solve for x: 2x = +12, so x = +6.

The oxidation number of each Cr is +6.

Recap

To find an unknown oxidation number, you can use the rules: 1. The oxidation number of oxygen in most compounds is -2. 2. The total oxidation number of all elements in a compound is 0. 3. The total oxidation number in an ion equals the charge on the ion.

(iii) When (NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇ is heated the following reaction occurs. (NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇ → Cr₂O₃ + 4H₂O + N₂. Calculate the volume of nitrogen gas produced at r.t.p., in cm³, when 1.26 g of (NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇ is heated... [3]

The Mᵣ of (NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇ is 252.
Calculate the number of moles of (NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇ used.
Determine the number of moles of N₂ formed.
Calculate the volume of N₂ formed at r.t.p. in cm³.

Moles of (NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇:
Moles = Mass / Mᵣ = 1.26 g / 252 g/mol = 0.005 mol

Moles of N₂ formed:
From the equation, the stoichiometry (mole ratio) of (NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇ to N₂ is 1:1. Therefore, moles of N₂ = 0.005 mol

Volume of N₂ formed:
Volume = Moles × Molar Gas Volume = 0.005 mol × 24 dm³/mol = 0.12 dm³ Volume in cm³ = 0.12 × 1000 = 120 cm³

Recap

Chemical calculations often follow these steps: 1. Calculate moles using: Moles = Mass / Mᵣ. 2. Use the stoichiometry (mole ratio) from the balanced equation to find moles of the desired substance. 3. Calculate the volume of gas using: Volume (dm³) = Moles × 24 dm³ (at r.t.p.). Remember 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³.

(c) Sodium sulfide, Na₂S, is an ionic compound. Complete Fig. 5.1 to show the electronic configurations of the ions in sodium sulfide. Show the charges on the ions. [3]

To complete the diagram:

  1. Each of the two Sodium (Na) atoms loses its single outer electron. They become Na⁺ ions with a charge of +1 and a stable outer shell of 8 electrons (in the second shell).
  2. The one Sulfur (S) atom gains these two electrons (one from each Na). It becomes an S²⁻ ion with a charge of 2- and a stable outer shell of 8 electrons (6 original 'x' and 2 new '•').

An ionic bond forms by the transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal. Positive ions (cations) are formed by electron loss, and negative ions (anions) are formed by electron gain. The resulting ions have a stable, full outer shell of electrons.

(d) Rubidium has only two naturally occurring atoms, ⁸⁵Rb and ⁸⁷Rb.

(i) State the term given to these naturally occurring atoms of rubidium. [1]

Isotopes.

Recap

Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same number of protons) but with different numbers of neutrons in the nucleus. This results in them having the same proton number but different mass numbers.

(ii) Complete Table 5.1 to show the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in the atom and ion of rubidium shown. [3]

For ⁸⁵Rb atom:

  • protons: 37 (from Periodic Table)
  • neutrons: 48 (Mass Number 85 - Proton Number 37)
  • electrons: 37 (to balance the 37 protons in a neutral atom)

For ⁸⁷Rb⁺ ion:

  • protons: 37 (element is still Rb)
  • neutrons: 50 (Mass Number 87 - Proton Number 37)
  • electrons: 36 (a +1 charge means it has lost one electron, 37 - 1)

Recap

  • Proton Number (Atomic Number) = Number of protons.
  • Mass Number (Nucleon Number) = Number of protons + neutrons.
  • Number of Neutrons = Mass Number - Proton Number.
  • In a neutral atom: Number of electrons = Number of protons.
  • In an ion: The charge indicates the imbalance (e.g., +1 means 1 fewer electron than protons).

(iii) The relative atomic mass of rubidium to one decimal place is 85.5. Determine the relative abundance of ⁸⁵Rb in rubidium. Express your answer as a percentage. [1]

Let the abundance of ⁸⁵Rb = x
Then the abundance of ⁸⁷Rb = (1 - x)

(85 × x) + (87 × (1 - x)) = 85.5
85x + 87 - 87x = 85.5
-2x = 85.5 - 87
-2x = -1.5
x = 0.75

The relative abundance is 0.75, or 75%.

Recap

The relative atomic mass (Aᵣ) is the weighted average mass of the isotopes of an element. It is calculated by: Aᵣ = (Mass of Isotope 1 × % Abundance 1) + (Mass of Isotope 2 × % Abundance 2) + ...

Paper 0620/42/M/J/25 Q6 | Topics: Organic Chemistry, Equilibrium, Rates, Stoichiometry
Ethene, C₂H₄, is the first member of a family of similar compounds which contains the alkene functional group.

(a) State the term for a family of similar compounds which contain the same functional group. [1]

Homologous series.

Recap

A homologous series is a family of similar compounds that have similar chemical properties because they contain the same functional group (e.g., C=C in alkenes, -OH in alcohols).

(b) Determine the difference in relative molecular mass between C₂H₄ and the next member in this family of similar compounds. [1]

The next member is propene, C₃H₆. The difference in formula is CH₂. The Mᵣ of CH₂ is 12 + (2 × 1) = 14.

Recap

A characteristic of a homologous series is that each successive member differs from the next by a −CH₂− group.

(c) Write the symbol equation for the complete combustion of C₂H₄. [2]

C₂H₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 2H₂O

Recap

The complete combustion of any hydrocarbon (a compound of carbon and hydrogen) in excess oxygen produces only carbon dioxide and water. Remember to balance the equation afterwards.

(d) C₂H₄ reacts with steam to form ethanol. C₂H₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ C₂H₅OH(g) ΔH = -45 kJ/mol The process happens in a closed system and the reaction reaches an equilibrium. The conditions for this process are 300 °C and 60 atm pressure. H₃PO₄ is used as a catalyst.

(i) Complete Table 6.1 to show the effect, if any, on the concentration of C₂H₄(g) when the following changes to the conditions are applied. [4]

  • temperature is decreased: decreases
  • some C₂H₅OH(g) is removed: decreases
  • pressure is increased: decreases
  • a more effective catalyst is used: no change

Recap

For a reaction at equilibrium:

  • Temperature: The reaction is exothermic (ΔH is negative). Decreasing the temperature shifts the equilibrium to the right (product side) to release heat, so [C₂H₄] decreases.
  • Concentration: Removing a product (C₂H₅OH) shifts the equilibrium to the right to replace it, so [C₂H₄] decreases.
  • Pressure: There are 2 moles of gas on the left and 1 mole of gas on the right. Increasing the pressure shifts the equilibrium to the side with fewer moles of gas (the right), so [C₂H₄] decreases.
  • Catalyst: A catalyst increases the rate of both forward and backward reactions equally. It has no effect on the position of equilibrium, only on how fast equilibrium is reached.

(ii) Explain, in terms of collision theory, why the rate of the forward reaction increases if the temperature increases. [3]

When the temperature increases, the particles gain more kinetic energy and move faster. This leads to two effects:

  1. The frequency of collisions between particles increases.
  2. A higher percentage (or proportion) of these collisions will have energy greater than or equal to the activation energy (Eₐ), leading to more successful collisions.

Recap

Collision theory explains reaction rates. For a reaction to occur, particles must collide with the correct orientation and with sufficient energy (equal to or greater than the activation energy). Increasing temperature increases both the collision frequency and, more significantly, the proportion of collisions that are effective (have enough energy).

(The repeat unit shows the central C=C bond becoming a C-C single bond, which forms the polymer backbone. The two H atoms, the -CH₂-COOH group, and the -COOH group are attached to this backbone as side groups. Continuation bonds are drawn on either side of the backbone.)

(iv) Explain why 1 mol of compound B reacts with 2 mol of sodium hydroxide, NaOH. [1]

Compound B is a dicarboxylic acid; it has two carboxylic acid (-COOH) groups. Each acidic -COOH group reacts with 1 mole of the base NaOH in a neutralisation reaction. Therefore, 2 moles of NaOH are required to neutralise 1 mole of compound B.

Recap

Carboxylic acids (like ethanoic acid) are acids. They react with bases (like sodium hydroxide) in a neutralisation reaction to form a salt and water. The mole ratio for this reaction is 1:1 per acid group.

(v) Calculate the volume, in cm³, of 0.250 mol/dm³ NaOH that reacts with 0.100 mol of compound B. [2]

Step 1: Find moles of NaOH needed.
From (e)(iv), the mole ratio of B : NaOH is 1:2. Moles of NaOH = 0.100 mol B × 2 = 0.200 mol.

Step 2: Calculate volume.
Volume (dm³) = Moles / Concentration = 0.200 mol / 0.250 mol/dm³ = 0.8 dm³.

Step 3: Convert to cm³.
Volume (cm³) = 0.8 dm³ × 1000 = 800 cm³.

Recap

The relationship between concentration, moles, and volume is: Concentration (mol/dm³) = Moles / Volume (dm³). This can be rearranged to find any of the three values. Remember that 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³.
