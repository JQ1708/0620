0620/43/O/N/20 Q3(c) | Topic: Equilibrium and Rate

(c) Some of the ammonia made by the Haber process is converted into nitric acid. The first stage of this process is the oxidation of ammonia to make nitrogen monoxide.

4NH₃(g) + 5O₂(g) ⇌ 4NO(g) + 6H₂O(g)

Oxidation is the gain of oxygen, loss of hydrogen, or loss of electrons.

Oxidation is loss of electrons (OIL) and reduction is gain of electrons (RIG).

Increasing temperature: Effect on rate: increase. Effect on equilibrium yield of NO: decrease.

Increasing temperature increases rate but favors the endothermic direction. Since the forward reaction is exothermic, yield decreases. Increasing pressure favors the side with fewer moles of gas. Here, left=9 moles, right=10 moles, so equilibrium shifts left.

Question 4

Zinc is manufactured from zinc blende. Zinc blende is an ore which consists mainly of zinc sulfide, ZnS.

0620/43/O/N/20 Q4(a) | Topic: Metal Extraction

(a) Zinc blende is roasted in air. One of the products is zinc oxide. Name the other product formed in this reaction. [1]

The other product formed is sulfur dioxide.

Roasting sulfide ores usually produces sulfur dioxide as a byproduct, which is a pollutant causing acid rain.

0620/43/O/N/20 Q4(b) | Topic: Metal Extraction

(b) Zinc oxide is then converted into zinc. Zinc oxide and coke, a source of carbon, are heated in a furnace. Hot air is blown into the furnace.

(i) Give two reasons why coke is needed. [2]

1. Coke acts as a fuel (releases heat).
2. Coke acts as a reducing agent (converts zinc oxide to zinc).

Carbon (coke) is used to extract metals below it in the reactivity series. It reduces the metal oxide.

(ii) Write a chemical equation for the formation of zinc in the furnace. [1]

ZnO + C → Zn + CO or 2ZnO + C → 2Zn + CO₂.

(iii) Zinc has a melting point of 420°C and a boiling point of 907°C. The temperature inside the furnace is 1200°C. Explain how this information shows that the zinc produced inside the furnace is a gas. [1]

The temperature inside the furnace (1200°C) is higher than the boiling point of zinc (907°C).

(iv) The gaseous zinc is converted to molten zinc. Name this change of state. [1]

This change of state is condensation.

Condensation is the change in physical state from gas to liquid.

0620/43/O/N/20 Q4(c) | Topic: Salts

(c) Zinc reacts with dilute sulfuric acid to produce aqueous zinc sulfate. Zn(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂(g). Hydrated zinc sulfate crystals are made from aqueous zinc sulfate. Step 1: Solid zinc is added to dilute sulfuric acid until zinc is in excess. Step 2: Excess zinc is separated from aqueous zinc sulfate by filtration. Step 3: Aqueous zinc sulfate is heated until the solution is saturated. Step 4: The saturated solution is allowed to cool and crystallise. Step 5: The crystals are removed and dried.

(i) Name the residue in step 2. [1]

The residue is zinc.

(ii) In step 3, a saturated solution is produced. Describe what a saturated solution is. [2]

A saturated solution is a solution containing the maximum amount of solute dissolved at a given temperature.

A saturated solution contains the maximum concentration of a solute dissolved in the solvent.

(iii) Name two compounds each of which react with dilute sulfuric acid to produce aqueous zinc sulfate. [2]

1. Zinc oxide.
2. Zinc carbonate (or zinc hydroxide).

Soluble salts are made by adding excess metal oxide or metal carbonate to an acid.

0620/43/O/N/20 Q4(d) | Topic: Calculations (Moles)

(d) When hydrated magnesium sulfate crystals, MgSO₄·xH₂O, are heated they give off water. MgSO₄·xH₂O(s) → MgSO₄(s) + xH₂O(g). A student carries out an experiment to determine the value of x.

(i) Describe how the student can ensure that all the water is given off. [2]

The student should heat again and weigh again until the mass is constant.

(ii) In an experiment, all the water was removed from 1.23g of MgSO₄·xH₂O. The mass of MgSO₄ remaining was 0.60 g. Determine the value of x. [4]

Moles of MgSO₄ = 0.005 (0.60 / 120).
Mass of H₂O given off = 0.63 g (1.23 - 0.60).
Moles of H₂O given off = 0.035 (0.63 / 18).
x = 7 (0.035 / 0.005).

Number of moles = mass of substance (in grams) / mass of 1 mole of the substance (in grams). The ratio of moles gives the value of x.

Group I elements, Group VII elements and transition elements are found in different parts of the Periodic Table.

0620/43/O/N/20 Q5(a) | Topic: Periodic Table

(a) Describe the trend in the reactivity of Group I elements. [1]

The reactivity increases down the group.

The reactivity of the alkali metals increases down the group. Reactivity decreases down Group VII (halogens).

(i) Write a chemical equation for the reaction that occurs when aqueous potassium iodide is added to chlorine. [2]

Cl₂ + 2KI → 2KCl + I₂

A more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive halogen from a solution of its halide. Chlorine is more reactive than iodine.

(ii) State the final colour of the reaction mixture. [1]

0620/43/O/N/20 Q5(d) | Topic: Electrolysis

(d) Sodium is extracted from sodium chloride by electrolysis.

(i) State the meaning of the term electrolysis. [2]

Electrolysis is the breakdown of an ionic compound when molten or in aqueous solution by the passage of electricity.

(ii) State what must be done to sodium chloride before it can be electrolysed to produce sodium. [1]

It must be heated until it melts (molten).

For electrolysis to occur, the ions must be free to move. This happens when the compound is molten or in solution.

(iii) Write an ionic half-equation for the change that occurs at the cathode during this electrolysis. [1]

Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na
