Q3 | 0620/43/O/N/22 | Electrolysis & Metals
3 Aluminium is extracted from its ore by electrolysis.
(a) Name the ore of aluminium which consists mainly of aluminium oxide.

Bauxite.

Recap

Bauxite is the main ore of aluminium. It contains aluminium oxide.

(b) State what is meant by the term electrolysis.

The breakdown of an ionic compound in molten or aqueous state by the passage of electricity.

Recap

Electrolysis is the decomposition of an ionic compound when molten or in aqueous solution by the passage of an electric current.

(c) Electrolysis is carried out on aluminium oxide dissolved in molten cryolite.
(i) Give two reasons why the electrolysis is carried out on aluminium oxide dissolved in molten cryolite instead of electrolysing molten aluminium oxide only.

1. It improves the conductivity of the electrolyte.
2. It lowers the operating temperature (melting point).

Recap

Cryolite helps dissolve the aluminium oxide and lowers the melting point of the electrolyte. The melting point of aluminium oxide on its own is very high.

(ii) Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction occurring at the negative electrode.

Al3+ + 3e- → Al

Recap

Positive ions (cations) move towards the cathode (negative electrode) where they gain electrons (reduction).

(iii) The positive electrodes are made of carbon.
Explain why the positive carbon electrodes are replaced regularly.

The carbon anodes react with the oxygen produced to form carbon dioxide.

Recap

The anodes are replaced regularly because they react with the oxygen formed during electrolysis.

(d) Aluminium is more reactive than copper.
When aluminium is added to aqueous copper(II) sulfate, no immediate reaction is seen.
Explain why.

Aluminium has an unreactive coating of aluminium oxide.

Recap

Aluminium appears unreactive because it forms a layer of unreactive aluminium oxide on its surface which adheres to the metal.

(e) Aluminium reacts with oxygen to form an amphoteric oxide.
(i) State what is meant by the term amphoteric.

It reacts with (neutralises) both acids and alkalis.

Recap

Amphoteric oxides such as aluminium and zinc oxides react with acids as well as with bases.

(ii) The reaction between aluminium oxide and aqueous sodium hydroxide forms a salt containing the negative ion $AlO_2^-$. The only other product is water.
Write a chemical equation for the reaction between aluminium oxide and aqueous sodium hydroxide.

Al2O3 + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO2 + H2O

Recap

Symbol equations are balanced by ensuring the same number of each type of atom appears on both sides. Sodium aluminate is formed here.

(f) Gallium is in the same group as aluminium and forms similar compounds.
Predict the formulae of:
gallium(III) chloride
gallium(III) sulfate.

GaCl3
Ga2(SO4)3

Recap

Elements in the same group have similar chemical properties. Aluminium forms $Al^{3+}$, so Gallium forms $Ga^{3+}$. Chloride is $Cl^-$, Sulfate is $SO_4^{2-}$.

$PCl_5(g) \rightleftharpoons PCl_3(g) + Cl_2(g)$
(i) Complete the table using only the words increases, decreases or no change.

Increasing temp: Rate increases.
Decreasing pressure: Rate decreases. Yield increases.
Adding catalyst: Rate increases.

Recap

Increasing temperature increases rate. Decreasing pressure decreases rate (less collisions). Equilibrium: Decreasing pressure moves equilibrium to the side with more moles of gas. Catalysts increase the rate of both forward and backward reactions.

(ii) The table shows that when the temperature increases, the equilibrium yields of $PCl_3(g)$ and $Cl_2(g)$ increase.
State what conclusion can be made from this.

The forward reaction is endothermic.

Recap

For an endothermic reaction, an increase in temperature moves the position of equilibrium in the direction of the products (to absorb heat).

(b) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement in a molecule of phosphorus(III) chloride, $PCl_3$. Show outer shell electrons only.

3 shared pairs (bonding pairs) between P and Cl. Each Cl has 6 non-bonding electrons. P has one lone pair (2 non-bonding electrons).

Recap

Covalent bonds are formed by sharing pairs of electrons. The octet rule suggests atoms gain stability with 8 outer electrons.

(c) Phosphorus oxychloride has the formula $POCl_3$.
Phosphorus oxychloride is the only product of the reaction between phosphorus(V) chloride, $PCl_5$, and phosphorus(V) oxide, $P_4O_{10}$.
Write a chemical equation for the reaction between phosphorus(V) chloride and phosphorus(V) oxide.

P4O10 + 6PCl5 → 10POCl3

Recap

Equations must balance the number of atoms of each element on both sides. Reactants: 10 P, 10 O, 30 Cl. Products: 10 P, 10 O, 30 Cl.

Empirical formula is the simplest whole number ratio of atoms. Divide mass/% by Ar, then divide by the smallest number.

(c) Dilute sulfuric acid and aqueous potassium hydroxide are used to make aqueous potassium sulfate.
$H_2SO_4 + 2KOH \rightarrow K_2SO_4 + 2H_2O$
The method includes use of the following apparatus.

Q6 | 0620/43/O/N/22 | Organic Chemistry & Energy
6 (a) Chloroethene ($CH_2=CHCl$) can be manufactured from 1,2-dichloroethane ($CH_2ClCH_2Cl$).
The equation can be represented as shown.
$CH_2ClCH_2Cl \rightarrow CH_2=CHCl + HCl$
(i) Some bond energies are given.
[Table: C-C 350, C=C 610, C-Cl 340, C-H 410, H-Cl 430]
Use the bond energies in the table to calculate the energy change, in $kJ/mol$, of the reaction.
Calculate energy needed to break bonds.
Calculate energy released when bonds form.
Calculate the energy change of the reaction.

Energy to break: 1(C-C) + 4(C-H) + 2(C-Cl) = 350 + 1640 + 680 = 2670 kJ.
Energy released: 1(C=C) + 3(C-H) + 1(C-Cl) + 1(H-Cl) = 610 + 1230 + 340 + 430 = 2610 kJ.
Energy change = 2670 - 2610 = +60 kJ/mol.

Recap

Enthalpy change = Energy to break bonds - Energy released forming bonds. Bond breaking is endothermic (+). Bond making is exothermic (-).

(ii) Deduce whether the energy change for this reaction is exothermic or endothermic.
Give a reason for your answer.

Endothermic. The overall energy change has a positive sign.

Recap

Endothermic reactions absorb thermal energy (positive $\Delta H$). Exothermic reactions release thermal energy (negative $\Delta H$).
