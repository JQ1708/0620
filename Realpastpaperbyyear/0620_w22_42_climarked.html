Question 1 | 0620/42/O/N/22 | Structure and Bonding
Diamond and graphite are different solid forms of carbon. The carbon atoms in diamond and graphite are arranged in different ways.

(a) State the number of covalent bonds each carbon atom has in diamond. [1]

Each carbon atom in diamond has 4 covalent bonds.

Recap

Giant covalent structures: Diamond is a giant covalent structure where each carbon atom forms four covalent bonds with other carbon atoms in a tetrahedral arrangement.

(b) State the term used to describe the structure of diamond. [1]

The term used to describe the structure is giant covalent (or macromolecular).

Recap

Giant covalent structure: A structure with a continuous three-dimensional network of covalent bonds, e.g., diamond and graphite.

(c) Name an oxide that has a similar structure to diamond. [1]

The oxide is silicon dioxide (or silicon(IV) oxide).

Recap

Silicon dioxide (SiO₂) has a giant covalent structure similar to diamond. Each oxygen atom is attached to two silicon atoms, and each silicon atom is attached to four oxygen atoms.

(d) Describe the arrangement of atoms in graphite. [2]

The atoms are arranged in layers of hexagonal rings of carbon atoms.

Recap

Graphite structure: Graphite is a giant covalent structure with each carbon atom forming three covalent bonds with other carbon atoms. This creates a layered structure of hexagonal rings.

(e) Explain how graphite conducts electricity. [1]

Graphite conducts electricity because it has mobile electrons (or delocalised electrons).

Recap

Graphite conducts electricity because some of the electrons (delocalised electrons) can move along the layers.

(f) Buckminsterfullerene is a simple molecular form of carbon. The relative molecular mass of Buckminsterfullerene is 720. Determine the number of carbon atoms in one molecule of Buckminsterfullerene. [1]

The number of atoms is 60 (Calculation: 720 ÷ 12 = 60).

Recap

Relative atomic mass (Ar): Carbon has an Ar of 12. To find the number of atoms in a molecule from its relative molecular mass (Mr), divide the Mr by the Ar of the atom.

(g) All forms of carbon burn to produce carbon dioxide. Name the substance used to test for carbon dioxide. [1]

The substance used is limewater.

Recap

Test for gases: Carbon dioxide turns limewater milky.

Question 2 | 0620/42/O/N/22 | Group I Metals & Acids/Bases
Sodium is a reactive metal.

(a) Suggest why sodium is stored under oil. [1]

It is stored under oil to prevent contact with air / oxygen and / or water.

Recap

Group I Reactivity: Alkali metals are highly reactive. They react rapidly with oxygen in the air and with water/moisture.

(b) Sodium burns in air to form sodium oxide, Na₂O.
(i) State the term given to a reaction in which a substance burns. [1]

The term is combustion.

Recap

Combustion: Burning, usually in a reaction with oxygen gas, is called combustion.

(ii) State the colour of the flame seen when sodium burns. [1]

The flame colour is yellow.

Recap

Flame tests: Sodium (Na⁺) gives a yellow flame. Lithium gives red, potassium gives lilac.

(iii) Write a chemical equation for the reaction which takes place when sodium burns in air to form sodium oxide. [2]

4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O

Recap

Balancing equations: Ensure the number of atoms of each element is the same on both sides. Oxygen is diatomic (O₂). Sodium is Group I (+1) and Oxide is Group VI (-2), so the formula is Na₂O.

(iv) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement and charges of the ions in sodium oxide. [3]

1. Eight crosses in the second shell of both Na ions.
2. Six dots and two crosses in the second shell of the O ion.
3. + charge on each Na ion and 2- charge on the O ion.

Recap

Ionic Bonding: Positive ions are formed by loss of electrons (Na loses 1). Negative ions are formed by gain of electrons (O gains 2). Each ion has a stable noble gas configuration (full outer shell).

(c) Sodium reacts vigorously with water to form aqueous sodium hydroxide, NaOH, which is a strong base.
(i) Explain in terms of proton transfer what is meant by a base. [1]

A base is a proton acceptor.

Recap

Acids and Bases: Acids are proton (H⁺) donors. Bases are proton (H⁺) acceptors.

(ii) State a pH number that indicates the presence of a strong alkali. [1]

pH 14.

Recap

pH Scale: A solution of pH 7 is neutral. Solutions below pH 7 are acidic. Solutions above pH 7 are alkaline. Strong alkalis have a pH of 13-14.

(iii) State the colour of methyl orange in aqueous sodium hydroxide. [1]

The colour is yellow.

Recap

Indicators: Methyl orange indicator is red in acid and yellow in alkali.

(iv) The equation for the reaction is shown.
2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g)
Calculate the concentration of NaOH(aq) formed, in g/dm³, when 0.345 g of sodium is added to 50.0 cm³ of distilled water. Assume there is no change in volume.
Use the following steps.
- Calculate the number of moles of Na added.
- Determine the number of moles of NaOH formed.
- Calculate the concentration of NaOH in mol/dm³.
- Determine the Mr of NaOH and calculate the concentration of NaOH in g/dm³. [5]

Moles of Na = 0.345 / 23 = 0.015(00) mol.
Moles of NaOH = 0.015(00) mol (1:1 ratio).
Concentration (mol/dm³) = 0.015 / (50/1000) = 0.3(00) mol/dm³.
Mr of NaOH = 23 + 16 + 1 = 40.
Concentration (g/dm³) = 40 x 0.3 = 12.(0) g/dm³.

Recap

Calculations:
Moles = Mass ÷ Molar Mass.
Concentration (mol/dm³) = Moles ÷ Volume (dm³).
Concentration (g/dm³) = Concentration (mol/dm³) x Molar Mass.
Remember: 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³.

(d) When NaOH(aq) is added to aqueous iron(III) chloride, FeCl₃(aq), a solid product is formed.
(i) Name the type of reaction where a solid is formed from two solutions. [1]

This is a precipitation reaction.

Recap

Precipitate: A solid formed when two solutions are mixed. This occurs when an insoluble salt is produced.

(ii) State the colour of this solid product. [1]

The colour is red-brown.

Recap

Test for Cations: Iron(III) ions (Fe³⁺) give a red-brown precipitate with sodium hydroxide. Iron(II) gives a green precipitate.

(iii) Name this solid product. [1]

The product is iron(III) hydroxide.

Recap

Insoluble Bases: Most metal hydroxides are insoluble (except Group I). Iron(III) hydroxide is formed by the reaction: Fe³⁺ + 3OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₃.

(iv) Write the ionic equation for the reaction. Include state symbols. [3]

Fe³⁺(aq) + 3OH⁻(aq) → Fe(OH)₃(s)

Recap

Ionic Equations: Show only the ions that react to form the product. Spectator ions are removed. State symbols: (aq) for aqueous ions, (s) for the solid precipitate.

Question 3 | 0620/42/O/N/22 | Sulfur & Contact Process
Sulfuric acid is manufactured by an industrial process. Sulfur is obtained from sulfur-containing metal ores. The sulfur in the metal ore is converted to sulfur dioxide which is then oxidised to sulfur trioxide as shown.
2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)

(a) Name a metal ore which contains sulfur. [1]

Zinc blende (or Galena / Iron pyrites).

Recap

Ores: Metal oxides and sulfides are common ores. Zinc blende is zinc sulfide (ZnS).

(b) Describe the process which converts metal ores to sulfur dioxide. [1]

Roasting in air (or strong heating in air/oxygen).

Recap

Sulfur dioxide production: SO₂ is formed by burning sulfur in air or roasting sulfide ores.

(c) Name the industrial process used to manufacture sulfuric acid. [1]

The Contact process.

Recap

Contact Process: The industrial process for making sulfuric acid using sulfur, oxygen, and water.

(d) The reaction that produces sulfur trioxide is an equilibrium. The forward reaction is exothermic.
(i) State the temperature and pressure used to make sulfur trioxide. [2]

Temperature = 450 °C.
Pressure = 1-2 atm (or 100-200 kPa).

Recap

Contact Process Conditions: 450°C, 2 atmospheres pressure (approx 200 kPa), and a Vanadium(V) oxide catalyst.

(ii) Name the catalyst used. [1]

Vanadium(V) oxide.

Recap

Catalyst: A substance that increases the rate of reaction without being used up. Vanadium(V) oxide (V₂O₅) is the specific catalyst for the Contact process.

(iii) Describe two features of an equilibrium. [2]

1. The rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction.
2. The concentrations of reactants and products are constant.

Recap

Dynamic Equilibrium: Occurs in a closed system where forward and backward reactions happen at the same rate.

(iv) State the effect, if any, on the position of equilibrium when the following changes are made. Explain your answers.
- temperature is increased
- pressure is increased [4]

Temperature: Position moves to the left-hand side because the reaction is exothermic.
Pressure: Position moves to the right-hand side because there are fewer gaseous moles on the right (2 moles) than on the left (3 moles).

Recap

Le Chatelier's Principle:
- Increasing temperature favors the endothermic direction.
- Increasing pressure favors the side with fewer moles of gas.

(v) Explain, in terms of particles, what happens to the rate of reaction when the temperature is increased. [3]

1. The rate increases because particles have more energy.
2. More collisions occur per second (higher frequency).
3. More collisions have energy equal to or above the activation energy (more successful collisions).

Recap

Collision Theory: For a reaction to occur, particles must collide with activation energy. Higher temperature increases kinetic energy, leading to more frequent and more effective collisions.

(e) Name the compound formed when sulfuric acid reacts with ammonia. [1]

Ammonium sulfate.

Recap

Fertilisers: Ammonia (base) reacts with sulfuric acid to form the salt ammonium sulfate, commonly used as a nitrogenous fertiliser.

Question 4 | 0620/42/O/N/22 | Salt Preparation
A student prepares magnesium sulfate crystals, MgSO₄, by adding excess magnesium to dilute sulfuric acid.

(a) Write the chemical equation for this reaction. [1]

Mg + H₂SO₄ → MgSO₄ + H₂

Recap

Acid Reactions: Acid + Metal → Salt + Hydrogen. Magnesium is above hydrogen in the reactivity series, so it reacts with dilute acids.

(b) Describe two observations which show the reaction has finished. [2]

1. No more fizzing (bubbles stop).
2. Solid remains visible (magnesium stops dissolving).

Recap

Making Soluble Salts: Excess solid is added to the acid to ensure all the acid is neutralised. The reaction is complete when the solid stops disappearing and bubbling ceases.

(c) The excess magnesium is removed by filtration. State the general name given to a solid separated from a solution by filtration. [1]

Residue.

Recap

Filtration: The solid left on the filter paper is the residue. The liquid that passes through is the filtrate.

(d) The aqueous magnesium sulfate is heated until crystals begin to appear.
(i) Suggest the name for a solution in which no more solute can dissolve. [1]

Saturated.

Recap

Saturation: A saturated solution contains the maximum concentration of solute dissolved in the solvent at a particular temperature.

(ii) Suggest why more crystals of magnesium sulfate appear on cooling. [1]

Because solubility decreases as temperature decreases.

Recap

Crystallisation: As a hot saturated solution cools, the solid becomes less soluble and falls out of solution as crystals.

(e) Magnesium sulfate crystals have the formula, MgSO₄·xH₂O, where x is a whole number of molecules of water. The student heats the crystals to remove the molecules of water.
MgSO₄·xH₂O(s) → MgSO₄(s) + xH₂O(g)
(i) Name the term given to crystals containing molecules of water. [1]

Hydrated.

Recap

Hydration: Hydrated crystals contain water of crystallisation. Anhydrous crystals contain no water.

(ii) The student heats a sample of MgSO₄·xH₂O and finds it has lost 0.140 moles of H₂O and has 2.40 g of MgSO₄ remaining. Determine the value of x. [3]

Mr of MgSO₄ = 24 + 32 + (4x16) = 120.
Moles of MgSO₄ = 2.40 / 120 = 0.02(00) mol.
Ratio H₂O : MgSO₄ = 0.140 : 0.02 = 7 : 1.
x = 7.

Recap

Empirical Formula Calculation: Calculate moles of each component. Find the simplest whole number ratio by dividing by the smallest number of moles.

(f) If the student uses dilute nitric acid instead of dilute sulfuric acid, the salt formed is magnesium nitrate, Mg(NO₃)₂. Write the chemical equation for the reaction when solid magnesium nitrate is heated. [2]

2Mg(NO₃)₂ → 2MgO + 4NO₂ + O₂

Recap

Thermal Decomposition of Nitrates: Group 2 nitrates decompose to form the metal oxide, nitrogen dioxide (brown gas), and oxygen.

Question 5 | 0620/42/O/N/22 | Organic Chemistry & Energetics
Ethane is an alkane which undergoes a photochemical reaction with chlorine as shown.
C₂H₆(g) + Cl₂(g) → C₂H₅Cl(l) + HCl(g)

(a) Write the general formula of alkanes. [1]

CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.

Recap

Alkanes: Saturated hydrocarbons with single bonds only. General formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.

(b) State why this reaction is described as a photochemical reaction. [1]

It needs ultraviolet (light) to occur.

Recap

Photochemical Reaction: A reaction that depends on the presence of light (specifically UV light for alkanes + chlorine) to provide activation energy.

(c) In this reaction, an atom of hydrogen is replaced with a chlorine atom. State the name of this type of organic reaction. [1]

Substitution.

Recap

Substitution Reaction: A reaction in which one atom or group of atoms is replaced by another atom or group of atoms. Alkanes undergo substitution with halogens.

(d) In this reaction, one of the products is chloroethane. Name the other product. [1]

Hydrogen chloride.

Recap

Reaction Products: In the substitution reaction C₂H₆ + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + HCl, the products are chloroethane and hydrogen chloride gas.

(e) The energy profile diagram of this reaction is shown.
(i) Name the energy change labelled A. [1]
(ii) Name the energy change labelled B. [1]
(iii) State how the energy profile diagram shows this is an exothermic reaction. [1]

(i) A is the activation energy.
(ii) B is the energy change of reaction (or enthalpy change).
(iii) The energy of the products is lower than the energy of the reactants.

Recap

Energy Profiles:
- Activation Energy: Minimum energy for colliding particles to react (hump height).
- Exothermic: Reactants energy > Products energy (energy released).

(f) Use the bond energies in the table to calculate the energy change in this reaction. [3]

Energy needed to break bonds: (6 x 410) + 350 + 240 = 3050 kJ.
Energy released in making bonds: (5 x 410) + 350 + 340 + 430 = 3170 kJ.
Energy change = 3050 - 3170 = -120 kJ/mol.

Recap

Bond Energy Calculations:
- Bond Breaking is Endothermic (+).
- Bond Making is Exothermic (-).
- Enthalpy Change = Total Energy In - Total Energy Out.

Question 6 | 0620/42/O/N/22 | Organic Chemistry
A carboxylic acid Y has the structure shown (H-C(=O)-O-H).

(a) State the general formula of carboxylic acids. [1]

CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH.

Recap

Carboxylic Acids: Homologous series containing the -COOH functional group.

(b) Name carboxylic acid Y. [1]

Methanoic acid.

Recap

Naming Organic Compounds: 1 Carbon atom = 'Meth-'. Acid functional group = '-oic acid'.

(c) Write the molecular formula of carboxylic acid Y. [1]

CH₂O₂ (or HCOOH).

Recap

Molecular Formula: Shows the total number of atoms of each element in a molecule.

(d) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the arrangement of electrons in a molecule of carboxylic acid Y. [3]

1. All single bonding dot and cross pairs correct (C-H, C-O, O-H).
2. Double C=O bond dot and cross pairs correct (4 electrons shared).
3. Four non-bonding cross electrons on each O atom.

Recap

Covalent Bonding: Atoms share pairs of electrons. C forms 4 bonds, O forms 2 bonds, H forms 1 bond. Double bonds involve sharing two pairs of electrons.

(e) Carboxylic acid Y will react with propan-1-ol, C₃H₇OH, to form ester Z and one other product.
(i) Name and draw the structure of ester Z. Show all of the atoms and all of the bonds. [3]

Name: propyl methanoate.
Structure: Correct structure of propyl methanoate shown with ester linkage and all atoms/bonds.

Recap

Esters: Formed by reacting a carboxylic acid with an alcohol. The first part of the name comes from the alcohol (propyl), the second part from the acid (methanoate). Linkage: -COO-.

(ii) Name the other product formed when carboxylic acid Y reacts with propan-1-ol. [1]

Water.

Recap

Condensation Reaction: Esterification produces water as a byproduct. Acid + Alcohol ⇌ Ester + Water.

(iii) Name: an ester which is a structural isomer of ester Z; a carboxylic acid which is a structural isomer of ester Z. [2]

Ester: Methyl propanoate (or ethyl ethanoate).
Carboxylic acid: Butanoic acid.

Recap

Isomers: Compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural formulae. Ester Z (C₄H₈O₂) is isomeric with other esters and carboxylic acids of the same formula.
