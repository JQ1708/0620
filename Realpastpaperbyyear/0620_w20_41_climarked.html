Question 1 | 0620/41/O/N/20 | Topic: The Periodic Table
1 (a) This question is about elements.
aluminium carbon iron hydrogen oxygen silicon sodium sulfur
Answer the following questions about these elements.
Each element may be used once, more than once or not at all.

(i) Name the element that can be used as a fuel. [1]
The element that can be used as a fuel is hydrogen or carbon.

Recap

Hydrogen is the first element in the Periodic Table. It is placed in Group I but is not a metal. Hydrogen is a gas at room temperature and pressure.
(ii) Name the element that forms an oxide with a similar structure to diamond. [1]
The element is silicon (which forms silicon(IV) oxide).

Recap

Silicon(IV) oxide has a giant covalent structure similar to diamond. Each silicon atom is attached to four oxygen atoms and each oxygen atom is attached to two silicon atoms.
(iii) Name the element that forms an amphoteric oxide. [1]
The element is aluminium.

Recap

Amphoteric oxides such as aluminium oxide react with acids as well as with bases.
(iv) Name the element that has oxidation states of +2 and +3. [1]
The element is iron.

Recap

Transition elements have variable oxidation states. For example, iron can form Fe2+ and Fe3+ ions.
(v) Name the element extracted from bauxite. [1]
The element extracted from bauxite is aluminium.

Recap

Bauxite is an ore of aluminium which contains aluminium oxide. Aluminium is extracted from purified bauxite by electrolysis.
(vi) Name the element that has atoms with the electronic structure 2,6. [1]
The element is oxygen.

Recap

The electronic configuration of oxygen is 2,6. It is in Group VI and Period 2 of the Periodic Table.
(b) Iron rusts when it is in contact with oxygen and water.
(i) Explain how sacrificial protection prevents rusting. [2]
Sacrificial protection uses a metal higher in reactivity series (more reactive) than iron, such as zinc or magnesium. This more reactive metal corrodes or oxidises in preference to the iron.

Recap

Sacrificial protection prevents rusting because a more reactive metal on the surface of the iron corrodes instead of the iron. This is because the more reactive metal loses electrons more easily than iron.
(ii) State one other method of rust prevention. [1]
Another method is using a barrier method such as painting, greasing, or plastic coating.

Recap

Rusting can be prevented by barrier methods such as coating the iron with paint, plastic, metal or grease. This prevents oxygen and water from reaching the iron.
Question 2 | 0620/41/O/N/20 | Topic: Metals and Reactivity
2 Zinc is extracted from an ore containing zinc sulfide.
(a) State the name of this zinc ore. [1]
The name of the zinc ore is zinc blende.

Recap

Ores are rocks containing a metal or metal compound from which a metal can be economically extracted.
(b) This ore is converted to zinc oxide, ZnO. Zinc oxide is then reacted with carbon.
(i) Write a chemical equation for the reaction of zinc oxide with carbon. [1]
ZnO + C → Zn + CO OR 2ZnO + C → 2Zn + CO2

Recap

Metal oxides below carbon in the reactivity series can be extracted by reduction with carbon or carbon monoxide when heated.
(ii) State what type of chemical change happens to the zinc in zinc oxide in this reaction. Explain your answer.
chemical change
explanation [2]
The chemical change is reduction because oxygen is lost from the zinc oxide.

Recap

Reduction is loss of oxygen from a compound. Oxidation is gain of oxygen.
(iii) Explain why aluminium is not extracted from aluminium oxide by heating with carbon. [1]
Aluminium is more reactive than carbon (so carbon cannot displace it).

Recap

Metal oxides above carbon in the reactivity series are extracted by electrolysis.
(iv) Suggest an alternative method for the extraction of zinc from zinc oxide. [1]
An alternative method is electrolysis.

Recap

Zinc can be extracted either by electrolysis or by reduction with carbon.
(c) Brass is an alloy of zinc.
Explain, in terms of particles, why brass is harder than pure zinc. [3]
Brass exists as layers of atoms. It contains different sized atoms (copper and zinc). This makes it more difficult for the layers of atoms to slide over each other.

Recap

Alloys are mixtures of metal atoms with atoms of other elements. The different sized atoms in an alloy prevent the layers of metal atoms from sliding over each other easily, making alloys harder and stronger.
Question 3 | 0620/41/O/N/20 | Topic: Stoichiometry and Industrial Processes
3 (a) Aqueous ammonium sulfate, (NH4)2SO4 is warmed with aqueous sodium hydroxide.
The pungent-smelling gas ammonia, NH3, is produced.
Balance the equation for this reaction.
(NH4)2SO4 + ......NaOH → .....NH3 + .........H2O + Na2SO4 [1]
(NH4)2SO4 + 2NaOH → 2NH3 + 2H2O + Na2SO4

Recap

Alkalis react with ammonium salts to produce a salt, water and ammonia.
(b) A 2.8g sample of impure ammonium sulfate is found to contain 0.7 g of impurities.
Calculate the percentage of ammonium sulfate in this sample. [1]
percentage of ammonium sulfate = ________ %
Percentage = 75 %
(Calculation: Mass of pure = 2.8 - 0.7 = 2.1g. Percentage = (2.1 / 2.8) x 100 = 75%)

Recap

Percentage purity = (mass of pure substance / mass of impure substance) x 100.
(c) Describe a test for ammonia gas.
test
result [2]
Test: Use damp red litmus paper.
Result: The litmus paper goes blue.

Recap

Ammonia turns damp red litmus paper blue. Ammonia is an alkaline gas.
(d) Ammonia gas is prepared at the front of a laboratory.
The pungent smell of ammonia spreads throughout the laboratory slowly.
(i) Name the process that occurs when ammonia gas spreads throughout the laboratory. [1]
The process is diffusion.

Recap

Diffusion is the spreading out and mixing of different particles because of their random movement.
(ii) Explain, using ideas about particles, why ammonia gas spreads throughout the laboratory. [2]
Ammonia gas spreads because particles move from an area of high to low concentration, and the particles move randomly.

Recap

Particles in a gas move randomly in all directions.
(iii) Explain why carbon dioxide gas, CO2, will spread throughout the laboratory at a slower rate than ammonia gas, NH3. [1]
CO2 molecules are heavier (have a higher relative molecular mass) than NH3 molecules.

Recap

The rate of diffusion is faster the lower the relative molecular mass of a substance.
(e) Ammonia is produced in the Haber process.
The equation for the reaction is shown.
N2(g) + 3H2(g) → 2NH3(g)
(i) In the Haber process, a temperature of 450°C and a pressure of 200 atmospheres are used in the presence of finely-divided iron.
A larger equilibrium yield of ammonia would be produced if a lower temperature and a higher pressure are used.
Explain why a lower temperature and a higher pressure are not used.
lower temperature
higher pressure [2]
Lower temperature: The rate of reaction would be slower.
Higher pressure: It requires expensive or specialist equipment.

Recap

A compromise temperature (not too high or low) is used in the Haber process which gives a good yield of ammonia with a fast enough rate of reaction.
(ii) State the role of iron in the Haber process. [1]
The iron acts as a catalyst.

Recap

A catalyst speeds up a reaction (by lowering the activation energy) and is not used up.
(f) Ammonia is a weak base.
(i) Explain the meaning of the term base. [1]
A base is a proton acceptor.

Recap

Bases are proton (H+) acceptors. Acids are proton (H+) donors.
(ii) Suggest the pH of aqueous ammonia. [1]
Any value greater than 7 up to 12 (e.g., pH 11).

Recap

A solution of 0.1 mol/dm3 ammonia in water has a pH of about 11.
Question 4 | 0620/41/O/N/20 | Topic: Air and Carbon Dioxide
4 Air is a mixture of gases.
(a) State the percentage of clean dry air which is oxygen. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. [1]
21 %

Recap

Clean, dry air contains 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, about 1% argon and 0.04% carbon dioxide.
(b) Oxygen and nitrogen are useful gases that can be obtained from air.
(i) Name the process used to separate oxygen and nitrogen from liquid air. [2]
The process is fractional distillation.

Recap

Fractional distillation is used to separate a mixture of liquids which have boiling points which are close to each other.
(ii) State the property of oxygen and nitrogen that allows these gases to be separated using this process. [1]
They have different boiling points.

Recap

Fractional distillation uses the difference in boiling points to separate liquids.
(c) Carbon dioxide, CO2, is a covalent molecule.
Complete the diagram to show the electron arrangement in one molecule of CO2.
Show only the outer electrons. [2]
The diagram should show 2 double bonds (2 pairs of electrons shared between C and each O) and the whole molecule correct including 2 lone pairs on each oxygen atom.

Recap

A double covalent bond is formed when two pairs of electrons are shared between two atoms.
(d) The graph shows the concentration of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere over a 60-year period, measured in parts per million (ppm).
The data shown in the graph is of global concern.
Explain why. [3]
There is an increase in the concentration of carbon dioxide. Carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas which contributes to climate change or global warming.

Recap

Carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas that causes global warming. Increased global warming causes climate change.
(e) Name the process in the carbon cycle by which plants remove carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. [1]
The process is photosynthesis.

Recap

Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. It is the reaction between carbon dioxide and water to produce glucose and oxygen.
Question 5 | 0620/41/O/N/20 | Topic: Electrolysis and Sulfuric Acid
5 (a) Dilute sulfuric acid is electrolysed using the apparatus shown in the diagram.
(i) State what is meant by the term electrolysis. [2]
Electrolysis is the breakdown of an ionic compound in molten or aqueous state by the passage of electricity.

Recap

Electrolysis is the decomposition of an ionic compound when molten or in an aqueous solution by the passage of an electric current.
(ii) Explain why inert electrodes are used. [1]
Inert electrodes are used because they do not react.

Recap

Graphite or platinum electrodes are used in electrolysis because they are inert and do not react with the electrolyte.
(iii) Name the products formed at each electrode.
negative electrode
positive electrode [2]
Negative electrode: hydrogen.
Positive electrode: oxygen.

Recap

Electrolysis of dilute sulfuric acid produces bubbles of oxygen at the anode and bubbles of hydrogen at the cathode.
(iv) Write an ionic half-equation for the reaction at the negative electrode. [2]
2H+ + 2e- → H2

Recap

Ions gain electrons at the cathode. Hydrogen ions form hydrogen gas: 2H+(aq) + 2e- → H2(g).
(b) Sulfuric acid is manufactured using the Contact process. This manufacture involves four stages.
(i) Stage 1 involves the combustion of sulfur to form sulfur dioxide.
Write the chemical equation for stage 1. [1]
S + O2 → SO2

Recap

The Contact process uses sulfur dioxide, SO2, from burning sulfur or roasting sulfide ores.
(ii) The equation for stage 2 is shown.
2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g)
The reaction can reach equilibrium.
Explain what is meant by the term equilibrium. [2]
Equilibrium means the rate of the forward reaction is equal to the rate of the reverse reaction and the concentration of reactants and products is constant.

Recap

An equilibrium reaction is one where the reactants are converted to the products at the same rate as the products are converted back to reactants.
(iii) The energy level diagram for the forward reaction in stage 2 is shown.
Explain what the diagram shows about the energy changes in the forward reaction. [2]
The reaction is exothermic (energy is released). The products have lower energy than reactants.

Recap

In an exothermic reaction pathway diagram, the energy level of the reactants is higher than the energy level of the products.
(c) In stage 3 sulfur trioxide, SO3, is converted to oleum, H2S2O7.
In stage 4 oleum reacts to form sulfuric acid, H2SO4.
State what oleum reacts with in stage 4. [1]
Oleum reacts with water (H2O).

Recap

(No specific recap for this step in textbook, but part of Contact process knowledge).
(d) A sample of sulfuric acid, H2SO4, has a concentration of 0.75 mol/dm3.
Calculate the concentration of sulfuric acid in g/dm3. [2]
Concentration = 73.5 g/dm3
(Calculation: Mr = 98. Concentration = 0.75 x 98 = 73.5 g/dm3)

Recap

Concentration (in g/dm3) = Concentration (in mol/dm3) x Mr.
Question 6 | 0620/41/O/N/20 | Topic: Organic Chemistry
6 (a) Ethane, propane and butane are members of the same homologous series.
(i) Name this homologous series. [1]
Alkanes.

Recap

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons.
(ii) State two ways members of the same homologous series are similar. [2]
They have the same chemical properties and the same functional group (or same general formula).

Recap

A homologous series is a family of similar compounds with similar chemical properties due to the presence of the same functional group.
(b) One mole of ethane, C2H6, contains 6.02 x 1023 molecules.
Calculate how many molecules are in 15g of ethane. [1]
3.01 x 1023 molecules.
(Calculation: Mr of Ethane = 30. Moles = 15/30 = 0.5 mol. Molecules = 0.5 x 6.02 x 1023).

Recap

Number of particles = number of moles x Avogadro constant.
(c) Propane reacts with chlorine.
(i) Write the formula of the product which does not contain carbon. [1]
HCl.

Recap

Alkanes react with chlorine in the presence of ultraviolet light by a substitution reaction.
(ii) Draw the structure of an organic product formed. Show all of the atoms and all of the bonds. [1]
Structure of chloropropane (e.g., CH3CH2CH2Cl or CH3CHClCH3).

Recap

A substitution reaction is one in which one atom or group of atoms replaces another.
(iii) State the name of this type of reaction. [1]
Substitution.

Recap

Alkanes react with chlorine in the presence of ultraviolet light and chlorine by a substitution reaction.
(d) (i) Aqueous bromine was added to a sample of ethene.
Give the colour change seen.
from _____ to _____ [2]
From orange (or brown) to colourless.

Recap

Unsaturated hydrocarbons turn aqueous bromine from orange to colourless when the hydrocarbon is in excess.
(ii) Explain, in terms of bonding, why there is no colour change when aqueous bromine is added to ethane. [1]
Ethane contains no double bonds (or only single bonds).

Recap

Saturated hydrocarbons (alkanes) only have single bonds.
(e) There are two structural isomers with the formula C4H10.
(i) Draw the structures of both of these isomers, showing all of the atoms and all of the bonds. [2]
Structure 1: Butane (straight chain).
Structure 2: Methylpropane (branched chain).

Recap

Structural isomers are compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural formulae.
(ii) Butane is formed when longer chain hydrocarbons are cracked.
Complete the chemical equation to show the other product when butane is formed by cracking.
C6H14 → C4H10 + ______ [1]
C2H4 (Ethene).

Recap

Cracking is the breakdown of long-chain alkanes into shorter-chain alkanes and alkenes.
(f) A compound contains 85.7% carbon and 14.3% hydrogen by mass.
(i) Calculate the empirical formula of this compound. Show your working. [2]
Empirical formula = CH2.
(Working: C: 85.7/12 = 7.14. H: 14.3/1 = 14.3. Ratio 7.14:14.3 is approx 1:2).

Recap

Empirical formula is the simplest whole number ratio of the different atoms in a compound.
(ii) The molecular mass of the compound is 112.
Calculate the molecular formula of this compound. [1]
Molecular formula = C8H16.
(Working: Mass of CH2 = 14. 112/14 = 8. So 8 x CH2).

Recap

Molecular formula is deduced using the empirical formula and the relative molecular mass.
Question 7 | 0620/41/O/N/20 | Topic: Alcohols and Esters
7 (a) Ethanol can be manufactured by two different methods.
Method 1: fermentation of a sugar, C6H12O6
C6H12O6 → 2C2H5OH + 2CO2
Method 2: reaction of ethene with steam
C2H4 + H2O → C2H5OH
(i) Give one advantage of using fermentation compared with Method 2. [1]
Advantage: It uses renewable resources (sugar) OR it uses low energy / low temperature.

Recap

Production of ethanol by fermentation is a low energy process.
(ii) Give one disadvantage of using fermentation compared with Method 2. [1]
Disadvantage: It is a slow process OR produces impure ethanol.

Recap

Fermentation is a slow rate of reaction and forms impure ethanol.
(b) Ethanol reacts with acidified potassium manganate (VII) to form water and a product that turns litmus red.
(i) State the name of the product that turns the litmus red. [1]
The product is ethanoic acid.

Recap

The oxidation of ethanol produces ethanoic acid.
(ii) State the type of reaction that ethanol undergoes when it reacts with acidified potassium manganate(VII). [1]
The reaction type is oxidation.

Recap

The oxidation of ethanol is carried out by heating acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII).
(c) Ethanol reacts with methanoic acid to form an ester.
(i) Name the ester formed in this reaction. [1]
The ester is ethyl methanoate.

Recap

When naming esters the name begins with the alkyl group from the alcohol (ethyl) and ends with the part of the carboxylic acid (methanoate).
(ii) Draw the structure of the ester formed. Show all of the atoms and all of the bonds. [1]
H-COO-CH2-CH3 (Displayed formula showing ester linkage).

Recap

Carboxylic acids react with alcohols to produce an ester. The H is removed from the OH of the alcohol group.
(d) The table shows the melting points of ethanol and sodium chloride.
substance melting point/ °C
ethanol -114
sodium chloride 801
The difference in melting points is due to differences in attractive forces between particles in these substances.
Name the type of attractive force in each substance, which is responsible for the difference in melting points.
ethanol
sodium chloride [2]
Ethanol: Intermolecular forces (or forces between molecules).
Sodium chloride: Ionic bonding (or electrostatic forces between ions).

Recap

Ionic structures have high melting points because a lot of energy is needed to break the strong ionic bonds. Simple molecular structures have low melting points because the forces between the molecules are weak.
