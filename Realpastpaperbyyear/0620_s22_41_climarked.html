3 This question is about sodium and compounds of sodium.

(a) (i) Describe the bonding in a metallic element such as sodium. You may include a diagram as part of your answer. [3]

It consists of positive ions (cations) in a sea of electrons / mobile electrons / delocalised electrons. There is a strong attraction between the positive ions and the electrons.

Metallic bonding is the electrostatic attraction between the positive ions in a giant metallic lattice and a 'sea' of delocalised electrons.

Metals conduct electricity because the delocalised electrons are able to move through the metallic lattice.

conductor of electricity when molten
soluble in water.

Ionic bonding.

(ii) Explain why sodium chloride conducts electricity when molten. [1]

The ions move / are mobile / flow.

Recap

Ionic structures conduct electricity when molten or in aqueous solution because the ions can move from place to place.

(c) A student determines the concentration of a solution of dilute sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄, by titration with aqueous sodium hydroxide, NaOH.
step 1 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ NaOH is transferred into a conical flask.
step 2 Three drops of methyl orange indicator are added to the conical flask.
step 3 A burette is filled with H₂SO₄.
step 4 The acid in the burette is added to the conical flask until the indicator changes colour. The volume of acid is recorded. This process is known as titration.
step 5 The titration is repeated several times until a suitable number of results is obtained.
(i) Name the piece of apparatus used to measure exactly 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ NaOH in step 1. [1]

A pipette (volumetric pipette).

Recap

A volumetric pipette is used to measure out solutions accurately, typically for transferring a fixed volume like 25.0 cm³ into a flask.

(ii) State the colour change of the methyl orange indicator in step 4. [1]

From yellow to orange (or pink).

Recap

Methyl orange indicator is red in acid and yellow in alkali.

At least two results are within 0.2 cm³ or less (concordant results).

Recap

In a titration, results are considered consistent if they are within a close range, typically 0.1 or 0.2 cm³.

(iv) 20.0 cm³ of H₂SO₄ reacts with 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ NaOH.
The equation for the reaction is shown.
H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O
Calculate the concentration of H₂SO₄ using the following steps.
Calculate the number of moles in 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ NaOH. [1]

Moles = concentration x volume (dm³) = 0.200 x (25.0 / 1000) = 0.005 mol (or 5 x 10⁻³).

Recap

Concentration of solution = amount of solute in moles / volume of solution in dm³.

Determine the number of moles of H₂SO₄ that react with the NaOH. [1]

Ratio of NaOH to H₂SO₄ is 2:1.
Moles H₂SO₄ = 0.005 / 2 = 0.0025 mol (or 2.5 x 10⁻³).

Recap

The stoichiometry of a reaction is the ratio of each reactant and product in the balanced chemical equation.

Calculate the concentration of H₂SO₄. [1]

Concentration = moles / volume (dm³) = 0.0025 / (20.0 / 1000) = 0.125 mol/dm³.

Recap

We can find the concentration of an acid by calculating the number of moles and dividing by the volume in dm³.

Question 5 | 0620/41/M/J/22 | Topic: Equilibrium, Organic Chemistry
5 Ethanoic acid is manufactured by the reaction of methanol with carbon monoxide. An equilibrium mixture is produced.
CH₃OH(g) + CO(g) ⇌ CH₃COOH(g)

(a) State two characteristics of an equilibrium. [2]

The rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction. The concentrations of reactants and products are constant.

Recap

In an equilibrium reaction, the reactants are converted to products at the same rate as products are converted back to reactants. This can only take place in a closed system.

(b) The purpose of the industrial process is to produce a high yield of ethanoic acid at a high rate of reaction.
The manufacture is carried out at a temperature of 300 °C.
The forward reaction is exothermic.
Use this information to state why the manufacture is not carried out at temperatures:
• below 300 °C
• above 300 °C. [2]

Below 300 °C: reaction too slow. Above 300 °C: yield of ethanoic acid too low.

Recap

For an exothermic reaction, increasing the temperature moves the position of equilibrium in the direction of the formation of more reactants (lowers yield). However, increasing temperature increases the rate of reaction.

(c) Complete the table using only the words increases, decreases or no change. [3]

effect on the rate of the forward reaction effect on the equilibrium yield of CH₃COOH(g)
adding a catalyst increases no change
decreasing the pressure decreases decreases
Adding a catalyst: rate increases. Decreasing pressure: rate decreases, yield decreases.

Recap

Catalysts increase the rate but have no effect on the position of equilibrium. Decreasing pressure moves equilibrium to the side with more moles of gas (reactants side here: 2 moles vs 1 mole).

(d) Suggest which of the following metals is a suitable catalyst for the reaction. Give a reason for your answer.
aluminium, calcium, cobalt, magnesium, potassium [2]

Suitable catalyst: cobalt. Reason: it is a transition element.

Recap

Transition elements act (as elements and compounds) as catalysts.

(g) When carboxylic acids react with alcohols, esters are produced. The formula of ester X is CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃.
(i) Name ester X. [1]

In naming esters, the name begins with the alkyl group from the alcohol (methyl) and ends with the acid part (butanoate).

Recap

Carboxylic acids react with alcohols in the presence of an acid catalyst to produce an ester and water.

(h) Ester Y has the following composition by mass: C, 48.65%; H, 8.11%; O, 43.24%. Calculate the empirical formula of ester Y. [3]

Moles C: 48.65 / 12 = 4.05 Moles H: 8.11 / 1 = 8.11 Moles O: 43.24 / 16 = 2.70 Ratio: 4.05 : 8.11 : 2.70 Divide by smallest (2.70): 1.5 : 3 : 1 Multiply by 2: 3 : 6 : 2 Empirical formula = C₃H₆O₂

Recap

Empirical formula is calculated by taking the mass (or percentage) of each element and dividing by its relative atomic mass to get the simplest ratio.

(i) Ester Z has the empirical formula C₂H₄O and a relative molecular mass of 88. Determine the molecular formula of ester Z. [1]

Recap

Molecular formula is deduced using the empirical formula and the relative molecular mass.
