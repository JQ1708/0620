Question 1

Topic: Periodic Table, Properties of Elements

1 This question is about the first 30 elements in the Periodic Table.

Name the element which:

(a) is 78% of clean, dry air [1]

The element is nitrogen.

Recap

Clean, dry air contains 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, about 1% argon and 0.04% carbon dioxide.

(b) has atoms with an electronic structure of 2,8,1 [1]

The element is sodium.

Recap

The group number in the Periodic Table is equal to the number of outer shell electrons (for Groups I to VII). An element with 1 outer electron is in Group I. The period number is equal to the number of occupied electron shells. This element has 3 shells (2, 8, 1), so it is in Period 3. The element in Group I, Period 3 is sodium (Na).

(c) is extracted from hematite [1]

The element is iron.

Recap

An ore is a rock containing a compound from which a metal can be extracted. Hematite is a common ore of iron, which contains iron(III) oxide. Iron is extracted from hematite in a blast furnace.

(d) forms an oxide with a giant covalent structure [1]

The element is silicon.

Recap

Silicon dioxide (SiO₂), also known as silica, has a giant covalent structure similar to diamond. Each silicon atom is bonded to four oxygen atoms, and each oxygen atom is bonded to two silicon atoms. This network of strong covalent bonds gives it a very high melting point.

(e) is the gas with the slowest rate of diffusion at room temperature [1]

The element is chlorine (as Cl₂).

Recap

Diffusion is the spreading out and mixing of different particles. The rate of diffusion is faster the lower the relative molecular mass (Mr) of a substance. Of the gaseous elements in the first 30, chlorine (Cl₂, Mr = 71) is the heaviest, so it diffuses the slowest.

(f) has an anhydrous chloride which turns pink when water is added [1]

The element is cobalt.

Recap

The chemical test for water involves using anhydrous salts. Anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride paper is blue and turns pink when water is added. Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate is a white solid that turns blue.

(g) has aqueous ions which form a white precipitate when added to aqueous silver ions [1]

The element is chlorine (as chloride ions, Cl⁻).

Recap

Aqueous silver nitrate (AgNO₃) is used to test for halide ions (Group VII ions). Chloride (Cl⁻) ions give a white precipitate of silver chloride (AgCl). Bromide (Br⁻) ions give a cream precipitate, and iodide (I⁻) ions give a yellow precipitate.

(h) forms a blue hydroxide which dissolves in aqueous ammonia [1]

The element is copper (as Cu²⁺ ions).

Recap

When testing for positive ions (cations), aqueous sodium hydroxide or ammonia is added. Copper(II) (Cu²⁺) ions produce a light blue precipitate with a few drops of either. Uniquely, this precipitate dissolves in excess aqueous ammonia to form a dark blue solution.

(i) is added to molten iron to remove impurities in the steel making process [1]

The element is oxygen.

Recap

Steel is an alloy of iron made by adding controlled amounts of other metals or by adding or removing carbon. To convert molten iron from the blast furnace into steel, oxygen is blown through it to react with and remove impurities like excess carbon (as CO₂) and silicon (as SiO₂).

(j) is used to galvanise iron. [1]

The element is zinc.

Recap

Galvanising is a method of preventing rust. It involves covering iron with a layer of zinc. This works as a barrier method (stopping air and water from reaching the iron) and as sacrificial protection (zinc is more reactive than iron, so it corrodes first).

2 A student adds excess large pieces of magnesium carbonate, MgCO₃, to dilute hydrochloric acid, HCl, and measures the volume of carbon dioxide gas, CO₂, given off.

(a) Add the missing state symbols to the chemical equation for the reaction. [1]

MgCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)

Recap

State symbols are used in chemical equations to show the physical state of reactants and products:

  • (s) for solid
  • (l) for liquid
  • (g) for gas
  • (aq) for aqueous solution (dissolved in water)

(b) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement of the ions in magnesium chloride. The inner shells have been drawn. Give the charges on the ions. [3]

The magnesium atom (Group II) loses two outer shell electrons to form a magnesium ion, Mg²⁺, with a stable 2,8 electron arrangement. Two chlorine atoms (Group VII) each gain one electron to form two chloride ions, Cl⁻, each with a stable 2,8,8 electron arrangement.

  • The Mg ion should have 8 crosses in its second shell (outer shell) and a 2+ charge.
  • Each Cl ion should have 7 dots and 1 cross in its third shell (outer shell) and a - (or 1-) charge.

Recap

Ionic bonds form between metals and non-metals. Metals lose electrons to form positive ions (cations), and non-metals gain electrons to form negative ions (anions). The ions are held together by a strong electrostatic force of attraction between these opposite charges.

(c) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement in a molecule of carbon dioxide. Show outer shell electrons only. [2]

The carbon atom forms a double covalent bond with each of the two oxygen atoms.

  • The central Carbon (C) atom shares two pairs of electrons with each Oxygen (O) atom.
  • Each Oxygen (O) atom also has two pairs of non-bonding electrons (4 electrons) on its outer shell.
  • This gives all three atoms a stable outer shell (8 electrons).

Recap

A covalent bond is formed when atoms share a pair of electrons. A double covalent bond is formed when two pairs of electrons are shared between two atoms. This allows atoms to achieve a stable outer shell, typically a noble gas configuration.

(d) The graph shows how the volume of carbon dioxide gas changes with time.

(i) Describe how the graph shows that the rate of this reaction decreases as time increases. [1]

The gradient (steepness) of the line decreases with time.

Recap

On a graph of product formed (y-axis) against time (x-axis), the rate of reaction is represented by the gradient (slope) of the curve. A steeper gradient means a faster rate. As the reaction proceeds, the curve becomes less steep, indicating the rate is slowing down.

(ii) Explain, in terms of particles, why the rate of this reaction decreases as time increases. [2]

As the reaction proceeds, the concentration of the hydrochloric acid decreases. This means there are fewer acid particles in a given volume, leading to a lower frequency of collisions between the acid particles and the magnesium carbonate particles.

Recap

The collision theory states that for a reaction to occur, particles must collide with sufficient energy (activation energy). Increasing the concentration of reactants increases the number of particles in a given volume, which increases the frequency of collisions and therefore increases the rate of reaction. The opposite is true as concentration falls.

(iii) The student repeats the experiment using powdered MgCO₃ instead of large pieces. All other conditions stay the same. On the grid, draw the line expected when powdered MgCO₃ is used instead of large pieces. [2]

The new line should:

  1. Start from the origin (0,0).
  2. Be steeper than the original line.
  3. Level off earlier (at a shorter time) than the original line.
  4. Level off at the same final volume of gas (because the amount of acid, the limiting reactant, is the same).

Recap

Increasing the surface area of a solid reactant (e.g., by using a powder instead of large lumps) increases the rate of reaction. This is because more reactant particles are exposed, leading to a higher frequency of collisions with the other reactant particles.

(e) Determine the volume of CO₂ gas given off when excess MgCO₃ is added to 25.0 cm³ of 0.400 mol/dm³ HCl at room temperature and pressure.

MgCO₃ + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂ [3]

Use the following steps.
Calculate the number of moles of HCl in 25.0 cm³ of 0.400 mol/dm³ of acid.
.......................... mol
Determine the number of moles of CO₂ gas given off.
.......................... mol
Calculate the volume of CO₂ gas given off in cm³.
.......................... cm³

1. Moles of HCl:
Volume in dm³ = 25.0 cm³ ⁄ 1000 = 0.0250 dm³
Moles = Concentration x Volume (in dm³)
Moles = 0.400 mol/dm³ x 0.0250 dm³ = 0.0100 mol

2. Moles of CO₂:
From the equation, the molar ratio HCl : CO₂ is 2 : 1.
Moles CO₂ = Moles HCl ⁄ 2
Moles CO₂ = 0.0100 mol ⁄ 2 = 0.00500 mol

3. Volume of CO₂:

To convert cm³ to dm³, you must divide by 1000.
The number of moles in a solution is calculated by: `moles = concentration (mol/dm³) x volume (dm³)`.
The volume of one mole of any gas at room temperature and pressure (r.t.p.) is 24 dm³ (or 24 000 cm³).
The stoichiometry of a reaction is the ratio of moles of reactants and products in the balanced chemical equation.

Topic: Air Pollution, Nitrogen Compounds, Redox

3 Nitrogen dioxide, NO₂, is an atmospheric pollutant and is formed in car engines.

(a) Explain how nitrogen dioxide is formed in car engines. [2]

The high temperatures inside a car engine provide enough energy to cause nitrogen and oxygen from the air to react together.

Recap

Air is 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen. Normally, these two gases do not react. However, in the high-temperature environments of car engines or high-temperature furnaces, they have sufficient energy to combine and form oxides of nitrogen (like NO and NO₂).

(b) Nitrogen dioxide causes respiratory problems. State one other adverse effect of nitrogen dioxide. [1]

Nitrogen dioxide dissolves in rainwater to form acid rain.

Recap

Acid rain is formed when pollutants like sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen react with oxygen and water in the atmosphere. Acid rain has harmful effects, such as the erosion of limestone buildings and the acidification of lakes, which kills fish and other aquatic life.

This reaction is (thermal) decomposition.

Recap

Thermal decomposition is the breakdown of a substance into two or more simpler substances by heating.

(ii) One molecule of urea breaks down to form one molecule of ammonia and one other molecule. Complete the chemical equation to show the formula of the other molecule formed in this reaction.

Recap

To balance a chemical equation, you must ensure there is the same number and type of each atom on both the reactant and product sides.
Reactants: 2 x N, 4 x H, 1 x C, 1 x O
Products (NH₃): 1 x N, 3 x H
Missing: (2-1) x N, (4-3) x H, 1 x C, 1 x O

Recap

Ammonia (NH₃) is the only common alkaline gas. It can be identified by its effect on damp red litmus paper, which it will turn blue. It must be damp so the ammonia can dissolve and form alkaline OH⁻ ions.

(d) The ammonia formed reacts with nitrogen dioxide to form nitrogen and water.

(i) Balance the equation for this reaction. [2]

.........NO₂ + .........NH₃ → .........N₂ + 12H₂O

6NO₂ + 8NH₃ → 7N₂ + 12H₂O

Recap

To balance an equation, you adjust the coefficients (the large numbers in front) until the number of atoms of each element is the same on both sides.
Start with H (from 12H₂O): There are 12 x 2 = 24 H atoms on the right. We must have 24 H atoms on the left. This requires 8NH₃.
Balance N: We now have 8 N from NH₃ on the left. Let's call the NO₂ coefficient 'x' and N₂ 'y'. So, `x + 8 = 2y`.
Balance O: We have 12 O from H₂O on the right. We must have 12 O on the left. This requires 6NO₂ (since 6 x 2 = 12).
Check N: With x=6 and y=7: Left N = 6 + 8 = 14. Right N = 7 x 2 = 14. It is balanced.

(ii) State how the equation shows that the nitrogen in nitrogen dioxide is reduced. [1]

The nitrogen in NO₂ loses oxygen to become N₂.

Recap

Reduction can be defined as the loss of oxygen from a compound. Oxidation is the gain of oxygen. (In this reaction, the nitrogen in NH₃ is oxidised, as it gains oxygen to form H₂O, although it's more complex. The simpler definition applies to the NO₂.)

(iii) This reaction is a redox reaction. State the meaning of the term redox. [1]

A redox reaction is one where reduction and oxidation occur at the same time.

Recap

Redox stands for Reduction-Oxidation. These reactions always happen together. If one substance is reduced (loses oxygen, gains electrons, or decreases in oxidation number), another substance must be oxidised (gains oxygen, loses electrons, or increases in oxidation number).

4 This question is about chemical reactions and electricity.

(a) The diagram shows the apparatus used in the production of electrical energy in a simple cell.

The zinc electrode dissolves in the electrolyte forming Zn²⁺(aq) ions.

(i) Draw an arrow on the diagram to show the direction of electron flow. [1]

The arrow for electron flow should be drawn on the external wire, pointing from the zinc electrode to the iron electrode.

Recap

In a simple electrochemical cell, the more reactive metal (zinc) loses electrons (is oxidised) and becomes the negative electrode. The less reactive metal (iron) is the positive electrode. Electrons flow through the external wire from the more reactive metal to the less reactive metal.

(ii) Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction that occurs when the zinc electrode dissolves. [2]

Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻

Recap

Oxidation is the loss of electrons. The zinc atom is neutral (0 charge) and becomes a zinc ion with a 2+ charge, so it must have lost two negatively charged electrons (e⁻). This is an ionic half-equation.

(b) The reading on the voltmeter can be increased if either zinc or iron is replaced by another metal.

(i) Name a metal that can replace zinc and increase the reading on the voltmeter. [1]

Any metal above zinc in the reactivity series, for example, magnesium (or calcium, sodium, potassium).

Recap

The voltage (potential difference) in a simple cell depends on the difference in reactivity between the two metals. To increase the voltage, you must increase this difference. Replacing zinc with a more reactive metal (like magnesium) will increase the gap between it and iron, thus increasing the voltage.

(ii) Name a metal that can replace iron and increase the reading on the voltmeter. [1]

Any metal below iron in the reactivity series, for example, copper (or silver, gold).

Recap

To increase the voltage, you can also increase the reactivity gap by replacing the less reactive metal (iron) with an even less reactive one (like copper). This increases the difference in reactivity between the negative electrode (zinc) and the new positive electrode (copper).

(c) Fuel cells are used to generate electricity.

(i) Name the reactants in a fuel cell. [1]

The reactants are hydrogen and oxygen.

Recap

A hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell is an electrochemical cell that generates electricity by reacting hydrogen and oxygen together. They are supplied continuously to the electrodes.

(ii) Name the waste product of a fuel cell. [1]

The only waste product is water (H₂O).

Recap

The overall reaction in a hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell is 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. This is a major advantage, as water is a non-polluting product, unlike the carbon dioxide and other pollutants released by burning fossil fuels.

(d) Electricity can be used to break down aqueous or molten ionic compounds.

(i) Name the process which uses electricity to break down aqueous or molten ionic compounds. [1]

The process is electrolysis.

Recap

Electrolysis is the decomposition (breaking down) of an ionic compound, when molten or in an aqueous solution, by the passage of an electric current.

(ii) Explain why the ionic compound needs to be aqueous or molten. [1]

In the solid state, the ions are fixed in a lattice and are not free to move. When molten or dissolved in water, the ions are mobile and can move to the electrodes to carry the current.

Recap

Ionic compounds have giant lattice structures. They do not conduct electricity when solid because the ions cannot move from their fixed positions. Electrical conductivity in ionic substances requires mobile ions, which are present when the compound is molten (liquid) or dissolved (aqueous).

(e) Brine is concentrated aqueous sodium chloride.

(i) Name three substances which are manufactured by passing electricity through brine. [3]

1 ..........................
2 ..........................
3 ..........................

1. Hydrogen (gas)
2. Chlorine (gas)
3. Sodium hydroxide (solution)

Recap

During the electrolysis of concentrated aqueous sodium chloride (brine):

  • At the cathode (negative electrode), hydrogen gas is produced (because H⁺ ions from water are less reactive than Na⁺ ions).
  • At the anode (positive electrode), chlorine gas is produced (because the Cl⁻ ions are at a high concentration).
  • Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) is left behind in the solution as Na⁺ and OH⁻ ions remain.

(ii) Name a different substance formed when molten sodium chloride is used instead of concentrated aqueous sodium chloride. [1]

The different substance formed is sodium (metal).

Recap

When molten sodium chloride is electrolysed, the only ions present are Na⁺ and Cl⁻. There is no water, so there are no H⁺ or OH⁻ ions. Therefore, at the cathode, sodium metal is formed (Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na). Chlorine gas is still formed at the anode.

Topic: Organic Chemistry (Alkanes/Alkenes), Energetics

5 This question is about alkanes and alkenes.

(a) Short-chain alkanes and alkenes can be formed from long-chain alkanes in a chemical reaction.

(i) Name the type of chemical reaction which forms short-chain alkanes and alkenes from long-chain alkanes. [1]

The reaction is cracking.

Recap

Cracking is the thermal decomposition (breakdown using heat) of long-chain alkanes into shorter-chain alkanes and alkenes. This process requires a high temperature and a catalyst. It is done because shorter-chain hydrocarbons (like petrol) are in higher demand than long-chain fractions.

(ii) Decane has 10 carbon atoms. It forms ethane and ethene as the only products in this type of chemical reaction. Write the chemical equation for this reaction. [3]

C₁₀H₂₂ → 4C₂H₄ + C₂H₆

Recap

Alkanes have the general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂. So, decane (n=10) is C₁₀H₂₂. Ethane (n=2) is C₂H₆.
Alkenes have the general formula CₙH₂ₙ. So, ethene (n=2) is C₂H₄.
To balance the equation C₁₀H₂₂ → xC₂H₄ + yC₂H₆:
Balance C: 10 = 2x + 2y
Balance H: 22 = 4x + 6y
Solving these simultaneous equations (e.g., multiply C equation by 2: 20 = 4x + 4y) gives y=1 and x=4.

(b) Ethane reacts with chlorine at room temperature to form chloroethane, C₂H₅Cl, and one other product.

(i) Name the other product formed. [1]

The other product is hydrogen chloride (HCl).

Recap

Alkanes react with halogens (like chlorine) in a substitution reaction. One hydrogen atom on the alkane is replaced by a halogen atom. The hydrogen atom that is removed then bonds with the other halogen atom. Example: C₂H₆ + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + HCl.

(ii) State the condition needed for this reaction to take place. [1]

The condition is ultraviolet light (UV light).

Recap

The substitution reaction of alkanes with halogens is a photochemical reaction, meaning it requires energy from light (specifically UV light) to start. This energy is needed to break the Cl-Cl bond and initiate the reaction.

(c) Ethene reacts with chlorine at room temperature to form dichloroethane, C₂H₄Cl₂.

C₂H₄ + Cl₂ → C₂H₄Cl₂

(i) State why this is an addition reaction. [1]

It is an addition reaction because two reactant molecules (ethene and chlorine) combine to form only one product.

Recap

An addition reaction is a reaction in which two or more compounds react to form only one product. This is characteristic of unsaturated compounds like alkenes, where the double bond breaks to allow new atoms to "add on".

(ii) The chemical equation for this reaction can be represented as shown.

The energy change for the reaction is –180 kJ/mol. Use the bond energies in the table to calculate the bond energy of a C–Cl bond, in kJ/mol. [4]

bondC–HC=CCl–ClC–C
bond energy in kJ/mol410610240350

Let the unknown C-Cl bond energy be X.

Step 1: Calculate energy needed to break bonds (Energy In, Endothermic)
Bonds broken: 4 x (C-H), 1 x (C=C), 1 x (Cl-Cl)
Energy In = [4 x 410] + [1 x 610] + [1 x 240]
Energy In = 1640 + 610 + 240 = 2490 kJ

Step 2: Determine energy released when bonds are formed (Energy Out, Exothermic)
Overall Enthalpy Change (ΔH) = Energy In - Energy Out
–180 = 2490 - Energy Out
Energy Out = 2490 + 180 = 2670 kJ

Step 3: Determine the energy of a C-Cl bond
Bonds formed: 4 x (C-H), 1 x (C-C), 2 x (C-Cl)
Energy Out = [4 x 410] + [1 x 350] + [2 x X]
2670 = 1640 + 350 + 2X
2670 = 1990 + 2X
2X = 2670 - 1990
2X = 680
X = 340 kJ/mol

Recap

Bond breaking is an endothermic process (energy is absorbed, +).
Bond making is an exothermic process (energy is released, -).
The overall enthalpy change (ΔH) of a reaction is the difference between the energy absorbed to break all bonds in the reactants and the energy released when forming all bonds in the products.
ΔH = Energy In (bonds broken) - Energy Out (bonds formed)
A negative ΔH value means the reaction is exothermic overall.

6 The names of four esters are listed.

(a) Esters are a family of organic compounds with similar chemical properties. They can be represented by the formula CₙH₂ₙO₂

(i) State the name given to a family of organic compounds with similar chemical properties. [1]

This is called a homologous series.

Recap

A homologous series is a family of similar organic compounds. They all have the same functional group, which gives them similar chemical properties. They also show a trend in physical properties and have the same general formula.

(ii) Explain why members of a family of organic compounds have similar chemical properties. [1]

They have similar chemical properties because they all possess the same functional group.

Recap

A functional group is an atom or group of atoms that determines the chemical properties of a homologous series. For esters, this is the ester linkage (–COO–). For alcohols, it is the hydroxyl group (–OH).

(iii) State the name given to a formula such as CₙH₂ₙO₂. [1]

This is a general formula.

Recap

A general formula is a formula that applies to all compounds in a particular homologous series. For example, the general formula for alkanes is CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, and for alkenes it is CₙH₂ₙ.

(iv) Determine the value of 'n' in butyl propanoate. [1]

The value of 'n' is 7.

Recap

Esters are named from the alcohol and carboxylic acid that form them.
• The first part, 'butyl', comes from the alcohol butanol (which has 4 carbon atoms).
• The second part, 'propanoate', comes from the carboxylic acid propanoic acid (which has 3 carbon atoms).
• Total carbon atoms (n) = 4 + 3 = 7.
• The formula is C₇H₁₄O₂, which fits the general formula CₙH₂ₙO₂.

Recap

Fractional distillation is a method used to separate a mixture of liquids that have different boiling points. The esters in the list are all liquids and, as members of a homologous series, will have different (but similar) boiling points, making this the ideal separation technique.

A polyester is a condensation polymer. It can be formed from two different monomers: a dicarboxylic acid (with a -COOH group at each end) and a diol (with an -OH group at each end). The -OH from the acid and the -H from the alcohol are removed to form water, creating an ester linkage (–COO–).

(ii) Name the type of polymerisation that takes place when this polymer forms. [1]

This is condensation polymerisation.

Recap

In condensation polymerisation, monomers join together to form a polymer, and a small molecule (like water, H₂O) is eliminated (lost) in the process. This is different from addition polymerisation, which has only one product.

(iii) Name a polyester. [1]

Terylene (also known as PET).

Recap

Common examples of synthetic polymers include polyesters (like Terylene or PET) and polyamides (like Nylon). Natural polymers include proteins (polyamides) and complex carbohydrates (like starch).
