Complete the dot-and-cross diagrams in Fig. 3.2 to show the electronic configurations of one magnesium ion and one fluoride ion. Show the charges on the ions. [3]

The magnesium atom loses its two outer shell electrons. This leaves it with two full shells (2,8) and a charge of 2+.

Recap

Positive ions (cations) are formed when metal atoms lose their outer shell electrons. Negative ions (anions) are formed when non-metal atoms gain electrons to achieve a full outer shell. An ionic bond is the strong electrostatic force of attraction between these oppositely charged ions.

(ii) Deduce the formula of magnesium fluoride. [1]

The formula is MgF₂.

Recap

The formula of an ionic compound is determined by balancing the charges of the ions. Magnesium forms a Mg²⁺ ion, and fluorine forms a F⁻ ion. To be neutral, one Mg²⁺ ion must combine with two F⁻ ions.

(iii) When solid magnesium fluoride is dissolved in water it forms a solution that conducts electricity.

State one other change that can be made to solid magnesium fluoride to allow it to conduct electricity. [1]

The solid magnesium fluoride can be melted (made molten).

Recap

Ionic compounds have high melting points and only conduct electricity when molten (liquid) or in aqueous solution. This is because in the solid state, the ions are held in fixed positions in the ionic lattice, but when molten or dissolved, the ions are free to move and carry charge.

Question 4 | 0620/43/M/J/23 | Topics: Rates of Reaction, Stoichiometry, Test for Gases

(a) A student carries out an experiment using excess zinc and dilute sulfuric acid.

The rate of reaction decreases as the reaction progresses. The rate eventually becomes zero.

(i) Explain why the rate of reaction decreases as the reaction progresses. [1]

The rate decreases because the concentration of the sulfuric acid decreases as it is used up. This results in a lower frequency of collisions between the acid particles and the zinc.

Recap

The rate of reaction is fastest at the start and decreases as the reaction proceeds. This is because the concentration of the reactants decreases, leading to less frequent collisions between reacting particles.

(ii) Explain why the rate of reaction eventually becomes zero. [1]

The rate becomes zero because all the sulfuric acid has reacted. The acid was the limiting reactant and has been completely used up.

Recap

A reaction stops when one of the reactants is completely used up. This reactant is called the limiting reactant. The other reactant, which has some left over, is the excess reactant.

(b) Explain, in terms of collision theory, why the rate of reaction increases if powdered zinc is used. [2]

Powdered zinc has a greater surface area than lumps of zinc. This exposes more zinc particles to the acid, which increases the frequency of collisions between the reactant particles, thus increasing the reaction rate.

Recap

For a given mass of a solid, smaller pieces have a larger total surface area than one large piece. According to collision theory, increasing the surface area increases the frequency of collisions, which in turn increases the rate of reaction.

(c) ...Calculate the volume of H₂ formed at room temperature and pressure (r.t.p.)... [3]

Calculate the number of moles of H₂SO₄ used.

Moles of H₂SO₄:
Moles = Concentration (mol/dm³) × Volume (dm³)
Volume = 25.0 cm³ = 0.0250 dm³
Moles = 2.00 × 0.0250 = 0.0500 mol

Moles of H₂:
From the equation Zn(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂(g), the mole ratio of H₂SO₄ to H₂ is 1:1.
Therefore, moles of H₂ = moles of H₂SO₄ = 0.0500 mol

Volume of H₂:
Volume of gas (dm³) = Moles × Molar Volume (at r.t.p.)
Volume = 0.0500 × 24 = 1.20 dm³

Recap

To find the volume of a product, you must use the stoichiometry (mole ratio) from the balanced equation.

  1. Calculate moles of the known reactant (Moles = Conc × Vol).
  2. Use the equation's ratio to find moles of the desired product.
  3. Calculate the volume of the gas (Volume = Moles × 24 dm³ at r.t.p.).

Question 5 | 0620/43/M/J/23 | Topics: Electrolysis, Electroplating, Fuel Cells

(a) (i) State what is meant by the term electrolyte. [2]

An electrolyte is an ionic compound that, when molten or in aqueous solution, undergoes decomposition by the passage of an electric current.

Recap

Electrolysis is the decomposition of an ionic compound, when molten or aqueous, by an electric current. The electrolyte is the substance that is decomposed.

(ii) State the term given to the Roman numeral, (II), in the name copper(II) sulfate. [1]

This is the oxidation number (or oxidation state).

Recap

Transition elements often have variable oxidation states. The Roman numeral in a compound's name, such as copper(II), indicates the oxidation number of that element, which in this case is +2 (Cu²⁺).

(iii) State what happens to the colour of the aqueous copper(II) sulfate as this electrolysis progresses. [1]

The blue colour of the solution fades and it becomes colourless.

Recap

The blue colour of aqueous copper(II) sulfate is due to the presence of aqueous Cu²⁺ ions. During electrolysis with inert electrodes, these Cu²⁺ ions are removed from the solution at the cathode. As their concentration decreases, the blue colour fades.

(iv) Write an ionic half-equation for the formation of copper at the cathode. [2]

Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu

Recap

During electrolysis, positive ions (cations) are attracted to the cathode (negative electrode). Here, they gain electrons in a process called reduction. The ionic half-equation must be balanced for both atoms and charge.

(v) Give the formula of the ion that forms oxygen at the anode. [1]

The hydroxide ion, OH⁻.

Recap

In the electrolysis of aqueous solutions, water partially ionises to form H⁺ and OH⁻ ions. At the anode (positive electrode), negative ions are attracted. If halide ions are not present (sulfate SO₄²⁻ is present here), hydroxide ions (OH⁻) are discharged in preference, producing oxygen gas.

(b) The electrolysis of aqueous copper(II) sulfate is repeated using copper electrodes. State what happens to the anode. [1]

The copper anode dissolves, or gets smaller/thinner.

Recap

When using active electrodes (like copper) instead of inert ones (like graphite), the anode itself can react. In the purification of copper, the impure copper anode dissolves (is oxidised) to form Cu²⁺ ions, which then move to the cathode to be plated as pure copper.

(c) Spoons can be electroplated with silver.

(i) Name the substances used as: [3]

the anode (positive electrode)

the cathode (negative electrode)

the electrolyte.

Anode: A piece of pure silver.
Cathode: The spoon (the object to be plated).
Electrolyte: An aqueous solution of a soluble silver salt, such as silver nitrate (AgNO₃).

Recap

During electroplating, three things are required:

  1. The anode must be made of the plating metal (e.g., silver).
  2. The cathode must be the object to be plated (e.g., the spoon).
  3. The electrolyte must be a solution containing ions of the plating metal (e.g., silver ions).

(ii) State two reasons why spoons are electroplated. [2]

1. To improve the appearance (make it look more attractive).
2. To prevent corrosion (improve its resistance to corrosion).

Recap

Electroplating is a process that uses electrolysis to coat one metal with another. This is done for two main reasons: to improve appearance and to improve resistance to corrosion.

(d) (i) State one adverse effect of carbon dioxide and carbon monoxide. [2]

Carbon dioxide: Contributes to global warming (or climate change).
Carbon monoxide: It is toxic (poisonous) because it reduces the blood's ability to carry oxygen.

Recap

Carbon dioxide (CO₂) is a greenhouse gas, which absorbs thermal energy and contributes to global warming. Carbon monoxide (CO), from incomplete combustion, is a toxic gas.

(ii) State one disadvantage, other than cost, of using hydrogen-oxygen fuel cells to power cars compared to using petrol. [1]

A disadvantage is that hydrogen is a gas that is difficult to store safely, often requiring high pressure tanks, and it is not widely available.

Recap

While hydrogen-oxygen fuel cells are non-polluting (producing only water), they have disadvantages. These include the difficulty and danger of storing hydrogen (which is flammable and must be kept under high pressure) and the lack of availability (few refueling stations).

Question 6 | 0620/43/M/J/23 | Topics: Haber Process, Reversible Reactions, Acids & Bases

(a) (i) State the source of nitrogen for the Haber process. [1]

The source of nitrogen is the air.

Recap

Clean, dry air is a mixture of gases containing approximately 78% nitrogen. This nitrogen is the reactant for the Haber process.

(ii) State the source of hydrogen for the Haber process. [1]

The source of hydrogen is methane (from natural gas), which is reacted with steam.

Recap

Hydrogen for the Haber process is produced by reacting methane (the main constituent of natural gas) with steam.

(iii) State the typical conditions used in the Haber process. [2]

Temperature: 450 °C
Pressure: 200 atm (or 20 000 kPa)

Recap

The essential conditions for the Haber process are a compromise temperature of 450 °C, a high pressure of 200 atm, and an iron catalyst. These conditions balance the rate of reaction and the equilibrium yield.

(iv) Name the catalyst used in the Haber process. [1]

The catalyst is iron.

Recap

An iron catalyst is used in the Haber process to increase the rate at which equilibrium is reached, without affecting the final position of the equilibrium.

(v) State what is meant by the term catalyst. [2]

A catalyst is a substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction but remains unchanged at the end of the reaction.

Recap

A catalyst works by providing an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy. This means a greater proportion of collisions are successful, thus increasing the rate of reaction.

(b) (i) ...explain why a temperature less than 900 °C is not used... explain why a pressure greater than 7 atm is not used. [2]

Temperature: A temperature lower than 900 °C is not used because the rate of reaction would be too low (too slow).

Pressure: A pressure greater than 7 atm is not used because it would shift the position of equilibrium to the left (towards the reactants). This is because there are fewer moles of gas on the left (4 + 5 = 9 moles) than on the right (4 + 6 = 10 moles), and the equilibrium opposes the increase in pressure by favoring the side with fewer moles.

Recap

In reversible reactions, conditions are chosen to balance rate and equilibrium yield.

  • Temperature: A lower temperature always decreases the rate of reaction. Even if it favors the yield (as in this exothermic reaction), it may be economically too slow.
  • Pressure: Increasing the pressure moves the position of equilibrium to the side with the fewer number of moles of gas molecules.

(ii) Balance the symbol equation for the reaction. [1]

.....NO + .....O₂ + .....H₂O → .....HNO₃

4NO + 3O₂ + 2H₂O → 4HNO₃

Recap

A symbol equation is balanced by writing large numbers (coefficients) in front of the formulae to ensure there is the same number of each type of atom on both sides of the arrow.

(c) (i) Write the symbol equation for this reaction. [2]

Recap

Acids react with metal carbonates to produce three products: a salt, water, and carbon dioxide. Copper(II) ions are Cu²⁺ and nitrate ions are NO₃⁻, so copper(II) nitrate is Cu(NO₃)₂.

(ii) State two observations that indicate the copper(II) carbonate is in excess. [2]

1. Undissolved solid (the green copper(II) carbonate) is visible at the bottom of the container.
2. Effervescence (fizzing) stops and no more gas bubbles are produced, even if more solid is added.

(iii) Name one compound, other than copper(II) carbonate, that can be added to dilute nitric acid to produce aqueous copper(II) nitrate. [1]
