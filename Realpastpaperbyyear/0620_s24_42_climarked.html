Recap

Positive ions, also known as cations, are formed when a neutral atom loses electrons. Metals, such as those in Groups I, II, and III, have a tendency to lose their outer shell electrons to achieve a stable, full outer shell, like the nearest noble gas.

Recap

The tendency to form positive ions is related to a metal's reactivity. The reactivity series places the most reactive metals at the top. Potassium (K) is a Group I alkali metal and is the most reactive metal in the given list, meaning it loses its outer electron most easily.

A metal from the list that is not likely to show catalytic properties is magnesium (or potassium).

Recap

Catalytic properties are a characteristic feature of transition elements (like iron and copper). Metals in Groups I (like potassium) and Group II (like magnesium) are generally too reactive and do not typically act as catalysts in the same way.

(iv) State which of the five metals in the list is a major component of stainless steel. [1]

The metal from the list that is a major component of stainless steel is iron.

Recap

Stainless steel is an alloy, which is a mixture of metals. Its main component is iron, mixed with other elements like chromium and nickel. This makes it harder and more resistant to corrosion than pure iron.

Question 3 (0620/42/M/J/24) | Topics: Haber Process, Reversible Reactions, Energetics, Stoichiometry

3 The symbol equation for the industrial production of ammonia is shown.

N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)     $\Delta H = -90 \text{ kJ/mol}$

(a) Name this industrial process. [1]

This industrial process is the Haber process.

Recap

The Haber process is the industrial method for manufacturing ammonia (NH₃) from nitrogen (from the air) and hydrogen gas.

(b) State the meaning of $\Delta H$. [1]

$\Delta H$ is the symbol for enthalpy change, which represents the thermal energy change during a chemical reaction.

Recap

Enthalpy change ($\Delta H$) measures the heat energy transferred between the reaction and its surroundings. A negative $\Delta H$ value, as seen here, signifies an exothermic reaction (energy is released). A positive $\Delta H$ value signifies an endothermic reaction (energy is absorbed).

(c) State the typical conditions and name the catalyst used in the industrial production of ammonia. [3]

temperature and units: 450 °C
pressure and units: 200 atm (or 20 000 kPa)
catalyst used: iron

Recap

These specific conditions are chosen as a compromise. The iron catalyst increases the rate of reaction. The high pressure (200 atm) favors a high yield of ammonia (as there are fewer moles of gas on the product side). The compromise temperature (450 °C) is high enough for a fast rate but low enough to still get a reasonable equilibrium yield, as the forward reaction is exothermic.

(d) State two methods of increasing the rate of this reaction. [2]

Two methods of increasing the rate of this reaction are to increase the temperature and to increase the pressure. (A third method is using the iron catalyst).

Recap

The rate of reaction is a measure of how fast reactants are used up. It can be increased by:

  • 1. Increasing temperature: This gives particles more kinetic energy, leading to more frequent and more energetic collisions.
  • 2. Increasing pressure (for gases): This pushes gas particles closer together, increasing the collision frequency.
  • 3. Using a catalyst: This lowers the activation energy, meaning a greater proportion of collisions will be successful.

(e) The symbol equation for the reaction can be represented as shown in Fig. 3.1.

Table 3.1 shows some bond energies.

bond N≡N H-H
bond energy in kJ/mol 945 435

$\Delta H = -90 \text{ kJ/mol}$

Use the bond energies in Table 3.1 and $\Delta H$ to calculate the bond energy of an N-H bond, in kJ/mol.

Use the following steps.

• Calculate the energy needed to break bonds in the reactants. [3]

• Energy needed to break bonds (reactants):

Energy = (1 x N≡N) + (3 x H-H)
Energy = (1 x 945) + (3 x 435) = 945 + 1305 = 2250 kJ

• Calculate the energy released when bonds form (products):

$\Delta H$ = Energy in (bonds broken) - Energy out (bonds formed)
-90 = 2250 - Energy out
Energy out = 2250 + 90 = 2340 kJ

• Calculate the energy of an N-H bond:

The product is 2NH₃, which contains a total of 6 N-H bonds (2 molecules x 3 bonds each).
Energy per bond = Total energy out / 6
Energy = 2340 / 6 = 390 kJ/mol.

Recap

The enthalpy change of a reaction ($\Delta H$) can be calculated from bond energies. It is the total energy absorbed to break bonds (an endothermic process) minus the total energy released when making bonds (an exothermic process). If the final $\Delta H$ is negative, the reaction is exothermic, as more energy was released than absorbed.

4 A carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol to produce an ester and water.

Under certain conditions, this reaction can be reversed so an ester reacts with water to produce a carboxylic acid X and an alcohol Y.

The forward reaction is endothermic.

The molecular formula of the ester (CH₃CH₂COOCH₃) is C₄H₈O₂. The simplest whole-number ratio of atoms is C₂H₄O.

Recap

The molecular formula shows the actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule (e.g., C₄H₈O₂). The empirical formula shows the simplest whole-number ratio of these atoms. To find it, divide the subscripts by their greatest common divisor (in this case, 2).

(b) Name the ester. [1]

The name of the ester is methyl propanoate.

Recap

Esters are named in two parts. The first part comes from the alcohol (the -OCH₃ group is from methyl alcohol, or methanol). The second part comes from the carboxylic acid (the CH₃CH₂COO- group is from propanoic acid, and its name changes to propanoate).

Recap

This reaction is the hydrolysis (splitting by water) of an ester. The ester CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ splits at the ester linkage. The CH₃CH₂COO- part (propanoate) reacts with water to re-form the carboxylic acid, propanoic acid. This homologous series contains the –COOH functional group.

(d) Name alcohol Y and give its structural formula. [2]

name: methanol
structural formula: CH₃OH

Recap

The -OCH₃ (methyl) part of the ester reacts with water during hydrolysis to re-form the alcohol, methanol. The alcohol homologous series contains the –OH functional group.

Recap

This is a reversible reaction ($\rightleftharpoons$) at equilibrium. The forward reaction is stated to be endothermic ($\Delta H$ is positive), which means the reverse reaction is exothermic.

  • Temperature decrease: This favors the exothermic (reverse) reaction. The equilibrium shifts to the left, so the concentration of X decreases.
  • [Ester] decrease: The equilibrium shifts to the left to oppose the change (to produce more ester), so the concentration of X decreases.
  • [Y] increase: The equilibrium shifts to the left to oppose the change (to use up the added alcohol Y), so the concentration of X decreases.
  • Catalyst: A catalyst speeds up both the forward and reverse reactions equally. It allows equilibrium to be reached faster, but has no effect on the position of equilibrium.

(f) At the beginning of the reaction between the ester and water, no carboxylic acid is present in the reaction mixture.

(i) Suggest how the pH of the reaction mixture changes from the start of the reaction until equilibrium is reached.
Assume alcohols and esters are neutral. [2]

Recap

At the start, only the ester and water are present, which are neutral (pH 7). As the reaction proceeds, carboxylic acid X (propanoic acid) is formed. Carboxylic acids are weak acids, meaning they partially dissociate in water to produce H⁺ ions. This lowers the pH into the acidic range (e.g., pH 3-6).

(ii) Identify the ion that causes the change in pH. [1]

The H⁺(aq) ion (hydrogen ion) causes the change in pH.

Recap

Acidity and the pH scale are measures of the concentration of hydrogen ions (H⁺) in an aqueous solution. The formation of the carboxylic acid increases the [H⁺], thus lowering the pH.

Universal indicator could be used.

Recap

Universal indicator is a mixture of dyes that shows a range of colours across the entire pH scale. It would change from green (at pH 7) to yellow or orange as the acidic solution forms.

Question 5 (0620/42/M/J/24) | Topics: Atomic Structure, Isotopes, Ionic Bonding, Redox

5 Sulfur is a Group VI element.

(a) A sample of sulfur contains two isotopes, ${}^{32}S$ and ${}^{34}S$.

(i) Complete Table 5.1 to show the number of protons and neutrons in one atom of each isotope of sulfur. [2]

${}^{32}S$ ${}^{34}S$
protons
neutrons
${}^{32}S$ ${}^{34}S$
protons 16 16
neutrons 16 18

Recap

The proton number (atomic number) of an element is found on the Periodic Table. For sulfur, it is 16. This is the same for all its isotopes. The mass number (the top number, e.g., 32) is the total of protons + neutrons.

  • For ${}^{32}S$: Neutrons = Mass Number - Protons = 32 - 16 = 16.
  • For ${}^{34}S$: Neutrons = Mass Number - Protons = 34 - 16 = 18.

(ii) State why these isotopes have identical chemical properties. [1]

Isotopes have identical chemical properties because they have the same electronic configuration (or the same number of outer shell electrons).

Recap

Chemical properties are determined by the number of outer shell electrons, as these are the electrons involved in chemical bonding. Isotopes have the same number of protons and therefore the same number of electrons, giving them the same electronic configuration and thus the same chemical behaviour.

(iii) State the mass of $6.02 \times 10^{23}$ atoms of ${}^{34}S$. Include units in your answer. [1]

The mass is 34 g (grams).

Recap

$6.02 \times 10^{23}$ is the Avogadro constant, which is the number of particles in one mole of a substance. The mass of one mole of atoms is equal to its relative atomic mass (or in this case, the mass number of the specific isotope) in grams.

(iv) State the name of the amount of substance which contains $6.02 \times 10^{23}$ atoms. [1]

This amount of substance is 1 mole.

Recap

The mole is the unit for the amount of substance. It is defined as containing a specific number of particles (atoms, ions, or molecules) known as the Avogadro constant ($6.02 \times 10^{23}$).

(v) Table 5.2 shows the relative abundance of these isotopes of sulfur in the sample.

atom ${}^{32}S$ ${}^{34}S$
relative abundance 95% 5%

Calculate the relative atomic mass of sulfur in this sample to one decimal place. [2]

Aᵣ = [($\%$ abund. A $\times$ mass A) + ($\%$ abund. B $\times$ mass B)] / 100
Aᵣ = [(95 $\times$ 32) + (5 $\times$ 34)] / 100
Aᵣ = [3040 + 170] / 100
Aᵣ = 3210 / 100 = 32.1

Recap

The relative atomic mass (Aᵣ) of an element is the weighted average of the masses of its naturally occurring isotopes. It is calculated by multiplying the mass of each isotope by its percentage abundance, summing these values, and then dividing the total by 100.

(b) Sulfur reacts with magnesium to form magnesium sulfide, MgS, an ionic compound.

(i) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram in Fig. 5.1 of the ions in magnesium sulfide. Give the charges on the ions. [3]

The magnesium atom (electron configuration 2.8.2) loses its 2 outer electrons to form a Mg²⁺ ion (configuration 2.8). The sulfur atom (electron configuration 2.8.6) gains 2 electrons to form a S²⁻ ion (configuration 2.8.8).

The completed diagram should show:

  • The Mg ion with 2 electron shells (2 electrons in the first, 8 crosses in the second).
  • The Mg ion should have a charge of [2+] written outside the bracket.
  • The S ion with 3 electron shells (2 electrons in the first, 8 in the second, and 8 in the third).
  • The outer shell of the S ion should show 6 dots (from S) and 2 crosses (from Mg).
  • The S ion should have a charge of [2-] written outside the bracket.

Recap

This diagram shows the formation of an ionic bond. The magnesium atom (a metal) loses its two outer shell electrons to achieve a stable, full outer shell. The sulfur atom (a non-metal) gains these two electrons to complete its outer shell. The resulting oppositely charged ions (Mg²⁺ and S²⁻) are held together by strong electrostatic forces.

(ii) State why MgS has a high melting point. [1]

MgS has a high melting point because there are strong electrostatic forces of attraction (strong ionic bonds) between the oppositely charged ions in its giant ionic lattice. A large amount of energy is required to overcome these strong forces.

Recap

Ionic compounds like MgS form a giant ionic lattice structure. This regular, repeating arrangement of positive (Mg²⁺) and negative (S²⁻) ions is held together by very strong electrostatic forces, which are also known as ionic bonds. A large amount of thermal energy is needed to break these strong bonds, resulting in a high melting point.

(iii) State why molten MgS conducts electricity. [1]

Molten MgS conducts electricity because its ions are mobile (free to move) and are able to carry charge.

Recap

For a substance to conduct electricity, it must contain mobile charged particles. In the solid state, the ions in an ionic lattice are held in fixed positions and cannot move. When molten (or dissolved in water), these ions are free to move (mobile) and can flow to carry an electric current.

(c) An acid containing sulfur reacts with sodium hydroxide, NaOH, to form a salt and water. The salt has the formula Na₂SO₃.

(i) Deduce the formula of this acid. [1]

The formula of the acid is H₂SO₃ (sulfurous acid).

Recap

A salt (like Na₂SO₃) and water are formed when an acid reacts with a base (like NaOH). A salt is formed when the hydrogen ions (H⁺) of an acid are replaced by metal ions (Na⁺). Since the sulfite ion is SO₃²⁻ (to balance two Na⁺ ions), the parent acid must have two H⁺ ions, making it H₂SO₃.

(ii) Deduce the formula of the anion in Na₂SO₃. [1]

The formula of the anion is SO₃²⁻ (the sulfite ion).

Recap

This is an ionic compound. Sodium (Na) is in Group I of the Periodic Table, so it always forms a Na⁺ ion. Since the formula is Na₂SO₃, there are two Na⁺ ions, giving a total positive charge of +2. To balance this, the anion (the sulfite ion) must have a charge of -2, making its formula SO₃²⁻.

(d) Na₂SO₃ is oxidised by acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII).

(i) State what VII refers to in the name potassium manganate(VII). [1]

The (VII) refers to the oxidation number (or oxidation state) of manganese (Mn), which is +7.

Recap

Roman numerals in a compound's name indicate the oxidation number (or oxidation state) of a specific element. This is common for transition elements like manganese, which can have variable oxidation states. Here, manganese (Mn) is in the +7 oxidation state.

(ii) State the colour change when this reaction happens. [2]

from: purple
to: colourless

Recap

Acidified potassium manganate(VII) (KMnO₄) is a strong oxidising agent, which is used to test for reducing agents. The manganate(VII) ion (MnO₄⁻) has a deep purple colour. When it successfully oxidises another substance (like the sulfite ion), the manganese gets reduced (to Mn²⁺ ions), which are colourless.

Question 6 (0620/42/M/J/24) | Topics: Photosynthesis, Fermentation, Alcohols, Alkenes

6 Glucose is involved in two processes.

(a) Glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆, is made in plants from carbon dioxide and water.

(i) Name this process. [1]

This process is photosynthesis.

Recap

Photosynthesis is the essential chemical process used by plants and other organisms to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose (a sugar for energy) and oxygen.

(ii) Write the symbol equation for this process. [1]

The symbol equation is: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.

Recap

This balanced equation shows that six molecules of carbon dioxide and six molecules of water react, using light energy, to produce one molecule of glucose and six molecules of oxygen.

(iii) State two essential conditions needed for this process to happen. [2]

1: energy from light (or sunlight)
2: presence of chlorophyll

Recap

Photosynthesis is an endothermic reaction that requires an energy source, which is provided by light. This light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll, the green pigment found in plants, which acts as the catalyst for the reaction.

(b) Glucose is converted to ethanol.

(i) Name this process. [1]

This process is fermentation.

Recap

Fermentation is the chemical process where microorganisms like yeast break down glucose into ethanol and carbon dioxide. This reaction must occur in the absence of air (anaerobic conditions) and at a warm temperature (e.g., 25-35 °C).

(ii) Name the other product formed when glucose is converted to ethanol. [1]

The other product formed is carbon dioxide.

Recap

The breakdown of glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) by yeast during fermentation produces two main products: ethanol (2C₂H₅OH) and carbon dioxide (2CO₂).

This is an addition reaction because two reactant molecules (ethene and steam) combine to form only one product (ethanol).

In an addition reaction, the double bond (C=C) in the alkene (ethene) breaks open, allowing the atoms from the other reactant (H₂O) to "add" across the bond. This results in a single, saturated product. No other molecule is produced.
