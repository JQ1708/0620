Question 1 | 0620/41/O/N/24 | Topic: Chemical/Physical Changes
A list of chemical and physical processes, A to H, is shown.

A combustion
B diffusion
C melting
D neutralisation
E photosynthesis
F reversible reaction
G roasting
H thermal decomposition

Answer the following questions about processes A to H.
Each letter may be used once, more than once or not at all.
State which of the processes A to H:
(a) happens when an acid reacts with an alkali [1]
The process is D (neutralisation).

Recap

Neutralisation is the reaction of an acid with a base (such as an alkali) to form a salt and water. For an acid reacting with an alkali this is represented by H+(aq) + OH-(aq) → H2O(l).
(b) reaches a position of equilibrium [1]
The process is F (reversible reaction).

Recap

In a reversible reaction, the products can react together to re-form the original reactants. Equilibrium is the point where the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the backward reaction.
(c) involves particles changing from fixed positions to being mobile, but still touching [1]
The process is C (melting).

Recap

Melting is the change of physical state from solid to liquid. In a solid, particles are in fixed positions (vibration only). In a liquid, particles can slide over each other (mobile) but remain close together.
(d) are physical changes [1]
The processes are B (diffusion) AND C (melting).

Recap

A physical change does not involve the formation of a new substance (e.g., changes of state). A chemical change involves the formation of new substances (e.g., combustion, neutralisation).
(e) is caused by gas particles colliding with each other. [1]
The process is B (diffusion).

Recap

Diffusion is the spreading out and mixing of different particles due to their random movement and collisions.
Question 2 | 0620/41/O/N/24 | Topic: Atomic Structure / Periodic Table
This question is about atomic structure and the Periodic Table.
(a) Define the term nucleon number. [1]
Nucleon number is the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom.

Recap

The mass number (or nucleon number) is the sum of protons plus neutrons. The proton number (atomic number) is the number of protons only.
(b) State the connection between the number of occupied electron shells in an atom and the period number of that element. [1]
They are the same.

Recap

The number of occupied electron shells corresponds to the period number. For example, Magnesium (Period 3) has electrons in 3 shells (2,8,2).
(c) Write the electronic configuration of the following atom and ion.
Si
37Cl- [2]
Si: 2,8,4
37Cl-: 2,8,8

Recap

Silicon (Proton number 14) has 14 electrons: 2 in 1st, 8 in 2nd, 4 in 3rd. A Chloride ion (Cl-) gains one electron (17+1=18), resulting in a stable noble gas configuration of 2,8,8.
(d) Complete Table 2.1. [5]
1. Na: Number of electrons = 11
2. 19F-: Number of electrons = 10
3. Bottom row: Symbol = Ga, Mass Number = 69 (31 protons + 38 neutrons), Charge = 3+ (31 protons - 28 electrons).

Recap

Protons = Atomic number. Neutrons = Mass number - Atomic number. Electrons = Protons - Charge.
(e) A sample of thallium, Tl, contains two isotopes, 203Tl and 205Tl.
(i) Define the term isotopes. [2]
Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same proton number) with a different number of neutrons.

Recap

Isotopes have the same chemical properties because they have the same electronic configuration.
(ii) The relative abundance of 203Tl to 205Tl is in the ratio 3:7.
Calculate the relative atomic mass of thallium in the sample to one decimal place.
relative atomic mass = [2]
Calculation: (203 x 3 + 205 x 7) / 10
= 2044 / 10
Answer: 204.4

Recap

Relative atomic mass (Ar) is the weighted average mass of the isotopes. It is calculated using the mass and abundance of each isotope.
(iii) Suggest why these two isotopes have identical chemical properties. [1]
They have the same number of electrons (or same electronic configuration).

Recap

Chemical properties depend on the number of outer shell electrons. Since isotopes have the same proton number, they have the same electron count.
Question 3 | 0620/41/O/N/24 | Topic: Salts / Electrolysis
Copper(II) sulfate has the formula CuSO4. Aqueous copper(II) sulfate is a blue solution.
A sample of aqueous copper(II) sulfate is made by adding excess copper(II) oxide, CuO, to hot dilute sulfuric acid, H2SO4.
(a) Complete the symbol equation for this reaction. Include state symbols.
CuO(..........) + H2SO4(........) → CuSO4(........) + ................................(l) [2]
CuO(s) + H2SO4(aq) → CuSO4(aq) + H2O(l)

Recap

Acids react with metal oxides to produce a salt and water. Copper(II) oxide is an insoluble base (solid).
(b) State one observation which shows that copper(II) oxide is added in excess. [1]
No more solid dissolves (or solid remains at the bottom).

Recap

When making soluble salts from insoluble bases, the base is added in excess to ensure all the acid is neutralised. The excess solid is visible.
(c) Describe how aqueous copper(II) sulfate can be separated from the reaction mixture. [1]
Filtration.

Recap

Filtration separates a solid residue from a liquid filtrate. Here, it removes the excess copper(II) oxide.
(d) Crystals of hydrated copper(II) sulfate can be obtained from aqueous copper(II) sulfate by crystallisation.
(i) State what is meant by the term hydrated. [1]
A substance that is chemically combined with water.

Recap

Hydrated crystals contain water of crystallisation. Anhydrous substances contain no water.
(ii) Write the formula of hydrated copper(II) sulfate. [1]
CuSO4·5H2O

Recap

Copper(II) sulfate crystals are typically pentahydrate, meaning 5 molecules of water are associated with each formula unit.
(iii) Describe how this crystallisation is done. [2]
Heat (to evaporate some water) until saturation point is reached, then allow to cool.

Recap

Crystallisation involves heating a solution to evaporate water until the solution is saturated (crystallisation point), then cooling to allow crystals to form.
(e) Aqueous copper(II) sulfate undergoes electrolysis using graphite electrodes.
(i) State why aqueous copper(II) sulfate conducts electricity. [1]
It has mobile ions.

Recap

Electrolytes conduct electricity because the ions are free to move (in molten or aqueous states) and carry charge.
(ii) Give two reasons why the electrodes are made of graphite. [2]
1. It conducts electricity.
2. It is inert.

Recap

Graphite is used for electrodes because it is a good electrical conductor and is chemically inert (does not react with the electrolyte or products).
(iii) Describe how the appearance of the electrolyte changes during the electrolysis of aqueous copper(II) sulfate. [1]
It becomes lighter blue (or colour fades).

Recap

The blue colour of the solution is due to Cu2+ ions. As these ions are removed to form copper metal at the cathode, the concentration decreases, fading the colour.
(iv) Describe what is seen at the cathode during the electrolysis of aqueous copper(II) sulfate. [1]
A pink solid forms.

Recap

At the cathode (negative electrode), positive metal ions (Cu2+) gain electrons to form metal atoms (copper), which deposit as a solid.
(v) Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction at the anode. [3]
4OH- → 2H2O + O2 + 4e-

Recap

At the anode (positive electrode) in aqueous solutions containing sulfate ions, hydroxide ions (OH-) from water are discharged in preference to sulfate ions, producing oxygen gas and water.
(vi) State two differences seen if the electrolysis is repeated using copper electrodes instead of graphite electrodes. [2]
1. The colour remains constant (blue).
2. No bubbles at the anode (or anode dissolves/gets smaller).

Recap

With copper electrodes (active electrodes), the anode dissolves (Cu → Cu2+ + 2e-) replenishing the copper ions in solution, so the colour doesn't fade. No gas is produced at the anode.
Question 4 | 0620/41/O/N/24 | Topic: Redox / Moles / Bonding
When magnesium nitrate is heated strongly, magnesium oxide is formed.
(a) The equation for this reaction is shown.
2Mg(NO3)2 → 2MgO + 4NO2 + O2
(i) State the change in oxidation number of nitrogen, N, in this reaction. [2]
from _______ to _______
from +5 to +4.

Recap

Oxidation number rules: O is usually -2. In NO3-, N + 3(-2) = -1 → N = +5. In NO2, N + 2(-2) = 0 → N = +4. Decrease in oxidation number = Reduction.
(ii) Identify the element which is oxidised in this reaction. [1]
Oxygen.

Recap

Oxygen changes from -2 in the nitrate ion to 0 in O2 gas. An increase in oxidation number means oxidation.
(iii) Calculate the volume of NO2 gas, at r.t.p., formed when 7.40 g of Mg(NO3)2 is heated.
[4]
1. Mr of Mg(NO3)2 = 24 + 2(14 + 48) = 148
2. Moles of Mg(NO3)2 = 7.40 / 148 = 0.0500 mol
3. Moles of NO2 = 0.0500 x (4/2) = 0.100 mol
4. Volume of NO2 = 0.100 x 24,000 = 2400 cm3

Recap

Moles = Mass / Mr. Reaction stoichiometry (ratio) is used to find product moles. Volume of gas (cm3) = Moles x 24,000.
(b) Magnesium oxide, MgO, is an ionic compound.
Complete the dot-and-cross diagram in Fig. 4.1 of the ions in magnesium oxide.
Give the charges on each of the ions. [3]
Mg ion: Eight crosses in the second shell (2,8). Charge: 2+.
O ion: Six dots and two crosses in the second shell (2,8). Charge: 2-.

Recap

In ionic bonding, metal atoms lose electrons to form positive ions, and non-metal atoms gain electrons to form negative ions, achieving stable noble gas configurations.
(c) Oxygen is a covalent molecule.
Complete the dot-and-cross diagram in Fig. 4.2 of a molecule of oxygen. [2]
Two dot-cross pairs shared in the intersection (double bond).
Four non-bonding electrons on each Oxygen atom.

Recap

A covalent bond is a shared pair of electrons. Oxygen (Group VI) needs 2 electrons to complete its octet, so it shares 2 pairs, forming a double bond.

Advantage: Water is the only product (no CO2 / no pollutants) OR more efficient.
Disadvantage: Hydrogen is hard to store (needs high pressure/heavy tanks) OR lack of filling stations.

Recap

Fuel cells reduce pollution but face storage and infrastructure challenges compared to petrol engines.
(b) Hydrogen gas can be made from petroleum by a two-step procedure.
(i) Name the process used in step 1 (separation). [1]
Fractional distillation.

Recap

Fractional distillation separates petroleum into fractions based on different boiling points.
(ii) Name the process used in step 2 (conversion to smaller molecules). [1]
Cracking.

Recap

Cracking is the thermal decomposition of large alkane molecules into smaller alkanes, alkenes, and hydrogen.
(c) Calculate the number of hydrogen atoms in 44.0g of the ester methyl propanoate, CH3CH2COOCH3. [4]
1. Mr = 88.
2. Moles = 44.0 / 88 = 0.5.
3. Moles of H atoms = 0.5 x 8 = 4.0.
4. Number of atoms = 4.0 x 6.02 x 1023 = 2.408 x 1024.

Recap

Number of particles = Moles x Avogadro constant. Always check the formula to count the number of specific atoms (8 Hydrogens here).
(d) For each of the homologous series shown, name a member that contains six hydrogen atoms. [4]
alkanes: Ethane (C2H6)
alkenes: Propene (C3H6)
alcohols: Ethanol (C2H5OH)
carboxylic acids: Propanoic acid (C2H5COOH)
(Answers revealed above)

Recap

General formulas: Alkanes (CnH2n+2), Alkenes (CnH2n), Alcohols (CnH2n+1OH), Acids (CnH2n+1COOH).
(e) Unsaturated alkenes are converted into saturated alkanes by reaction with hydrogen gas.
(i) State why alkenes and alkanes are hydrocarbons. [1]
They contain carbon and hydrogen atoms only.

Recap

A hydrocarbon is a compound consisting ONLY of hydrogen and carbon.
(ii) State why alkenes are unsaturated. [1]
They have a carbon-carbon double bond (C=C).

Recap

Unsaturated compounds contain C=C bonds, allowing them to undergo addition reactions. Saturated compounds contain only single C-C bonds.
(iii) Name the catalyst needed to convert alkenes into alkanes. [1]
Nickel.

Recap

Hydrogenation of alkenes (adding hydrogen) requires a nickel catalyst and heat (150°C).
(iv) Explain why the conversion of alkenes into alkanes is an addition reaction. [1]
Only one product is formed.

Recap

In an addition reaction, two molecules combine to form a single larger molecule with no by-products.
