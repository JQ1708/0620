Question 1

Topics: Atomic Structure, Isotopes, Moles, Periodic Table

This question is about elements X, Y and Z.

An atom of element X is represented as ${}_{16}^{34}X$.

(a) (i) Name the different types of particles found in the nucleus of this atom of X. [2]

The particles found in the nucleus are protons and neutrons.

The nucleus of an atom contains protons and neutrons. Electrons are found outside the nucleus in electron shells (energy levels).

(a) (ii) What is the term for the total number of particles in the nucleus of an atom? [1]

This is the nucleon number (or mass number).

The mass number is the number of protons + neutrons in the nucleus. In atom symbols like ${}_{6}^{12}C$, the number at the top is the mass number.

(a) (iii) What is the total number of particles in the nucleus of an atom of ${}_{16}^{34}X$? [1]

The total number of particles in the nucleus (the nucleon number) is 34.

In the symbol ${}_{16}^{34}X$, the top number (34) is the mass number, which represents the total count of protons and neutrons. The bottom number (16) is the proton number.

(a) (iv) What is the electronic structure of the ion X²⁻? [1]

Element X has 16 protons, so it has 16 electrons with an electronic structure of 2.8.6. To form the X²⁻ ion, it gains two electrons. The new electronic structure is 2.8.8.

Electronic configuration is the arrangement of electrons in shells. Negative ions are formed when atoms gain one or more electrons, usually to achieve a stable, full outer shell, like a noble gas.

(a) (v) Suggest the formula of the compound formed between aluminium and X. [1]

Aluminium (Group III) forms an $Al^{3+}$ ion. Element X (Group VI) forms an $X^{2-}$ ion. To balance the charges, two $Al^{3+}$ ions (total charge 6+) are needed for every three $X^{2-}$ ions (total charge 6-). The formula is Al₂X₃.

The formula of an ionic compound can be found by balancing the positive and negative charges of the ions present. The overall charge of the compound must be zero.

(b) (i) What term is used to describe atoms of the same element with different numbers of particles in the nucleus? [1]

The term is isotopes.

Isotopes are atoms with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons. They have the same chemical properties because they have the same electronic configuration.

(b) (ii) Identify the atom against which the relative masses of all other atoms are compared. [1]

This atom is carbon-12 (${}^{12}C$).

Relative atomic mass, $A_r$, is the average mass of the isotopes of an element compared to $\frac{1}{12}$th the mass of an atom of carbon-12.

(b) (iii) What is the name of the amount of any substance that contains 6.02 x 10²³ particles? [1]

This amount is a mole.

A mole is the amount of substance that contains 6.02 x 10²³ defined particles (e.g., atoms, molecules, or ions).

(b) (iv) The constant 6.02 x 10²³ has a name. What is the name of this constant? [1]

This is the Avogadro constant.

The Avogadro constant is the number (6.02 x 10²³) of defined particles present in a mole of those particles. The number of particles = number of moles x Avogadro constant.

(d) (i) Element Z is in Period 3 and Group V. Identify element Z. [1]

Element Z is Phosphorus (P).

The Period number is the same as the number of occupied electron shells. The Group number (for Groups I to VII) is the same as the number of outer shell electrons. Therefore, an element in Period 3 and Group V has 3 electron shells and 5 outer electrons, giving an electronic configuration of 2.8.5. This element has 15 protons, which is Phosphorus.

(d) (ii) Explain in terms of electron transfer why Z behaves chemically as a non-metal. [2]

Element Z (Phosphorus) has 5 outer electrons. To achieve a stable, full outer shell, it is easier for it to gain three electrons rather than lose five. Non-metals typically gain electrons to form negative ions.

Negative ions are formed when atoms gain one or more electrons. This behaviour is characteristic of non-metals, which are found on the right side of the Periodic Table.

Question 2

Topics: Metallic Bonding, Ionic Bonding, Chemical Equations, Thermal Decomposition

Magnesium is a metal.

(a) Name and describe the bonding in magnesium. [4]

name

description of bonding

Name: Metallic bonding

Description: The bonding consists of a giant lattice of positive ions ($Mg^{2+}$) which are held together by a 'sea' of delocalised electrons. There is a strong electrostatic attraction between these positive ions and the negative electrons.

Metallic bonding is the strong electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and the sea of delocalised electrons. Delocalised electrons are electrons which are not associated with any particular atom.

(b) (i) Magnesium oxide, MgO, is formed when magnesium burns in oxygen. Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement of the ions in magnesium oxide. The inner shells have been drawn. Give the charges on the ions. [3]

Magnesium, a metal, loses its two outer shell electrons to form a positive ion with a stable octet. Oxygen, a non-metal, gains these two electrons to form a negative ion also with a stable octet.

  • The magnesium ion should be drawn with its (now outer) shell containing eight dots and enclosed in brackets with the charge 2+.
  • The oxide ion should be drawn with its outer shell containing six crosses (its original electrons) and two dots (from magnesium), enclosed in brackets with the charge 2-.

An ionic bond is the electrostatic force of attraction between positive and negative ions. Positive ions are formed by the loss of electrons (usually by metals) and negative ions are formed by the gain of electrons (usually by non-metals). Each ion typically achieves a stable noble gas configuration.

(b) (ii) Write the chemical equation for the reaction that occurs when magnesium burns in oxygen. [2]

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

(1 mark for correct species, 1 mark for correct balancing).

In a symbol equation, there must be the same number of each type of atom on each side of the equation. We use large numbers (coefficients) in front of formulas to balance the equation.

(c) (i) Magnesium oxide also forms when magnesium nitrate, $Mg(NO₃)₂$, is heated strongly. This is an endothermic reaction. Write the chemical equation for this reaction. [2]

2Mg(NO₃)₂ → 2MgO + 4NO₂ + O₂

(1 mark for correct product species, 1 mark for correct balancing).

Thermal decomposition is the breakdown of a substance into two or more different substances by heating. Group II nitrates (like magnesium nitrate) typically decompose to form the metal oxide, nitrogen dioxide, and oxygen.

(c) (ii) What type of reaction is this? [1]

This reaction is (thermal) decomposition.

Thermal decomposition is the breakdown of a substance into two or more different substances by heating strongly.

(c) (iii) Name two other compounds of magnesium that form magnesium oxide when heated. [2]

1. Magnesium carbonate ($MgCO₃$)
2. Magnesium hydroxide ($Mg(OH)₂$)

Metal carbonates and metal hydroxides (except for Group I) are bases that typically undergo thermal decomposition when heated. Carbonates decompose to form the metal oxide and carbon dioxide. Hydroxides decompose to form the metal oxide and water.

Question 3

Topics: Sulfuric Acid (Contact Process), Equilibrium, Rates, Organic Chemistry

Sulfur dioxide, $SO₂$, is used in the manufacture of sulfuric acid.

(a) In the first stage of the process, sulfur dioxide is obtained from sulfur-containing ores. Name one of these ores. [1]

A common sulfur-containing ore is zinc blende (which is zinc sulfide, $ZnS$).

The Contact process is used to make sulfuric acid. It uses sulfur dioxide, $SO₂$, which can be obtained from burning sulfur or by roasting sulfide ores (like zinc blende or pyrites).

(b) The next stage of the process is a reaction which can reach equilibrium. The equation for this stage is shown.

$2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) \rightleftharpoons 2SO₃(g)$

(i) Describe two features of an equilibrium. [2]

1. The reaction is reversible.
2. The rate of the forward reaction is equal to the rate of the reverse reaction. (This also means the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant in a closed system).

An equilibrium reaction is one where the reactants are converted to the products at the same rate as the products are converted back to reactants. This can only take place in a closed system.

(ii) Name the catalyst used in this stage. [1]

The catalyst is vanadium(V) oxide ($V₂O₅$).

The conditions for the Contact process (the reaction $2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) \rightleftharpoons 2SO₃(g)$) are 200 kPa pressure, 450 °C, and a vanadium(V) oxide catalyst, $V₂O₅$.

(iii) Why is a catalyst used? [1]

A catalyst is used to increase the rate of reaction.

A catalyst is a substance which increases the rate of a chemical reaction but is not used up in the reaction. It does this by lowering the activation energy.

(iv) Explain, in terms of particles, why a high temperature increases the rate of this reaction. [3]

At a higher temperature, the particles have more kinetic energy and move faster. This leads to two effects:

  1. The frequency of collisions increases (particles collide more often).
  2. A higher proportion of these collisions will have energy greater than the activation energy (E > Eₐ), leading to more successful collisions.

Increasing the temperature increases the rate of reaction because particles have more kinetic energy. This results in an increased frequency of collisions, and more importantly, more of the colliding particles have energy above the activation energy, leading to more effective collisions.

(v) In this stage, only a moderate temperature of 450 °C is used. What does this suggest about the forward reaction? [1]

This suggests the forward reaction is exothermic. A high temperature would increase the rate but decrease the equilibrium yield, so a moderate (compromise) temperature is used.

For an exothermic reaction (where $\Delta H$ is negative), an increase in temperature moves the position of equilibrium in the direction of the reactants (to the left), decreasing the yield. A compromise temperature is often used to get a fast enough rate and an acceptable yield.

(vi) Calculate the percentage by mass of sulfur in sulfur trioxide, $SO₃$. [2]

1. Find the relative formula mass ($M_r$) of $SO₃$:
$M_r$($SO₃$) = $A_r$(S) + 3 x $A_r$(O) = 32 + (3 x 16) = 80.
2. Calculate the percentage by mass of sulfur:
% S = (Mass of S / $M_r$ of $SO₃$) x 100 = (32 / 80) x 100 = 40%.

Percentage composition by mass = $\frac{\text{mass of a particular element in a mole of a compound}}{\text{mass of one mole of the compound}} \times 100$. You must sum the relative atomic masses ($A_r$) to find the relative formula mass ($M_r$) first.

(c) Concentrated sulfuric acid is a dehydrating agent which can chemically remove water from substances. Both hydrated copper(II) sulfate crystals and sucrose (a sugar), $C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁$, can be completely dehydrated by concentrated sulfuric acid. Name the solid product formed in each case. [2]

hydrated copper(II) sulfate crystals

sucrose

hydrated copper(II) sulfate crystals: anhydrous copper(II) sulfate
sucrose: carbon

A hydrated solid contains water of crystallisation, which can be removed to form the anhydrous solid (e.g., blue $CuSO₄ \cdot 5H₂O$ becomes white $CuSO₄$). When dehydrating carbohydrates like sucrose ($C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁$), the elements of water (H and O) are removed, leaving behind black carbon.

(d) When propan-1-ol is heated with concentrated sulfuric acid as a catalyst an unsaturated hydrocarbon of relative molecular mass 42 is formed and one other product.

(i) What is meant by the term unsaturated? [1]

Unsaturated means the hydrocarbon contains at least one C=C double bond (or C≡C triple bond); it does not all contain single C-C bonds.

Unsaturated compounds contain C=C double bonds or C≡C triple bonds. Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons because they have one or more C=C double bonds.

(ii) Write the chemical equation for this reaction. [2]

Propan-1-ol ($C₃H₇OH$) dehydrates to form an alkene ($C₃H₆$) and water. The $M_r$ of $C₃H₆$ = (3 x 12) + (6 x 1) = 36 + 6 = 42, which matches the question.
C₃H₇OH → C₃H₆ + H₂O

The dehydration of an alcohol (e.g., propanol) is the reverse of the hydration of an alkene (e.g., propene). This reaction is an elimination reaction, where a small molecule (water) is removed from a larger molecule.

(iii) Name the unsaturated hydrocarbon formed. [1]

The hydrocarbon $C₃H₆$ is propene.

Organic compounds are named based on the number of carbon atoms: meth- (1C), eth- (2C), prop- (3C), but- (4C). An alkene (unsaturated hydrocarbon with a C=C bond) has the ending -ene. Therefore, a 3-carbon alkene is propene.

Question 4

Topics: Acids and Bases, Titrations, Salts

This question is about reactions of bases and acids.

(a) Ammonia is a gas at room temperature. What is the test for ammonia gas? Describe the positive result of this test. [2]

test

result

Test: Place damp red litmus paper in the gas.
Result: The litmus paper turns blue.

Ammonia ($NH₃$) is an alkaline gas. It turns damp red litmus paper blue. The litmus paper must be damp so the ammonia gas can dissolve and form hydroxide ions ($OH⁻$).

(b) Ammonia reacts with water to form ions.

$NH₃ + H₂O \rightleftharpoons NH₄⁺ + OH⁻$

(i) How does this equation show that ammonia, $NH₃$, behaves as a base? [1]

It acts as a base because it accepts a proton (H⁺) from the water molecule to form the ammonium ion ($NH₄⁺$).

An acid is a proton (H⁺) donor. A base is a proton (H⁺) acceptor. In this equilibrium, the $H₂O$ donates a proton, so it acts as an acid, and the $NH₃$ accepts it, so it acts as a base.

(b) (ii) Aqueous ammonia is described as a weak base. Suggest the pH of aqueous ammonia. [1]

pH =

pH = 9 (Any pH from 8-11 is acceptable). A strong base would be pH 13-14.

Weak acids and weak bases are only partially dissociated (ionised) in aqueous solution, resulting in a lower concentration of H⁺ (for weak acids) or OH⁻ (for weak bases) compared to a strong acid/base of the same concentration. This means a weak base has a pH above 7 but lower than a strong base (e.g., pH 9-11 instead of 13-14).

(b) (iii) Describe what is seen when aqueous ammonia is added to aqueous copper(II) sulfate, until no further change is seen. [3]

First, a light blue precipitate (of copper(II) hydroxide) is formed.
Then, as excess ammonia is added, this precipitate dissolves.
This forms a deep blue solution (or dark blue solution).

Ions of copper(II), $Cu^{2+}$, give coloured precipitates with aqueous ammonia (or sodium hydroxide). A key test is that the light blue precipitate of copper(II) hydroxide dissolves in excess aqueous ammonia to form a deep blue solution, but it does not dissolve in excess sodium hydroxide.

(c) (i) Aqueous sodium hydroxide, $NaOH(aq)$, is a strong alkali that reacts with dilute sulfuric acid exothermically. What type of reaction is this? [1]

This is a neutralisation reaction.

A neutralisation reaction occurs between an acid and a base. Bases are substances which react with acids to produce a salt and water.

(c) (ii) Complete the equation for the reaction between aqueous sodium hydroxide and dilute sulfuric acid. [2]

$2NaOH + H₂SO₄ →$

$2NaOH + H₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O$

Acids react with metal hydroxides (which are bases) to produce a salt and water. The salt formed here is sodium sulfate. Remember to balance the equation: 2 $Na$ atoms and (2+4)=6 $O$ atoms and (2+2)=4 $H$ atoms on both sides.

(d) (i) A student wanted to find the concentration of some dilute sulfuric acid by titration. The student found that 25.0 cm³ of 0.0400 mol/dm³ $NaOH(aq)$ reacted exactly with 20.0 cm³ of $H₂SO₄(aq)$. Name a suitable indicator to use in this titration. [1]

A suitable indicator would be methyl orange (or thymolphthalein).

An acid-base indicator is a substance that changes colour at the end point of a titration. For a strong acid ($H₂SO₄$) and strong base ($NaOH$) titration, both methyl orange (red in acid, yellow in alkali) and thymolphthalein (colourless in acid, blue in alkali) are suitable.

(d) (ii) Calculate the concentration of the $H₂SO₄(aq)$ in mol/dm³ using the following steps. [3]
  • Calculate the number of moles of NaOH in 25.0 cm³.
  • Deduce the number of moles of $H₂SO₄$ that reacted with the 25.0 cm³ of $NaOH(aq)$.
  • Calculate the concentration of $H₂SO₄(aq)$ in mol/dm³.

Step 1: Moles of NaOH
Volume = 25.0 cm³ = 0.0250 dm³
Moles = Concentration x Volume
Moles = 0.0400 mol/dm³ x 0.0250 dm³ = 0.00100 mol

Step 2: Moles of $H₂SO₄$
From the equation $2NaOH + H₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O$, the mole ratio is 2 : 1.
Moles $H₂SO₄$ = Moles NaOH / 2
Moles $H₂SO₄$ = 0.00100 / 2 = 0.000500 mol

Step 3: Concentration of $H₂SO₄$
Volume = 20.0 cm³ = 0.0200 dm³
Concentration = Moles / Volume
Concentration = 0.000500 mol / 0.0200 dm³ = 0.0250 mol/dm³

To find the concentration of an unknown solution by titration:

  1. Calculate the moles of the known solution (moles = conc. x vol in dm³).
  2. Use the mole ratio from the balanced chemical equation to find the moles of the unknown solution.
  3. Calculate the concentration of the unknown solution (conc. = moles / vol in dm³).

(d) (iii) Calculate the concentration of the 0.0400 mol/dm³ $NaOH(aq)$ in g/dm³. [2]

1. Find the relative formula mass ($M_r$) of NaOH:
$M_r$(NaOH) = $A_r$(Na) + $A_r$(O) + $A_r$(H) = 23 + 16 + 1 = 40 g/mol.
2. Convert concentration (mol/dm³) to (g/dm³):
Conc (g/dm³) = Conc (mol/dm³) x $M_r$
Conc (g/dm³) = 0.0400 x 40 = 1.6 g/dm³.

Concentration can be measured in g/dm³ or mol/dm³. To convert between them, use the formula: $\text{mass} = \text{moles} \times M_r$. Therefore, $\text{g/dm³} = \text{mol/dm³} \times M_r$ (relative formula mass).

(b) Alcohols can be oxidised to form carboxylic acids. Name a suitable oxidising agent for this reaction. [1]

A suitable oxidising agent is (acidified) potassium manganate(VII).

The oxidation of ethanol produces ethanoic acid. This can be achieved by heating with acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII) (which turns from purple to colourless) or by bacterial oxidation (used to make vinegar).

(c) Alcohols can be partially oxidised to form aldehydes. Aldehydes are a homologous series of organic compounds. Partial oxidation is achieved by reacting an alcohol with the oxidising agent in distillation apparatus as shown.

(i) Name apparatus A. [1]

Apparatus A is a (Liebig) condenser.

Distillation is a process used to separate liquids with different boiling points, or a solvent from a solute. It involves boiling the liquid in a flask and then condensing the vapour back into a liquid using a condenser, which is collected as the distillate.

(c) (ii) On the diagram, use one arrow to show where water enters apparatus A. [1]

An arrow should be drawn pointing into the lower inlet of the condenser's outer jacket.

For a condenser to work efficiently, the outer jacket must be completely filled with cold water. To achieve this, water enters at the lowest point (the lower inlet) and exits at the highest point (the upper outlet).

A homologous series is a family of similar compounds. Each member differs from the next by a −CH₂− group. The prefixes meth- (1C), eth- (2C), prop- (3C), and but- (4C) denote the number of carbon atoms.

(d) (ii) Deduce the general formula of aldehydes. [1]

The general formula is CₙH₂ₙO (for n≥1).

A general formula can be written for each homologous series. For example, alkanes are $CₙH₂ₙ₊₂$ and alkenes are $CₙH₂ₙ$. Based on the pattern in the table ($C₂H₄O$, $C₃H₆O$), the general formula for aldehydes is $CₙH₂ₙO$.

(e) (i) The structural formula of ethanal is shown. [...] What is the name given to the reactive part of any organic molecule? [1]

This is the functional group.

A functional group is an atom or group of atoms that gives a compound its particular chemical properties and defines its homologous series. For aldehydes, the functional group is the -CHO group.

(e) (ii) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement of a molecule of ethanal. Inner shells have been drawn. [3]

The completed diagram should show:

  • One C-C single bond (one shared pair of electrons, 1 dot and 1 cross).
  • Four C-H single bonds (each with one shared pair).
  • One C=O double bond (two shared pairs, 2 dots and 2 crosses).
  • The oxygen atom must also have two lone pairs of electrons (4 non-bonding electrons) in its outer shell.

A single covalent bond is a shared pair of electrons. A double covalent bond is two shared pairs of electrons. In dot-and-cross diagrams, electrons from different atoms are shown with different symbols (dots and crosses) to show their origin.

(f) (i) Propanone belongs to a homologous series called ketones. [...] Propanone has the same molecular formula as propanal, $C₃H₆O$. What term is used to describe molecules with different structures but with the same molecular formula? [1]

The term is (structural) isomers.

Structural isomers are compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural formulae (i.e., the atoms are arranged in a different way).

(f) (ii) Suggest the structure of propanone, $C₃H₆O$. Show all of the atoms and all of the bonds. [2]

The structure should show a chain of three carbon atoms. The central carbon atom (carbon-2) is bonded to the oxygen atom with a C=O double bond. The two end carbon atoms are each bonded to three hydrogen atoms.
(Displayed formula for $CH₃-CO-CH₃$).

Structural isomers have different arrangements. Propanal (an aldehyde) has the C=O bond on the end carbon: $CH₃-CH₂-CHO$. Propanone (a ketone) must have the C=O bond on a non-end carbon, which for a 3-carbon chain means the central carbon: $CH₃-CO-CH₃$.
