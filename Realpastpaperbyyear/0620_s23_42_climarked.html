Question 1: Oxides

0620/42/M/J/23 Q1 | Topics: Oxides, Reactivity, Industrial Chemistry

A list of oxides, A to H, is shown.

  • A calcium oxide
  • B aluminium oxide
  • C silicon(IV) oxide
  • D sulfur dioxide
  • E carbon dioxide
  • F iron(III) oxide
  • G silver oxide
  • H carbon monoxide

Answer the following questions about the oxides, A to H. Each letter may be used once, more than once or not at all.

State which of the oxides, A to H:

(a) is responsible for acid rain [1]

The oxide responsible for acid rain is D, sulfur dioxide.

Recap

Acid rain is rain with a pH of less than about 5. It forms when sulfur dioxide (and oxides of nitrogen) react with oxygen and water in the atmosphere. Sulfur dioxide primarily comes from the combustion of fossil fuels that contain sulfur impurities.

(b) has a giant covalent structure [1]

The oxide with a giant covalent structure is C, silicon(IV) oxide.

Recap

A giant covalent structure consists of many atoms joined by strong covalent bonds in a continuous three-dimensional lattice. Silicon(IV) oxide, similar to diamond, has this structure, which gives it a very high melting point and makes it hard.

(c) is a reducing agent in the blast furnace [1]

The reducing agent in the blast furnace is H, carbon monoxide.

Recap

In the blast furnace, carbon (coke) burns to form carbon dioxide, which is then reduced by more hot carbon to form carbon monoxide. This carbon monoxide is the main reducing agent that reduces the iron(III) oxide (iron ore) to molten iron.

(d) is the main constituent of bauxite [1]

The main constituent of bauxite is B, aluminium oxide.

Recap

Bauxite is the primary ore from which aluminium is extracted. The bauxite is first purified to obtain aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃). This compound is then dissolved in molten cryolite and extracted by electrolysis.

(e) is the main impurity in iron ore [1]

The main impurity in iron ore is C, silicon(IV) oxide.

Recap

Iron ore (hematite) is not pure iron(III) oxide; it contains acidic impurities, primarily silicon(IV) oxide (sand). In the blast furnace, calcium oxide (from limestone) is added to react with this impurity. This basic oxide reacts with the acidic oxide to form molten slag (calcium silicate), which is removed.

(f) can be reduced by heating with copper [1]

The oxide that can be reduced by heating with copper is G, silver oxide. This is because copper is more reactive than silver.

Recap

The ability of a metal to reduce another metal's oxide depends on their relative positions in the reactivity series. A more reactive metal can displace a less reactive metal from its compound. Since copper is more reactive than silver, it can reduce silver oxide to silver, while the copper itself is oxidised.

Question 2: Atomic Structure & Halogens

0620/42/M/J/23 Q2 | Topics: Group VII, Atomic Structure, Isotopes, Reactivity

Fluorine, chlorine and bromine are in Group VII of the Periodic Table.

(a) State the name given to Group VII elements. [1]

The name given to Group VII elements is the halogens.

Recap

The Group VII elements are known as the halogens. They are diatomic non-metals, meaning they exist as molecules of two atoms (e.g., Cl₂, Br₂).

(b) Explain why Group VII elements have similar chemical properties. [1]

Group VII elements have similar chemical properties because they all have the same number of electrons in their outer shell (seven outer electrons).

Recap

An element's chemical properties are primarily determined by the number of electrons in its outer shell (valence electrons). All halogens are in Group VII, which means they all have seven outer electrons. This similarity in electronic configuration causes them to have similar chemical properties, such as forming ions with a -1 charge.

Recap

The physical states and colours of the halogens show a clear trend. (e) Chlorine displaces bromine from aqueous potassium bromide but does not displace fluorine from aqueous sodium fluoride.

(i) Write the symbol equation for the reaction between chlorine and aqueous potassium bromide. [2]

Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂

Recap

This is a displacement reaction. A more reactive halogen will displace a less reactive halogen from its halide salt solution. Chlorine is higher in Group VII than bromine, so it is more reactive.

(ii) State why chlorine does not displace fluorine from aqueous sodium fluoride. [1]

Chlorine does not displace fluorine because chlorine is less reactive than fluorine.

Recap

The reactivity of the halogens decreases down the group. Fluorine is at the top of the group and is the most reactive halogen. Chlorine is below fluorine, so it is less reactive and cannot displace fluorine from a solution of its halide.

(f) Aqueous silver nitrate is a colourless solution containing Ag⁺(aq) ions.

(i) Describe what is seen when aqueous silver nitrate is added to aqueous sodium chloride. [1]

A white precipitate is seen.

Recap

Aqueous silver nitrate is used as a test for halide ions. It forms a precipitate (an insoluble salt) with chloride, bromide, and iodide ions. For chloride ions (Cl⁻), a white precipitate of silver chloride (AgCl) is formed.

(ii) Write the ionic equation for the reaction between aqueous silver nitrate and aqueous sodium chloride. Include state symbols. [3]

Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s)

Recap

An ionic equation shows only the species that change during the reaction. In this precipitation reaction, the sodium ions (Na⁺) and nitrate ions (NO₃⁻) are spectator ions and do not change. The equation shows the silver ions and chloride ions combining to form the insoluble solid precipitate.

The Contact process is the industrial method used for manufacturing sulfuric acid.

The sulfur dioxide needed for the Contact process is obtained either by burning sulfur, which is found as an element, or by roasting sulfide ores (e.g., zinc blende, ZnS) in a current of air.

The conversion of sulfur dioxide to sulfur trioxide is a reversible reaction which can reach equilibrium.

2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)

(ii) State two features of an equilibrium. [2]

  1. ...............................................................................................................................................
  2. ...............................................................................................................................................
  1. The rate of the forward reaction is equal to the rate of the reverse reaction.
  2. The concentrations of all reactants and products remain constant.

Recap

A chemical equilibrium is a dynamic state that can only be reached in a closed system. It has two key features: 1. The forward and reverse reactions are occurring at the same rate. 2. The concentrations of reactants and products are constant (they are not changing).

(iii) State the typical conditions and name the catalyst used in the conversion of sulfur dioxide to sulfur trioxide. [3]

temperature .............................. °C

Recap

The key stage in the Contact process is the reversible reaction 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g). The typical industrial conditions used are a temperature of 450 °C, a pressure of 200 kPa (2 atmospheres), and a vanadium(V) oxide catalyst.

(iv) Complete Table 3.1 to show the effect, if any, when the following changes are applied to the conversion of sulfur dioxide to sulfur trioxide.

2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)

The forward reaction is exothermic. Only use the words increases, decreases or no change. [4]

change effect on the rate of the forward reaction effect on the concentration of SO₃(g) at equilibrium

change effect on the rate of the forward reaction effect on the concentration of SO₃(g) at equilibrium

Recap

According to Le Chatelier's principle:

  • Temperature: The forward reaction is exothermic. Decreasing the temperature will favour the exothermic direction, shifting the equilibrium to the right and increasing the concentration of SO₃.
  • Pressure: There are 3 moles of gas on the left (2 + 1) and 2 moles on the right. Increasing the pressure will shift the equilibrium to the side with fewer moles of gas, which is the right side, increasing the concentration of SO₃.
  • Catalyst: A catalyst increases the rate of *both* the forward and reverse reactions equally. It does not affect the position of equilibrium, only the speed at which equilibrium is reached.

(v) Explain in terms of collision theory why reducing the temperature decreases the rate of the forward reaction. [3]

Reducing the temperature decreases the kinetic energy of the particles. This causes the particles to move more slowly, leading to a decrease in the frequency of collisions. More importantly, a smaller proportion of these collisions will have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy, so the frequency of successful collisions decreases.

Recap

Collision theory states that for a reaction to occur, particles must collide with sufficient energy. Activation energy (Eₐ) is the minimum energy required for a collision to be successful. When temperature decreases, particles have less kinetic energy. This leads to two effects: 1. Fewer collisions per second (lower collision frequency). 2. A much smaller fraction of these collisions will have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy.

Question 4: Acids and Bases

0620/42/M/J/23 Q4 | Topics: Acids, Bases, pH, Titrations, Covalent Bonding

Solid sodium hydroxide is a base which dissolves to form an aqueous solution, NaOH(aq).

(a) State what is meant by the term base. [1]

A base is a proton acceptor.

Recap

In terms of proton transfer, an acid is a proton (H⁺) donor, and a base is a proton (H⁺) acceptor. Bases are also substances that react with acids to produce a salt and water.

(b) State the term given to a base which dissolves to form an aqueous solution. [1]

The term given is an alkali.

Recap

An alkali is a soluble base. When dissolved in water, an alkali releases hydroxide ions (OH⁻), which makes the solution alkaline (pH > 7).

(c) State the colour of thymolphthalein in NaOH(aq). [1]

The colour will be blue.

Recap

Acid-base indicators change colour at different pH values. Thymolphthalein is colourless in acidic solutions and blue in alkaline solutions. NaOH(aq) is a strong alkali.

(d) Complete the word equation for the reaction of NaOH(aq) with ammonium chloride. [3]

sodium hydroxide + ammonium chloride → .................... + .................... + ....................

sodium hydroxide + ammonium chloride → sodium chloride + water + ammonia

Recap

A characteristic property of alkalis (soluble bases) is their reaction with ammonium salts. When warmed, they produce a salt, water, and ammonia gas. This reaction is used as a test for ammonium ions.

(e) Some metal oxides react with NaOH(aq).

(i) State the term given to metal oxides which react with bases such as NaOH(aq). [1]

The term is amphoteric oxides.

Recap

Amphoteric oxides are oxides that can react with both acids and bases (or alkalis) to form a salt and water. Most metal oxides are basic, but some, like aluminium oxide and zinc oxide, are amphoteric.

(ii) Name a metal oxide which reacts with NaOH(aq). [1]

Aluminium oxide or zinc oxide.

Recap

Aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃) and zinc oxide (ZnO) are the two common amphoteric oxides you need to know. They react with acids (e.g., HCl) and also with strong alkalis (e.g., NaOH(aq)).

(f) Ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH, is a weak acid.

(i) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram in Fig. 4.1 of a molecule of ethanoic acid. [3]

The dot-and-cross diagram for ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH) should show:

  • The first carbon atom bonded to 3 hydrogen atoms (single bonds) and the second carbon atom (single bond).
  • The second carbon atom bonded to the first carbon (single bond), one oxygen atom (double bond), and another oxygen atom (single bond).
  • The single-bonded oxygen atom also bonded to a hydrogen atom (single bond).
  • All outer shells should be complete: 8 electrons for each C and O, 2 electrons for each H.
  • The C=O double bond should show 4 shared electrons.

Recap

Dot-and-cross diagrams show the outer shell electrons. A covalent bond is a shared pair of electrons. Ethanoic acid has single C-H, C-C, and C-O bonds, a double C=O bond, and a single O-H bond. Each atom (except H) should achieve a stable octet (8 outer electrons), and H should achieve a stable duet (2 outer electrons).

(ii) Suggest the pH of dilute ethanoic acid. [1]

pH = ..............................

pH = 4 (or any value from 3 to 6)

Recap

Ethanoic acid is a weak acid, meaning it only partially dissociates (ionises) in water. This produces a lower concentration of H⁺ ions compared to a strong acid of the same concentration. Therefore, its pH will be acidic (less than 7) but not extremely low (e.g., pH 3-6).

(iii) Complete the symbol equation to show the dissociation of ethanoic acid. [3]

CH₃COOH ⇌ .................... + ....................

CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺

Recap

Weak acids, like ethanoic acid, only partially ionise in water. This is shown by a reversible reaction sign (⇌). They form a negative ion (the ethanoate ion) and a hydrogen ion (H⁺), which is responsible for the acidity.

(iv) Write the ionic equation for the reaction when an acid neutralises a soluble base. [1]

H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O

Recap

Neutralisation is the reaction between an acid and a base. In aqueous solution, all acids produce H⁺ ions and all soluble bases (alkalis) produce OH⁻ ions. The essential reaction of neutralisation is the combination of these two ions to form neutral water.

(g) In a titration, 25.0 cm³ of 0.0800 mol/dm³ aqueous potassium hydroxide, KOH(aq), is neutralised by 20.0 cm³ of dilute sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄(aq).

2KOH(aq) + H₂SO₄(aq) → K₂SO₄(aq) + 2H₂O(l)

Calculate the concentration of H₂SO₄, in g/dm³, using the following steps. [5]

  • Calculate the number of moles of KOH used.
  • Determine the number of moles of H₂SO₄ which react with the KOH.
  • Calculate the concentration of H₂SO₄ in mol/dm³.
  • Calculate the concentration of H₂SO₄ in g/dm³.
  • Moles of KOH:
    Volume = 25.0 cm³ = 0.0250 dm³
    Moles = Concentration x Volume = 0.0800 mol/dm³ x 0.0250 dm³ = 0.00200 mol
  • Moles of H₂SO₄:
    From the equation, the mole ratio is 2 KOH : 1 H₂SO₄.
    Moles H₂SO₄ = Moles KOH / 2 = 0.00200 / 2 = 0.00100 mol
  • Concentration of H₂SO₄ (mol/dm³):
    Volume = 20.0 cm³ = 0.0200 dm³
    Concentration = Moles / Volume = 0.00100 mol / 0.0200 dm³ = 0.0500 mol/dm³
  • Concentration of H₂SO₄ (g/dm³):
    Mᵣ of H₂SO₄ = (2 x 1) + 32 + (4 x 16) = 2 + 32 + 64 = 98 g/mol
    Concentration (g/dm³) = Concentration (mol/dm³) x Mᵣ
    = 0.0500 mol/dm³ x 98 g/mol = 4.90 g/dm³

Recap

Titration calculations involve several steps:

  1. Calculate the moles of the 'known' substance (here, KOH) using moles = concentration × volume. (Remember to convert cm³ to dm³ by dividing by 1000).
  2. Use the molar ratio from the balanced equation to find the moles of the 'unknown' substance (H₂SO₄).
  3. Calculate the concentration of the 'unknown' substance using concentration = moles / volume.
  4. To convert from mol/dm³ to g/dm³, multiply by the relative formula mass (Mᵣ).

Question 5: Organic Chemistry (Alkanes & Alkenes)

0620/42/M/J/23 Q5 | Topics: Organic Chemistry, Alkanes, Alkenes, Isomerism

Propane and propene both react with chlorine.

(a) When a molecule of propane, C₃H₈, reacts with chlorine in the presence of ultraviolet light, one atom of hydrogen is replaced by one atom of chlorine.

(i) State the term given to reactions in which one atom in an alkane is replaced by another atom. [1]

The term is substitution.

Recap

A substitution reaction is one in which one atom or group of atoms replaces another atom or group of atoms. Alkanes undergo substitution with halogens in the presence of UV light.

(ii) State the purpose of ultraviolet light in this reaction. [1]

The ultraviolet light provides the activation energy needed to start the reaction (by breaking the Cl-Cl bonds).

Recap

Activation energy (Eₐ) is the minimum energy that colliding particles must have in order to react. Some reactions, like the substitution of alkanes, have a high activation energy that can be supplied by ultraviolet (UV) light.

(iii) State the term given to any reaction which requires ultraviolet light. [1]

The term is photochemical reaction.

Recap

A photochemical reaction is a chemical reaction that is initiated by the absorption of energy in the form of light. Photosynthesis and the chlorination of methane are common examples.

(iv) Write the symbol equation for the reaction between propane and chlorine. [2]

C₃H₈ + Cl₂ → C₃H₇Cl + HCl

Recap

In this substitution reaction, one chlorine atom from a Cl₂ molecule replaces one hydrogen atom on the propane molecule. The remaining chlorine atom combines with the displaced hydrogen atom to form hydrogen chloride (HCl).

(b) A molecule of propene, C₃H₆, is unsaturated and will react with chlorine at room temperature.

(i) State why propene is an unsaturated molecule. [1]

Propene is unsaturated because it has a carbon-carbon double bond (C=C).

Recap

Unsaturated hydrocarbons contain one or more C=C double bonds or C≡C triple bonds. Saturated hydrocarbons (like alkanes) contain only C-C single bonds.

(ii) Give the structural formula of the product of this reaction. [1]

CH₃CHClCH₂Cl (or CH₂ClCHClCH₃)

Recap

Alkenes undergo addition reactions. The double bond breaks, and an atom from the reacting molecule (e.g., Cl₂) adds to each of the two carbon atoms from the original double bond. This reaction does not require UV light.

(c) Propene undergoes addition reactions with steam. There are two possible products, A and B. Draw the displayed formula and name each product. [4]

Product A:

Name: propan-1-ol

Product B:

Name: propan-2-ol

Recap

The addition of steam (H₂O) to an alkene (like propene) is called hydration and produces an alcohol. The H and OH from the water add across the double bond. With propene, the OH can add to the first carbon (forming propan-1-ol) or the second carbon (forming propan-2-ol), resulting in structural isomers.

Question 6: Esters and Polymers

Carboxylic acids can be converted to esters.

(a) Name the ester formed when butanoic acid, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH, reacts with ethanol, CH₃CH₂OH. [1]

ethyl butanoate

Recap

When naming esters, the first part of the name comes from the alcohol (e.g., ethanol → ethyl) and the second part comes from the carboxylic acid (e.g., butanoic acid → butanoate).

(b) Identify the other product formed in this reaction. [1]

water

Recap

The formation of an ester from a carboxylic acid and an alcohol is a condensation reaction. The -OH group from the carboxylic acid and the -H from the alcohol's -OH group are removed to form a molecule of water (H₂O).

Molecular formula of ethyl butanoate: C₃H₇COOC₂H₅ = C₆H₁₂O₂

The simplest whole number ratio is C₃H₆O.

Recap

The molecular formula shows the actual number of each atom in one molecule. The empirical formula shows the simplest whole number ratio of the atoms. To find it, divide the subscripts in the molecular formula by their highest common factor (in this case, 2).

One full repeat unit consists of the parts from both monomers.

Recap

PET (polyethylene terephthalate) is a polyester. A repeat unit is the smallest section of the polymer chain that, if repeated, would build the entire chain. For a condensation polymer like PET, the repeat unit is formed from the two different monomers.

(ii) Draw the structures of the monomers which make up PET. Draw the functional groups using displayed formulae. [2]

Recap

Polyesters are formed from two different monomers: a dicarboxylic acid (a molecule with two -COOH groups) and a diol (a molecule with two -OH groups). A small molecule, water, is eliminated during the reaction.

The type of polymerisation is condensation polymerisation.
