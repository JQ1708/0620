Question 1 (0620/41_Winter_2017_Q3) - Redox Reactions, Making Use Of Metals

(a) When magnesium is added to aqueous copper(II) sulfate a reaction occurs. The ionic equation for the reaction is shown.

Mg + Cu²⁺ → Mg²⁺ + Cu

(i) Give one change you would observe during this reaction. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
A brown/orange solid forms, and/or the blue solution becomes paler/colourless.

(ii) Explain why this is a redox reaction. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
It is a redox reaction because magnesium is oxidised (loses electrons) and copper(II) ions are reduced (gain electrons).

(iii) Identify the oxidising agent in this reaction. Give a reason for your answer. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The oxidising agent is the copper(II) ion (Cu²⁺) because it gains electrons (is reduced).

(iv) A redox reaction occurs when magnesium is heated with iron(III) oxide. Write a chemical equation for the reaction between magnesium and iron(III) oxide. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

3Mg + Fe₂O₃ → 3MgO + 2Fe

(b) The metal iron and the alloy steel are commonly used materials. A problem with them is that they rust.

(i) How does painting iron and steel prevent rusting? [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Painting provides a barrier to prevent air/oxygen and water from reaching the surface of the iron/steel.

(ii) Magnesium blocks can be attached to the bottom of steel boats. Explain how the magnesium blocks prevent the whole of the bottom of the boat from rusting. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
This is sacrificial protection. Magnesium is more reactive than iron/steel, so the magnesium corrodes preferentially (sacrificially) instead of the steel.

(iii) Replacing the magnesium blocks with copper blocks does not prevent rusting. Explain why the copper blocks do not prevent rusting. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Copper is less reactive than iron/steel, so it cannot provide sacrificial protection.

  • a chemical equation for the reduction of iron(III) oxide,
  • an explanation for using limestone in the blast furnace. [6]

Tap to reveal answer
The carbon atoms are of a different size to the iron atoms. This disrupts the regular layers, making it more difficult for them to slide.

Question 3 (0620/41_Winter_2017_Q6) - Making Use Of Metals, The Behavior Of Metals, Electricity And Chemical Changes

Aluminium is extracted from aluminium oxide by electrolysis.

(a) Why is aluminium not extracted by heating aluminium oxide with carbon? [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Aluminium is more reactive than carbon.

(b) Aluminium oxide is an ionic compound with a high melting point.

(i) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement in one of the oxide ions present in aluminium oxide. Include the charge on the oxide ion. One of the aluminium ions is shown. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The oxide ion should have a full outer shell with 8 electrons (6 dots and 2 crosses to show they came from aluminium) and a charge of 2-.

(ii) The melting point of aluminium oxide is above 2000 °C. Explain why aluminium oxide has a high melting point. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
It has a giant ionic lattice structure with strong electrostatic forces of attraction between the positive and negative ions, which require a large amount of energy to overcome.

(c) Aluminium can be extracted by electrolysis using the apparatus shown.

(i) Name the type of particle responsible for the transfer of charge in the wires, and the electrolyte. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
In the wires: electrons.
In the electrolyte: ions.

(ii) Give two reasons why cryolite is used. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
1. It lowers the operating temperature, saving energy costs.
2. It acts as a solvent for the aluminium oxide.

(iii) Write the ionic half-equation for the formation of aluminium during the electrolysis. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al

(iv) Explain how carbon dioxide gas is formed at the anodes. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
Oxygen is produced at the positive anodes. The anodes are made of carbon. At the high temperatures, the oxygen reacts with the carbon anode to form carbon dioxide.

(d) When a piece of aluminium is placed in dilute hydrochloric acid, there is no immediate visible reaction. If the aluminium is left in the dilute hydrochloric acid for several hours, bubbles start to form. Explain why aluminium does not react immediately with dilute hydrochloric acid. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Aluminium is coated with a protective, unreactive layer of aluminium oxide.

Question 5 (0620/41_Summer_2018_Q3) - Making Use Of Metals

This question is about iron.

(a) Three of the raw materials added to a blast furnace used to extract iron from hematite are coke, hematite and limestone. Name one other raw material added to the blast furnace. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
(Hot) air

(b) A series of reactions occurs in a blast furnace during the extraction of iron from hematite. Describe these reactions. Include: one chemical equation for the reduction of hematite, one chemical equation for the formation of slag. [5]

Tap to reveal answer
First, coke burns in hot air to produce heat and carbon dioxide (C + O₂ → CO₂).
The carbon dioxide is then reduced by more hot coke to form carbon monoxide (C + CO₂ → 2CO).
The carbon monoxide then reduces the hematite (iron(III) oxide) to iron: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂.
Limestone decomposes to form calcium oxide (CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂), which reacts with silica impurities to form slag.
Equation for slag formation: CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃.

(c) The iron extracted from hematite using a blast furnace is impure. Identify the main impurity in this iron and explain how it is removed in the steel-making process. [3]

main impurity

how it is removed

Tap to reveal answer
Main impurity: Carbon.
How it is removed: Oxygen is blown through the molten iron. The carbon reacts with the oxygen to form carbon dioxide gas, which escapes.

(b) The Mᵣ of SnO₂ is 151. Calculate the percentage of tin by mass in SnO₂. [1]

(f) Copper(II) nitrate, Cu(NO₃)₂, decomposes when it is heated. The only solid product is copper(II) oxide, CuO. There are two gaseous products. One of the gaseous products is oxygen.

(i) Describe a test for oxygen. [2]

(g) Iron does not rust when it is completely coated with zinc. When the zinc is scratched, the iron still does not rust.

(i) Explain why the iron does not rust when it is completely coated with zinc. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

(ii) Explain why the iron still does not rust when the zinc is scratched. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
This is sacrificial protection. Zinc is more reactive than iron. Therefore, the zinc loses electrons and corrodes/oxidises in preference to the iron.
