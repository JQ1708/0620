Question 1 (0620/41_Winter_2017_Q3) - Redox Reactions, Making Use Of Metals

(a) When magnesium is added to aqueous copper(II) sulfate a reaction occurs. The ionic equation for the reaction is shown.

Mg + Cu²⁺ → Mg²⁺ + Cu

(i) Give one change you would observe during this reaction. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
A brown/orange solid forms, and/or the blue solution becomes paler/colourless.

(ii) Explain why this is a redox reaction. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
It is a redox reaction because magnesium is oxidised (loses electrons) and copper(II) ions are reduced (gain electrons).

(iii) Identify the oxidising agent in this reaction. Give a reason for your answer. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The oxidising agent is the copper(II) ion (Cu²⁺) because it gains electrons (is reduced).

(iv) A redox reaction occurs when magnesium is heated with iron(III) oxide. Write a chemical equation for the reaction between magnesium and iron(III) oxide. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

3Mg + Fe₂O₃ → 3MgO + 2Fe

(b) The metal iron and the alloy steel are commonly used materials. A problem with them is that they rust.

(i) How does painting iron and steel prevent rusting? [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Painting provides a barrier to prevent air/oxygen and water from reaching the surface of the iron/steel.

(ii) Magnesium blocks can be attached to the bottom of steel boats. Explain how the magnesium blocks prevent the whole of the bottom of the boat from rusting. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
This is sacrificial protection. Magnesium is more reactive than iron/steel, so the magnesium corrodes preferentially (sacrificially) instead of the steel.

(iii) Replacing the magnesium blocks with copper blocks does not prevent rusting. Explain why the copper blocks do not prevent rusting. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Copper is less reactive than iron/steel, so it cannot provide sacrificial protection.

Question 5 (0620/41_Summer_2018_Q3) - Making Use Of Metals

This question is about iron.

(a) Three of the raw materials added to a blast furnace used to extract iron from hematite are coke, hematite and limestone. Name one other raw material added to the blast furnace. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
(Hot) air

(b) A series of reactions occurs in a blast furnace during the extraction of iron from hematite. Describe these reactions. Include: one chemical equation for the reduction of hematite, one chemical equation for the formation of slag. [5]

Tap to reveal answer
First, coke burns in hot air to produce heat and carbon dioxide (C + O₂ → CO₂).
The carbon dioxide is then reduced by more hot coke to form carbon monoxide (C + CO₂ → 2CO).
The carbon monoxide then reduces the hematite (iron(III) oxide) to iron: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂.
Limestone decomposes to form calcium oxide (CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂), which reacts with silica impurities to form slag.
Equation for slag formation: CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃.

(c) The iron extracted from hematite using a blast furnace is impure. Identify the main impurity in this iron and explain how it is removed in the steel-making process. [3]

main impurity

how it is removed

Tap to reveal answer
Main impurity: Carbon.
How it is removed: Oxygen is blown through the molten iron. The carbon reacts with the oxygen to form carbon dioxide gas, which escapes.
