Question 25 (0620/43_Winter_2022_Q3) - Making Use Of Metals, Acids And Bases, Atoms Combining

Aluminium is extracted from its ore by electrolysis.

(a) Name the ore of aluminium which consists mainly of aluminium oxide. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Bauxite

(b) State what is meant by the term electrolysis. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Electrolysis is the breakdown of an ionic compound, when in a molten or aqueous state, by the passage of electricity.

(c) Electrolysis is carried out on aluminium oxide dissolved in molten cryolite.

(i) Give two reasons why the electrolysis is carried out on aluminium oxide dissolved in molten cryolite instead of electrolysing molten aluminium oxide only. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
1. It lowers the operating temperature, which saves energy costs.
2. It improves the conductivity of the electrolyte.

(ii) Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction occurring at the negative electrode. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al

(iii) The positive electrodes are made of carbon. Explain why the positive carbon electrodes are replaced regularly. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The oxygen produced at the positive electrodes reacts with the carbon anodes at high temperatures, forming carbon dioxide and causing them to burn away.

(d) Aluminium is more reactive than copper. When aluminium is added to aqueous copper(II) sulfate, no immediate reaction is seen. Explain why. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Aluminium is protected by a thin but strong, unreactive layer of aluminium oxide.

(e) Aluminium reacts with oxygen to form an amphoteric oxide.

(i) State what is meant by the term amphoteric. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
An amphoteric substance is one that can react with and neutralise both acids and alkalis.

(ii) The reaction between aluminium oxide and aqueous sodium hydroxide forms a salt containing the negative ion AlO₂⁻. The only other product is water. Write a chemical equation for the reaction between aluminium oxide and aqueous sodium hydroxide. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO₂ + H₂O

(f) Gallium is in the same group as aluminium and forms similar compounds. Predict the formulae of: [2]

gallium(III) chloride

gallium(III) sulfate.

Tap to reveal answer
Gallium(III) chloride: GaCl₃
Gallium(III) sulfate: Ga₂(SO₄)₃

Question 30 (0620/43_Winter_2023_Q5) - Atoms Combining, Making Use Of Metals, The Behavior Of Metals

This question is about iron.

(a) (i) Describe the bonding in a metallic element such as iron. You may include a labelled diagram as part of your answer. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
Metallic bonding consists of a regular lattice of positive ions surrounded by a 'sea' of delocalised electrons. There are strong electrostatic forces of attraction between the positive ions and the mobile electrons.

(ii) Explain why iron conducts electricity when it is solid. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The delocalised electrons are mobile and free to move throughout the structure, carrying charge.

(b) Iron is extracted from hematite in the blast furnace as shown in Fig. 5.1.

(i) Give two reasons why coke is added to the blast furnace. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
1. It acts as a fuel to provide a high temperature.
2. It acts as a reducing agent (by forming carbon monoxide).

(ii) Explain how limestone removes the impurities in the hematite. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Limestone thermally decomposes to form calcium oxide (a base). The calcium oxide then reacts with the acidic impurity, silicon(IV) oxide, to form molten slag (calcium silicate).

(iii) Hematite contains iron(III) oxide. Write a symbol equation for the conversion of iron(III) oxide to iron in the blast furnace. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂

(iv) Suggest why the iron produced in the blast furnace is molten. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The temperature inside the furnace is above the melting point of iron.

(c) Most iron is converted into steel. Steel is an alloy. Steel is more useful than pure iron because it is harder and stronger. Explain why the structure of alloys causes them to be harder and stronger than pure metals. You may include a diagram as part of your answer. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Alloys contain atoms of different sizes. This disrupts the regular layers of atoms found in a pure metal, making it more difficult for the layers to slide over each other.

(d) Iron forms rust. Rusting is prevented by coating iron with zinc.

(i) Name the substances that react with iron to form rust. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Oxygen and water.

(ii) Name the process in which zinc is used to coat iron to prevent rusting. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Galvanising

(iii) Explain how the coating of zinc prevents rusting if the zinc is not scratched. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
It provides a protective barrier that stops oxygen and water from reaching the iron.

(iv) When zinc is scratched the iron becomes exposed. Explain how the zinc continues to prevent rusting. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it is preferentially oxidised (corrodes) instead of the iron.
