Question 15

(0620/41_Winter_2023_Q5) - Energy Changes And Reversible Reactions, Air And Water

Methane reacts with steam to produce hydrogen gas.

CH₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + 3H₂(g)     ΔH = +200 kJ/mol

The reaction takes place at 1000 °C and 100 kPa pressure.

(a) The reaction is reversible and reaches an equilibrium in a closed system.
State two features of an equilibrium. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
1. The rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction.
2. The concentrations of reactants and products are constant.

(b) State and explain, in terms of equilibrium, what happens to the concentration of hydrogen when:

(i) the pressure is increased [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The concentration of hydrogen decreases. The equilibrium shifts to the left because there are fewer moles of gas on the left-hand side (2 moles) compared to the right-hand side (4 moles).

(ii) the temperature is increased [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The concentration of hydrogen increases. The equilibrium shifts to the right because the forward reaction is endothermic and absorbs the added heat.

(iii) a catalyst is used. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
There is no change to the concentration of hydrogen. A catalyst increases the rate of both the forward and reverse reactions equally but does not affect the position of equilibrium.

(c) Methane is a greenhouse gas which contributes to global warming.

(i) Name a greenhouse gas found in clean, dry air. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
carbon dioxide

(ii) Explain, in terms of thermal energy, how greenhouse gases cause global warming. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
Greenhouse gases absorb thermal energy (infrared radiation) that is reflected or emitted from the Earth's surface. They prevent this energy from escaping into space, which leads to an increase in the Earth's atmospheric temperature.
