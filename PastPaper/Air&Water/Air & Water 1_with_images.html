Question 1

(0620/41_Winter_2018_Q3) - Acids And Bases, Air And Water, Using Moles

(a) Copper(II) nitrate decomposes when heated. Two gases, oxygen and nitrogen dioxide, and a solid are made in the reaction.

A sample of copper(II) nitrate was decomposed using the apparatus shown.

(i) Complete the chemical equation for the reaction. [2]

2Cu(NO₃)₂ → O₂ + ......NO₂ + ...................................

Tap to reveal answer
The complete chemical equation is 2Cu(NO₃)₂ → O₂ + 4NO₂ + 2CuO. The products are oxygen, nitrogen dioxide, and copper(II) oxide.

(ii) Only oxygen gas is collected at X.
Explain why. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Nitrogen dioxide is an acidic gas and therefore reacts with the aqueous sodium hydroxide, which is an alkali. Oxygen does not react and is collected over water.

(b) Nitrogen dioxide and other oxides of nitrogen are formed in car engines.
Explain how nitrogen dioxide is formed in car engines. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
In a car engine, the high temperatures and pressures cause nitrogen and oxygen from the air to react together.

(c) A teacher heated 18.8g of copper(II) nitrate.

(i) Calculate the number of moles of copper(II) nitrate present in the 18.8g. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Mᵣ of Cu(NO₃)₂ = 64 + 2(14 + 3x16) = 188
moles = mass⁄Mᵣ = 18.8⁄188 = 0.1 mol.

(ii) Calculate the maximum number of moles of oxygen that can be made by heating 18.8 g of copper(II) nitrate. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
From the equation in (a)(i), the molar ratio of Cu(NO₃)₂ : O₂ is 2 : 1. Therefore, the moles of oxygen = 0.1 ⁄ 2 = 0.05 mol.

(iii) Calculate the maximum volume of oxygen at room temperature and pressure, in cm³, that can be made by heating 18.8 g of copper(II) nitrate. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Volume of gas = moles × 24000 cm³ = 0.05 × 24000 = 1200 cm³.

(d) A sample of copper(II) nitrate was dissolved in water to form an aqueous solution.

The aqueous solution was split into three portions. A separate test was done on each portion as shown.

testreagent addedresult
1aqueous sodium hydroxidelight blue precipitate forms
2zinc powdersolution changes from blue to colourless and a brown solid forms
3ammonia gas is produced

(i) Give the formula of the light blue precipitate formed in test 1. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The formula for the light blue precipitate is Cu(OH)₂.

(ii) Explain the changes seen in test 2. [3]

Tap to reveal answer
Zinc is more reactive than copper, so a displacement reaction occurs. The zinc displaces the copper from the solution, forming a brown solid (copper metal). The blue color of the copper(II) nitrate solution fades and becomes colourless as zinc nitrate is formed and the copper(II) ions are removed.

(iii) Identify the two reagents that must be added to the aqueous copper(II) nitrate in test 3. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The two reagents required to test for nitrate ions are aqueous sodium hydroxide and aluminium foil, followed by warming.

(e) Copper(II) nitrate can be made by reacting copper(II) carbonate with nitric acid. One of the products is carbon dioxide.

(i) Write a chemical equation for the reaction of copper(II) carbonate with nitric acid. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
CuCO₃ + 2HNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + CO₂ + H₂O. The products are copper(II) nitrate, carbon dioxide, and water.

(ii) Carbon dioxide is added to the air by living things.
Name the chemical process by which living things add carbon dioxide to the air. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The process is respiration.

(iii) Carbon dioxide is removed from the air by plants.
Name the chemical process by which plants remove carbon dioxide from the air. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The process is photosynthesis.

0.2     0.4     0.8     1.2

Tap to reveal answer

(ii) State the property of oxygen and nitrogen that allows these gases to be separated using this process. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
