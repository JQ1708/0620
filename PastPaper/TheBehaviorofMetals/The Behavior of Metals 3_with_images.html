Question 19

(0620/43_Winter_2023_Q5) - Atoms Combining, Making Use Of Metals, The Behavior Of Metals

This question is about iron.

(a) (i) Describe the bonding in a metallic element such as iron. You may include a labelled diagram as part of your answer. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Metallic bonding consists of a giant lattice of positive ions (cations) which are attracted to a surrounding sea of delocalised electrons. This strong electrostatic attraction holds the structure together.

(ii) Explain why iron conducts electricity when it is solid. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The delocalised electrons are mobile and free to move throughout the structure to carry an electrical charge.

(b) Iron is extracted from hematite in the blast furnace as shown in Fig. 5.1.

(i) Give two reasons why coke is added to the blast furnace. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

1. It acts as a fuel, burning to release heat and raise the temperature of the furnace.

2. It reacts to form carbon monoxide, which acts as the main reducing agent to reduce the iron(III) oxide.

(ii) Explain how limestone removes the impurities in the hematite. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The heat of the furnace causes the limestone to decompose into calcium oxide. This calcium oxide is a basic oxide which reacts with acidic impurities like silicon(IV) oxide to produce molten slag (calcium silicate).

(iii) Hematite contains iron(III) oxide. Write a symbol equation for the conversion of iron(III) oxide to iron in the blast furnace. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ OR Fe₂O₃ + 3C → 2Fe + 3CO

(iv) Suggest why the iron produced in the blast furnace is molten. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The temperature inside the furnace is above the melting point of iron.

(c) Most iron is converted into steel. Steel is an alloy. Steel is more useful than pure iron because it is harder and stronger. Explain why the structure of alloys causes them to be harder and stronger than pure metals. You may include a diagram as part of your answer. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Alloys contain atoms of different sizes. This distorts the regular arrangement of the layers of atoms found in a pure metal. As a result, the layers cannot slide over each other easily, making the alloy harder and stronger.

(d) Iron forms rust. Rusting is prevented by coating iron with zinc.

(i) Name the substances that react with iron to form rust. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Oxygen AND water

(ii) Name the process in which zinc is used to coat iron to prevent rusting. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Galvanising

(iii) Explain how the coating of zinc prevents rusting if the zinc is not scratched. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The zinc coating acts as a physical barrier which prevents oxygen and water from reaching the iron surface.

(iv) When zinc is scratched the iron becomes exposed. Explain how the zinc continues to prevent rusting. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

This is sacrificial protection. Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it will corrode or be oxidised in preference to the iron, thus protecting it.
