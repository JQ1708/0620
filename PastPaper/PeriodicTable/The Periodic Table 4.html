Question 26 (0620/41_Winter_2023_Q3) - The Behavior Of Metals, The Periodic Table

Order of reactivity can be determined by displacement reactions.

(a) A student investigates the reactivities of four metals by carrying out a series of experiments. Each of the metals lead, manganese, silver and zinc are added separately to aqueous metal nitrates of the other metals.

(i) Table 3.1 shows some of the results. Complete Table 3.1 and place the four metals in their order of reactivity with the most reactive first. [3]

Table 3.1
aqueous solution lead Pb manganese Mn silver Ag zinc Zn
lead(II) nitrate ✓ ✗ ✓
manganese(II) nitrate ✗ ✗ ✓
silver nitrate ✓ ✓ ✓
zinc nitrate ✗ ✗ ✗

key: ✓ = displacement reaction occurs, ✗ = displacement reaction does not occur

Tap to reveal answer

Order of reactivity:

  1. Manganese (Mn)
  2. Zinc (Zn)
  3. Lead (Pb)
  4. Silver (Ag)

(ii) Suggest why the metal nitrates and not the metal sulfates of these four metals are used as the aqueous solutions. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Because all nitrates are soluble, whereas lead(II) sulfate is insoluble which would prevent a reaction.

(iii) Write the symbol equation for the reaction between zinc and silver nitrate. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Zn + 2AgNO3 → Zn(NO3)2 + 2Ag

from colourless to orange.

(iii) The reactivity trend seen in Cl, Br and I applies to all the elements in Group VII. Use the Periodic Table to identify the Group VII element which cannot displace any other Group VII elements. [1]

Question 28 (0620/42_Winter_2023_Q2) - Atoms And Elements, The Periodic Table, Energy Changes And Reversible Reactions

Cobalt and copper are transition elements.

(a) Copper has two naturally occurring isotopes, 63Cu and 65Cu. Cobalt has only one naturally occurring isotope, 59Co.

(i) Complete Table 2.1 to show the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in the 59Co atom and the 65Cu2+ ion. [3]

Table 2.1
protonsneutronselectrons
59Co273227
65Cu2+293627

Tap to reveal answer

For 59Co: p=27, n=59-27=32, e=27.
For 65Cu2+: p=29, n=65-29=36, e=29-2=27.

(ii) Table 2.2 shows the relative abundance of the two naturally occurring isotopes of copper. Calculate the relative atomic mass of copper to one decimal place. [2]

Table 2.2
isotope63Cu65Cu
relative abundance70%30%

Tap to reveal answer

Ar = (63 x 70) + (65 x 30)⁄100 = 4410 + 1950⁄100 = 6360⁄100 = 63.6

(b) One physical property of transition elements such as copper and cobalt is that they are hard. Other metals such as lithium are softer. State two other physical properties of copper and cobalt which are significantly different from lithium. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

They have high densities and high melting points.

(c) Both copper and cobalt can form coloured compounds. Some of these compounds contain water of crystallisation.

(i) Define the term water of crystallisation. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Water of crystallisation refers to water molecules that are present within hydrated crystals.

(ii) State the colour and formula of hydrated cobalt(II) chloride crystals. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Colour: pink

Formula: CoCl2·6H2O

(iii) State the colour change seen when a few drops of water are added to anhydrous copper(II) sulfate. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

from white to blue

(iv) State how this colour change can be reversed. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

By heating the hydrated copper(II) sulfate.
