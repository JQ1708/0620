Question 12 (0620/42_Summer_2021_Q3) - Energy Changes And Reversible Reactions, Using Moles, In The Lab (chemical Test & Salt Analysis)

Sodium hydrogencarbonate is found in baking powder. When sodium hydrogencarbonate is heated it forms three products. 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂

(a) Name the type of reaction that takes place when sodium hydrogencarbonate reacts in this way. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Thermal decomposition

(b) Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed at room temperature and pressure when 12.6g of NaHCO₃ is heated using the following steps: [4]

Tap to reveal answer

1. Mass of one mole of NaHCO₃ = 23 + 1 + 12 + (3x16) = 84 g

2. Moles of NaHCO₃ = 12.6g / 84 g/mol = 0.15 mol

3. Moles of CO₂ = 0.15 mol / 2 = 0.075 mol

4. Volume of CO₂ = 0.075 mol x 24 dm³/mol = 1.8 dm³

(c) Limewater is aqueous calcium hydroxide. Carbon dioxide turns limewater milky because a white precipitate forms. Write the formula of: calcium hydroxide, the white precipitate that forms when limewater turns milky. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Calcium hydroxide: Ca(OH)₂

White precipitate: CaCO₃

Question 13 (0620/41_Summer_2021_Q5) - Acids And Bases, In The Lab (chemical Test & Salt Analysis), Reacting Masses And Chemical Equations

This question is about salts.

(a) Salts that are insoluble in water are made by precipitation. Lead(II) iodide, PbI₂, is insoluble in water. All nitrates are soluble in water. All sodium salts are soluble in water. You are provided with solid lead(II) nitrate, Pb(NO₃)₂, and solid sodium iodide, NaI. Describe how you would make a pure sample of lead(II) iodide by precipitation. Your answer should include: practical details, a chemical equation for the precipitation reaction. [5]

Tap to reveal answer

1. Dissolve both solid lead(II) nitrate and solid sodium iodide in separate beakers of water to make aqueous solutions.

2. Mix the two solutions together to form a precipitate.

3. Filter the mixture to separate the solid lead(II) iodide.

4. Wash the residue with distilled water and then dry it.

5. Equation: Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2NaI(aq) → PbI₂(s) + 2NaNO₃(aq)

(b) Nitrates decompose when heated.

(i) When hydrated zinc nitrate is heated, oxygen gas is given off. Describe a test for oxygen. [2]

test ...........................................................

observations .............................................................

Tap to reveal answer

Test with a glowing splint; it will relight.

(ii) Complete the equation for the decomposition of hydrated zinc nitrate. [2]

2Zn(NO₃)₂·6H₂O → ............ + 4NO₂ + O₂ + .....H₂O

Tap to reveal answer

2Zn(NO₃)₂·6H₂O → 2ZnO + 4NO₂ + O₂ + 12H₂O

(c) Some sulfates are hydrated. When hydrated sodium sulfate crystals, Na₂SO₄·xH₂O, are heated, they give off water. Na₂SO₄·xH₂O(s) → Na₂SO₄(s) + xH₂O(g). A student carries out an experiment to determine the value of x in Na₂SO₄·xH₂O.

step 1 Hydrated sodium sulfate crystals are weighed.

step 2 The hydrated sodium sulfate crystals are then heated.

step 3 The remaining solid is weighed.

(i) Describe how the student can check that all the water has been given off. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Heat the sample again, allow it to cool, and then weigh it. Repeat this process until the mass is constant.

(ii) In an experiment, 1.61 g of Na₂SO₄·xH₂O is heated until all the water is given off. The mass of Na₂SO₄ remaining is 0.71g. Determine the value of x. [4]

Tap to reveal answer

Moles of Na₂SO₄ = 0.71g / 142 g/mol = 0.005 mol

Mass of H₂O = 1.61g - 0.71g = 0.90 g

Moles of H₂O = 0.90g / 18 g/mol = 0.05 mol

Value of x (ratio of moles H₂O:Na₂SO₄) = 0.05 / 0.005 = 10

Question 15 (0620/43 Winter 2021 Q5) - Acids And Bases, In The Lab (chemical Test & Salt Analysis)

(a) Dilute sulfuric acid and aqueous sodium hydroxide can be used to prepare sodium sulfate crystals using a method that involves titration.

20.0 cm³ of dilute sulfuric acid neutralises 25.0 cm³ of 1.00mol/dm³ aqueous sodium hydroxide. At the end of the titration the conical flask contains aqueous sodium sulfate with the dissolved indicator as an impurity.

(i) Suggest why universal indicator is not suitable for this titration. [1]

(ii) Name an indicator that can be used in this titration. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

(b) Describe how to prepare a pure sample of sodium sulfate crystals from the original solutions of dilute sulfuric acid and aqueous sodium hydroxide of the same concentrations. You are not required to give details of how to carry out the titration. [5]

Tap to reveal answer

2. Gently heat the solution to evaporate some of the water until the point of crystallisation is reached.

4. Filter the crystals from the solution.

5. Wash with a little cold water and dry the crystals.
