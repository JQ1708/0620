start colour ...........................................................

(b) Copper(II) carbonate reacts with dilute nitric acid. One of the products of the reaction is a solution of copper(II) nitrate.

(i) Describe tests for copper(II) ions and nitrate ions. Include the results of the tests. [4]

copper(II) ions

(ii) Copper(II) nitrate undergoes thermal decomposition. Balance the chemical equation for the thermal decomposition of copper(II) nitrate. [1]

.....Cu(NO₃)₂ → ......CuO + ......NO₂ + .....O₂

(c) Nitrogen dioxide, NO₂, exists in equilibrium with dinitrogen tetroxide, N₂O₄. Nitrogen dioxide is brown and dinitrogen tetroxide is colourless.

2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g)
brown              colourless

(i) A sample of nitrogen dioxide and dinitrogen tetroxide at equilibrium was placed in a closed gas syringe. The syringe plunger was pushed in. This increased the pressure in the gas syringe. The temperature was kept constant.

State how the colour of the gas in the syringe changed. Explain your answer in terms of the position of the equilibrium. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

The colour of the gas becomes paler. According to Le Chatelier's principle, when the pressure is increased, the equilibrium moves to the right, favouring the side with fewer moles of gas to counteract the change.

(ii) A sealed tube containing nitrogen dioxide and dinitrogen tetroxide at equilibrium was cooled in an ice bath at constant pressure. The contents of the tube became paler. Suggest an explanation for this observation in terms of the position of the equilibrium. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

As the temperature is decreased, the equilibrium moved to the right, producing more colourless N₂O₄. This indicates that the forward reaction is exothermic, as the system tries to counteract the cooling by releasing heat.

Question 2 (0620/42_Winter_2017_Q5) - In The Lab (chemical Test & Salt Analysis), Energy Changes And Reversible Reactions

(a) Aqueous potassium chromate(VI), K₂CrO₄, is a yellow solution. Aqueous potassium dichromate(VI), K₂Cr₂O₇, is an orange solution. The two compounds interconvert when the pH of the solution changes.

2K₂CrO₄ + H₂SO₄ ⇌ K₂Cr₂O₇ + K₂SO₄ + H₂O
(yellow)                           (orange)

Solution Y is a mixture of aqueous potassium chromate(VI) and aqueous potassium dichromate(VI) at equilibrium.

  • Explain, in terms of the position of the equilibrium, what you would see if sulfuric acid were added to solution Y.
  • Explain, in terms of the position of the equilibrium, what you would see if sodium hydroxide were added to solution Y.

[5]

Tap to reveal answer

If sulfuric acid is added, the concentration of the acid has increased, so the equilibrium moves to the right-hand side to use up the added acid. This would cause the solution to become more orange.

If sodium hydroxide is added, it reacts with and neutralises the sulfuric acid. This decrease in acid concentration causes the equilibrium to move to the left-hand side to produce more acid. This would cause the solution to become more yellow.

(b) Hydrogen can be manufactured using a reversible reaction between methane and steam.

(i) What volume of hydrogen is produced from 100 cm³ of methane under these conditions? [2]

Tap to reveal answer

The volume produced is 210 cm³.

(Based on the stoichiometry, 100 cm³ of methane would produce 300 cm³ of hydrogen at 100% yield. A 70% yield is 0.70 x 300 cm³).

(ii) If the pressure is increased, the yield of hydrogen becomes less than 70%. Explain why, in terms of the position of the equilibrium. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The yield decreases because increasing the pressure shifts the equilibrium position to the side with fewer moles of gas, which is the left-hand side (2 moles of gas on the left vs 4 on the right).

(iii) If the temperature is decreased, the yield of hydrogen decreases. What does this information indicate about the reaction between methane and steam? [1]

Tap to reveal answer

This indicates that the forward reaction is endothermic.

(iv) Why is a catalyst used in this reaction? [1]

Tap to reveal answer

A catalyst is used to increase the rate of reaction.

Question 3 (0620/43_Winter_2018_Q4) - Using Moles, Separating Substances, In The Lab (chemical Test & Salt Analysis)

(a) Dilute sulfuric acid and aqueous potassium hydroxide can be used to make potassium sulfate crystals using a method that includes titration.

A student titrated 25.0 cm³ of 0.0500 mol/dm³ aqueous potassium hydroxide with dilute sulfuric acid in the presence of an indicator. The volume of dilute sulfuric acid needed to neutralise the aqueous potassium hydroxide was 20.0 cm³.

The equation for the reaction is shown.

H₂SO₄ + 2KOH → K₂SO₄ + 2H₂O

Determine the concentration of the dilute sulfuric acid. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

1. Moles of KOH used = (25.0 ÷ 1000) dm³ x 0.0500 mol/dm³ = 0.00125 mol.

2. Moles of H₂SO₄ needed = 0.00125 mol ÷ 2 = 0.000625 mol (from 1:2 molar ratio).

3. Concentration of H₂SO₄ = 0.000625 mol ÷ (20.0 ÷ 1000) dm³ = 0.03125 mol/dm³.

(b) After the titration has been completed, the conical flask contains an aqueous solution of potassium sulfate and some of the dissolved indicator. Describe how to prepare a pure, dry sample of potassium sulfate crystals from new solutions of dilute sulfuric acid and aqueous potassium hydroxide of the same concentrations as used in the titration. Include a series of key steps in your answer. [5]

Tap to reveal answer

1. Repeat the experiment using the same volumes of acid and alkali but without the indicator.

2. Gently heat the resulting solution to evaporate some of the water until the solution becomes saturated (point of crystallisation).

3. Leave the solution to cool slowly, allowing crystals to form.

4. Filter the crystals from the solution.

5. Wash the crystals with a small amount of cold distilled water and then dry them, for example, by pressing between sheets of filter paper.

(ii) The chemical equation for the reaction can be represented as shown.

Use the bond energies in the table to calculate the energy change, in kJ/mol, which occurs when one mole of NH₃ reacts. [4]

Tap to reveal answer

Bonds broken: [4 x 3 x N-H] + [5 x O=O] = [12 x 391] + [5 x 498] = 4692 + 2490 = 7182 kJ

Energy change (for 4 moles NH₃) = 7182 - 7916 = -734 kJ

Energy change per mole of NH₃ = -734 ÷ 4 = -183.5 kJ/mol
