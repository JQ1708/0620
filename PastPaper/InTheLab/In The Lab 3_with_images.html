Question 17 (0620/41_Winter_2022_Q2) - Atoms And Elements, In The Lab (chemical Test & Salt Analysis), Acids And Bases

Potassium is a Group I element.

(a) Name and describe the bonding in potassium. [4]

name ...........................................................

description .............................................................

Tap to reveal answer

Name: metallic bonding.

Description: It consists of a giant lattice of positive potassium ions surrounded by a sea of delocalised electrons. There is a strong electrostatic attraction between the positive ions and the delocalised electrons.

(b) Potassium combines with sulfur to form an ionic compound, potassium sulfide, K₂S.

(i) Give two physical properties of ionic compounds. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

1. They have high melting and boiling points.

2. They can conduct electricity when molten or dissolved in water, but not as solids.

(Other possible answers include: they are often soluble in water and are crystalline solids).

(ii) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement and charges of the ions in potassium sulfide. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Each of the two potassium ions (K) should have a charge of 1+ and show 8 electrons in its outer shell (originally it has one, which it loses). The sulfide ion (S) should have a charge of 2- and show 8 electrons in its outer shell (6 of its own and 2 from the potassium atoms).

(iii) Write a chemical equation for the reaction which takes place when sodium burns in air to form sodium oxide. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

(iv) Complete the dot-and-cross diagram to show the electron arrangement and charges of the ions in sodium oxide. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Each of the two sodium ions (Na) should have a charge of 1+ and an empty outer shell. The oxide ion (O) should have a charge of 2- and a full outer shell with 6 of its own electrons and 2 electrons from the sodium atoms.

(c) Sodium reacts vigorously with water to form aqueous sodium hydroxide, NaOH, which is a strong base.

(i) Explain in terms of proton transfer what is meant by a base. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

A base is a proton acceptor.

(ii) State a pH number that indicates the presence of a strong alkali. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

pH 13 or 14.

(iii) State the colour of methyl orange in aqueous sodium hydroxide. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Yellow

(iv) The equation for the reaction is shown. 2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g). Calculate the concentration of NaOH(aq) formed, in g/dm³, when 0.345 g of sodium is added to 50.0 cm³ of distilled water. Assume there is no change in volume. [5]

Tap to reveal answer

1. Moles of Na = 0.345g / 23 g/mol = 0.015 mol

2. Moles of NaOH = 0.015 mol (from 2:2 or 1:1 ratio)

3. Conc. of NaOH (mol/dm³) = 0.015 mol / (50.0/1000) dm³ = 0.300 mol/dm³

4. Mᵣ of NaOH = 40

5. Conc. of NaOH (g/dm³) = 0.300 mol/dm³ x 40 g/mol = 12.0 g/dm³

Question 19 (0620/43_Winter_2022_Q5) - Some Non-metals And Their Compounds, Acids And Bases, In The Lab (chemical Test & Salt Analysis)

This question is about sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄, and salts that can be made from sulfuric acid.

(a) Sulfuric acid is manufactured by the Contact process.

stage 1 Molten sulfur burns in air to produce sulfur dioxide.

stage 2 Sulfur dioxide reacts with oxygen to form sulfur trioxide, SO₃.

stage 3 Sulfur trioxide reacts with concentrated sulfuric acid to form oleum, H₂S₂O₇.

stage 4 Oleum is converted into sulfuric acid.

(i) The equation for the reaction in stage 2 is shown. 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃. State the temperature and pressure used in stage 2. Name the catalyst used in stage 2. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Temperature: 450°C

Pressure: 1–2 atm

Catalyst: Vanadium(V) oxide

(ii) Write the chemical equation for the reaction in stage 3. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

SO₃ + H₂SO₄ → H₂S₂O₇

(iii) Name the substance that reacts with oleum in stage 4. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Water

(b) Name the black solid that is produced when concentrated sulfuric acid is added to sugar, C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Carbon

(c) Dilute sulfuric acid and aqueous potassium hydroxide are used to make aqueous potassium sulfate. H₂SO₄ + 2KOH → K₂SO₄ + 2H₂O. The method includes use of the following apparatus.

(i) Calculate the volume of 0.0625 mol/dm³ dilute sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄, that completely reacts with 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ potassium hydroxide, KOH, to produce aqueous potassium sulfate. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

1. Moles of KOH = 0.100 mol/dm³ x (25.0/1000) dm³ = 0.0025 mol

2. Moles of H₂SO₄ = 0.0025 mol / 2 = 0.00125 mol

3. Volume of H₂SO₄ = 0.00125 mol / 0.0625 mol/dm³ = 0.02 dm³ = 20 cm³

(ii) The experiment is repeated using the same volume and concentration of potassium hydroxide and the same concentration of dilute sulfuric acid. In this second experiment, the product is aqueous potassium hydrogensulfate, KHSO₄. H₂SO₄ + KOH → KHSO₄ + H₂O. Use your answer to (c)(i) and the equation to deduce the volume of H₂SO₄ required. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The mole ratio is 1:1, requiring twice the moles of H₂SO₄ compared to the 1:2 ratio. Therefore, the volume required is 20 cm³ x 2 = 40 cm³.

(d) Aqueous potassium hydrogensulfate, KHSO₄(aq), contains the ions K⁺(aq), H⁺(aq) and SO₄²⁻(aq). Describe the observations in the following tests.

(i) A flame test is carried out on aqueous potassium hydrogensulfate. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Lilac flame

(ii) Solid copper(II) carbonate is added to aqueous potassium hydrogensulfate. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

There is effervescence, the green solid dissolves, and a blue solution forms.

(iii) An acidic solution containing aqueous barium ions, Ba²⁺(aq), is added to aqueous potassium hydrogensulfate. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

A white precipitate forms.

(e) Write the ionic equation for the reaction in (d)(iii). Include state symbols. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s)

Question 20 (0620/43_Summer_2023_Q4) - The Speed Of A Reaction, Reacting Masses And Chemical Equations, Using Moles, In The Lab (chemical Test & Salt Analysis)

Hydrogen is produced by the reaction between zinc and dilute sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄. Zn(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂(g)

(a) A student carries out an experiment using excess zinc and dilute sulfuric acid. The student measures the volume of hydrogen produced at regular time intervals using the apparatus shown in Fig. 4.1. Lumps of zinc are used.

(i) Explain why the rate of reaction decreases as the reaction progresses. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The concentration of the sulfuric acid decreases, leading to fewer successful collisions per unit time.

(ii) Explain why the rate of reaction eventually becomes zero. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The reaction stops because all the sulfuric acid has been used up (it is the limiting reactant).

(b) The experiment is repeated using powdered zinc instead of lumps of zinc. All other conditions remain the same. Explain, in terms of collision theory, why the rate of reaction increases if powdered zinc is used. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Powdered zinc has a greater surface area than lumps of zinc. This increases the frequency of successful collisions between the reactant particles.

(c) The equation for the reaction is shown. Zn(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂(g). 25.0 cm³ of 2.00 mol/dm³ H₂SO₄(aq) is added to excess zinc. Calculate the volume of H₂ formed at room temperature and pressure (r.t.p.). The volume of one mole of any gas is 24 dm³ at r.t.p. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

1. Moles of H₂SO₄ = 2.00 mol/dm³ x (25.0/1000) dm³ = 0.0500 mol

2. Moles of H₂ = 0.0500 mol (from 1:1 ratio)

3. Volume of H₂ = 0.0500 mol x 24 dm³/mol = 1.2 dm³

(d) Hydrogen can also be produced by the reaction of zinc with dilute hydrochloric acid.

(i) Write a symbol equation for this reaction. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂

(ii) State the test for hydrogen gas. [1]

test ...........................................................

positive result .............................................................

Tap to reveal answer

Use a lighted splint; it will make a 'squeaky pop'.
