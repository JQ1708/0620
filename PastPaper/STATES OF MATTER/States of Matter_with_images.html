Question 1 (0620/42_Winter_2017_Q1) - States Of Matter

(a) Dust particles in the air move around in a random way.

(i) What term describes the random movement of the dust particles? [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The term that describes this movement is Brownian motion.

(ii) Identify the particles in the air which cause the random movement of the dust particles. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The particles in the air which cause the random movement are molecules of nitrogen and oxygen.

(iii) Explain why the dust particles move in this way.

Tap to reveal answer
The dust particles move because invisible particles in the air, such as nitrogen and oxygen, collide with or bombard the dust particles. These air particles move randomly, causing the dust to move in a random path.

(b) When chlorine gas, Cl₂, is put into a gas jar, it spreads out to fill the gas jar. When bromine gas, Br₂, is put into a gas jar, it also spreads out to fill the gas jar. The process takes longer for bromine gas than for chlorine gas.

(i) What term describes the way that the gas particles spread out? [1]

Tap to reveal answer
The term that describes the way gas particles spread out is diffusion.

(ii) Use data from the Periodic Table to explain why bromine gas takes longer to fill a gas jar than chlorine gas. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The relative molecular mass of bromine (Br₂) is 160, while the relative molecular mass of chlorine (Cl₂) is 71. Since bromine molecules are heavier, they diffuse more slowly than the lighter chlorine molecules.

(iii) Explain why increasing the temperature increases the rate at which the gas particles spread out. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Increasing the temperature gives the particles more energy, causing them to move faster. This results in a faster rate of diffusion.

Question 4 (0620/41_Summer_2019_Q2) - States Of Matter

Z is a covalent substance. In an experiment, a sample of pure solid Z was continually heated for 11 minutes.

The graph shows how the temperature of the sample of pure Z changed during the first 9 minutes.

(a) What is the melting point of pure Z? [1]

Tap to reveal answer
80 °C

(b) The sample of pure Z began to boil at 9 minutes. It was boiled for 2 minutes. Use this information to sketch on the grid how the temperature of the sample of pure Z changed between 9 minutes and 11 minutes. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
A horizontal line should be drawn from the end of the graph at minute 9 to minute 11.

(c) The sample of pure Z was continually heated between 2 minutes and 5 minutes. Explain, in terms of attractive forces, why there was no increase in the temperature of the sample of pure Z between 2 minutes and 5 minutes. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The energy supplied is used to break bonds or overcome the attraction between the molecules, rather than increasing the kinetic energy of the particles.

(d) Describe how the motion of particles of pure Z changed from 0 minutes to 2 minutes. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
The vibrations of the particles increase as they gain kinetic energy.

Tap to reveal answer
Melting point: The melting point decreases and occurs over a range of temperatures.
Boiling point: The boiling point increases.

(f) A sample of pure Z was allowed to cool from 120 °C to 20 °C. The total time taken was 8 minutes. Starting from point x, sketch on the grid how the temperature of the sample of pure Z changed between 0 minutes and 8 minutes.

Tap to reveal answer
The sketch should show a decrease from 120 °C to a horizontal line at 80 °C (freezing point). It should then show a further decrease from the horizontal line to finish at 20 °C at 8 minutes.

Question 6 (0620/41_Winter_2020_Q3) - Using Moles, In The Lab (chemical Test& Salt Analysis), States Of Matter

(NH₄)₂SO₄ + ......NaOH → .....NH₃ + ...........H₂O + Na₂SO₄

Tap to reveal answer
(NH₄)₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → 2NH₃ + 2H₂O + Na₂SO₄

(b) A 2.8g sample of impure ammonium sulfate is found to contain 0.7 g of impurities. Calculate the percentage of ammonium sulfate in this sample. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Mass of pure ammonium sulfate = 2.8 g - 0.7 g = 2.1 g.
Percentage = (2.1⁄2.8) x 100 = 75%

(c) Describe a test for ammonia gas. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Test: Place damp red litmus paper at the mouth of the test tube.
Result: The litmus paper turns blue.

(d) Ammonia gas is prepared at the front of a laboratory. The pungent smell of ammonia spreads throughout the laboratory slowly.

(i) Name the process that occurs when ammonia gas spreads throughout the laboratory. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Diffusion.

(ii) Explain, using ideas about particles, why ammonia gas spreads throughout the laboratory. [2]

Tap to reveal answer
Particles move randomly from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration.

(iii) Explain why carbon dioxide gas, CO₂, will spread throughout the laboratory at a slower rate than ammonia gas, NH₃. [1]

Tap to reveal answer
Carbon dioxide molecules are heavier (Mr = 44) than ammonia molecules (Mr = 17) and therefore diffuse more slowly.
