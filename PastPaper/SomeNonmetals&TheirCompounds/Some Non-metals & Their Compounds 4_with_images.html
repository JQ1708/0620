Over 200 million tonnes of sulfuric acid are manufactured every year.

(a) State the name of the process used to manufacture sulfuric acid. [1]

(b) Part of the manufacture of sulfuric acid involves converting sulfur dioxide to sulfur trioxide.

(i) Describe two methods by which sulfur dioxide is obtained. [2]

2. By roasting sulfide ores (like zinc blende) in air.

(ii) State two features of an equilibrium. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

(v) Explain in terms of collision theory why reducing the temperature decreases the rate of the forward reaction. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Reducing the temperature decreases the kinetic energy of the particles. This leads to a lower frequency of collisions between particles. Additionally, a lower proportion of the collisions have energy greater than or equal to the activation energy, resulting in fewer successful collisions.

(c) Sulfuric acid contains SO₄²⁻ ions. The oxidation number of O atoms in SO₄²⁻ ions is –2. Determine the oxidation number of S atoms in SO₄²⁻ ions. Show your working. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Let the oxidation number of sulfur be S.

The overall charge of the ion is -2.

S + (4 × -2) = -2

S - 8 = -2

S = +6

The oxidation number of sulfur is +6.

Question 26

(0620/43_Summer_2023_Q6) - Some Non-metals And Their Compounds, Acids And Bases

This question is about nitrogen and compounds of nitrogen.

(a) Ammonia is manufactured by the reaction between nitrogen and hydrogen in the Haber process.

(i) State the source of nitrogen for the Haber process. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The source of nitrogen is the air.

(ii) State the source of hydrogen for the Haber process. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The source of hydrogen is methane (from natural gas) or water.

(iii) State the typical conditions used in the Haber process. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Temperature: 450 °C

Pressure: 200 atm

(iv) Name the catalyst used in the Haber process. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The catalyst is iron.

(v) State what is meant by the term catalyst. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

A catalyst is a substance that increases the rate of a reaction and remains unchanged at the end of the reaction.

(b) Ammonia is converted into nitric acid.

(i) The first stage is the conversion of ammonia into nitrogen monoxide, NO. The forward reaction is exothermic. Using explanations that do not involve cost: explain why a temperature less than 900 °C is not used [2] and explain why a pressure greater than 7 atm is not used. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

A temperature less than 900 °C is not used because the rate of reaction would be too low.

A pressure greater than 7 atm is not used because the position of equilibrium would shift to the left-hand side (towards reactants), as there are fewer moles of gas on the reactant side (9 moles) compared to the product side (10 moles).

(ii) In the second stage, nitrogen monoxide reacts with water and oxygen to produce nitric acid. Balance the symbol equation for the reaction. [1]

....NO + .....O₂ + .....H₂O → .....HNO₃

Tap to reveal answer

4NO + 3O₂ + 2H₂O → 4HNO₃

(c) A student makes aqueous copper(II) nitrate by adding an excess of solid copper(II) carbonate to dilute nitric acid.

(i) Write the symbol equation for this reaction. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

CuCO₃ + 2HNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + CO₂ + H₂O

(ii) State two observations that indicate the copper(II) carbonate is in excess. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

1. Effervescence stops even when more copper(II) carbonate is added.

2. There is undissolved solid remaining at the bottom of the beaker.

(iii) Name one compound, other than copper(II) carbonate, that can be added to dilute nitric acid to produce aqueous copper(II) nitrate. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Copper(II) oxide or copper(II) hydroxide.

(v) Explain, using collision theory, why the rate of the reaction is slower at the decreased temperature. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

At a decreased temperature, particles have less kinetic energy, so they move slower. This leads to a lower frequency of collisions. Also, fewer particles have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy, meaning fewer collisions are successful.
