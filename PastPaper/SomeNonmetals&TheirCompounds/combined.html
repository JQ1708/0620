(c) Silicon has a giant structure which is similar to the structure of diamond.

(i) Name the type of bond which is present between silicon atoms in silicon. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The type of bond is covalent.

(ii) Suggest two physical properties of silicon. Use your knowledge of structure and bonding to explain why silicon has these physical properties. [4]

Tap to reveal answer

Property 1: High melting/boiling point.

Reason 1: The strong covalent bonds between atoms require a large amount of energy to break.

Property 2: Does not conduct electricity.

(d) Samples of air taken from industrial areas are found to contain small amounts of carbon monoxide.

(i) Explain how this carbon monoxide is formed. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Carbon monoxide is formed from the incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels (like fossil fuels) when there is an insufficient supply of oxygen.

(ii) State why carbon monoxide should not be inhaled. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Carbon monoxide is toxic because it binds irreversibly to haemoglobin in red blood cells, reducing their ability to transport oxygen.

(e) Carbon dioxide, CO₂, is a gas at room temperature and pressure, whereas silicon(IV) oxide, SiO₂, is a solid.

(i) Name the type of structure which the following compounds have.

carbon dioxide [1]

silicon(IV) oxide [1]

Silicon(IV) oxide: Giant covalent (macromolecular) structure.

(ii) Use your knowledge of structure and bonding to explain why carbon dioxide is a gas at room temperature and pressure, whereas silicon(IV) oxide is a solid. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Carbon dioxide has a simple molecular structure with weak intermolecular forces of attraction between molecules, which require little energy to overcome. Silicon(IV) oxide has a giant covalent structure with strong covalent bonds throughout the lattice, which require a large amount of energy to break.

(0620/42_Summer_2017_Q3) - Some Non-metals And Their Compounds, The Speed Of A Reaction

This question is about nitrogen and some of its compounds.

(a) Nitrogen in the air can be converted into ammonia by the Haber process. The chemical equation for the reaction is shown.

N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃

(i) State the temperature and pressure used in the Haber process. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

Temperature: 450°C

Pressure: 200 atmospheres

(ii) Name the catalyst used in the Haber process. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The catalyst used is iron.

(b) The ammonia produced in the Haber process can be oxidised to nitrogen(II) oxide at 900°C. The reaction is exothermic.

(i) Balance the chemical equation for this reaction. [2]

4NH₃ + .....O₂ ⇌ .....NO + .....H₂O

Tap to reveal answer

4NH₃ + 5O₂ ⇌ 4NO + 6H₂O

(ii) Suggest a reason, other than cost, why a temperature greater than 900°C is not used. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

Since the reaction is exothermic, a higher temperature would shift the equilibrium to the left, resulting in a lower yield of nitrogen(II) oxide.

(iii) Suggest a reason why a temperature less than 900°C is not used. [1]

Tap to reveal answer

A lower temperature would result in a slower rate of reaction.

(c) Nitrogen(II) oxide can be reacted with oxygen and water to produce nitric acid as the only product. Write a chemical equation for this reaction. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

4NO + 3O₂ + 2H₂O → 4HNO₃

(d) Describe how you would prepare a pure dry sample of copper(II) nitrate crystals in the laboratory using dilute nitric acid and solid copper(II) carbonate. Include a series of key steps in your answer. You should include a chemical equation for the reaction. [6]

Tap to reveal answer

Step 1: Add solid copper(II) carbonate to dilute nitric acid until it is in excess (i.e., until no more effervescence is seen and solid remains).

Step 2: Filter the mixture to remove the excess unreacted copper(II) carbonate.

Step 3: Gently heat the filtrate (the copper(II) nitrate solution) to evaporate some of the water and form a saturated solution.

Step 4: Allow the saturated solution to cool slowly to allow crystals to form.

Step 5: Filter the crystals, wash them with a small amount of cold distilled water, and then dry them (e.g., between filter papers or in a warm oven).

Equation: CuCO₃ + 2HNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + CO₂ + H₂O

(d) In a titration, a student added 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ aqueous sodium hydroxide to a conical flask.

(i) What was the colour of the methyl orange in the aqueous sodium hydroxide? [1]

Tap to reveal answer

The colour would be yellow.

(ii) Determine the concentration of the dilute sulfuric acid in g/dm³. [4]

Tap to reveal answer

Moles of NaOH: 0.200 mol/dm³ × (25.0⁄1000) dm³ = 0.00500 mol.

Moles of H₂SO₄: From the equation (2NaOH + H₂SO₄), the ratio is 2:1. So, moles of H₂SO₄ = 0.00500⁄2 = 0.00250 mol.

Concentration of H₂SO₄ (mol/dm³): 0.00250 mol⁄(20.0⁄1000) dm³ = 0.125 mol/dm³.

Mᵣ of H₂SO₄: 98.

Concentration of H₂SO₄ (g/dm³): 0.125 mol/dm³ × 98 g/mol = 12.25 g/dm³.

(e) Iron(II) sulfate decomposes when heated strongly. 15.20 g of FeSO₄(s) was heated and formed 4.80 g of Fe₂O₃(s). Calculate the percentage yield for this reaction. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

Moles of FeSO₄ used: 15.20 g⁄152 g/mol = 0.100 mol.

Theoretical moles of Fe₂O₃: From the equation (2FeSO₄ → Fe₂O₃), the ratio is 2:1. So, theoretical moles of Fe₂O₃ = 0.100⁄2 = 0.0500 mol.

Theoretical mass of Fe₂O₃: 0.0500 mol × 160 g/mol = 8.00 g.

Actual moles of Fe₂O₃ produced: 4.80 g⁄160 g/mol = 0.0300 mol.

Percentage yield: (Actual yield⁄Theoretical yield) × 100 = (4.80 g⁄8.00 g) × 100 = 60%.

(b) Ammonia is converted into nitric acid.

(i) The first stage is the conversion of ammonia into nitrogen monoxide, NO. The forward reaction is exothermic. Using explanations that do not involve cost: explain why a temperature less than 900 °C is not used [2] and explain why a pressure greater than 7 atm is not used. [2]

Tap to reveal answer

A temperature less than 900 °C is not used because the rate of reaction would be too low.

A pressure greater than 7 atm is not used because the position of equilibrium would shift to the left-hand side (towards reactants), as there are fewer moles of gas on the reactant side (9 moles) compared to the product side (10 moles).

(v) Explain, using collision theory, why the rate of the reaction is slower at the decreased temperature. [3]

Tap to reveal answer

At a decreased temperature, particles have less kinetic energy, so they move slower. This leads to a lower frequency of collisions. Also, fewer particles have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy, meaning fewer collisions are successful.
