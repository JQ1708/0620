Organic Formulae

Organic Chemistry is the scientific study of the structure, properties, and reactions of organic compounds.

  • Organic compounds are those which contain carbon.
  • For conventional reasons, metal carbonates, carbon dioxide and carbon monoxide are not included.

Hydrocarbon

A compound that contains only hydrogen and carbon atoms.

Representing Compounds

Organic compounds can be shown using:

  • Displayed Formulae
  • General Formulae
  • Structural Formulae

Displayed Formula

The displayed formula shows the spatial arrangement of all the atoms and all the bonds in a molecule.

For example, this displayed formula tells us:

  • It has 5 carbon atoms
  • It has 12 hydrogen atoms
  • It has only single bonds

Structural Formula (Extended)

Enough information is shown to make the structure clear, but most of the actual covalent bonds are omitted. Only bonds that are important to show the structure (like in branches) are shown.

Example: Butane is CH3CH2CH2CH3

Example: 2-methylpropane is CH3CH(CH3)CH3

General Formula

An algebraic formula that can describe any member of a homologous series.

Example: The general formula for alkanes is CnH2n+2.

Homologous Series

Homologous Series

A family of compounds with similar chemical properties whose successive members differ by the addition of a -CH2- group.

Properties of a Homologous Series

Members of a homologous series have:

  • The same general formula.
  • The same functional group.
  • Similar chemical properties.
  • A gradual change in physical properties (e.g., boiling points, density).

Functional Group

An atom or group of atoms which are responsible for the characteristic reactions of a compound.

Main Homologous Series

Series Functional Group General Formula Example
Alkane C-C (single bonds) CnH2n+2 Ethane
Alkene C=C (double bond) CnH2n Ethene
Alcohol -OH (hydroxyl) CnH2n+1OH Ethanol

Saturated & Unsaturated Compounds

Saturated

Compounds where the molecules have only single bonds between carbon atoms.

Example: Alkanes

Unsaturated

Compounds where the molecules have at least one double or triple bond between carbon atoms.

Example: Alkenes

Test for Unsaturation

The test for unsaturation uses aqueous bromine or bromine water, which is an orange/brown colour.

  • Saturated compounds: No change. The bromine water stays orange/brown.
  • Unsaturated compounds: The bromine water is decolourised (it turns from orange/brown to colourless). This is an addition reaction.

Naming Organic Compounds (Nomenclature)

We use a systematic way of naming compounds, set by IUPAC. The name is usually made of parts:

  • Stem: Indicates the number of carbon atoms in the longest chain.
  • Suffix: Indicates which homologous series the compound belongs to.
  • Prefix: Indicates the position and identity of any side chains or functional groups.

Naming Alkanes

Saturated hydrocarbons with the general formula CnH2n+2. The suffix is -ane.

Methane: CH4
Ethane: C2H6
Propane: C3H8
Butane: C4H10

Naming Alkenes

Unsaturated hydrocarbons with at least one C=C double bond. General formula CnH2n. The suffix is -ene.

Ethene: C2H4
Propene: C3H6
Butene: C4H8

(Extended tier: a number can show the double bond's position, e.g. but-1-ene).

Naming Alcohols

Contain the -OH (hydroxyl) functional group. General formula CnH2n+1OH. The suffix is -ol.

Methanol: CH3OH
Ethanol: C2H5OH
Propanol: C3H7OH
Butanol: C4H9OH

Naming Carboxylic Acids

Contain the -COOH (carboxyl) functional group. General formula CnH2n+1COOH. The suffix is -oic acid.

Methanoic acid: HCOOH
Ethanoic acid: CH3COOH
Propanoic acid: C2H5COOH
Butanoic acid: C3H7COOH

Naming Esters

Formed from an alcohol and a carboxylic acid. They contain the -COO- (ester link) functional group.

Naming has two parts:

Part 1: From the ALCOHOL

Change the -anol ending to -yl.

Example: Ethanol → Ethyl

Part 2: From the CARBOXYLIC ACID

Change the -oic acid ending to -oate.

Example: Propanoic acid → Propanoate

Ethanol + Propanoic acid → Ethyl propanoate
