Water: Chemical Tests

Let's learn how to identify water and test its purity. Use the tabs below to switch between topics.

Identifying the Presence of Water

Anhydrous Cobalt(II) Chloride

This test is usually done with cobalt chloride paper.

  • Anhydrous CoCl₂ is blue.
  • Hydrated CoCl₂•6H₂O is pink.
  • The presence of water causes a colour change from blue to pink.

anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride + water → hydrated cobalt(II) chloride

CoCl₂(s) + 6H₂O(l) → CoCl₂•6H₂O(s)

Anhydrous Copper(II) Sulfate

This substance is a white powder.

  • Anhydrous CuSO₄ is white.
  • Hydrated CuSO₄•5H₂O is blue.
  • The presence of water causes a colour change from white to blue.

anhydrous copper(II) sulfate + water → hydrated copper(II) sulfate

CuSO₄(s) + 5H₂O(l) → CuSO₄•5H₂O(s)

Determining the Purity of Water

A pure substance has a sharp, fixed melting and boiling point.

  • Pure water boils at exactly 100°C.
  • Pure water freezes at exactly 0°C.

An impure substance will melt and boil over a range of temperatures, not at a fixed point.

  • Impurities raise the boiling point (e.g., > 100°C).
  • Impurities lower the freezing point (e.g., < 0°C).

Substances in Water from Natural Sources

Because water is such a good solvent, it can easily dissolve substances and become polluted. Let's explore the common culprits.

Water that flows over and through rocks dissolves small amounts of compounds. This is usually harmless and can provide beneficial minerals:

  • Calcium ions (Ca²⁺)
  • Magnesium ions (Mg²⁺)
  • Carbonate ions (CO₃²⁻)

Excess fertilisers containing nitrates and phosphates can be washed into rivers and lakes. This leads to a dangerous process called eutrophication.

  1. Fertilisers cause a rapid growth of algae, known as an algal bloom.
  2. This blocks sunlight, causing underwater plants to die.
  3. Bacteria decompose the dead plants, using up dissolved oxygen in the water.
  4. Aquatic animals like fish die due to lack of oxygen.

Industrial waste can release toxic metals into the water, which are poisonous to aquatic life and humans. Key examples include:

  • Lead
  • Mercury

If sewage is not treated properly before being discharged into water bodies, it releases harmful bacteria. These microbes can cause diseases in humans if the contaminated water is consumed.

Water Treatment

Water from natural sources needs to be treated to make it potable (safe to drink). Follow the two main steps.

1️⃣

Filtration

Water is passed through filter beds made of sand and gravel. This removes large, insoluble substances like leaves, soil, and grit.

2️⃣

Chlorination

A small amount of chlorine gas is bubbled through the water. This kills harmful bacteria and other microorganisms, preventing diseases.

Fertilisers

Fertilisers are essential for crop growth, providing three key elements. They are known as NPK fertilisers.

Nitrogen (N)

Promotes healthy leaves.

Needed to make chlorophyll and protein.

Sources:

Ammonium ions (NH₄⁺)

Nitrate ions (NO₃⁻)

Potassium (K)

Promotes healthy fruit and flowers.

Source:

Potassium ions (K⁺)

(Most common potassium compounds are water-soluble)

Phosphorus (P)

Promotes healthy roots.

Source:

Phosphate ions (PO₄³⁻)

Common Fertiliser Compounds

These compounds must contain water-soluble ions of N, P, or K.

  • Ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃ (Provides Nitrogen)
  • Ammonium phosphate, (NH₄)₃PO₄ (Provides Nitrogen and Phosphorus)
  • Potassium nitrate, KNO₃ (Provides Potassium and Nitrogen)

Proportions in Fertilisers

Different fertilisers contain different amounts of these compounds. Therefore, each type of fertiliser will have a unique proportion of Nitrogen, Phosphorus, and Potassium, designed for specific plants or soil types.

Examiner's Tip!

If you're asked to choose the most effective fertiliser from a list, look for the compound (or combination of compounds) that contains the most elements from Nitrogen, Phosphorus, and Potassium.
