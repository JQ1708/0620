The Periodic Table

A simple guide to the elements and their properties.

Structure of the Table

The periodic table arranges over 100 elements in order of increasing atomic number. This arrangement means elements with similar properties end up in the same vertical columns (Groups).

Periods (Rows)

  • The period number (1-7) tells you the number of electron shells an atom has.
  • E.g., all elements in Period 2 have 2 electron shells.

Groups (Columns)

  • The group number (1-7 & 0) tells you the number of outer electrons.
  • Elements in the same group have similar chemical properties.

Metals and Non-Metals

A staircase-like dividing line separates the metals on the left from the non-metals on the right.

Group 1: The Alkali Metals

Physical Properties & Trends (Down the Group)

  • They are soft metals (softness increases) with low densities (density generally increases).
  • They have low melting and boiling points that decrease down the group.

Chemical Properties & Trends

  • Very reactive metals that form +1 ions by losing one electron.
  • Reactivity increases down the group.
  • They react vigorously with water to produce a metal hydroxide and hydrogen gas.

Group 7: The Halogens

Physical Properties & Trends (Down the Group)

  • Exist as diatomic molecules (F₂, Cl₂, etc.).
  • Melting/boiling points increase.
  • Colours get darker.
ElementColour & State (Room Temp)
Fluorine (F₂)Pale yellow gas
Chlorine (Cl₂)Pale green gas
Bromine (Br₂)Red-brown liquid
Iodine (I₂)Grey-black solid
Astatine (At)Black solid (predicted)

Chemical Properties & Trends

  • Reactive non-metals that form -1 ions (halides) by gaining one electron.
  • Reactivity decreases down the group. This is because as the atoms get larger, the outer shell is further from the nucleus, making it harder to attract a new electron.
  • A more reactive halogen will displace a less reactive halide from its solution (e.g., chlorine displaces bromine from potassium bromide solution).

Group 0: The Noble Gases

Properties

  • They are colourless gases and are monatomic (exist as single atoms).
  • They are chemically inert (unreactive) because they have a full outer shell of electrons, which is a very stable arrangement.
  • Their boiling points increase down the group.

Uses

  • Helium is used in balloons and airships because it is lighter than air and non-flammable.
  • Neon is used in red advertising signs.
  • Argon is used in light bulbs to provide an inert atmosphere that stops the filament from burning away.

Transition Metals

Key Properties

  • They are hard, strong metals with high melting points and high densities.
  • They are much less reactive than Group 1 metals.
  • They form coloured compounds. Examples: Copper(II) compounds are blue, Iron(II) compounds are pale green, Iron(III) compounds are orange/brown.
  • They can form ions with different charges (e.g., Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺). This is also known as having variable oxidation states.
  • They are often used as catalysts. Examples: Iron in the Haber process, Nickel in the hydrogenation of alkenes.
