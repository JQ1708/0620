1. Principles of Extraction

The Earth's crust contains useful metals, but they are often chemically combined with other elements in rocks called ores. An ore is a rock containing enough metal to make it profitable to extract. Most ores are oxides (e.g. hematite and bauxite), so extracting the metal is a reduction process where oxygen is removed.

Unreactive metals are found as the uncombined element as they do not easily react with other substances due to their chemical stability. These are called native metals, like gold and platinum, which can be mined directly from the Earth.

The Reactivity Series is Key!

The position of a metal in the reactivity series determines the method of extraction, as more reactive metals hold onto oxygen more strongly.

Metal ReactivityExtraction MethodExamples
High (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al)Electrolysis of molten compoundAluminium from Bauxite
Medium (Zn, Fe, Cu)Reduction with Carbon (Blast Furnace)Iron from Hematite
Low (Ag, Au, Pt)Found as native element (uncombined)Gold mined directly

2. Case Study: Extraction of Iron

Iron is extracted from its ore, hematite (Fe₂O₃), in a huge reaction vessel called a Blast Furnace.

  • Iron Ore (Hematite): Provides the iron (Fe₂O₃).
  • Coke: Almost pure carbon. Burns to produce heat and creates the reducing agent, carbon monoxide.
  • Limestone: Calcium carbonate (CaCO₃). Removes sandy impurities from the ore.
  • Hot Air: Provides oxygen for the coke to burn, blasted in from the bottom.

A series of reactions occur at high temperatures inside the furnace:

  1. Coke burns: The reaction is very exothermic, raising the temperature to around 1500°C.

    C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g)

  2. Carbon monoxide is formed: At high temperatures, CO₂ reacts with more hot coke to form carbon monoxide, the true reducing agent.

    CO₂(g) + C(s) → 2CO(g)

  3. Iron(III) oxide is reduced: Carbon monoxide removes the oxygen from the iron ore to produce molten iron.

    Fe₂O₃(s) + 3CO(g) → 2Fe(l) + 3CO₂(g)

  4. Limestone decomposes: The heat breaks down the limestone into calcium oxide and carbon dioxide. This is thermal decomposition.

    CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g)

  5. Slag is formed: The calcium oxide (a basic oxide) reacts with acidic sandy impurities (silicon dioxide) to form molten slag (calcium silicate).

    CaO(s) + SiO₂(s) → CaSiO₃(l)

  • Molten Iron: Sinks to the bottom of the furnace as it is denser. It is tapped off. This impure iron is also called 'pig iron'.
  • Slag: Floats on top of the iron. It is tapped off separately and used for road construction and fertiliser.
  • Waste Gases: Mainly nitrogen (from the air which hasn't reacted) and carbon dioxide. They exit from the top.

3. Case Study: Extraction of Aluminium

Aluminium is too reactive to be reduced by carbon. It is extracted by electrolysis from its ore, bauxite (Al₂O₃).

The Cryolite Challenge

Aluminium oxide has a very high melting point (over 2000°C), which would be too expensive to maintain. To save energy, it is dissolved in molten cryolite. This lowers the operating temperature to around 950°C without interfering with the reaction.

At the Cathode (-)

(Graphite lining of the steel cell)

Aluminium ions gain electrons (reduction) to form molten aluminium, which collects at the bottom of the cell and is siphoned off.

Al³⁺(l) + 3e⁻ → Al(l)

At the Anode (+)

(Large graphite blocks)

Oxide ions lose electrons (oxidation) to form oxygen gas.

2O²⁻(l) → O₂(g) + 4e⁻

Problem: The Anodes Burn Away!

The hot oxygen gas produced reacts with the carbon anodes, producing carbon dioxide. This means the anodes burn away and must be replaced regularly, adding to the high cost of the process.

C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g)

Overall Reaction & Costs

The overall equation for the extraction is:

2Al₂O₃(l) → 4Al(l) + 3O₂(g)

This process is very expensive mainly due to the large amount of electricity required to melt the compounds and perform electrolysis.

Examiner Tip: OIL RIG

Use the mnemonic OIL RIG to remember what happens at the electrodes:

Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons) - Happens at the Anode.

Reduction Is Gain (of electrons) - Happens at the Cathode.
